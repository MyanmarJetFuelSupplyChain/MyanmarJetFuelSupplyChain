Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB)
Profile 8.4
Summary Assessment
Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) were the junta's primary state-owned foreign currency exchange banks and international financial gateways from the coup until their US designation in June 2023. OFAC cited their role in facilitating "the regime's profiteering from Burma's extractive industries" and "its use of foreign currency to procure arms and jet fuel abroad." Following designation, MFTB's transaction volumes collapsed from over USD 500 million in Q1 FY2023 to near zero by Q3. The junta shifted military procurement to MEB, which only Canada had sanctioned. At least 23 institutions held over 50 nostro accounts for MFTB; several confirmed termination following US sanctions. Neither MFTB nor MICB appears as the direct payment bank for aviation fuel purchases in 2023 (that was UAB), but MFTB served as the conduit for MOGE payments to oil and gas joint venture partners. Canada designated both in April 2012; the US and Australia in June 2023 and January 2024 respectively. The UK and EU have not designated either bank.

1
Role in the Myanmar Military Financial Infrastructure

MFTB and MICB enabled the junta to convert kyat to hard currency and transact with foreign entities, allowing both state enterprises such as MOGE to receive export revenues and the Ministry of Defence to purchase arms, dual-use goods, and aviation fuel abroad. The June 2023 OFAC designations explicitly cited these functions. MFTB was also directly used by MOGE to pay oil and gas joint venture partners: JFM documented over USD 11 million in Goldpetrol payments through MFTB between February and May 2022. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)

Neither bank is documented as the direct payment channel for aviation fuel purchases, which in 2023 ran through UAB. Their significance to this database is as the infrastructure that previously enabled junta procurement broadly, and whose effective isolation from the international financial system in mid-2023 pushed the junta toward MEB. (UNSR Jun 2024)

2
Impact of US June 2023 Sanctions on MFTB's Transaction Volumes

The US June 2023 designations were effective. MFTB's transactions collapsed from over USD 500 million in Q1 FY2023 to just over USD 80 million in Q2, with no incoming international payments in Q3. The junta's response was to shift military procurement entirely to MEB, which Canada alone had sanctioned at the time. This displacement is the clearest documented illustration in the supply chain of what happens when a single jurisdiction acts without coordinated follow-through from others. (UNSR Jun 2024)

3
International Correspondent Banking Relationships

The UNSR's Figure 5 documents at least 23 institutions holding over 50 MFTB nostro accounts, and at least 12 holding MICB nostro accounts. Confirmed terminations following June 2023 US sanctions: CIMB (before US sanctions, early 2023), UOB, Kasikorn Bank, Sonali Bank, and Groupe BPCE/Natixis. Not publicly confirmed as of the UNSR's June 2024 report: OCBC, DBS Bank, Maybank, Siam Commercial Bank, KTB, and others. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

MICB has been designated by Canada (SEMA, 24 April 2012), the United States (OFAC SDN, 21 June 2023) and Australia (DFAT, 31 January 2024). Neither MFTB nor MICB has been designated by the United Kingdom or the European Union as of April 2026. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report noted that under current UK and EU law, all Myanmar state-owned enterprises should be considered sanctioned given EU and UK sanctions on the SAC itself, but that both jurisdictions have failed to treat MFTB and MICB as sanctioned in practice. The UNSR described this as a "significant oversight" creating confusion for financial institutions. (UNSR Jun 2024)

5
Key Relationships
•Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3): The Myanmar state-owned bank that absorbed the junta's military procurement and gas revenue processing following the effective isolation of MFTB and MICB after June 2023 US sanctions. MEB was unsanctioned by all jurisdictions except Canada at the time of the MFTB and MICB designations and was therefore available as an alternative. By FY2023, MEB accounted for the entirety of the SAC's military procurement conducted through state-owned financial institutions. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)
•United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1): The private Myanmar bank used to process aviation fuel payments in calendar year 2023. UAB operated in parallel with and independently of MFTB and MICB. Aviation fuel procurement did not pass through MFTB or MICB in 2023; it passed through UAB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Kasikorn Bank (Thailand) (Profile 8.5): One of twelve financial institutions confirmed as holding nostro accounts for MICB, and one of at least twenty-three holding accounts for MFTB. Kasikorn Bank confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it terminated its relationships with junta-controlled banks following the June 2023 US sanctions. It is documented as having facilitated over USD 35 million in military procurement in FY2022, declining to less than USD 5 million in FY2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank (Thailand) (Profile 8.2): One of the financial institutions confirmed as holding a nostro account for MFTB. KTB is separately documented as the Thai-side receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023 and as a holder of nostro accounts for MEB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
6
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•24 April 2012. Canada designates both MFTB and MICB, the first jurisdiction to do so. Both remain functional for a further eleven years. (Sanctions data)
•21 June 2023. The US designates MFTB, MICB, and the Ministry of Defence. OFAC General License 5 allows a wind-down period to 5 August 2023. MFTB's transaction volumes collapse. CIMB, UOB, Kasikorn Bank, Sonali Bank, and Groupe BPCE/Natixis confirm termination of nostro accounts. The junta shifts procurement to MEB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•31 January 2024. Australia designates both banks. The UK and EU have not designated either bank as of April 2026. (Sanctions data)
•June 2024. The UNSR's Banking on the Death Trade report documents the volume collapse, confirms the MEB shift, and explicitly calls on the UK and EU to designate both banks. The UNSR describes their failure to act as a "significant oversight" and notes their own control tests should already render MFTB and MICB sanctioned in practice. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•May 2025. Justice for Myanmar confirms OCBC, DBS Bank, Maybank, KTB, and SCB held active nostro accounts for MFTB and/or MICB as of the UNSR's June 2024 writing. SCB denied its transactions were connected to the arms trade but did not confirm account termination. (JFM May 2025)
7
Recommended Actions
•Urge the UK and EU to designate MFTB and MICB. Canada, the US, and Australia have all designated both banks. The UK and EU have not. The UNSR explicitly called on both and described their inaction as a significant oversight, noting their own legal control tests should already treat these banks as sanctioned. This is a straightforward designation submission requiring no new evidence. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Engage OCBC, DBS Bank, Maybank, Siam Commercial Bank, and KTB on confirmed account termination. These institutions had not publicly confirmed termination of MFTB and/or MICB nostro accounts as of the UNSR's June 2024 report. SCB denied its transactions were arms-related but did not confirm account closure. Civil society and regulatory authorities in Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand should formally engage each institution requesting date-specific confirmation of termination. MAS, BNM, and the BOT are the relevant supervisory authorities. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Investigate residual MFTB and MICB international access. MFTB was still responsible for over USD 50 million in outgoing payments in Q3 FY2023 despite receiving no incoming payments, suggesting residual outgoing capacity through Russian or Indian correspondent channels. Investigative journalists and financial intelligence units should assess whether any such access remains active and whether it is being used for procurement or gas revenue processing. (UNSR Jun 2024)
