Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB)
Myanmar state-owned banks  ·  MFTB: Yangon, Myanmar  ·  MICB: Yangon, Myanmar  ·  Designated by the United States, Canada and Australia  ·  Profile 8.4
Summary Assessment
Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) are Myanmar state-owned banks that served as the junta's primary foreign currency exchange conduits and principal gateways to the international financial system from the time of the coup until their designation by the United States in June 2023. Both banks primarily functioned as foreign currency exchanges, enabling the conversion of kyat to US dollars and euros, allowing Myanmar's revenue-generating state-owned enterprises access to international markets and enabling the Ministry of Defence and other sanctioned entities to purchase arms, dual-use goods and aviation fuel abroad. The US designated both banks on 21 June 2023 under E.O. 14014, citing their role in facilitating the regime's profiteering from Burma's extractive industries and its use of foreign currency to procure arms and jet fuel abroad. Canada had previously designated both MFTB and MICB on 24 April 2012. Australia designated both on 31 January 2024. Neither bank has been designated by the United Kingdom or the European Union. Following the June 2023 US designations, international correspondent banks began withdrawing nostro account services, causing MFTB's transaction volumes to collapse: from over USD 500 million in Q1 FY2023 to just over USD 80 million in Q2, with no incoming international payments received in Q3. The junta responded by shifting military procurement to Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3), which was unsanctioned at the time. Neither MFTB nor MICB is directly documented as the payment channel for aviation fuel purchases in 2023; those payments passed through United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1), a private bank. However, MFTB was used by Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE) to pay oil and gas joint venture partners, linking it to the revenue flows that fund the junta's broader military operations including aviation fuel procurement.

1
Role in the Myanmar Military Financial Infrastructure

MFTB and MICB served as Myanmar's principal state-owned foreign currency exchange banks and the junta's primary gateways to the international financial system from the time of the coup until their designation by the United States in June 2023. Both banks functioned primarily as foreign currency exchanges, enabling the conversion of kyat to US dollars and euros. This allowed Myanmar's revenue-generating state-owned enterprises, including Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE), to access international markets through offshore accounts and to transact with foreign entities. The same infrastructure enabled the Ministry of Defence and other sanctioned military entities to purchase arms, dual-use goods and materiel from foreign sources. The US designated both banks on 21 June 2023 specifically citing their role in facilitating "the regime's profiteering from Burma's extractive industries" and its "use of foreign currency to procure arms and jet fuel abroad." (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)

MFTB was also directly used by MOGE to make payments to oil and gas joint venture partners. Justice For Myanmar's January 2025 reporting documents that MOGE paid Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company more than USD 11 million through MFTB between February and May 2022. This illustrates MFTB's role as a financial conduit for the gas revenues that constitute the junta's largest source of external income, which indirectly funds military procurement including aviation fuel purchases. (JFM Jan 2025)

Neither MFTB nor MICB is directly documented in any reviewed source as the payment bank for aviation fuel purchases. In calendar year 2023, aviation fuel payments passed through United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1), a private Myanmar bank. However, the broader financial architecture linking MFTB to gas revenue flows and military procurement situates these banks as part of the infrastructure sustaining the junta's capacity to fund aviation fuel purchases through separate channels. For cross-reference with all documented jet fuel shipments, see the master shipment table. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)

2
Impact of US June 2023 Sanctions on MFTB's Transaction Volumes

The impact of the US June 2023 designations on MFTB was immediate and severe. In Q1 FY2023 (April to June 2023, overlapping with the imposition of sanctions in June), MFTB handled over USD 500 million in transactions: over USD 180 million outgoing and over USD 310 million incoming. Following the designations, transaction volumes collapsed: in Q2 FY2023, MFTB's total volume fell to just over USD 80 million. By Q3, MFTB was receiving no incoming payments from abroad at all, and was responsible for just over USD 50 million in outgoing payments only. This near-complete collapse of MFTB's international access reflects the withdrawal of nostro account services by correspondent banks following the US designations. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The junta responded to the effective isolation of MFTB and MICB by shifting its military procurement and gas revenue processing to Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3), which remained unsanctioned by all jurisdictions except Canada at the time. By FY2023, MEB had assumed the entirety of the junta's military procurement conducted through state-owned financial institutions. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)

Institutions that confirmed termination of services are noted. (UNSR Jun 2024)

5
Key Relationships
•Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3): The Myanmar state-owned bank that absorbed the junta's military procurement and gas revenue processing following the effective isolation of MFTB and MICB after June 2023 US sanctions. MEB was unsanctioned by all jurisdictions except Canada at the time of the MFTB and MICB designations and was therefore available as an alternative. By FY2023, MEB accounted for the entirety of the SAC's military procurement conducted through state-owned financial institutions. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)
•United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1): The private Myanmar bank used to process aviation fuel payments in calendar year 2023. UAB operated in parallel with and independently of MFTB and MICB. Aviation fuel procurement did not pass through MFTB or MICB in 2023; it passed through UAB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Kasikorn Bank (Thailand) (Profile 8.5): One of twelve financial institutions confirmed as holding nostro accounts for MICB, and one of at least twenty-three holding accounts for MFTB. Kasikorn Bank confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it terminated its relationships with junta-controlled banks following the June 2023 US sanctions. It is documented as having facilitated over USD 35 million in military procurement in FY2022, declining to less than USD 5 million in FY2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank (Thailand) (Profile 8.2): One of the financial institutions confirmed as holding a nostro account for MFTB. KTB is separately documented as the Thai-side receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023 and as a holder of nostro accounts for MEB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
6
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•24 April 2012. Canada designated both MFTB and MICB under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA) on 24 April 2012, making Canada the first and for over a decade the only jurisdiction to sanction Myanmar's state-owned trade and investment banks. The designations predated the February 2021 coup by nearly nine years. (Sanctions data)
•January 2025 (reporting period: February to May 2022). Justice For Myanmar's January 2025 Fuelling Atrocities report documents that MOGE paid Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company more than USD 11 million through MFTB between February and May 2022, prior to US sanctions. This is the only documented instance in the reviewed sources of MFTB's specific role as a conduit for MOGE payments to oil and gas joint venture partners. (JFM Jan 2025)
•21 June 2023. The United States designated MFTB, MICB and the Myanmar Ministry of Defence under E.O. 14014, citing their role in facilitating the regime's profiteering from Burma's extractive industries and its use of foreign currency to procure arms and jet fuel abroad. This was the first US designation of Myanmar's state-owned trade and investment banks. OFAC simultaneously issued General License 5 (GL 5) to allow entities transacting with MFTB and MICB to wind down those relationships in an orderly fashion; GL 5 expired on 5 August 2023, after which all transactions with an MFTB or MICB nexus became prohibited without authorisation. The designations triggered withdrawal of nostro account services by multiple international correspondent banks, causing MFTB's transaction volumes to collapse from over USD 500 million in Q1 FY2023 to just over USD 80 million in Q2, with no incoming international payments in Q3. CIMB had already withdrawn from MFTB and MICB in early 2023; UOB, Kasikorn Bank, Sonali Bank and Groupe BPCE/Natixis confirmed termination following the June 2023 designations. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)
•31 January 2024. Australia designated MFTB and MICB under the Myanmar Sanctions Regime, becoming the third jurisdiction after Canada and the United States to sanction both banks. As of April 2026, the United Kingdom and the European Union have not designated MFTB or MICB. (Sanctions data)
•June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report documents the impact of the June 2023 designations on MFTB's transaction volumes in detail and confirms that the junta had shifted military procurement to MEB in response. The report explicitly recommends that the UK and EU designate MFTB and MICB, noting that their own "control" tests as applied to existing SAC sanctions should already render these banks sanctioned in practice. The UNSR describes the UK and EU's failure to designate these banks as a "significant oversight." (UNSR Jun 2024)
•May 2025. Justice For Myanmar's Fuelling Atrocities report confirms that OCBC Bank, DBS Bank, Maybank, Krung Thai Bank and Siam Commercial Bank continued to hold nostro accounts for MFTB and/or MICB as of the time of the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report. CIMB's earlier withdrawal is noted as a positive contrast. The UK and EU have not designated MFTB or MICB as of April 2026. (JFM May 2025)
7
Recommended Actions
•Urge the UK and EU to designate MFTB and MICB: Despite Canadian sanctions on MFTB dating to April 2012, and US and Australian designations in 2023 and 2024 respectively, the UK and EU have not designated either bank. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report provides a detailed evidentiary basis for designation and explicitly calls on both jurisdictions to act, noting that their own legal frameworks should already treat these banks as sanctioned. Civil society should engage UK and EU sanctions authorities with the UNSR's findings, noting that MFTB and MICB are already designated by three of the five major sanctions jurisdictions and that the UK and EU's failure to act has created the "significant oversight" identified by the UNSR. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Engage institutions that have not confirmed termination of MFTB and MICB nostro accounts: Of the institutions confirmed as holding nostro accounts for MFTB and/or MICB, a number have confirmed termination: CIMB (early 2023), UOB, Kasikorn Bank, Sonali Bank and Groupe BPCE/Natixis (all following June 2023 US sanctions). However, as of the UNSR's June 2024 report, OCBC, DBS Bank, Maybank, Siam Commercial Bank, KTB and others had not publicly confirmed termination. SCB's June 2024 statement denied that its transactions were connected to the arms trade but did not confirm termination of its MFTB and MICB nostro accounts. JFM May 2025 confirms that several of these relationships remained active as of the UNSR's writing. Civil society and regulatory authorities in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand should formally engage these institutions and request confirmation that all MFTB and MICB nostro account relationships have been terminated. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Investigate whether MFTB and MICB retain any residual international access through non-sanctioning jurisdictions: The UNSR's June 2024 data shows MFTB still responsible for over USD 50 million in outgoing payments in Q3 FY2023, despite receiving no incoming international payments. This suggests some residual outgoing transfer capacity remained even after the designations. Investigative journalists and financial intelligence units should investigate whether MFTB or MICB retain international correspondent banking access through institutions in non-sanctioning jurisdictions such as Russia or India, and whether any such access is being used to facilitate junta procurement or gas revenue processing. (UNSR Jun 2024)
