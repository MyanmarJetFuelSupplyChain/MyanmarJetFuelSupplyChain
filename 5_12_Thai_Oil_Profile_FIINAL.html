Thai Oil Public Company Limited
Profile 5.12
Summary Assessment
Thai Oil Public Company Limited is a Thai integrated refiner listed on the SET and a subsidiary of PTT Group, the Thai state-owned energy conglomerate. One confirmed post-coup shipment is documented: 4,942 MT of Jet A-1 loaded at Thai Oil's Sriracha marine terminal in May 2022 and delivered to Thilawa aboard the BHUREEMAS. Thai Oil's customer was Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. A letter obtained by Amnesty International, written by Asia Sun Trading to Myanmar's Customs Department, stated the fuel was "for use by the Tatmadaw (Air Force)," directly contradicting the civilian-use warranties Thai Oil had received. Following pre-publication engagement by Amnesty International, Thai Oil suspended Myanmar aviation fuel sales on 14 October 2022, before Deadly Cargo was published. The suspension language is explicitly conditional: "until no such concerned issue." No permanent public policy commitment has been issued. Thai Oil has not appeared in any subsequent investigated shipment. It has not been sanctioned in connection with Myanmar.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

The BHUREEMAS (IMO 9547568) loaded 4,942 MT of Jet A-1 at the Thai Oil Marine Terminal, Sriracha, between 24 and 27 May 2022 and arrived at Thilawa on 5 June 2022. Thai Oil had a sales contract dated 29 April 2022 with Asia Sun Trading for approximately 4,942 MT valued at over USD 6 million. Thai Oil's due diligence comprised World-Check One screening of Asia Sun Trading (no concerns found) and a contractual civilian-use warranty. A letter from Asia Sun Trading to Myanmar's Customs Department, obtained by Amnesty International, stated the fuel was procured "for use by the Tatmadaw (Air Force)," directly contradicting that warranty. Asia Sun Trading was subsequently designated by the UK (January 2023), the US (March 2023), and Australia (February 2024). Thai Oil's screening had found no concerns; the subsequent five-jurisdiction designation of the Asia Sun Group network illustrates the limits of sanctions-list screening as a primary due diligence tool for Myanmar supply chains. (AI Nov 2022)

Thai Oil is documented as a positive outcome of pre-publication civil society engagement. It suspended Myanmar aviation fuel sales before Deadly Cargo was published and has not appeared in any of the four subsequent Amnesty International investigations through January 2026. However, the suspension remains conditional in its stated terms and no permanent public commitment has been made. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)

Key Relationships
•Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. (Profile 1.4): Thai Oil's direct customer for the June 2022 Jet A-1 shipment. Asia Sun Trading is a Myanmar-registered entity within the Asia Sun Group network, the documented domestic importer and distributor of aviation fuel for Myanmar's military. Asia Sun Trading provided Thai Oil with contractual assurances that the fuel was for civilian use but simultaneously declared to Myanmar's Customs Department that the fuel was for use by the Tatmadaw (Air Force). Asia Sun Trading was subsequently designated by the UK (31 January 2023), the US OFAC (24 March 2023) and Australia (1 February 2024). The owner of Asia Sun Trading, Zaw Min Tun, is designated by multiple jurisdictions. The Asia Sun Group and its network are documented in Profile 1.4. (AI Nov 2022)
•BHUREEMAS (IMO 9547568): The vessel that transported the June 2022 shipment from Sriracha to Thilawa. A chemical and oil products tanker built in 2010 by East Star Shipbuilding, Nanjing, China. Thai flag. 5,683 GT; 7,099 DWT; approximately 101 metres by 19 metres. Registered owner at time of shipment: Bhureemas Navee Co., Ltd., Bangkok, Thailand. P&I insurer: Shipowners Mutual Protection and Indemnity Association (Luxembourg), affiliated with the Shipowners' Club. Thai Oil had divested its wholly-owned marine subsidiary Thaioil Marine Company Limited (subsequently renamed Truth Maritime Company Limited) to Phurich Marine Company Limited, a subsidiary of Prima Marine Public Company Limited (SET: PRM), on 30 April 2021 for approximately THB 1.6 billion; the Bhureemas was therefore not a Thai Oil vessel at the time of this delivery. The vessel is not identified in reviewed sources as a sanctioned or subsequently flagged entity. (AI Nov 2022)
•PTT Group: Thai Oil is a subsidiary of PTT, the Thai state-owned energy conglomerate. PTT Group is separately implicated in Myanmar's energy sector through its subsidiary PTT Exploration and Production Public Company Limited (PTTEP), which operates offshore gas fields in joint ventures with the designated Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE) (Profile 9.2). Thai Oil's supply chain relationship with Myanmar's military aviation fuel network is distinct from PTT's broader gas sector engagement. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal, Thilawa (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The delivery point for the June 2022 shipment. At the time of the BHUREEMAS delivery on 5 June 2022, the Thilawa terminal was under Puma Energy's operational management. Puma Energy completed the sale of the terminal to the Shoon Group in December 2022, after which it was operated under the Shoon Energy / Myan Oil Terminal network. (AI Nov 2022)

Advocacy Interventions and Outcome
•22 September 2022. Thai Oil writes to Amnesty International confirming the June 2022 BHUREEMAS shipment and describing its due diligence: World-Check One screening of Asia Sun Trading (no concerns found) and a contractual civilian-use warranty and indemnity. (AI Nov 2022)
•14 October 2022. Thai Oil notifies Amnesty International by email of its conditional suspension: "our business unit has decided to hold a selling of Jet A-1 to Myanmar until no such concerned issue." This suspension predates the Deadly Cargo publication. It remains in effect through all subsequent reviewed investigations. (AI Nov 2022)
•3 November 2022. Deadly Cargo is published. Thai Oil's suspension is cited as one of the most significant immediate outcomes of the investigation. Amnesty International uses the Asia Sun Trading military-use declaration to Myanmar Customs as the paradigm illustration of the inadequacy of warranty-based due diligence. (AI Nov 2022)
•March 2023 to January 2026. Four subsequent Amnesty International investigations document Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. Thai Oil does not appear in any of them. (AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Confirm the current status of the suspension and seek a permanent public commitment. The October 2022 suspension was conditional and communicated privately by email. No public policy statement has been issued. Civil society should engage Thai Oil to confirm the suspension remains in effect, clarify whether it is permanent or reversible, and request a formal public commitment not to supply aviation fuel to Myanmar's military or any entity in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain. Thai Oil has not been publicly contacted or responded in any reviewed source since October 2022. (AI Nov 2022)
•Engage PTT Group under the UN Guiding Principles. PTT holds approximately 48 percent of Thai Oil and is majority-owned by the Thai government through the Ministry of Finance. Its human rights due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles extend to Thai Oil's conduct. Civil society and governments should call on PTT to require Thai Oil to publish a permanent Myanmar aviation fuel supply policy. PTT Group is separately implicated through PTTEP's gas operations with MOGE (Profile 9.2), making the broader PTT Group Myanmar exposure a coherent advocacy focus. (AI Nov 2022)
•Present the Thai Oil case to the UK government as evidence for supplier-side due diligence obligations. Thai Oil screened Asia Sun Trading, found no concerns, obtained civilian-use warranties, and still supplied fuel that reached the Myanmar Air Force. The US addressed this systemic gap with its August 2023 OFAC sector determination. The UK, as the most active jurisdiction in Myanmar aviation fuel sanctions enforcement, should be presented with the Thai Oil case as documented evidence that entity-level screening is insufficient and that analogous supplier-side due diligence measures are needed. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Monitor Thai Oil in light of the Clean Fuel Project. When the Clean Fuel Project at Sriracha reaches commercial operation (targeted Q3 2028), capacity increases from 275,000 to 400,000 barrels per day and Thai Oil's export ratio to CLMV countries including Myanmar is expected to rise from 11 to 40 percent. Any resumed aviation fuel supply to Myanmar should be treated as an immediate re-engagement trigger. (AI Nov 2022)
