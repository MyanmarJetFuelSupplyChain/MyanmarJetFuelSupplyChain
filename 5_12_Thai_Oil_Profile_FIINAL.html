Thai Oil Public Company Limited
Profile 5.12
Summary Assessment
Thai Oil Public Company Limited (Thaioil; SET: TOP) is a Thai integrated refiner and petrochemicals company listed on the Stock Exchange of Thailand and a subsidiary of PTT Group, the Thai state-owned energy conglomerate, which holds approximately 48% of Thai Oil directly and indirectly. Thai Oil operates the largest single-site refinery in Thailand at Sriracha, Chonburi Province, with a capacity of approximately 275,000 barrels per day. One confirmed post-coup aviation fuel shipment is documented: the BHUREEMAS (IMO 9547568) loaded 4,942 MT of Jet A-1 at Thai Oil's Marine Terminal in Sriracha in late May 2022 and delivered the cargo to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon on 5 June 2022. Thai Oil's customer was Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd., subsequently designated by the UK (31 January 2023), the US (24 March 2023) and Australia (1 February 2024). A letter obtained by Amnesty International, written by Asia Sun Trading to Myanmar's Customs Department in May 2022, stated the fuel was procured "for use by the Tatmadaw (Air Force)," directly contradicting contractual assurances Thai Oil had received from its customer. Following Amnesty International's pre-publication engagement, Thai Oil confirmed in writing on 14 October 2022 that it had decided "to hold a selling of Jet A-1 to Myanmar until no such concerned issue." This language is explicitly conditional, not a permanent policy commitment, and no public statement committing to a permanent exit from the Myanmar aviation fuel market has been issued by Thai Oil. Thai Oil does not appear in any of the four subsequent Amnesty International investigations into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain published through January 2026. Thai Oil has not been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. The Thai Oil case is documented as a model of how CSO engagement can produce rapid corporate compliance responses even in the absence of sanctions. Thai Oil's Clean Fuel Project at Sriracha, expected to increase refinery capacity and the company's export ratio to CLMV countries including Myanmar, is a continuing monitoring concern.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Thai Oil's one confirmed post-coup contribution to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain was the June 2022 shipment carried by the BHUREEMAS (IMO 9547568). Because Thai Oil had divested its marine subsidiary Thaioil Marine Company Limited approximately thirteen months earlier, the Bhureemas was not a Thai Oil vessel at the time of this delivery. The vessel loaded 4,942 MT of Jet A-1 at jetty no. 4 of the Thai Oil Marine Terminal in Sriracha between 24 and 27 May 2022, and arrived at the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, on 5 June 2022. Amnesty International obtained a sales contract dated 29 April 2022 under which Thai Oil committed to selling 39,850 barrels (approximately equivalent to 4,942 MT) of Jet A-1 for over USD 6 million to Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. Prior to the transaction, Thai Oil stated that its responsible unit conducted due diligence on the purchaser using the World-Check One database and obtained a legally binding contractual representation that the fuel was solely for commercial and civilian use. Thai Oil's screening identified no concerns. (AI Nov 2022)

The contractual assurances Thai Oil received were directly contradicted by documentary evidence obtained by Amnesty International. A letter written by Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. and addressed to the Director General of Myanmar's Customs Department, dated May 2022, stated that Asia Sun Trading had procured and imported the Jet A-1 from Thai Oil "for use by the Tatmadaw (Air Force)." Amnesty International used this case as the paradigm illustration of the inadequacy of end-use contractual clauses as a due diligence mechanism for Myanmar aviation fuel supply, concluding that selling aviation fuel to a counterparty in Myanmar carried inherent human rights risk regardless of contractual warranties. Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. was subsequently designated by the UK (31 January 2023), the US (24 March 2023) and Australia (1 February 2024), and forms part of the Asia Sun Group network documented in Profile 1.4. Thai Oil's World-Check One due diligence, conducted in early 2022, had identified no concerns about Asia Sun Trading; the company's subsequent designation across three jurisdictions illustrates the limitations of sanctions-list screening as a primary due diligence tool for Myanmar supply chains at that time. (AI Nov 2022)

The June 2022 shipment is the only documented Thai Oil contribution to Myanmar's post-coup aviation fuel supply chain and appears as row 6 in the master shipment table. Thai Oil does not appear in any of the four Amnesty International investigations into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain published after November 2022: the March 2023 follow-up (which confirmed the suspension was still in effect), the January 2024 investigation, the July 2024 investigation, and the January 2026 investigation documenting Iran-linked ghost ships. No subsequent Thai Oil aviation fuel supply to Myanmar has been identified in any reviewed source.

Key Relationships
•Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. (Profile 1.4): Thai Oil's direct customer for the June 2022 Jet A-1 shipment. Asia Sun Trading is a Myanmar-registered entity within the Asia Sun Group network, the documented domestic importer and distributor of aviation fuel for Myanmar's military. Asia Sun Trading provided Thai Oil with contractual assurances that the fuel was for civilian use but simultaneously declared to Myanmar's Customs Department that the fuel was for use by the Tatmadaw (Air Force). Asia Sun Trading was subsequently designated by the UK (31 January 2023), the US OFAC (24 March 2023) and Australia (1 February 2024). The owner of Asia Sun Trading, Zaw Min Tun, is designated by multiple jurisdictions. The Asia Sun Group and its network are documented in Profile 1.4. (AI Nov 2022)
•BHUREEMAS (IMO 9547568): The vessel that transported the June 2022 shipment from Sriracha to Thilawa. A chemical and oil products tanker built in 2010 by East Star Shipbuilding, Nanjing, China. Thai flag. 5,683 GT; 7,099 DWT; approximately 101 metres by 19 metres. Registered owner at time of shipment: Bhureemas Navee Co., Ltd., Bangkok, Thailand. P&I insurer: Shipowners Mutual Protection and Indemnity Association (Luxembourg), affiliated with the Shipowners' Club. Thai Oil had divested its wholly-owned marine subsidiary Thaioil Marine Company Limited (subsequently renamed Truth Maritime Company Limited) to Phurich Marine Company Limited, a subsidiary of Prima Marine Public Company Limited (SET: PRM), on 30 April 2021 for approximately THB 1.6 billion; the Bhureemas was therefore not a Thai Oil vessel at the time of this delivery. The vessel is not identified in reviewed sources as a sanctioned or subsequently flagged entity. (AI Nov 2022)
•PTT Group: Thai Oil is a subsidiary of PTT, the Thai state-owned energy conglomerate. PTT Group is separately implicated in Myanmar's energy sector through its subsidiary PTT Exploration and Production Public Company Limited (PTTEP), which operates offshore gas fields in joint ventures with the designated Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE) (Profile 9.2). Thai Oil's supply chain relationship with Myanmar's military aviation fuel network is distinct from PTT's broader gas sector engagement. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal, Thilawa (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The delivery point for the June 2022 shipment. At the time of the BHUREEMAS delivery on 5 June 2022, the Thilawa terminal was under Puma Energy's operational management. Puma Energy completed the sale of the terminal to the Shoon Group in December 2022, after which it was operated under the Shoon Energy / Myan Oil Terminal network. (AI Nov 2022)
4
Advocacy Interventions and Outcome
Documented positive advocacy outcome (conditional suspension): Thai Oil suspended Myanmar aviation fuel sales in October 2022 following direct engagement by Amnesty International as part of its pre-publication process for the Deadly Cargo investigation. Thai Oil confirmed the suspension in writing on 14 October 2022 using language that is explicitly conditional: "to hold a selling of Jet A-1 to Myanmar until no such concerned issue." This is a business decision pending resolution of concerns, not a permanent policy withdrawal from the Myanmar aviation fuel market, and no subsequent public policy statement committing to a permanent exit has been issued. The suspension has been maintained through at least March 2023 and Thai Oil does not appear in any reviewed investigation through January 2026. Thai Oil and Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar (Profile 6.9) both committed to stopping Myanmar aviation fuel supply before the Deadly Cargo report was published; Pan Ocean (Profile 6.10) made the same commitment after publication.
•22 September 2022. Thai Oil writes to Amnesty International confirming the June 2022 BHUREEMAS shipment. The letter describes Thai Oil's due diligence process: screening through the World-Check One database; a legally binding contractual representation and indemnity from Asia Sun Trading that the fuel was solely for civilian use; and a statement that the screening had identified no concerns. Thai Oil declines to identify the customer by name for commercial confidentiality reasons. (AI Nov 2022)
•14 October 2022. Thai Oil notifies Amnesty International by email of its suspension decision: "to avoid any doubt about the compliance of good corporate governance policy, our business unit has decided to hold a selling of Jet A-1 to Myanmar until no such concerned issue." This is the documented conditional suspension. The language is explicit that the hold is "until no such concerned issue," making it a business pause contingent on the resolution of the concerns raised by Amnesty International's investigation, not a permanent policy commitment. (AI Nov 2022)
•3 November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo report, produced in research collaboration with Justice for Myanmar, is published. It documents the June 2022 BHUREEMAS shipment and Thai Oil's suspension decision. The report identifies Thai Oil's response, alongside Wilhelmsen's, as among the most significant immediate advocacy outcomes of the investigation. It uses the Thai Oil case as the paradigm illustration of the inadequacy of end-use contractual warranties, drawing a direct contrast between Thai Oil's civilian-use assurances and Asia Sun Trading's military-use declaration to Myanmar's Customs Department. (AI Nov 2022)
•1 March 2023. The follow-up investigation published by Amnesty International, Global Witness and Burma Campaign UK confirms that Thai Oil had put on hold any shipments of aviation fuel to Myanmar. Thai Oil does not appear as a supplier in this investigation. (AI Mar 2023)
•January 2024. Amnesty International publishes its third Myanmar aviation fuel investigation, "Myanmar: New Data Suggests Military Still Importing Fuel for Deadly Air Strikes Despite Sanctions," documenting continued supply through evasion techniques including resale through Vietnamese storage intermediaries. Thai Oil does not appear. (AI Jan 2024)
•July 2024. Amnesty International publishes its fourth Myanmar aviation fuel investigation, "Myanmar: Reckless Shipments of Jet Fuel Continue as Air Strikes Multiply." Thai Oil does not appear. (AI Jul 2024)
•27 January 2026. Amnesty International publishes its fifth Myanmar aviation fuel investigation, "Myanmar: Jet Fuel Used in Deadly Air Strikes Flowing in on Ghost Ships with Suspected Links to Iran," documenting a supply chain that has shifted to Iran-linked ghost ships using AIS manipulation and ship-to-ship transfers to evade detection. Thai Oil does not appear. Across all four post-2022 investigations, the supply chain documented has moved progressively further from the model of direct commercial sales by named suppliers, toward obscured networks using sanctioned-country intermediaries and ghost fleet tactics. Thai Oil's exit from the documented supply chain has been sustained through this period of escalating evasion. (AI Jan 2026)
•December 2022 to February 2024: Asia Sun Group network designated across five jurisdictions. Following the Deadly Cargo publication, the Asia Sun Group network, within which Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. was Thai Oil's direct customer under the June 2022 contract, was designated by five sanctions authorities. Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. specifically was designated by the UK (31 January 2023), the US OFAC (24 March 2023) and Australia (1 February 2024). The parent entity, Asia Sun Group, was designated by Canada (December 2022) and the EU (20 February 2023). The collective designations across all five major Myanmar sanctions jurisdictions confirm that the entities at the heart of the transaction Thai Oil conducted in June 2022 were subsequently found by multiple governments to be operating in the Myanmar military's aviation fuel supply chain. Thai Oil's World-Check One screening in early 2022 had identified no concerns; the subsequent five-jurisdiction response to the same network illustrates the limits of sanctions-list-based screening as the primary due diligence mechanism for Myanmar supply chains at that time. Thai Oil itself was not designated. See Profile 1.4 for the full Asia Sun Group designation record. (AI Mar 2023)
5
Recommended Actions
•Confirm the current status of the suspension and seek a permanent public commitment: Thai Oil's October 2022 suspension was communicated conditionally and privately by email to Amnesty International. The language used, "until no such concerned issue," makes the suspension explicitly contingent on the resolution of the concerns raised, not a permanent policy withdrawal from the Myanmar aviation fuel market. No public policy statement committing Thai Oil to a permanent exit has been issued. Civil society should engage Thai Oil directly to confirm the suspension remains in effect as of April 2026, to clarify whether the company treats it as a permanent decision or a reversible business hold, and to request a formal public statement committing not to supply aviation fuel to Myanmar's military or to any entity within the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain. Given that Thai Oil has not been publicly contacted or publicly responded in any reviewed source since October 2022, the conditionality of its position warrants active re-engagement. (AI Nov 2022)
•Engage PTT Public Company Limited under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights: PTT holds approximately 48% of Thai Oil directly and indirectly, making it the de facto controlling entity, and is itself majority-owned by the Thai government through the Ministry of Finance. This ownership chain means PTT's human rights due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights extend to Thai Oil's conduct in Myanmar, and advocacy targeting PTT simultaneously engages the Thai state's own international obligations. Civil society and governments with Myanmar sanctions programmes should call on PTT to require Thai Oil to publish a formal, permanent Myanmar aviation fuel supply policy rather than relying on a conditional communications-level hold, and to integrate Myanmar supply chain risk into PTT Group's own human rights due diligence framework. PTT Group is separately implicated in Myanmar's energy sector through PTTEP's gas operations with the designated Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (Profile 9.2); advocacy on Thai Oil's conditional suspension is most effective when situated within that broader PTT Group exposure to Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022)
•Engage the Stock Exchange of Thailand and the Thai Securities and Exchange Commission regarding Thai Oil's sustainability disclosures: As a SET-listed company, Thai Oil is subject to mandatory sustainability and human rights due diligence disclosure obligations under Thai SEC and SET requirements. Thai Oil was rated in the top 1% in S&P Global's Corporate Sustainability Assessment for the tenth consecutive year in 2025. This top-tier ESG rating sits in direct tension with the fact that Thai Oil's documented supply of aviation fuel to the Myanmar Air Force via Asia Sun Trading, and the compliance failure identified by Amnesty International regarding the wholly inadequate counterparty-warranty due diligence mechanism Thai Oil relied upon, have not been addressed in any publicly available sustainability disclosure. Civil society should engage the SET and Thai SEC to request that Thai Oil's sustainability reports address its Myanmar supply chain history, the limitations of its 2022 due diligence approach as documented in the Deadly Cargo investigation, and the current status of its Myanmar supply policy. The gap between Thai Oil's internationally recognised ESG rating and its undisclosed Myanmar aviation fuel exposure is a specific and concrete disclosure failure that SET and Thai SEC have a regulatory basis to require be remedied. (AI Nov 2022)
•Present the Thai Oil case to the UK government as evidence for supplier-side due diligence obligations in Myanmar aviation fuel supply: Thai Oil conducted World-Check One screening on Asia Sun Trading prior to the June 2022 sale, found no concerns, obtained civilian-use contractual warranties, and still supplied fuel that reached the Myanmar Air Force. Within seven to nine months, Asia Sun Trading was designated by the UK (31 January 2023) and the US OFAC (24 March 2023); within twenty months by Australia. The US addressed the systemic gap this exposed by issuing its jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 in August 2023, establishing that any foreign person operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy may be sanctioned regardless of whether a specific Myanmar-side buyer was listed at the time of sale. Civil society should present the Thai Oil case to the UK government as a documented illustration that entity-level screening of Myanmar-side buyers is insufficient: Thai Oil's customer was clean on every available sanctions screen at the time of sale, yet the Asia Sun Group network of which it was part was found by five jurisdictions to be operating in the Myanmar military's aviation fuel supply chain within two years. The UK, as the most active jurisdiction in Myanmar aviation fuel sanctions enforcement, is the most appropriate addressee for advocacy supporting analogous UK measures that address due diligence obligations on the supplier side rather than relying solely on designation of Myanmar-based counterparties. (AI Nov 2022)
•Monitor Thai Oil's ongoing trade with Myanmar, with particular attention to the Clean Fuel Project: No source reviewed after January 2024 documents any Thai Oil aviation fuel sale to Myanmar. However, the conditionality of Thai Oil's suspension, combined with two structural developments, warrants continued monitoring. First, the Clean Fuel Project at Sriracha, originally approved at USD 4.82 billion and subsequently subject to additional investment approved at a February 2025 extraordinary general meeting that brought total committed expenditure to approximately USD 7.15 billion, is designed to increase refinery capacity from 275,000 to 400,000 barrels per day. When complete, it is expected to increase Thai Oil's export ratio to CLMV countries, including Myanmar, from 11% to 40% of total exports. The CFP was approximately 97% complete as of June 2024; Thai Oil terminated the main contractor in April 2025 and engaged Foster Wheeler to complete the remaining work, with commercial operations now targeted for Q3 2028. When fully operational, Thai Oil's aviation fuel production capacity will increase substantially and Myanmar's inclusion in its stated CLMV export growth target makes resumed supply commercially plausible. Second, the four post-2022 AI investigations document that Myanmar's military has progressively shifted toward ghost ships and obscured Iran-linked supply chains; any re-entry by a named Thai supplier would be significant regardless of how the broader supply chain had changed character. Civil society and institutional investors should monitor shipping and trade data for any Thai Oil jet fuel exports to Myanmar, and should treat any resumed supply as a matter for immediate re-engagement, formal disclosure requests, and engagement with Thai government regulators. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
