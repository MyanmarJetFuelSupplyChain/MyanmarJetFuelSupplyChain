BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd.
Profile 5.2
Summary Assessment
BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. is the Singapore-registered branch of BB Energy, a Dubai-headquartered independent energy trading company. Vietnamese customs data reviewed by Amnesty International identified it as the second-to-last seller in at least three of the seven aviation fuel shipments that transited Vietnam before reaching Myanmar in 2023. In each case BB Energy sold fuel to a Vietnamese company, which received it at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal before it was sold on to Myanmar purchasers. Amnesty International acknowledged uncertainty about whether BB Energy knew the fuel would reach Myanmar but found that its value chain due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles were engaged. BB Energy did not respond to Amnesty International. It has not been sanctioned by any government and has not appeared in any documented Myanmar aviation fuel shipment since January 2024.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Vietnamese customs data identified BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller in at least three of the seven 2023 aviation fuel shipments that transited Vietnam before reaching Myanmar. The structure was consistent: BB Energy sold fuel to a Vietnamese company, which received it at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal and stored it for between a few hours and several days before it was sold to the Myanmar purchaser and transported by vessel to Thilawa. Amnesty International did not specify which three of the seven shipments involved BB Energy, and the source record does not identify the associated vessels, dates, or volumes. (AI Jan 2024)

Amnesty International explicitly acknowledged it was unclear whether BB Energy knew the fuel would reach Myanmar, but assessed that its value chain due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights were engaged regardless. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination does not require proof of knowledge of the Myanmar end-destination. A trader of BB Energy's scale selling fuel that transits Vietnam to Myanmar within days would be expected, at minimum, to have conducted due diligence on end-use. BB Energy did not respond to Amnesty International. (AI Jan 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

Key Relationships
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, Vietnam): The terminal operator at Cai Mep that received the fuel sold by BB Energy after its purchase by the Vietnamese intermediary company. All seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments, including the at least three in which BB Energy was the second-to-last seller, loaded at Hai Linh's Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal. Hai Linh is unsanctioned. See Profile 4.1. (AI Jan 2024)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited / Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal (Thilawa, Yangon): The destination terminal in Myanmar to which all seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments were delivered, including those in which BB Energy participated as second-to-last seller. During 2023 the terminal was operating under the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited. See Profile 1.3. (AI Jan 2024)
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.: Named by Amnesty International in its January 2024 investigation as one of four companies with engaged UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations, alongside BB Energy, Hai Linh and Royal Vopak. Subsequently identified in Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation as the second-to-last seller for the February 2024 shipment (row 19), based on Vietnamese customs data. It is profiled separately. See Profile 5.4. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Unnamed Vietnamese intermediary companies: The direct purchasers from BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. in the at least three shipments in which it participated. These companies received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal and then sold it to the Myanmar-side purchaser. Their identities are not disclosed in any reviewed source. (AI Jan 2024)

Sanctions gap: BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. has not been sanctioned by any government. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the primary legal basis for a potential US designation, covering any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy. Amnesty International acknowledges uncertainty as to whether BB Energy knew the fuel was destined for Myanmar, which bears on any designation submission; however, the July 2024 Amnesty International investigation established that Vietnamese customs data is capable of demonstrating that traders "should have been aware" of the Myanmar transit destination, providing an evidentiary avenue for addressing the knowledge question. The most immediately actionable parallel pressure point is the UN Guiding Principles standard: BB Energy faces a documented due diligence obligation that it has not publicly discharged. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
4
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•January 2024. Amnesty International identifies BB Energy (Asia) as the second-to-last seller in at least three of the seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments. Amnesty International contacts BB Energy; no response is received. No designation or regulatory action follows. (AI Jan 2024)
•July 2024 onwards. BB Energy does not appear in any subsequent documented shipment. The 2024 shipments identified different second-to-last sellers (Sahara Energy for January 2024, CNOOC Trading for February 2024). The supply chain shifted to direct Iranian deliveries from mid-2024, bypassing the Vietnamese transit model entirely. (AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Request disclosure from BB Energy on its 2023 due diligence and current policies. Civil society should write to BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. in Singapore and BB Energy Group Holding Ltd. in Dubai requesting: whether BB Energy had knowledge the fuel would reach Myanmar; what human rights due diligence it applied to the Vietnamese purchasers before each 2023 transaction; and whether it has ceased any business with Hai Linh or the Cai Mep terminal since its public naming. Engagement should reference the OFAC sector determination, which creates sanctions risk for any entity operating in Myanmar's jet fuel sector including through indirect supply chains. (AI Jan 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Submit a designation referral to OFAC. The August 2023 sector determination provides the legal authority. The submission should note that Amnesty International applied the same Vietnamese customs data methodology to Sahara Energy and CNOOC Trading for the 2024 shipments and found they "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." The same standard applies to BB Energy's 2023 transactions. BB Energy USA LLC in Houston creates a secondary US nexus worth flagging. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Submit a referral to UK OTSI regarding the London entity. B.B. Energy Trading Ltd. (Companies House 04511776), registered at 12-14 Ansdell Street, London W8 5BN, is the UK entity in the BB Energy group. The Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021 cover entities making economic resources available to Myanmar's security forces. A referral to the Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation should present the January 2024 Amnesty International evidence and request an assessment of exposure under UK trade sanctions law. (AI Jan 2024)
