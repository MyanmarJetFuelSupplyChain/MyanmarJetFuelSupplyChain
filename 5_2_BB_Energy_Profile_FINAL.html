BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd.
Profile 5.2
Summary Assessment
BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. is the Singapore-registered branch of BB Energy, a privately owned independent energy trading company headquartered in Dubai and described by Amnesty International as among the world's leading independent energy trading companies. Vietnamese customs data, reviewed by Amnesty International and published in January 2024, identified BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller in at least three of the seven documented aviation fuel shipments that transited Vietnam before reaching Myanmar in 2023. In each case, BB Energy sold fuel to a Vietnamese company, which received it at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. before the fuel was sold on to Myanmar purchasers and transported by vessel to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon. Amnesty International acknowledged uncertainty about whether BB Energy knew the fuel it was selling to Vietnamese companies would ultimately reach Myanmar. BB Energy did not respond to Amnesty International's contact. Amnesty International identified BB Energy as one of four companies whose full value chain due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights are engaged by these transactions. BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. has not been sanctioned by any government and does not appear in the opensanctions database.

Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain
Position in the 2023 transit supply chain

BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. is identified by Vietnamese customs data, as reviewed and published by Amnesty International in January 2024, as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel in at least three of the seven documented shipments that transited Vietnam before reaching Myanmar in 2023. All seven of those shipments loaded at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. before being transported by vessel to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon. The supply chain structure in which BB Energy operated was as follows: BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. sold aviation fuel to a Vietnamese company; that Vietnamese company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal; after storage of between a few hours and several days, the fuel was sold to the Myanmar purchaser and transported by vessel to Thilawa. Amnesty International described this structure as one in which Vietnam functioned as a transit layer designed to obscure the fuel's onward destination. (AI Jan 2024)

Scope of involvement and source limitations

Amnesty International confirmed BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd.'s involvement in at least three of the seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments, but did not specify which three shipments. The source record does not identify the specific vessels, dates or volumes associated with the BB Energy-linked shipments within the seven-shipment dataset. Amnesty International explicitly acknowledged uncertainty about whether BB Energy knew that the fuel it was selling to Vietnamese companies would subsequently transit Vietnam and reach Myanmar: "It is unclear whether the trading companies knew the fuel they were selling to Vietnamese companies would soon thereafter end up in Myanmar, or whether their actions could run afoul of existing sanctions." No evidence in the reviewed sources establishes that BB Energy had actual knowledge of the Myanmar end-destination at the point of sale. (AI Jan 2024)

Due diligence obligations

Amnesty International named BB Energy alongside CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd., Hai Linh Co. Ltd. and Royal Vopak as companies whose full value chain due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights are engaged by the documented transactions. This framing places on BB Energy a responsibility to understand and address adverse human rights impacts linked to its business relationships, even where it did not have direct control over the end-use of the fuel it sold. Amnesty International contacted BB Energy for comment in connection with its January 2024 investigation; no response was received or recorded. (AI Jan 2024)

The constructive knowledge question: Amnesty International's assessment is that BB Energy's value chain due diligence obligations are engaged, while simultaneously acknowledging that it is unclear whether BB Energy knew the fuel was destined for Myanmar. The August 2023 OFAC jet fuel sector determination, which authorises sanctions on any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy, does not require proof of knowledge of the Myanmar end-destination. The UN Guiding Principles standard is lower still: companies are expected to conduct due diligence to identify adverse human rights impacts throughout their value chains. Selling fuel to a Vietnamese company that then transits it to Myanmar within days would, on these facts, fall within the scope of that due diligence requirement regardless of actual knowledge. (AI Jan 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

Key Relationships
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, Vietnam): The terminal operator at Cai Mep that received the fuel sold by BB Energy after its purchase by the Vietnamese intermediary company. All seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments, including the at least three in which BB Energy was the second-to-last seller, loaded at Hai Linh's Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal. Hai Linh is unsanctioned. See Profile 4.1. (AI Jan 2024)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited / Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal (Thilawa, Yangon): The destination terminal in Myanmar to which all seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments were delivered, including those in which BB Energy participated as second-to-last seller. During 2023 the terminal was operating under the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited. See Profile 1.3. (AI Jan 2024)
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.: Named by Amnesty International in its January 2024 investigation as one of four companies with engaged UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations, alongside BB Energy, Hai Linh and Royal Vopak. Subsequently identified in Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation as the second-to-last seller for the February 2024 shipment (row 19), based on Vietnamese customs data. It is profiled separately. See Profile 5.4. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Unnamed Vietnamese intermediary companies: The direct purchasers from BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. in the at least three shipments in which it participated. These companies received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal and then sold it to the Myanmar-side purchaser. Their identities are not disclosed in any reviewed source. (AI Jan 2024)

Sanctions gap: BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. has not been sanctioned by any government. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the primary legal basis for a potential US designation, covering any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy. Amnesty International acknowledges uncertainty as to whether BB Energy knew the fuel was destined for Myanmar, which bears on any designation submission; however, the July 2024 Amnesty International investigation established that Vietnamese customs data is capable of demonstrating that traders "should have been aware" of the Myanmar transit destination, providing an evidentiary avenue for addressing the knowledge question. The most immediately actionable parallel pressure point is the UN Guiding Principles standard: BB Energy faces a documented due diligence obligation that it has not publicly discharged. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

Recommended Actions
•Request disclosure from BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. and BB Energy Group Holding Ltd.: Civil society should write to BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. at its Singapore office (3 Temasek Avenue #32-02, Centennial Tower) and to BB Energy Group Holding Ltd. at its DIFC, Dubai registered office requesting: confirmation of whether BB Energy (Asia) had knowledge that the fuel it sold to Vietnamese companies in 2023 would be transported onward to Myanmar; disclosure of what human rights due diligence it applied to each Vietnamese purchaser before each 2023 transaction; confirmation of what policies and operational controls BB Energy now applies to sales where a subsequent purchaser may transfer fuel to Myanmar or to entities associated with the Myanmar military; and confirmation of whether it has ceased any business with Hai Linh Co. Ltd. or with the Cai Mep terminal supply chain since its public naming in January 2024. Engagement should reference the August 2023 OFAC sector determination, which creates material sanctions risk for any company determined to operate in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy, including through indirect supply chains. (AI Jan 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Submit a designation submission to OFAC under the August 2023 sector determination: Submit evidence to OFAC for designation of BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. under Executive Order 14014 and the August 2023 sector determination, identifying both the Singapore entity and its parent BB Energy Group Holding Ltd. (DIFC, Dubai). The submission should address the knowledge question directly: the multi-hop, storage-transit structure documented in this supply chain is itself a deliberate evasion technique identified by Amnesty International, and a trader of BB Energy's scale and experience operating in this geography would reasonably be expected to have conducted due diligence on end-use. The July 2024 Amnesty International investigation established, using Vietnamese customs data, that Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. and CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd., two Singapore-based traders in the same supply chain, "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." The same Vietnamese customs data methodology applied to BB Energy (Asia)'s 2023 transactions is the key evidentiary avenue for establishing comparable awareness. Note also that BB Energy USA LLC in Houston, Texas creates a secondary US nexus: a US-incorporated entity in the same group as a designated Singapore entity could itself face OFAC compliance obligations. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage the Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation regarding the London entity: Submit a referral to the UK Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation (OTSI) regarding B.B. Energy Trading Ltd. (Companies House: 04511776), the London-registered entity in the BB Energy group, at 12-14 Ansdell Street, London W8 5BN. The Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021 permit designation of entities involved in making economic resources available to the Myanmar security forces. OTSI is responsible for enforcing UK trade sanctions. A referral should present the January 2024 Amnesty International evidence and request that OTSI assess whether the BB Energy group's London entity has exposure under UK trade sanctions law in connection with the documented 2023 transactions. (AI Jan 2024)
•Monitor for renewed involvement: No Amnesty International investigation published since January 2024 has identified BB Energy (Asia) in any Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain, and the January 2026 investigation confirmed that the Vietnam transit route through which BB Energy (Asia) operated in 2023 had been superseded by ghost fleet deliveries from Iran and the UAE. Any renewed involvement by BB Energy (Asia) in a Myanmar-bound aviation fuel supply chain should trigger immediate re-engagement on all the above fronts. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
