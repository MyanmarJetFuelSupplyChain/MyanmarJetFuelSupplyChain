Fujairah Offshore Anchorage Area (FOAA) and Hamriyah Port, Sharjah
Profile 7.2
Summary Assessment
The Fujairah Offshore Anchorage Area (FOAA) and Hamriyah Port in Sharjah are two UAE maritime locations linked to six documented aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar between April 2024 and mid-2025. FOAA is an offshore anchorage used for ship-to-ship fuel transfers; it has no named operator. Hamriyah Port is a commercial port in Sharjah. HUITONG 78 loaded at Hamriyah Port in April 2024 and staged at FOAA before its July 2024 delivery; YONG SHENG 56 staged at FOAA before its July 2024 delivery; NOBLE staged at FOAA before three confirmed 2025 deliveries. For NOBLE's June 2025 delivery, satellite imagery confirmed it was also at Bandar Abbas, Iran, after FOAA, establishing FOAA as an intermediate rather than ultimate fuel source. Neither FOAA nor Hamriyah Port is a corporate entity. No UAE enforcement or advocacy action targeting either location has been confirmed as of April 2026.

Hamriyah Port, Sharjah
Name:Hamriyah Port
Type:Commercial port. Distinct from the adjacent Hamriyah Free Zone.
Location:Sharjah emirate, United Arab Emirates. Located on the Arabian Gulf coast. Geographically and institutionally distinct from FOAA, which is in Fujairah on the Gulf of Oman.
Operator:No named operator identified in any reviewed source. UAE maritime authorities have jurisdiction over the port.
Role in supply chain:Load port for HUITONG 78 in April 2024. The cargo for that shipment was assessed by Amnesty International as quite likely to have been aviation fuel but could not be confirmed. (AI Jul 2024)
Note on co-location:Hamriyah Port is geographically proximate to the Hamriyah Free Zone, Sharjah. Sea Route Ship Management FZE (Profile 6.7), Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA (Profile 6.8) and Sea Hill Maritime SA (Profile 6.8) are all registered in the Hamriyah Free Zone. The port and the Free Zone are distinct facilities: the port is where HUITONG 78 physically loaded fuel; the Free Zone is where the corporate entities managing and owning the ghost fleet vessels are registered.

Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Ship-to-ship fuel transfers at offshore anchorage areas fall outside port state inspection and documentation requirements, making FOAA a structurally opaque staging point. The use of FOAA by three ghost fleet vessels across six shipments is consistent with documented evasion tactics: vessels load fuel offshore, deactivate AIS, and proceed directly to Thilawa. Hamriyah Port's single documented connection, for HUITONG 78's April 2024 delivery, was assessed by Amnesty International as quite likely aviation fuel but not confirmed. (AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)

Key Relationships
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (Profile 6.1): HUITONG 78 (IMO 9646479) is documented at both UAE locations covered by this profile. It loaded fuel at Hamriyah Port, Sharjah in April 2024 before a May 2024 delivery to Myanmar (table shipment 1) and was sighted at FOAA between 13 and 26 June 2024 before the July 2024 Myanmar delivery (table shipment 2). (AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
•YONG SHENG 56 / LS MERCURY (Profile 6.3): YONG SHENG 56 (IMO 9657507) was sighted at FOAA between 15 and 29 June 2024 before the 30-31 July 2024 Myanmar delivery (table shipment 3). (AI Jan 2026)
•NOBLE (Profile 6.6): NOBLE (IMO 9162928) loitered at FOAA, UAE, before travelling to Myanmar for multiple 2025 deliveries (table shipments 4, 5 and 6). For at least the June 2025 delivery, satellite imagery places NOBLE at Bandar Abbas, Iran, after FOAA and before arriving in Myanmar, indicating that FOAA was a staging stop rather than the point of Iranian fuel loading. (AI Jan 2026)
•Sea Route Ship Management FZE, Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Hill Maritime SA (Profiles 6.7 and 6.8): These three entities are registered in the Hamriyah Free Zone, Sharjah, and are the ISM and commercial manager and registered owners of REEF and NOBLE respectively. The Hamriyah Free Zone is geographically proximate to Hamriyah Port, Sharjah, where HUITONG 78 loaded fuel in April 2024, but the Free Zone corporate entities and the port are institutionally distinct. There is no evidence in any reviewed source of a direct connection between these corporate entities and HUITONG 78's Hamriyah Port loading operation. (JFM Feb 2026)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•July 2024. Amnesty International documents Hamriyah Port as a load point for HUITONG 78's April 2024 shipment and identifies Fujairah as the load location for the July 2024 HUITONG 78 delivery. These are the first documented UAE maritime references in the Myanmar supply chain. No UAE action or advocacy intervention follows. (AI Jul 2024)
•January 2026. Amnesty International's Ghost Ships investigation names FOAA specifically, documents its use by three vessels across six shipments, and identifies it as part of a ghost fleet evasion methodology. No UAE action or advocacy intervention targeted at FOAA or Hamriyah Port has been confirmed as of April 2026. (AI Jan 2026)

Recommended Actions
•Engage UAE maritime authorities to investigate vessel activity at FOAA and Hamriyah Port. The Federal Transport Authority and the Fujairah Port Authority have jurisdiction over FOAA; the Sharjah Port Authority has jurisdiction over Hamriyah Port. Engagement should request an investigation into the documented ghost fleet staging activity and require end-use declaration requirements for petroleum cargo at both locations. (AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
•Engage the UAE Executive Office of AML/CFT on offshore ship-to-ship transfers as a sanctions evasion typology. The UAE's 2025 AML Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025, in force from 14 October 2025) expanded sanctions-evasion offences. Offshore ship-to-ship transfers at anchorage areas fall outside port state inspection requirements, creating the regulatory gap the ghost fleet has exploited. The UAE Executive Office should be engaged to assess whether FOAA transactions linked to Myanmar aviation fuel deliveries constitute sanctions evasion under the domestic framework. The UAE's removal from the FATF grey list in February 2024 gives its AML authorities strong institutional incentive to act on documented evasion in its waters. (AI Jan 2026)
•Advocate for IMO reporting requirements for offshore anchorage area ship-to-ship transfers. The use of offshore anchorage areas for fuel transfers that circumvent port state oversight is a systemic vulnerability in international maritime sanctions enforcement. Governments and civil society should engage the IMO to develop mandatory reporting and documentation requirements for ship-to-ship transfers at offshore anchorage areas, particularly for high-risk cargo including aviation fuel. (AI Jan 2026)
