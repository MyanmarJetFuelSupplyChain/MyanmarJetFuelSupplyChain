Fujairah Offshore Anchorage Area (FOAA) and Hamriyah Port, Sharjah
Profile 7.2
Summary Assessment
The Fujairah Offshore Anchorage Area (FOAA) and Hamriyah Port in Sharjah are two distinct maritime locations in the United Arab Emirates linked to six aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar's military junta between May 2024 and June 2025. FOAA is an offshore anchorage area near Fujairah designated for bunkering, ship-to-ship oil product transfers and other maritime services; it is not a fixed terminal and has no named operator identified in any reviewed source. Hamriyah Port is a commercial port in Sharjah emirate, geographically distinct from FOAA in Fujairah and institutionally distinct from the adjacent Hamriyah Free Zone. FOAA is confirmed or suspected as a staging point for five shipments: HUITONG 78 (IMO 9646479) loaded there before the July 2024 Myanmar delivery, YONG SHENG 56 (IMO 9657507) loaded there before its July 2024 Myanmar delivery, and NOBLE (IMO 9162928) loitered there before three deliveries in the first half of 2025. Hamriyah Port is the load port for a single HUITONG 78 shipment in April 2024 that Amnesty International assessed as quite likely to have been aviation fuel but could not confirm. Unlike the other transit terminals in this database, FOAA and Hamriyah Port are not corporate entities and have no identified owner, operator or commercial manager in any reviewed source. Neither location has been the subject of any government or civil society enforcement or advocacy action as of April 2026. The UAE authorities with jurisdiction over both locations are the relevant bodies for any intervention.

Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

FOAA and Hamriyah Port are two distinct UAE maritime locations linked to six aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar's military junta. Hamriyah Port was documented by Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation as a loading point for a HUITONG 78 shipment in April 2024, marking the first documented reference to a UAE port in this supply chain. FOAA was specifically identified by Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation, which also retrospectively linked the July 2024 HUITONG 78 delivery to that anchorage area. For cross-reference with all documented jet fuel shipments, see the master shipment table.

FOAA is an anchorage area used for offshore ship-to-ship fuel transfers, not a fixed terminal. Vessels documented at FOAA were using the anchorage to take on fuel offshore before continuing to Myanmar. The use of offshore anchorage areas for fuel loading is consistent with documented ghost fleet evasion tactics, as transactions conducted at sea are beyond port state inspection and documentation requirements that apply in ports. The six shipments involve three vessels: HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (Profile 6.1), which is linked to both UAE locations across two separate shipments (the Hamriyah Port load in April 2024 and the FOAA staging in June 2024); YONG SHENG 56 / LS MERCURY (Profile 6.3), which is linked to FOAA for one shipment in June 2024; and NOBLE (Profile 6.6), which is linked to FOAA as a suspected staging point for three shipments during the first half of 2025. In all FOAA cases, the vessels loitered at the offshore anchorage area before proceeding to Myanmar. For NOBLE's June 2025 delivery, satellite imagery confirms that the vessel was also at Bandar Abbas, Iran, after FOAA and before arriving in Myanmar, indicating that FOAA was an intermediate stop rather than the ultimate fuel source. Advocacy Interventions to Date
•July 2024. Amnesty International's Reckless Shipments investigation documents Hamriyah Port, Sharjah as the load port for HUITONG 78's April 2024 shipment (table shipment 1) and identifies Fujairah, UAE as the load location for the July 2024 HUITONG 78 delivery. These are the first documented references to UAE maritime locations in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain. No UAE government action or civil society advocacy intervention followed this documentation. (AI Jul 2024)
•January 2026. Amnesty International's ghost fleet investigation specifically identifies FOAA by name, provides its formal definition as an offshore area designated for bunkering and ship-to-ship oil product transfers, and documents its use by HUITONG 78 (sighted 13-26 June 2024), YONG SHENG 56 (sighted 15-29 June 2024) and NOBLE (multiple 2025 deliveries) before Myanmar deliveries. The investigation also retrospectively links the July 2024 HUITONG 78 delivery to FOAA specifically. The investigation identifies FOAA as part of a broader ghost fleet evasion methodology. No UAE government action or civil society advocacy intervention targeted at FOAA or Hamriyah Port has been confirmed as of April 2026. (AI Jan 2026)
4
Recommended Actions
•Engage UAE maritime authorities to investigate vessel activity at FOAA and Hamriyah Port: Amnesty International has documented the use of FOAA as an offshore staging area by three ghost fleet vessels linked to Myanmar aviation fuel deliveries and has documented Hamriyah Port as a loading point for a further shipment. The UAE's Federal Transport Authority and the Fujairah Port Authority have jurisdiction over FOAA; the Sharjah Port Authority has jurisdiction over Hamriyah Port. Civil society should engage these authorities and the UAE Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure to request an investigation into the use of UAE maritime locations as staging points in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain, and to require that vessels entering UAE waters or anchorage areas comply with end-use declaration requirements for petroleum cargo. (AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
•Engage the UAE Executive Office of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Terrorism Financing on ship-to-ship transfers at FOAA: Offshore ship-to-ship transfers at anchorage areas such as FOAA fall outside the port state inspection and documentation requirements that apply in ports, creating a regulatory gap that documented ghost fleet operations have exploited. The UAE Executive Office of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Terrorism Financing should be engaged to assess whether the use of UAE offshore anchorage areas for fuel transfers linked to Myanmar aviation fuel deliveries constitutes a sanctions evasion typology under the UAE's 2025 AML Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025), in force since 14 October 2025. The UAE's removal from the FATF grey list in February 2024 gives its AML authorities a strong institutional incentive to act on documented evasion activity in its waters. (AI Jan 2026)
•Advocate for international maritime regulations governing offshore anchorage area transactions: The use of offshore anchorage areas such as FOAA for ship-to-ship fuel transfers that circumvent port state oversight represents a systemic vulnerability in international maritime sanctions enforcement. Governments and civil society should engage the International Maritime Organisation to develop or strengthen reporting and documentation requirements for ship-to-ship transfers conducted at offshore anchorage areas, with particular reference to high-risk cargo categories including aviation fuel. This intervention would address the structural regulatory gap that documented ghost fleet operations have exploited in UAE waters and in comparable offshore areas globally. (AI Jan 2026)
