Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management
Profile 6.2
Summary Assessment
Abu Marine Co. Ltd. (Marshall Islands) is the registered owner of BARAAWE 1 (formerly HUITONG 78, IMO 9646479), which carried nine confirmed Jet A-1 deliveries to Myanmar between May 2023 and July 2024. Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management (UAE) is its ISM manager. Both were first named publicly in Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 sanctions call. A critical unresolved question is the relationship between Abu Marine and the CASIC affiliate that the West of England P&I Club identified as HUITONG 78's owner during the period of documented deliveries. Whether Abu Marine is the same entity, a front company, or a successor owner has not been established. Pacific Light did not respond to the Business and Human Rights Resource Centre. Neither entity has been sanctioned by any government.

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Both entities connect to the Myanmar supply chain exclusively through BARAAWE 1 (formerly HUITONG 78), which carried nine confirmed Jet A-1 deliveries to Myanmar between May 2023 and July 2024. Neither Abu Marine nor Pacific Light appeared in any reviewed source during the period of those deliveries. They were first publicly named in Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 press release, which also called for sanctions on Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC for LS MERCURY, and on Sea Route Ship Management FZE for REEF and NOBLE. See Profile 6.1 for the vessel's full delivery record. (JFM Feb 2026)

2
The Ownership Question: Abu Marine and the CASIC Affiliate

In July 2024 the West of England P&I Club told Amnesty International that HUITONG 78 was owned by an affiliate of CASIC, a Chinese state-owned defence company. The specific affiliate was not named. Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 press release identified Abu Marine Co. Ltd. as the registered owner based on publicly available information. No source has established whether Abu Marine is the same entity as, related to, or entirely separate from the CASIC affiliate. The vessel was renamed BARAAWE 1 in November 2024, approximately four months after its final documented Myanmar delivery. Whether that renaming coincided with a change of ownership has not been confirmed. If Abu Marine retains the CASIC affiliate as beneficial owner, the case for diplomatic engagement with China is significantly strengthened. If it represents a genuine ownership transfer, it indicates the vessel has been cycled through a network designed to sustain evasion. Either scenario warrants investigation. (AI Jul 2024; JFM Feb 2026)

The OFAC jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 (23 August 2023), and its equivalents in the UK, EU, Canada, and Australia, provide the applicable designation authority. The Marshall Islands is a major flag and corporate registration state for shipping: many vessels and shipping companies sanctioned in connection with Iranian oil and other illicit trades have been registered there. Abu Marine Co. Ltd.'s Marshall Islands registration is consistent with the broader pattern of shell company formation used in the shadow fleet sector. (JFM Feb 2026)

5
Recommended Actions
•Designate Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management alongside BARAAWE 1. Designating only the vessel without its owner and manager allows evasion through renaming and re-flagging. JFM's February 2026 call provides the submission framework. All five jurisdictions should act simultaneously to prevent displacement. (JFM Feb 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Investigate the beneficial ownership of Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and its relationship to the CASIC affiliate. Corporate registry searches in the Marshall Islands and UAE shipping databases are the most direct starting points. If a CASIC connection is confirmed, diplomatic engagement with China is warranted alongside designation submissions. (AI Jul 2024; JFM Feb 2026)
•Formally notify the Marshall Islands and UAE authorities of the vessel's documented history. The Marshall Islands maritime and corporate registry has cooperated with international sanctions enforcement and issued advisories on sanctions impact on registered vessels. UAE companies are subject to domestic anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance requirements. Both jurisdictions should be formally notified to trigger domestic compliance obligations. (JFM Feb 2026)
