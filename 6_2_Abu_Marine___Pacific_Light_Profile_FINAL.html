Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management
Profile 6.2
Summary Assessment
Abu Marine Co. Ltd., domiciled in the Marshall Islands, is the registered owner of BARAAWE 1 (formerly HUITONG 78, IMO 9646479), the oil products tanker that carried at least nine confirmed shipments of Jet A-1 aviation fuel to Myanmar's military junta between May 2023 and July 2024. Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management, domiciled in the UAE, is the ISM manager of BARAAWE 1. Both entities were identified by Justice For Myanmar in its February 2026 sanctions press release, which called for their coordinated designation across five jurisdictions. Neither entity appears in any reviewed source prior to February 2026, and neither has been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026. Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management did not respond to engagement by the Business and Human Rights Resource Centre following the February 2026 press release. An important unresolved question concerns the relationship between Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and the CASIC affiliate identified as HUITONG 78's owner during the 2023 and 2024 deliveries by the West of England P&I Club: whether Abu Marine Co. Ltd. is a successor owner, a front company for the same beneficial owner, or an entirely separate entity has not been established in any reviewed source.

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Both entities are connected to the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain exclusively through their roles in relation to BARAAWE 1, the vessel formerly known as HUITONG 78 (IMO 9646479). BARAAWE 1 carried at least nine confirmed shipments of Jet A-1 aviation fuel to Myanmar between May 2023 and July 2024 while operating as HUITONG 78, and was publicly named by Amnesty International in its January and July 2024 investigations as the primary vessel in Myanmar's 2023-2024 aviation fuel supply chain. See Profile 6.1 for the vessel's full delivery record and operational history. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)

Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management were not identified in any reviewed source during the period of HUITONG 78's documented deliveries. They were first named publicly in Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release, which drew on publicly available information. The press release called for sanctions on a set of distinct corporate entities covering all four ghost fleet vessels: alongside Abu Marine and Pacific Light in respect of BARAAWE 1, JFM called for sanctions on Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC in respect of LS MERCURY, and on Sea Route Ship Management FZE in respect of REEF and NOBLE. See Profile 6.7 for Sea Route Ship Management FZE. (JFM Feb 2026)

2
The Ownership Question: Abu Marine and the CASIC Affiliate

A significant and unresolved question concerns the relationship between Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and the previous beneficial owner of HUITONG 78. At the time of Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation, the vessel's Protection and Indemnity insurer, the West of England P&I Club, disclosed that HUITONG 78 was owned by an affiliate of China Aerospace Science and Industry Corp (CASIC), a Chinese state-owned defence company. The specific CASIC affiliate was not publicly named in any reviewed source. (AI Jul 2024)

Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release identified Abu Marine Co. Ltd. as the registered owner of BARAAWE 1 based on publicly available information. No reviewed source has established whether Abu Marine Co. Ltd. is the same entity as, or is related to, the CASIC affiliate previously identified by the West of England P&I Club as HUITONG 78's owner. The vessel was renamed from HUITONG 78 to BARAAWE 1 in November 2024, approximately four months after its final documented Myanmar delivery. Whether that renaming coincided with or followed a change of registered ownership has not been confirmed in any reviewed source. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jul 2024)

This unresolved ownership question is of direct relevance to the sanctions and advocacy case. If Abu Marine Co. Ltd. is a nominee or front company retaining the CASIC affiliate as beneficial owner, the CASIC connection would remain active and could support a diplomatic engagement with China as well as designation of the beneficial owner. If Abu Marine Co. Ltd. represents a genuine change of ownership following exposure, it would indicate the vessel has been transferred within a network designed to sustain evasion. Either scenario warrants investigation. (AI Jul 2024; JFM Feb 2026)

The Marshall Islands is a major flag and corporate registration state for shipping: many vessels and shipping companies sanctioned in connection with Iranian oil and other illicit trades have been registered there. Abu Marine Co. Ltd.'s Marshall Islands registration is consistent with the broader pattern of shell company formation used in the shadow fleet sector. (JFM Feb 2026)

5
Recommended Actions
•Designate Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management alongside BARAAWE 1: Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 call for coordinated sanctions on both entities and on the vessel itself remains unimplemented. Designation of the owner and ISM manager alongside the vessel is standard practice in vessel-sanctions frameworks and is necessary to close the evasion pathway: without designating the corporate entities behind a vessel, renaming and re-flagging alone suffice to evade vessel-level sanctions. Civil society should maintain pressure on all five jurisdictions to act on the JFM call. (JFM Feb 2026)
•Investigate the beneficial ownership of Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and its relationship to the CASIC affiliate: The West of England P&I Club's July 2024 disclosure that HUITONG 78 was owned by an affiliate of China Aerospace Science and Industry Corp (CASIC) has not been reconciled with Abu Marine Co. Ltd.'s identification as registered owner in February 2026. Investigative journalists, government intelligence agencies and civil society researchers should seek to establish through corporate registry searches, beneficial ownership registers and shipping databases whether Abu Marine Co. Ltd. is beneficially owned by or connected to the same CASIC affiliate. The Marshall Islands corporate registry and UAE shipping databases are the most direct starting points. If a CASIC connection is confirmed, the case for diplomatic engagement with China is significantly strengthened. (AI Jul 2024; JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage the Marshall Islands registry authority and the UAE on compliance obligations: Abu Marine Co. Ltd.'s Marshall Islands registration and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management's UAE domicile create potential jurisdiction-specific compliance obligations. The Marshall Islands maritime and corporate registry has historically cooperated with international sanctions enforcement and has issued advisories on the impact of sanctions on registered vessels. The UAE is a signatory to various international maritime conventions and its companies are subject to local anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance requirements. Civil society and governments should formally notify both the Marshall Islands and UAE relevant authorities of the vessel's documented history and the February 2026 sanctions call, to trigger domestic compliance obligations. (JFM Feb 2026)
