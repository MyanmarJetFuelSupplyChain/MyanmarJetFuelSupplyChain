Reliance Industries Limited
Profile 5.6
Summary Assessment
Reliance Industries Limited is an Indian multinational conglomerate listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE: 500325) and National Stock Exchange of India (NSE: RELIANCE), and is the largest public company in India by market capitalisation and revenue. It owns and operates the Jamnagar refinery complex in Gujarat, the world's largest oil refining hub, whose marine export terminal at Sikka port was identified by Amnesty International, Global Witness and Burma Campaign UK in their March 2023 investigation as the load port for the December 2022 PRIME V shipment of aviation fuel to Myanmar. That shipment arrived at the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, while Puma Energy retained control of the terminal pending completion of its sale to the Shoon Group, which was finalised in December 2022. The volume of the PRIME V shipment and the vessel's IMO number were not confirmed in the source. PRIME V was beneficially owned by Sea Trade Marine (Greece) and registered with the Japan P&I Club for protection and indemnity insurance; the Japan P&I Club responded to Amnesty International, stating it had complied with applicable sanctions at the time of the shipment and that cover may be terminated where a vessel is involved in illegal activity. Reliance Industries and Sea Trade Marine did not respond to Amnesty International's contact. Reliance Industries does not appear in any subsequent investigated supply chain. Reliance Industries Limited has not been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Reliance Industries Limited is identified in the March 2023 joint investigation by Amnesty International, Global Witness and Burma Campaign UK as the owner of the terminal at Sikka, India, which was the load port for the December 2022 PRIME V shipment of Jet A-1 aviation fuel to Myanmar. PRIME V departed the Sikka Reliance Terminal on 28 November 2022 and arrived at the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, on or about 10 December 2022. The volume of the shipment and the IMO number of PRIME V were not confirmed in the source. (AI Mar 2023)

The December 2022 shipment arrived during the same transition period as the immediately preceding October 2022 BIG SEA 104 shipment from the Bangchak refinery (row 9, Profile 5.5). Puma Energy had announced its Myanmar exit on 5 October 2022 and the sale to the Shoon Group was completed in December 2022. PRIME V arrived at Thilawa on or about 10 December 2022; the terminal remained under Puma Energy's control at the time of the delivery. PRIME V was beneficially owned by Sea Trade Marine, a Greek company, and registered with the Japan P&I Club for protection and indemnity insurance. The Japan P&I Club responded to Amnesty International, stating it had complied with applicable sanctions at the time of the shipment and that insurance cover may be terminated if a vessel is found to be involved in illegal activity; no suggestion was made that PRIME V had broken applicable laws. Neither Reliance Industries nor Sea Trade Marine responded to Amnesty International's contact. (AI Mar 2023)

Key Relationships
•PRIME V: The vessel that transported the December 2022 shipment from the Sikka Reliance Terminal, Jamnagar, to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon. Departed 28 November 2022, arrived on or about 10 December 2022. The IMO number and cargo volume were not confirmed in the source. Beneficially owned by Sea Trade Marine (Greece). (AI Mar 2023)
•Sea Trade Marine (Greece): The beneficial owner of PRIME V at the time of the December 2022 shipment. Its ownership of the vessel makes it a direct co-participant in the documented shipment alongside Reliance Industries as the refinery and terminal owner. Sea Trade Marine did not respond to Amnesty International's contact. (AI Mar 2023)
•Japan P&I Club (P&I insurer for PRIME V): Provided protection and indemnity insurance for PRIME V at the time of the December 2022 shipment. The Japan P&I Club responded to Amnesty International, stating that it had complied with applicable sanctions at the time and that insurance cover may be terminated if the vessel is found to have been involved in illegal activity. No suggestion was made that PRIME V broke applicable laws. (AI Mar 2023)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited / former PEAS terminal (Thilawa, Yangon): The destination terminal to which the December 2022 PRIME V shipment was delivered. At the time of delivery, the terminal was in the final stages of transition from Puma Energy control, with the sale to the Shoon Group completed in December 2022. The terminal operating company itself has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. Its direct owner, P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (Singapore), is sanctioned by the US. Its parent group entity Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. and the beneficial owner of the ownership chain, Khin Phyu Win, are sanctioned by the US, UK and Canada. See Profile 1.3 and Joint Profile 2.1. (AI Mar 2023)
•Puma Energy Holdings Pte. Ltd.: Puma Energy announced its Myanmar exit on 5 October 2022 and completed the sale to the Shoon Group in December 2022. The PRIME V shipment was one of two shipments (the other being the October 2022 BIG SEA 104 shipment from the Bangchak refinery, row 9, Profile 5.5) that arrived at Thilawa during this post-announcement, pre-completion transition period. See Profile 5.1. (AI Mar 2023)
•Bangchak Corporation Public Company Limited: The October 2022 BIG SEA 104 shipment (row 9) loaded at the Bangchak Phra Khanong Refinery, Bangkok Port, Thailand, departed approximately 8 October 2022 and arrived Thilawa approximately 15 October 2022. Together with the December 2022 PRIME V shipment from Sikka, these are the two documented shipments that arrived at the Thilawa terminal during the Puma Energy exit transition period. Both Bangchak and Reliance Industries failed to respond to Amnesty International's letters. See Profile 5.5. (AI Mar 2023)

5
Recommended Actions
•Seek clarification of Reliance Industries' commercial role in the December 2022 transaction: The source record establishes that Reliance Industries owns the terminal at Sikka that served as the load port for the PRIME V shipment, but does not confirm whether Reliance Industries was the direct seller of the Jet A-1 or whether an intermediary trader purchased the fuel at the terminal. Civil society should engage Reliance Industries directly, requesting disclosure of: the commercial structure of the December 2022 transaction, including the identity of the purchaser at the Sikka terminal; what end-use due diligence Reliance Industries conducted on the purchaser; and what policies Reliance Industries now applies to sales of aviation turbine fuel where the purchaser may transport it to Myanmar or to entities associated with the Myanmar military. As a BSE/NSE-listed company with GDRs on the London Stock Exchange, Reliance Industries is subject to engagement by institutional investors in multiple jurisdictions. (AI Mar 2023)
•Monitor for any future Reliance Industries terminal involvement in Myanmar-bound aviation fuel supply chains: Reliance Industries' documented connection is limited to the single December 2022 shipment. The company does not appear in any reviewed investigation after AI Mar 2023. However, the Jamnagar complex produces aviation turbine fuel at scale and the Sikka terminal is a major petroleum product export hub. If any future Myanmar-bound shipment is traced to the Sikka terminal or any other Reliance Industries export facility, that finding should trigger immediate re-engagement, formal disclosure requests, and, where evidence is sufficient, designation submissions to OFAC, OTSI, and equivalent bodies in the EU, Canada and Australia. (AI Mar 2023)
