PEIA Pte. Ltd. & P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. & Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.
Profile 2.1
Summary Assessment
Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. and its two subsidiaries, PEIA Pte. Ltd. and P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd., are Singapore-registered holding companies through which Khin Phyu Win (also known as Kyauk Kyar Shwe and Zhao Jia Shui), a central figure in the Asia Sun Group, controls the Shoon Group's aviation fuel interests in Myanmar. Each company serves a distinct function: PEIA Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Aviation Pte. Ltd., incorporated 30 October 2014) holds a 49% beneficial stake in National Energy Prime Aviation Services Company Limited (NEPAS), a joint venture with the military-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (51%) that is responsible for the import, storage, and sale of aviation fuel at 11 airports across Myanmar; P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd., incorporated 17 August 2012) is the owner of Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited, which operates the main aviation fuel terminal and storage tanks at Thilawa port, Yangon; and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd., incorporated 27 June 2018) is the parent holding company and is involved in aviation fuel export and distribution. All three entities were originally Puma Energy vehicles. In 2018 Puma Energy sold 30% of PEIA to Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd., giving the Asia Sun Group an approximately 16% indirect stake in NEPAS. In December 2022, as part of Puma Energy's full Myanmar exit, its remaining 70% stake in PEIA and its other Myanmar assets passed to Khin Phyu Win and the Shoon Group. All three are owned and controlled by Khin Phyu Win per OFAC's August 2023 designation statement. All three were designated by the US in August 2023. Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. was also designated by the UK on 27 March 2023 and is covered by Canada's sanctions framework through Asia Sun Group Company Limited under the Special Economic Measures (Burma) Regulations. PEIA Pte. Ltd. and P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. have not been designated by the UK, EU, Canada, or Australia, and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. has not been designated by the EU or Australia. Despite sanctions, all three remained active on Singapore's corporate registry as of May 2025, and the Thilawa terminal continued to receive Jet A-1 shipments as of January 2026.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain
Group structure and function

Justice for Myanmar describes the Shoon Group (formerly Asia Sun Group) as a group of Myanmar crony companies "involved in procuring, storing and distributing aviation fuel in partnership with the junta," with subsidiaries in both Singapore and Myanmar "that have been found to import aviation fuel and directly deliver it to the Myanmar military." The Singapore holding structure consists of Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. as the parent company, with PEIA Pte. Ltd. and P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. as its subsidiaries. The three companies serve complementary and interlocking functions: together they cover the NEPAS shareholding, the Thilawa terminal, and the export and distribution side of the aviation fuel trade. All three are owned and controlled by Khin Phyu Win. (JFM Aug 2023; JFM May 2025; OFAC Aug 2023)

PEIA Pte. Ltd.: beneficial shareholder of NEPAS

PEIA Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Aviation Pte. Ltd.) holds a 49% beneficial stake in National Energy Prime Aviation Services Company Limited (NEPAS), a joint venture in which the military-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise holds the remaining 51%. NEPAS operates storage and refuelling facilities at 11 airports across Myanmar and is the primary vehicle through which aviation fuel enters Myanmar's distribution network and reaches military air bases. PEIA's 49% stake makes it the minority partner in Myanmar's most strategically significant aviation fuel entity. (JFM Aug 2023; JFM May 2025; AI Nov 2022)

PEIA was originally incorporated in 2014 as Puma Energy Irrawaddy Aviation Pte. Ltd., Puma Energy's Singapore holding vehicle for its 49% NEPAS beneficial stake. In 2018, Puma Energy sold 30% of PEIA to Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd. (the entity now named Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.), which gave Asia Sun Aviation an indirect NEPAS beneficial stake of approximately 16%, as documented in Amnesty International's November 2022 Deadly Cargo report. In December 2022, as part of Puma Energy's full Myanmar exit, Puma Energy transferred its remaining 70% stake in PEIA to Khin Phyu Win and the Shoon Group. Following this transfer, PEIA holds the full 49% beneficial NEPAS stake, no longer shared with Puma Energy, and was renamed from Puma Energy Irrawaddy Aviation Pte. Ltd. to PEIA Pte. Ltd. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023; OFAC Aug 2023)

P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd.: owner of the Thilawa terminal

P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd.) is the Singapore holding company that owns Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited, formerly known as Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited. Myan Oil Terminal operates the major aviation fuel terminal and storage tanks at Thilawa port, Yangon, the primary entry point for imported Jet A-1 into Myanmar. This terminal is the physical infrastructure through which international Jet A-1 shipments are offloaded, stored, and passed into the NEPAS distribution network. Successive Amnesty International investigations, from March 2023 through January 2026, documented continued shipments of Jet A-1 arriving at this terminal, confirming its uninterrupted function as the gateway for imported aviation fuel. (JFM May 2025; AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)

Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.: export and distribution, and acquisition of Puma Energy's assets

Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd.) is the parent holding company and is described by Justice for Myanmar as involved in aviation fuel export and distribution. Under its former name, it held a 30% stake in PEIA, giving the Asia Sun Group an approximately 16% indirect beneficial interest in NEPAS (detailed in the PEIA section above). Following the December 2022 Puma Energy exit and the transfer of Puma's 70% of PEIA to Khin Phyu Win, Shoon Energy's beneficial connection to NEPAS is now exercised through its subsidiary PEIA's full 49% stake. (AI Nov 2022; JFM May 2025)

In December 2022, Puma Energy completed the sale of its Myanmar aviation fuel assets to Shoon Energy, then still named Asia Sun Aviation. Amnesty International assessed that following this transfer, the Asia Sun conglomerate took over management of the Thilawa port terminal and, jointly with military-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise, the import and distribution of aviation fuel across Myanmar. The company was renamed from Asia Sun Aviation to Shoon Energy ahead of UK and EU sanctions. Amnesty International assessed that given the close relationship between Shoon Energy and the Myanmar military, Puma Energy's assurance that the buyer had undertaken to comply with human rights law was "essentially meaningless." (AI Mar 2023)

Continued activity despite sanctions

Justice for Myanmar confirmed in May 2025 that all three Singapore companies remained active on Singapore's corporate registry despite sanctions imposed in 2023. Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation documented Jet A-1 arriving at the Myan Oil terminal in Yangon, the facility owned through P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd., confirming that the physical infrastructure of the supply chain continued to function. (JFM May 2025; AI Jan 2026)

Key Relationships
•Khin Phyu Win (also known as Kyauk Kyar Shwe and Zhao Jia Shui): The controlling individual behind all three Singapore companies, confirmed by OFAC's August 2023 designation statement and the UK's March 2023 designation of Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. and Khin Phyu Win simultaneously. The April 2022 transfer of 11 Asia Sun Group Myanmar companies to Zaw Min Tun covered Myanmar-registered entities only; the three Singapore holding companies were not transferred to Zaw Min Tun and remained under Khin Phyu Win's ownership and control throughout. Khin Phyu Win is sanctioned by the UK (27 March 2023, MYA0067), US (August 2023), and Canada. (AI Nov 2022; UK Mar 2023; OFAC Aug 2023)
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Company Limited (NEPAS): The joint venture between the Shoon Group (via PEIA Pte. Ltd., 49%) and Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (51%) that is responsible for the import, storage, and sale of aviation fuel across Myanmar, operating facilities at 11 airports. PEIA Pte. Ltd. holds the 49% minority stake, exercising the Shoon Group's controlling interest in the most strategically significant entity in Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. NEPAS is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 1.2. (JFM Aug 2023; JFM May 2025)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited (formerly Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited): The terminal operator at Thilawa port, Yangon, owned by P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. This is the facility through which imported Jet A-1 is offloaded and stored before entering the NEPAS distribution network. It was formerly operated by Puma Energy through Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited and passed to the Shoon Group following the December 2022 asset sale. See Profile 1.3. (JFM May 2025; AI Mar 2023)
•Asia Sun Group Company Limited / Shoon Group (Myanmar entities): The Myanmar-registered parent conglomerate of which the three Singapore companies are the holding arm. Asia Sun Group imported aviation fuel on behalf of the military and distributed it to air bases. Asia Sun Group Company Limited is sanctioned by the US, UK, EU, Canada, and Australia. See Profile 1.4. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
•Puma Energy: The Singapore-registered, Geneva-based multinational that previously held interests in NEPAS and managed the Thilawa port terminal through Puma Energy Irrawaddy Aviation Pte. Ltd. and Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd., the direct predecessors of PEIA Pte. Ltd. and P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. respectively. Puma Energy completed the sale of its Myanmar assets to Shoon Energy (then Asia Sun Aviation) in December 2022, transferring the NEPAS stake and Thilawa terminal control to the Shoon Group. Puma Energy's public exit statement named only "a locally owned private company" as the buyer. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)

Ltd. were designated for being owned or controlled by Khin Phyu Win. Shoon Energy Pte. (MYA0066) and Khin Phyu Win (MYA0067) on 27 March 2023, on Myanmar Armed Forces Day, on the basis of profiting from activity involving the supply of aviation fuel. The EU designated related Myanmar entities, Asia Sun Group Company Limited, Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd., and Asia Sun Energy Company Limited, in February 2023, but has not designated any of the three Singapore holding companies. Canada has not designated PEIA Pte. Ltd. or P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. as named entries; see the note below on Canada's coverage of Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. through the SEMA framework. Australia has not designated any of the three entities. (UK Mar 2023; OFAC Aug 2023)

Sanctions gap: Only the US has designated all three Singapore entities. The UK designated Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. but left PEIA Pte. Ltd. and P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. outside its sanctions perimeter. Australia has designated none of the three Singapore companies. PEIA's beneficial shareholding in NEPAS and P.E.I Energy's ownership of the Thilawa terminal operator are the structural links that give the Shoon Group direct control over Myanmar's aviation fuel import infrastructure; their designation by the UK, Canada, and Australia, alongside EU designation of all three entities, would close the most significant remaining gaps in this cluster. The EU has designated none of the three, despite having designated their Myanmar parent in February 2023. The absence of any Singapore-jurisdiction measures means all three entities operate without restriction in their country of registration. (OFAC Aug 2023; JFM May 2025)
Note on Canada / Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.: Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. does not appear as a named entry in Canada's Special Economic Measures (Burma) Regulations (SEMA) sanctions list. Canada is marked as sanctioned here because Canada's SEMA regulations include Asia Sun Group Company Limited, of which Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. is a subsidiary and affiliated company. Under Canadian sanctions law, entities owned or controlled by a designated person or entity are subject to the same prohibitions as the designated entity. Canada's October 2023 aviation fuel ban (SOR/2023-228, effective 31 October 2023) also prohibits the export, sale, supply and shipment of aviation fuel, and the provision of shipping insurance, to Myanmar, a prohibition that applies to all actors in the supply chain regardless of individual designation status. Advocates should verify the precise legal basis for any enforcement action under Canadian law.

5
Recommended Actions
•Designate PEIA Pte. Ltd. and P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd. in the UK, EU, Canada, and Australia, and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. in the EU and Australia: Only the US has designated all three Singapore entities. The UK has designated only Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.; Canada and Australia have designated neither PEIA nor P.E.I Energy, and Australia has not designated any of the three. PEIA's role as beneficial shareholder of NEPAS (49% stake in the joint venture with MPE covering 11 airports) and P.E.I Energy's ownership of the Thilawa terminal operator make them the more operationally significant entities in the cluster. The OFAC Aug 2023 designation basis, which covers entities owned or controlled by a designated person, maps directly to the UK, Canadian, and Australian Myanmar sanctions frameworks. Civil society should submit the JFM May 2025 corporate structure analysis and the OFAC Aug 2023 designation statement to OFSI, Global Affairs Canada, and the Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade as the primary evidentiary package. (OFAC Aug 2023; JFM May 2025)
•Designate all three Singapore companies in the EU: The EU designated Myanmar-registered entities in the Asia Sun Group in February 2023 but has not designated any of the three Singapore holding companies. The Singapore entities are the structural mechanism through which the Shoon Group controls NEPAS and the Thilawa terminal. The EU Myanmar sanctions framework covers entities providing economic resources that benefit the Myanmar military. The JFM May 2025 corporate structure analysis, AI Mar 2023, and OFAC Aug 2023 collectively provide the evidence base for EU designation submissions. (AI Mar 2023; OFAC Aug 2023; JFM May 2025)
•Engage Singapore authorities on enforcement and corporate registry: All three companies remain active on Singapore's corporate registry despite being sanctioned in one or more jurisdictions. Singapore has not imposed targeted sanctions on the Myanmar junta. Civil society should engage the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), the Ministry of Finance, and the Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA) to seek regulatory action against sanctioned entities using Singapore as an operating base. The continued registry activity of PEIA Pte. Ltd. and P.E.I Energy Pte. Ltd., both designated by the US, provides a direct basis for engagement with Singapore authorities on compliance with US financial sanctions obligations for Singapore-based financial institutions. (JFM May 2025; JFM Mar 2023)
