Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar Ltd.
Profile 6.9
Summary Assessment
Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar Ltd. (WSS Myanmar) is the Myanmar subsidiary of Wilh. Wilhelmsen Holding ASA, a Norwegian maritime group headquartered in Lysaker, Norway. WSS Myanmar acted as shipping agent for a number of Jet A-1 aviation fuel shipments delivered to the Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal at Thilawa, Yangon. Its documented functions included port captaincy at the berth and transmission of proforma disbursement accounts on behalf of vessel owners. Between October 2020 and June 2021, WSS Myanmar received at least MMK 15.7 million (approximately USD 7,500) from NEPAS and PUMA-related shipments for these services. Wilhelmsen's engagement with the supply chain was confirmed through both leaked corporate documents and Wilhelmsen's own correspondence with Amnesty International. Following Amnesty International's pre-publication engagement as part of the Deadly Cargo investigation, Wilhelmsen issued a cessation statement on 18 October 2022 committing WSS Myanmar to immediately cease providing agency services for any vessel or cargo owner discharging Jet A-1 aviation fuel at ports in Myanmar, irrespective of end-use designations. This commitment was made before the Deadly Cargo report was published and has been confirmed as maintained in subsequent investigations. Neither WSS Myanmar nor Wilh. Wilhelmsen Holding ASA has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. The Wilhelmsen case is documented alongside the Thai Oil case (Profile 5.12) as a model of how pre-publication CSO engagement can produce immediate cessation commitments from named supply chain participants.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar Ltd. provided shipping agency services to tankers discharging Jet A-1 aviation fuel at the Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal at Thilawa port, Yangon. Amnesty International confirmed WSS Myanmar's role through a combination of leaked corporate documents and Wilhelmsen's own written correspondence. Wilhelmsen acknowledged that its Myanmar entity acted as "Owner's Protective Agent for tankers discharging aviation fuel at the PEAS terminal in Thilawa port with HS Code: 10198210 Aviation Turbine Fuel (Jet A-1)." Its specific functions, as described in Wilhelmsen's own correspondence, were acting as "Owner's Representative on behalf of the vessel owner/operator for the transmission of proforma disbursement accounts and port captaincy on the berth." These are standard agency functions that make the agent an essential operational intermediary for each discharge. Between October 2020 and June 2021, WSS Myanmar received at least MMK 15.7 million (approximately USD 7,500) for managing NEPAS and PUMA shipments in this capacity. The total number of shipments for which WSS Myanmar provided agency services is not specified in reviewed sources. (AI Nov 2022)

Wilhelmsen maintained that the aviation fuel imported to the PEAS terminal for NEPAS was clearly described for domestic airline sales only. Amnesty International assessed that this characterisation could not be relied upon. Puma Energy had itself acknowledged that it was not able to "maintain controls to restrict supply to civilian only airlines," demonstrating that end-use warranties for fuel discharged at the PEAS terminal were structurally unreliable regardless of how shipments were described at the point of import. Wilhelmsen does not appear in any of the four Amnesty International investigations into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain published after November 2022: the March 2023 follow-up, the January 2024 investigation, the July 2024 investigation, and the January 2026 investigation documenting Iran-linked ghost ships. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)

Key Relationships
•Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal, Thilawa (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The terminal at which WSS Myanmar provided shipping agency services for Jet A-1 discharges. At the time of WSS Myanmar's documented activity, the terminal was operated under Puma Energy's management through the NEPAS joint venture. Puma Energy completed the sale of the terminal to the Shoon Group in December 2022. WSS Myanmar's cessation in October 2022 preceded the Puma Energy exit by approximately two months. The terminal is now operated under the Shoon Energy and Myan Oil Terminal network, which continues to receive aviation fuel supply. See the master shipment table for post-October 2022 deliveries to the former PEAS terminal. (AI Nov 2022)
•NEPAS (Profile 2.1): The National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. joint venture that managed the import and domestic distribution of aviation fuel at the Thilawa terminal on behalf of Myanmar's military. WSS Myanmar received documented payments from NEPAS-related shipments between October 2020 and June 2021. NEPAS was the primary commercial vehicle through which the Myanmar military's aviation fuel supply chain was managed during the period of WSS Myanmar's documented agency activity. (AI Nov 2022)
•Thai Oil Public Company Limited (Profile 5.12): WSS Myanmar and Thai Oil are the two named supply chain participants documented as committing to cease their respective contributions to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain before the Deadly Cargo report was published on 3 November 2022. Thai Oil suspended sales on 14 October 2022; WSS Myanmar issued its cessation statement on 18 October 2022. Both commitments were made following direct pre-publication engagement by Amnesty International. Pan Ocean Co. Ltd. (Profile 6.10) made the same commitment after publication. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
4
Advocacy Interventions and Outcome
Documented positive advocacy outcome (pre-publication cessation): Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar committed to immediately cease providing shipping agency services for any vessel or cargo owner discharging Jet A-1 aviation fuel at ports in Myanmar. This commitment was made on 18 October 2022 in direct response to Amnesty International's pre-publication engagement as part of the Deadly Cargo investigation, four days after Thai Oil's equivalent commitment. The cessation language is broader than a conditional hold: WSS Myanmar stated it would cease providing these services "irrespective of designations regarding end use" and that "this will remain the case until further notice." WSS Myanmar does not appear in any reviewed investigation into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain published after November 2022.
•Pre-November 2022 (documented period October 2020 to June 2021). WSS Myanmar acts as Owner's Protective Agent for tankers discharging Jet A-1 aviation fuel at the PEAS terminal, Thilawa. During the sub-period October 2020 to June 2021, WSS Myanmar received at least MMK 15.7 million (approximately USD 7,500) for these services in connection with NEPAS and PUMA shipments. Wilhelmsen's role is confirmed through both leaked corporate documents obtained by Amnesty International and Wilhelmsen's own correspondence. (AI Nov 2022)
•18 October 2022. Wilhelmsen issues its cessation statement to Amnesty International in response to pre-publication engagement: "Having thoroughly reviewed the latest information provided by Amnesty International with our case handling team, our ships agency colleagues in Myanmar, and senior management, we have determined that Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar will not risk contributing in any way towards the human rights violations outlined by your report. Therefore, Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar will immediately cease to provide agency services of any kind for vessel or cargo owners discharging Jet A-1 aviation fuel at ports in Myanmar, irrespective of designations regarding end use. This will remain the case until further notice." (AI Nov 2022)
•3 November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo report, produced in research collaboration with Justice for Myanmar, is published. It documents WSS Myanmar's role as shipping agent for Jet A-1 discharges at the PEAS terminal and its cessation commitment. The report identifies WSS Myanmar's response alongside Thai Oil's as among the most significant pre-publication advocacy outcomes of the investigation. (AI Nov 2022)
•1 March 2023. The follow-up investigation published by Amnesty International, Global Witness and Burma Campaign UK confirms that Wilhelmsen ceased providing shipping agency services to any vessel discharging Jet A-1 at ports in Myanmar following the Deadly Cargo investigation. WSS Myanmar does not appear as a party to any of the newly identified shipments. (AI Mar 2023)
•January 2024, July 2024 and 27 January 2026. Three further Amnesty International investigations document Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain: "Myanmar: New Data Suggests Military Still Importing Fuel for Deadly Air Strikes Despite Sanctions" (January 2024), "Myanmar: Reckless Shipments of Jet Fuel Continue as Air Strikes Multiply" (July 2024), and "Myanmar: Jet Fuel Used in Deadly Air Strikes Flowing in on Ghost Ships with Suspected Links to Iran" (27 January 2026). WSS Myanmar does not appear in any of these investigations. Across all four post-2022 investigations, the supply chain documented has shifted progressively toward Iran-linked ghost ships and Vietnamese intermediary routes. WSS Myanmar's exit from the documented supply chain has been sustained through this period. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Confirm the current status of the cessation and seek a formal public policy commitment from Wilh. Wilhelmsen Holding ASA: WSS Myanmar's October 2022 cessation was communicated privately to Amnesty International and was framed as an immediate commitment that "will remain the case until further notice" rather than a permanent policy withdrawal. No public statement from Wilh. Wilhelmsen Holding ASA or WSS Myanmar committing to a permanent exit from Myanmar aviation fuel agency services has been identified in reviewed sources. The cessation was also limited in its explicit scope to WSS Myanmar; the parent company's position on group-level policy for Myanmar aviation fuel has not been publicly stated. Civil society should engage Wilh. Wilhelmsen Holding ASA directly to confirm the cessation remains in effect, to clarify whether it constitutes group policy, and to request a formal public commitment. (AI Nov 2022)
•Monitor Wilhelmsen Group's maritime service activity in Myanmar, including at the former PEAS terminal: WSS Myanmar's cessation applied to agency services for Jet A-1 discharges at ports in Myanmar. The former PEAS terminal at Thilawa continues to receive aviation fuel deliveries under the Shoon Energy and Myan Oil Terminal network. Subsequent investigations through January 2026 document a supply chain that has shifted toward ghost ships and Iranian-linked intermediaries. Any resumption of Wilhelmsen Group maritime services to vessels supplying aviation fuel to Myanmar, whether directly or through subsidiaries or affiliate arrangements, should be treated as a matter for immediate re-engagement and disclosure requests. Civil society should monitor vessel agency service records and port call logs for the former PEAS terminal and other Myanmar ports in connection with Jet A-1 cargoes. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
