Myanma Economic Bank (MEB)
Profile 8.3
Summary Assessment
Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) is a Myanmar state-owned bank that became the primary conduit for military procurement conducted through state-owned financial institutions following the imposition of US sanctions on Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) in June 2023. As of the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report, MEB accounted for "the entirety of the SAC's military procurement conducted through state-owned financial institutions" and processed over USD 60 million in military procurement payments in the second half of 2023 alone. MEB also processed approximately USD 165 million in Thai Baht revenues on behalf of Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE) in a single fiscal quarter of FY2023 (approximately USD 55 million per month), making it the key gateway for oil and gas revenues that form the junta's largest single source of external income. Separately, at least eight international financial institutions have held nostro accounts for MEB since the coup. MEB is not directly documented in the reviewed sources as the payment channel for aviation fuel purchases, which in calendar year 2023 passed through United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1), a private bank. However, MEB's role as the state banking infrastructure through which the junta funds its broader military operations, including arms procurement and gas revenue processing, makes it an integral part of the financial architecture sustaining the airstrikes documented throughout this database. MEB has been designated by Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act. It has not been designated by the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union or Australia as of April 2026.

1
Role in the Myanmar Military Financial Infrastructure

MEB's significance to this database arises from its role as the primary state-owned bank through which the Myanmar junta conducts military procurement and processes gas revenues, following sanctions that rendered MFTB and MICB largely non-functional as international financial intermediaries. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report documents MEB's emergence as the junta's principal state banking infrastructure in the period after June 2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Absorption of military procurement following MFTB and MICB sanctions. When the United States designated MFTB and MICB in June 2023, international financial institutions began closing or restricting nostro accounts for those banks. The junta responded by shifting transactions to MEB, which at that point had been sanctioned only by Canada. The UN Special Rapporteur found that by FY2023 (April 2023 to March 2024), MEB had come to account for "the entirety of the SAC's military procurement conducted through state-owned financial institutions." MEB processed over USD 60 million in military procurement payments in the second half of 2023 alone. Transaction volumes at MEB grew sharply after June 2023 sanctions on MFTB and MICB: from less than USD 80 million total in Q1 FY2023 (April to June 2023), to over USD 470 million in Q2, and to nearly USD 500 million in Q3, which included receipt of over USD 330 million in incoming international payments. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)

Processing of MOGE gas revenues. MEB serves as the conduit through which Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE) receives gas revenues from Thailand. In Q3 FY2023, MEB processed approximately USD 165 million in Thai Baht revenues on behalf of MOGE, equivalent to approximately USD 55 million per month. These revenues constitute the junta's largest single source of external income and fund its broader military operations including aviation fuel procurement. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Sanctions evasion through renaming. The UN Special Rapporteur documented that the SAC's energy ministry attempted to open new MEB accounts for MOGE revenues under the name "Kant Kaw" rather than MOGE, in an effort to disguise the connection to MOGE and evade the US MOGE Financial Services Directive issued on 31 October 2023. This attempt was evidenced by a leaked letter from the energy ministry to the Central Bank of Myanmar. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Relationship to aviation fuel procurement. MEB is not directly documented in any reviewed source as the payment bank for aviation fuel purchases. Aviation fuel payments in calendar year 2023 passed through United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1), a private Myanmar bank. However, MEB sits within the same state financial architecture that funds military operations broadly, and MEB's processing of MOGE revenues and military procurement payments supports the junta's capacity to fund aviation fuel purchases through parallel channels. For cross-reference with all documented jet fuel shipments, see the master shipment table. (UNSR Jun 2024)

International Correspondent Banking Relationships

At least eight financial institutions have provided nostro account services to MEB since the coup, enabling MEB to conduct international settlements in foreign currencies. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report (Figure 5) documents the following institutions as having held nostro accounts for MEB:

•OCBC Bank (Singapore)
•UOB (Singapore)
•DBS Bank (Singapore)
•Novikom Bank (Russia)
•International Financial Club (Russia): subsequently halted all international transactions after being sanctioned by the United States in February 2024
•Bangkok Bank (Thailand)
•China Construction Bank (China)
•Krung Thai Bank (Thailand) (Profile 8.2): also documented as the Thai-side receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023

MEB has been designated by Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA). No start date is recorded in the available sanctions data for the Canadian designation. MEB has not been designated by the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union or Australia as of April 2026. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report specifically recommended that all five jurisdictions sanction MEB, alongside MFTB, MICB and Myanma Agricultural Development Bank, "for as long as the Myanmar military maintains control over them." The report also noted that under current UK and EU law all Myanmar state-owned enterprises should be considered sanctioned given EU and UK sanctions on the SAC itself, but that both jurisdictions have failed to treat MEB as sanctioned in practice. The UNSR described this as a "significant oversight" creating confusion for financial institutions. (UNSR Jun 2024)

4
Key Relationships
•Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) (Profile 8.4): MEB's fellow state-owned banks, both designated by the US, Canada and Australia. Following their designation by the US in June 2023, international correspondent banks began withdrawing nostro account services from MFTB and MICB, causing the junta to redirect military procurement through MEB. MFTB and MICB remain in operation but with severely diminished international access; MEB has assumed their former role as the primary international banking interface for the junta. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)
•United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1): The private Myanmar bank used to process aviation fuel payments in calendar year 2023. UAB and MEB are distinct entities operating in parallel within the junta's financial infrastructure: UAB handled aviation fuel procurement payments through international commercial channels, while MEB handled broader military procurement and gas revenues through state banking channels. Both are identified by the UN Special Rapporteur as facilitating junta procurement. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank (Thailand) (Profile 8.2): One of at least eight financial institutions that held a nostro account for MEB since the coup. KTB is separately documented as the Thai-side receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023, establishing KTB's dual exposure to both the aviation fuel payment chain and MEB's correspondent banking network. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE): MEB serves as the conduit through which MOGE receives gas revenues from Thailand, including approximately USD 55 million per month in Thai Baht payments during Q3 FY2023. These revenues are the junta's largest single source of external income. The SAC's energy ministry attempted to disguise MOGE revenue flows through MEB under the name "Kant Kaw" to evade the US MOGE Financial Services Directive of October 2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)
5
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•June 2023. The United States designated MFTB and MICB, but not MEB. MEB remained unsanctioned by all jurisdictions except Canada at the time of these designations. The UN Special Rapporteur's subsequent documentation confirmed that MEB assumed the entirety of the junta's military procurement through state-owned financial institutions following the MFTB and MICB designations, consistent with the risk that sanctioning only some state-owned banks would cause the junta to shift to unsanctioned alternatives. (JFM Jan 2025; UNSR Jun 2024)
•June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report documents MEB's emergence as the primary state-owned bank for junta military procurement, including its processing of over USD 60 million in military procurement payments in H2 2023 and approximately USD 165 million in MOGE gas revenues in Q3 FY2023. The report explicitly recommends the designation of MEB by all five major sanctions jurisdictions and notes that MEB's failure to be designated by the US, UK, EU and Australia represents a significant gap in the sanctions regime. The report also documents the "Kant Kaw" evasion attempt, demonstrating MEB's active participation in sanctions circumvention efforts. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•July 2024. A joint statement by 240 Myanmar and international organisations called on the international community to impose coordinated sanctions on MEB and MOGE, following the UNSR's June 2024 report. The statement highlighted that MEB had "become a key conduit for the junta's access to international financial services which it uses to buy weapons" and called on the US specifically to sanction MEB and Myanma Agricultural Development Bank. (Civil society joint statement, July 2024)
•January 2025. Justice For Myanmar's reporting confirms that following US sanctions on MFTB and MICB, the junta shifted transactions to MEB, which remained unsanctioned in most jurisdictions. JFM also flags the risk that MOGE could shift revenue payments to MEB to evade the US MOGE Financial Services Directive, noting Interra Resources' acknowledgment that MOGE payments are made through a Myanmar bank whose sanctions status it could not confirm. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•February 2025. A joint letter from Burma Campaign UK and a coalition of civil society organisations was sent to US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Secretary of State Marco Rubio on 4 February 2025, calling for immediate US sanctions on MEB. The letter argued that MEB had become the junta's principal remaining banking gateway to the international financial system and that sanctions on MEB would leave the junta with "no viable banking alternatives under its direct control." The letter noted that MEB's primarily domestic customer base means that MEB sanctions would have limited adverse humanitarian impact on Myanmar civilians, while significantly degrading the junta's access to international financial markets for weapons procurement. As of April 2026, the United States has not sanctioned MEB. (Civil society letter, February 2025)
•May 2025. Justice For Myanmar's Fuelling Atrocities report confirms that Krung Thai Bank (Profile 8.2) continued to hold a nostro account for MEB as of the time of publication, notwithstanding the UNSR's June 2024 findings and the December 2024 BOT/AMLO regulatory directive to Thai banks. MEB remains unsanctioned by the US, UK, EU and Australia as of April 2026. (JFM May 2025)
6
Recommended Actions
•Designate MEB under Myanmar-related sanctions authorities: The US, UK, EU and Australia each maintain Myanmar-specific sanctions authorities under which MEB should be designated, in accordance with the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 recommendation. The UNSR's documentation that MEB accounted for the entirety of the SAC's military procurement through state-owned financial institutions in FY2023, processed over USD 60 million in military procurement payments in H2 2023, and actively participated in sanctions circumvention through the "Kant Kaw" renaming scheme provides a clear evidentiary basis for designation submissions. The UK and EU should additionally note the UNSR's finding that their own "control" tests, as applied to their existing sanctions on the SAC, should already render MEB subject to their sanctions regimes. Failure to designate MEB explicitly perpetuates the "significant oversight" identified by the UNSR. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Engage all institutions holding MEB nostro accounts to request termination: At least eight financial institutions have held nostro accounts for MEB since the coup. Of these, International Financial Club (Russia) halted all international transactions after being sanctioned by the US in February 2024. The status of the remaining seven accounts held by OCBC, UOB, DBS Bank, Novikom Bank, Bangkok Bank, China Construction Bank and Krung Thai Bank has not been publicly confirmed as of April 2026. Civil society, regulatory authorities and governments with jurisdiction over these institutions should engage each of them with the UNSR's June 2024 findings and request confirmation that MEB nostro accounts have been terminated. The BOT/AMLO December 2024 directive on enhanced due diligence for Myanmar-linked transactions provides a specific regulatory basis for requiring KTB to confirm its MEB account status. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Investigate MOGE's use of MEB for both revenue receipts and outgoing payments: The UNSR's June 2024 report documents MEB already processing MOGE's incoming gas revenues from Thailand by FY2023. The "Kant Kaw" renaming attempt demonstrates that the SAC's energy ministry was actively seeking to disguise MOGE-linked revenue flows through MEB to evade the US MOGE Financial Services Directive. Separately, JFM has flagged the risk that MOGE could shift its outgoing payments to oil and gas joint venture partners, previously documented as passing through MFTB, to MEB in order to evade sanctions on MFTB. Investigative journalists, financial intelligence units and sanctions authorities should investigate both dimensions: whether MOGE revenue receipt flows through MEB have continued or expanded since the Directive, and whether MEB has become the channel for outgoing MOGE payments to joint venture partners previously routed through MFTB. International banks providing nostro accounts to MEB may be implicated in either direction of these flows. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
