Myanma Economic Bank (MEB)
Profile 8.3
Summary Assessment
Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) is the Myanmar state-owned bank that became the junta's primary financial gateway after US sanctions on MFTB and MICB in June 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur documented that by FY2023 MEB accounted for the entirety of the SAC's military procurement through state-owned financial institutions, processing over USD 60 million in military procurement payments in H2 2023 alone and approximately USD 165 million in MOGE gas revenues in a single quarter. At least eight international financial institutions have held nostro accounts for MEB since the coup. MEB is not the documented payment bank for aviation fuel purchases in 2023 (that role was played by UAB), but it sits at the centre of the broader military financing architecture that sustains the air campaign. MEB has been designated by Canada. It has not been designated by the US, UK, EU, or Australia.

1
Role in the Myanmar Military Financial Infrastructure

Military procurement gateway. When the US designated MFTB and MICB in June 2023, international banks began closing their nostro accounts for those banks. The junta shifted transactions to MEB, which Canada alone had sanctioned. MEB transaction volumes surged sharply: from under USD 80 million in Q1 FY2023 to over USD 470 million in Q2 and nearly USD 500 million in Q3, which included over USD 330 million in incoming international payments. The UNSR found MEB processed over USD 60 million in military procurement payments in H2 2023 alone. (UNSR Jun 2024)

MOGE gas revenue processing. MEB is the conduit through which MOGE receives gas revenues from Thailand, processing approximately USD 165 million in Thai Baht revenues on behalf of MOGE in Q3 FY2023, approximately USD 55 million per month. These revenues are the junta's largest single source of external income. The SAC attempted to open new MEB accounts under the name "Kant Kaw" rather than MOGE to conceal the connection and evade the US MOGE Financial Services Directive of 31 October 2023, as evidenced by a leaked energy ministry letter. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Aviation fuel procurement link. Aviation fuel payments in 2023 ran through UAB, not MEB. MEB's connection to this database is indirect: it funds the military procurement that sustains the air campaign through which aviation fuel is ultimately used. (UNSR Jun 2024)

2
International Correspondent Banking Relationships

The UNSR's Figure 5 documents eight institutions holding MEB nostro accounts since the coup: OCBC Bank, UOB, and DBS Bank (Singapore); Novikom Bank and International Financial Club (Russia); Bangkok Bank and Krung Thai Bank (Thailand); and China Construction Bank (China). International Financial Club halted all international transactions after its own US designation in February 2024. As of April 2026, the status of the remaining seven accounts has not been publicly confirmed. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

MEB has been designated by Canada under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA). No start date is recorded in the available sanctions data for the Canadian designation. MEB has not been designated by the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union or Australia as of April 2026. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report specifically recommended that all five jurisdictions sanction MEB, alongside MFTB, MICB and Myanma Agricultural Development Bank, "for as long as the Myanmar military maintains control over them." The report also noted that under current UK and EU law all Myanmar state-owned enterprises should be considered sanctioned given EU and UK sanctions on the SAC itself, but that both jurisdictions have failed to treat MEB as sanctioned in practice. The UNSR described this as a "significant oversight" creating confusion for financial institutions. (UNSR Jun 2024)

4
Key Relationships
•Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) (Profile 8.4): MEB's fellow state-owned banks, both designated by the US, Canada and Australia. Following their designation by the US in June 2023, international correspondent banks began withdrawing nostro account services from MFTB and MICB, causing the junta to redirect military procurement through MEB. MFTB and MICB remain in operation but with severely diminished international access; MEB has assumed their former role as the primary international banking interface for the junta. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)
•United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1): The private Myanmar bank used to process aviation fuel payments in calendar year 2023. UAB and MEB are distinct entities operating in parallel within the junta's financial infrastructure: UAB handled aviation fuel procurement payments through international commercial channels, while MEB handled broader military procurement and gas revenues through state banking channels. Both are identified by the UN Special Rapporteur as facilitating junta procurement. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank (Thailand) (Profile 8.2): One of at least eight financial institutions that held a nostro account for MEB since the coup. KTB is separately documented as the Thai-side receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023, establishing KTB's dual exposure to both the aviation fuel payment chain and MEB's correspondent banking network. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE): MEB serves as the conduit through which MOGE receives gas revenues from Thailand, including approximately USD 55 million per month in Thai Baht payments during Q3 FY2023. These revenues are the junta's largest single source of external income. The SAC's energy ministry attempted to disguise MOGE revenue flows through MEB under the name "Kant Kaw" to evade the US MOGE Financial Services Directive of October 2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)
5
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•June 2023. The US designates MFTB and MICB but not MEB. The junta immediately shifts military procurement to MEB, which only Canada has sanctioned. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•June 2024. The UNSR's Banking on the Death Trade report documents MEB's absorption of junta military procurement, the MOGE revenue processing, and the Kant Kaw evasion attempt. The UNSR explicitly recommends designation by all five jurisdictions and characterises the failure to designate MEB as a significant oversight. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•July 2024. A joint statement by 240 Myanmar and international civil society organisations calls for coordinated sanctions on MEB and MOGE. (Civil society joint statement, Jul 2024)
•4 February 2025. Burma Campaign UK and a civil society coalition write to US Treasury Secretary Bessent and Secretary of State Rubio calling for immediate MEB designation, arguing MEB is now the junta's only viable state banking gateway and that its primarily domestic customer base limits civilian harm from designation. No US action follows. (Civil society letter, Feb 2025)
•May 2025. Justice for Myanmar confirms Krung Thai Bank still holds a MEB nostro account. MEB remains unsanctioned by the US, UK, EU, and Australia as of April 2026. (JFM May 2025)
6
Recommended Actions
•Designate MEB under US, UK, EU, and Australian Myanmar sanctions. The UNSR explicitly recommended this and characterised the failure to do so as a significant oversight. The evidentiary basis is clear: MEB accounted for the entirety of the SAC's military procurement through state-owned financial institutions in FY2023, processed over USD 60 million in military procurement payments in H2 2023, and actively participated in sanctions circumvention. The UK and EU should also assess whether existing SAC designations already cover MEB under their own control tests. The February 2025 civil society letter to US Treasury provides a ready-made submission framework. (UNSR Jun 2024; Civil society letter, Feb 2025)
•Engage all seven remaining nostro account holders to request termination. OCBC, UOB, DBS Bank, Novikom Bank, Bangkok Bank, China Construction Bank, and Krung Thai Bank should each be formally contacted with the UNSR's Figure 5 findings and asked to confirm whether their MEB accounts remain open. For KTB, the BOT/AMLO December 2024 directive provides a specific regulatory basis for requiring confirmation. For Singapore banks, MAS should be engaged simultaneously. For Novikom Bank, US correspondent banking relationships provide a compliance lever. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Investigate whether MOGE has shifted revenue receipt and outgoing payments to MEB. The Kant Kaw evasion attempt demonstrates the SAC was actively seeking to route MOGE revenues through MEB to evade the US MOGE Financial Services Directive. JFM has also flagged the risk that outgoing MOGE payments to joint venture partners (previously through MFTB) may have shifted to MEB. Both dimensions should be investigated by financial intelligence units and investigative journalists with access to Thai and Myanmar banking data. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)
