Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE)
Profile 1.1
Summary Assessment
Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) is the junta-controlled state oil enterprise at the centre of Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. It holds a 51 percent controlling stake in NEPAS, which is the licensed importer and distributor of aviation fuel across eleven Myanmar airports, and co-controls the Myan Oil Terminal at Thilawa, the offloading point for every documented imported aviation fuel shipment since the coup. It also operates the Mann Thanbayarkan refinery in Magway Region, which has produced aviation turbine fuel for the military since the coup, with domestic output 174 percent above the prior year in early 2022. MPE has been a named sanctions target since November 2022. It has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

MPE controls the aviation fuel supply chain inside Myanmar through two mechanisms. It holds the controlling 51 percent stake in NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.), the joint venture with Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. that holds Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence and manages storage and refuelling at eleven airports. MPE also co-controls Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited at Thilawa port, the terminal that has received every documented imported aviation fuel shipment since the coup. Armed soldiers entered the terminal on the morning of the coup and have remained there. Puma Energy, which operated the terminal until December 2022, confirmed after the coup that MPE ran a separate tender for aviation fuel supply to the Myanmar Air Force, in which Puma did not participate. (AI Nov 2022; UNSR Jun 2024)

MPE also operates the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex in Magway Region, where it refines crude oil supplied by Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company into petroleum products including aviation turbine fuel, which MPE's own website lists as a Mann product. Mann sits between Magway Air Force Base and KaPaSa 13, a military gunpowder facility, and is the source of aviation fuel trucked to air bases at Meikhtila, Hmawbi, Nay Pyi Taw, Taungoo, and Monywa. Junta statistics recorded domestic aviation turbine fuel production at 174 percent above the same period a year earlier in early 2022. (JFM Jan 2025; AI Nov 2022)

MPE is the only entity in the supply chain with control over both the import infrastructure and domestic production. Every litre of imported aviation fuel documented since the coup has arrived at terminal infrastructure MPE co-controls. Every litre of domestically refined aviation fuel has passed through a facility MPE operates. Every crony entity in the same network has been designated by at least one jurisdiction. MPE has not. (Prior to May 2020, these functions were handled by MPE's predecessor entity, Myanma Petroleum Product Enterprise (MPPE). Some earlier sources use the old name.) (UNSR Jun 2024)

A further 37.8 million litres were delivered to NEPAS-managed civilian airports during the same period. (AI Nov 2022)

MPE's own admission of aviation fuel production

The junta-controlled Ministry of Electricity and Energy's website lists the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex under MPE and explicitly states that its products include ATF (Aviation Turbine Fuel). The current operating capacity of the complex is stated as 8,500 barrels per day (approximately 357,000 gallons per day), dependent on available local crude oil. This constitutes an unambiguous public admission by the controlling entity itself that MPE produces aviation fuel at the Mann facility. (MPE website)

Puma Energy's confirmation of MPE's post-coup role

According to Amnesty International, Puma Energy confirmed to researchers that following the military coup, MPE ran a tender for the supply and distribution of jet fuel to the Myanmar Air Force, in which Puma Energy chose not to participate. This statement establishes that MPE was the organising authority for military aviation fuel procurement in the immediate post-coup period, and that this role was recognised by Puma Energy, the sector's dominant international actor at the time. (AI Nov 2022)

The domestic refining chain

Justice for Myanmar and Finance Uncovered documented in January 2025 that all crude oil extracted by Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. from the Chauk and Yenangyaung oilfields is transferred by MOGE to MPE for refining at the Mann Thanbayarkan complex. Goldpetrol is 60 percent owned by SGX-listed Interra Resources Ltd. and 40 percent owned by North Petroleum International Co. Ltd., a subsidiary of the Chinese state-owned arms conglomerate NORINCO. An industry expert analysis of satellite-derived nighttime light data at Mann showed a significant and sustained increase in refinery activity after February 2021, establishing that the coup prompted an acceleration of domestic fuel production. The nighttime light analysis also shows that Mann's activity surge continued until approximately mid-2023, after which nighttime emissions ceased; the Chauk refinery showed a significant decrease throughout the same period. This pattern confirms Mann as the primary source of the domestic production surge and points to a possible decline in Mann's output from mid-2023 onwards, making the imported supply chain increasingly critical thereafter. Myanmar's own national statistics showed that domestic aviation turbine fuel production was 174 percent higher in the first quarter of 2022 than in the same quarter a year earlier. (JFM Jan 2025)

Key Relationships
•NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.; formerly National Energy Puma Aviation Services Co. Ltd.): MPE is a joint venture partner in NEPAS, the entity licensed to import, store and sell aviation fuel in Myanmar. NEPAS is the formal purchaser and consignee for the majority of aviation fuel shipments documented in this database. The other joint venture partner is Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (Singapore). See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Feb 2026)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited (formerly Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited / PEAS; also known as Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal): MPE co-controls this terminal at Thilawa port, Yangon. The terminal is owned by P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd., Singapore) and operated in partnership with MPE. It is the offloading point for every imported aviation fuel shipment in this database. See Profile 1.3. (AI Nov 2022; JFM May 2025)
•Asia Sun Group Company Limited (also known as Shoon Group): The Myanmar crony conglomerate that is MPE's private-sector partner in the NEPAS joint venture and in the co-management of the Thilawa terminal. Following Puma Energy's exit in December 2022, Asia Sun Group and MPE became the sole controlling entities of the entire imported aviation fuel supply chain inside Myanmar. Asia Sun Group is sanctioned by the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, Canada and Australia. See Profile 1.4. (AI Nov 2022; UNSR Jun 2024)
•MOGE (Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise): The junta-controlled state enterprise that receives crude oil produced by Goldpetrol under improved petroleum recovery contracts and transfers it to MPE for refining at the Mann Thanbayarkan complex. MOGE is sanctioned by the European Union (February 2022) and is subject to a US financial services directive (October 2023). It is not sanctioned by the United Kingdom, Canada or Australia. The EU's February 2022 designation reportedly caused Bank of China to stop transmitting euro-denominated payments to MOGE for Shwe gas exports, with over USD 500 million in revenues placed into escrow accounts and reported to remain outside the junta's reach. MOGE continues to earn at least USD 55 million monthly in Thai Baht revenues as of end 2023, now received via Myanma Economic Bank (MEB). See Profile 9.2. (JFM Jan 2025; UNSR Jun 2024)
•Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc.: The joint operating company that extracts crude oil from the Chauk and Yenangyaung oilfields in Magway Region and delivers it by boat along the Ayeyarwady River to the Mann Thanbayarkan complex for refining by MPE. Goldpetrol is 60 percent owned by Interra Resources Ltd. and 40 percent owned by North Petroleum International Co. Ltd. See Profile 9.1. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex (MPE-operated refinery, Magway Region): The only operational MPE refinery since the coup, producing aviation turbine fuel and other petroleum products from crude oil supplied by Goldpetrol via MOGE. Located adjacent to Magway Air Force Base and to KaPaSa 13, a military munitions factory. Aviation fuel produced at Mann is distributed to Myanmar Air Force bases at Meikhtila, Hmawbi, Nay Pyi Taw, Taungoo and Monywa. See Reference Entry 1.8. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Jan 2025; MPE website)

The central sanctions gap: The majority of private-sector entities in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain have not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. MPE, the state-owned enterprise that co-controls the import infrastructure and operates the domestic refinery, has not been designated anywhere. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report notes that because both the United Kingdom (21 June 2021) and the European Union (8 November 2022) have sanctioned the State Administration Council (SAC) itself, all Myanmar state-owned enterprises, including MPE, should already be treated as sanctioned entities under the "control" principle in EU and UK sanctions law. Neither government has publicly clarified whether it applies this interpretation to MPE. (UNSR Jun 2024)
Note on Canada / Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.: Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. does not appear as a named entry in Canada's Special Economic Measures (Burma) Regulations (SEMA) sanctions list. Canada is marked as sanctioned here because Canada's SEMA regulations include Asia Sun Group Company Limited, of which Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. is a subsidiary and affiliated company. Under Canadian sanctions law, entities owned or controlled by a designated person or entity are subject to the same prohibitions as the designated entity. Canada's October 2023 aviation fuel ban (SOR/2023-228) also prohibits the export, sale, supply and shipment of aviation fuel, and the provision of shipping insurance, to Myanmar, a prohibition that applies to all actors in the supply chain regardless of individual designation status. Advocates should verify the precise legal basis for any enforcement action under Canadian law.

6
Recommended Actions
•Submit designation evidence to all five jurisdictions. The evidentiary basis is well established: MPE's controlling stake in NEPAS, its co-control of the Myan Oil Terminal, its operation of Mann and its own website's confirmation of aviation turbine fuel production there, the documented deliveries to Myanmar Air Force bases, and the 174 percent production increase since the coup. For the US, the August 2023 OFAC sector determination removes any need to prove direct military end-use. MPE has been a named target in civil society submissions since November 2022. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Press UK and EU authorities on the control principle. The UN Special Rapporteur has argued that because the SAC itself has been sanctioned by both the EU and the UK, all Myanmar state-owned enterprises including MPE should already be treated as sanctioned under their respective control principles. A formal request to the UK Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation and the relevant EU directorate for a public ruling on whether existing SAC designations cover MPE would either produce an immediate practical effect or force a public explanation of why MPE is excluded. This argument requires no new legislation and no new evidence. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Link the MPE sanctions gap to the Interra/SGX process. The SGX inquiry into Interra Resources and its Goldpetrol joint venture is the most active current leverage point in the domestic crude chain. Submissions to SGX RegCo should explicitly connect Interra's crude oil deliveries to MPE's confirmed production of aviation fuel at Mann, making clear that the absence of an MPE designation is the regulatory gap that allows an SGX-listed company to remain a supplier to the military's fuel chain. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Alert commercial counterparties to the NEPAS-MPE link. Any shipping agent, P&I insurer, vessel operator, or fuel trader that interacts with NEPAS as a named counterparty should be put on notice that NEPAS is jointly controlled by MPE, a state enterprise of a junta designated by multiple jurisdictions for human rights violations. The UNSR's June 2024 finding that MPE is likely already covered by UK and EU SAC designations is the strongest argument available for commercial due diligence purposes. (UNSR Jun 2024)
