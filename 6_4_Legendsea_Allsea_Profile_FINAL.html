Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC
Profile 6.4
Summary Assessment
Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC are Vietnamese-domiciled companies identified by Justice for Myanmar in February 2026 as the registered owner and dual commercial and ISM manager of LS MERCURY (formerly YONG SHENG 56, IMO 9657507), which made one confirmed aviation fuel delivery to Myanmar in July 2024. Neither entity appears in any reviewed source before February 2026. Allsea maintains a public website (allseaco.com) and describes itself as a Ho Chi Minh City ship management company established in 2018. Legendsea has no publicly available website or address. Neither has been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026.

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Both entities connect to the Myanmar supply chain exclusively through LS MERCURY, which made one confirmed Jet A-1 delivery to Thilawa on 30 to 31 July 2024, loading at the FOAA anchorage in Fujairah. Neither was named in any reviewed source during the period of the delivery. Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 press release identified them alongside Abu Marine and Pacific Light for BARAAWE 1, and Sea Route Ship Management for REEF and NOBLE, as part of a coordinated sanctions call covering all four ghost fleet vessels. See Profile 6.3 for the vessel's full operational history. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)

2
Vietnamese Dimension of the Supply Chain

Vietnam has served as a transshipment node at multiple points in Myanmar's documented supply chain. Hai Linh Co. Ltd. operated the Cai Mep terminal through which eight confirmed HUITONG 78 deliveries were routed in 2023 and early 2024. The emergence of Vietnamese-owned and managed vessels in the mid-2024 ghost fleet phase suggests Vietnamese corporate infrastructure extended beyond the transshipment role into vessel ownership and management. No direct corporate connection between Legendsea or Allsea and Hai Linh has been established, but the pattern warrants investigation. Jet fuel imports to Myanmar almost doubled between 2024 and 2025, indicating the ghost fleet supply chain expanded significantly after LS MERCURY's single documented delivery. (AI Jan 2024; JFM Feb 2026)

Recommended Actions
•Designate Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC alongside LS MERCURY across all five jurisdictions. The vessel's renaming from YONG SHENG 56 to LS MERCURY after the January 2026 exposure confirms active evasion. Designating owner and manager alongside the vessel prevents further name-change evasion. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination and equivalents provide the legal authority. (JFM Feb 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Investigate whether Legendsea and Allsea's broader fleet is engaged in other illicit trades. Shipping data indicates Legendsea is associated with two vessels and Allsea manages a fleet of nine. The broader fleet profiles of both entities should be examined for sanctioned commodity transport or trades with sanctioned counterparties. Any such finding strengthens the designation basis and may reveal additional network connections. (JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage Vietnamese authorities across regulatory and diplomatic channels. Legendsea, Allsea, and Hai Linh are all Vietnamese-domiciled entities, giving Vietnamese authorities domestic jurisdiction over three critical nodes simultaneously. VINAMARINE (Vietnam's Maritime Administration) should be engaged on Allsea's ISM management of LS MERCURY; any future call by LS MERCURY or other Allsea-managed vessels at Vietnamese ports is an enforcement opportunity. Any regulatory complaint should name Allsea's Designated Person Ashore (Capt. Maeng Kyung Jae) as the individual with formal ISM Code accountability for LS MERCURY. Diplomatic engagement with Vietnam's Foreign Ministry should press for coordinated domestic action, referencing JFM's explicit characterisation of Vietnam's supply chain role as an ASEAN failure. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2024)
