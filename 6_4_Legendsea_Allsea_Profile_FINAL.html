Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC
Profile 6.4
Summary Assessment
Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC are Vietnamese-domiciled entities identified by Justice For Myanmar in its February 2026 sanctions press release as the registered owner and dual commercial and ISM manager, respectively, of LS MERCURY (formerly YONG SHENG 56, IMO 9657507). LS MERCURY made one confirmed delivery of aviation fuel to Myanmar's military junta on 30-31 July 2024. Neither entity appears in any reviewed source prior to February 2026. Allsea Management JSC maintains a public-facing website (allseaco.com) and describes itself as a Ho Chi Minh City-based ship management company established in 2018, with a fleet of managed vessels and services covering technical management, crewing, safety, drydocking and legal matters. Legendsea Co. Ltd. has no publicly available website or address in any reviewed source. Neither entity has been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026, though Justice For Myanmar called for coordinated designations across five jurisdictions in February 2026. Both entities are Vietnamese-domiciled, placing them within the broader pattern of Vietnamese corporate infrastructure that has served as a transshipment and management node in Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain.

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Both entities are connected to the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain exclusively through their roles in relation to LS MERCURY (formerly YONG SHENG 56, IMO 9657507). The vessel made one confirmed delivery of Jet A-1 aviation fuel to Myanmar on 30-31 July 2024, loading at the Fujairah Offshore Anchorage Area (FOAA) in the UAE and offloading at the Myan Oil Terminal at Thilawa, Yangon. This delivery was documented by Amnesty International's January 2026 Ghost Ships investigation and is the sole delivery by this vessel in any reviewed source. See Profile 6.3 for the vessel's full operational history. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)

Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC were not identified in any reviewed source during the period of YONG SHENG 56's documented delivery. They were first named publicly in Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release, drawing on publicly available information. The press release called for sanctions on both entities as part of a coordinated package targeting the owners and managers of four ghost fleet vessels involved in Myanmar aviation fuel deliveries, covering BARAAWE 1 (Profile 6.1), LS MERCURY (Profile 6.3), REEF (Profile 6.5) and NOBLE (Profile 6.6). (JFM Feb 2026)

2
Vietnamese Dimension of the Supply Chain

The identification of Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC as Vietnamese entities is notable in the context of the broader supply chain. Vietnam has served as a transshipment node at multiple points in Myanmar's documented aviation fuel supply chain. Most significantly, Hai Linh Co. Ltd., a Vietnamese company, operated the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam through which eight confirmed HUITONG 78 deliveries in 2023 and early 2024 were routed. Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation noted that the use of Vietnamese storage terminals allowed fuel to be blended to mask its origin and enabled sellers and storage managers to distance themselves from the Myanmar destination. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jan 2026)

The emergence of a Vietnamese-owned vessel (YONG SHENG 56) managed by Vietnamese entities (Legendsea and Allsea) as part of the mid-2024 ghost fleet delivery pattern suggests that Vietnamese corporate infrastructure extended beyond the Cai Mep transshipment role into vessel ownership and management. Myanma Port Authority data cited by Justice For Myanmar shows that jet fuel imports to Myanmar almost doubled between 2024 and 2025, from 64,562 metric tonnes to 106,604 metric tonnes, indicating that the ghost fleet supply chain of which LS MERCURY was a part expanded significantly after the vessel's single documented delivery. No reviewed source has established any direct corporate connection between Legendsea Co. Ltd. or Allsea Management JSC and Hai Linh Co. Ltd., and the coincidence of Vietnamese domicile alone does not imply coordination. However, the pattern warrants investigative attention given the documented centrality of Vietnamese entities to this supply chain. See Profile 4.1 for Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (AI Jan 2024; JFM Feb 2026)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•25 January 2026. Amnesty International publishes Myanmar: Jet Fuel Used in Deadly Air Strikes Flowing in on Ghost Ships with Suspected Links to Iran, independently confirming that YONG SHENG 56 (IMO 9657507, now renamed LS MERCURY) delivered one shipment of aviation fuel to Myanmar on 30-31 July 2024, documenting its presence at FOAA, Fujairah between 15-29 June 2024 before travelling to Myanmar to offload, and identifying the vessel as part of a broader ghost fleet evasion pattern. Amnesty International characterised the ghost fleet as employing tactics including turning off AIS transponders, broadcasting false positions, and repeatedly changing vessel name, flag or ownership. Amnesty International did not identify Allsea Management JSC or Legendsea Co. Ltd. by name. This investigation was the predicate for Justice For Myanmar's subsequent identification of both entities in February 2026. (AI Jan 2026)

(Vietnam) as the registered owner and Allsea Management JSC (Vietnam) as the commercial manager and ISM manager of LS MERCURY (formerly YONG SHENG 56). 5
Recommended Actions
•Designate Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC alongside LS MERCURY across all five jurisdictions: Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 call for coordinated sanctions on both entities and on the vessel itself remains unimplemented. Designation of the owner and managers alongside the vessel is necessary to prevent evasion through renaming and re-flagging. The vessel has already been renamed from YONG SHENG 56 to LS MERCURY following the January 2026 Amnesty International exposure, demonstrating that this risk is being acted upon. Civil society should maintain pressure on all five jurisdictions to act on the JFM call. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026)
•Investigate whether Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC have other vessels connected to illicit trades: MagicPort shipping data indicates that Legendsea Co. Ltd. is associated with two vessels and Allsea Management JSC manages a fleet of nine vessels. The broader fleet profiles of both entities should be examined to determine whether any other managed vessels are engaged in sanctioned commodity transport or trades with sanctioned counterparties, which could strengthen the basis for designation and reveal additional network connections. (JFM Feb 2026)
•Pursue coordinated engagement across Vietnamese regulatory, maritime and diplomatic channels: Legendsea Co. Ltd., Allsea Management JSC and Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1) are all Vietnamese-domiciled entities that have served as owner, manager and transshipment node respectively in Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain, giving Vietnamese authorities domestic jurisdiction over three critical nodes simultaneously. Three complementary tracks are available. First, civil society and governments should engage VINAMARINE (Vietnam's Maritime Administration, under the Ministry of Transport) to investigate Allsea Management JSC's role as commercial and ISM manager of LS MERCURY. As a Vietnamese-incorporated company, Allsea is subject to Vietnamese corporate and maritime regulation. VINAMARINE also exercises port state control over vessels calling at Vietnamese ports, and any future call by LS MERCURY or other Allsea-managed vessels at Vietnamese ports is an enforcement opportunity. Any regulatory complaint should name Capt. Maeng Kyung Jae directly, as Allsea's Designated Person Ashore and the individual with formal ISM Code accountability for LS MERCURY's operations under Allsea's management. Second, civil society and governments should formally notify the Panama Maritime Authority (AMP) of LS MERCURY's documented history as a ghost fleet vessel that delivered aviation fuel to Myanmar prior to reflagging from China to Panama. While AMP's flag revocation programme has to date targeted sanctioned vessels specifically, pre-designation notification establishes a record that positions AMP to act swiftly if and when a sanctions designation is made. Pairing a request for designation with simultaneous notification to AMP strengthens the enforcement chain. Third, diplomatic engagement with Vietnam's Foreign Ministry should press for coordinated domestic action. Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release explicitly identified Vietnam's involvement in this supply chain as an ASEAN failure, with JFM's spokesperson stating that "the involvement of Vietnam also underscores the failure of ASEAN, which continues to impede an effective international response to the Myanmar crisis while some of its members profit." Formal diplomatic engagement targeting Vietnam's Foreign Ministry alongside VINAMARINE regulatory pressure maximises the likelihood of coordinated Vietnamese action. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2024)
