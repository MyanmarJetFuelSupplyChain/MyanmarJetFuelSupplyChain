United Amara Bank (UAB)
Profile 8.1
Summary Assessment
United Amara Bank (UAB) is a private Myanmar bank that processed all documented Myanmar-side aviation fuel payments in calendar year 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 Banking on the Death Trade report documents UAB routing approximately USD 80 million from five Myanmar purchasers to two Thai fuel sellers, CB Energy and Tawan-Oil Trading, via Krung Thai Bank and Kasikorn Bank respectively. The five purchasers were NEPAS, its proxy Intraco, and three Asia Sun Group proxies: Swan Energy, Myan-Oil, and Rich Ray Trading. The UNSR recommended that financial institutions freeze existing relationships with UAB. In July 2025, Justice for Myanmar and Japanese civil society engaged JCB Co. Ltd. on its card partnership with UAB; JCB did not respond. UAB has not been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

UAB was the single domestic financial intermediary for all USD 80 million in documented aviation fuel payments in 2023. Every payment ran from a Myanmar purchaser through UAB to a Thai seller. The UNSR's Figure 7 documents the full payment architecture. The UNSR found that effective due diligence by the Thai receiving banks could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. The same standard applies to UAB in its role as the outgoing payment bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)

UAB has not been designated by any government as of April 2026. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report explicitly recommended that financial institutions "freeze existing relationships with UAB and A-Bank" in connection with UAB's role as the Myanmar-side payment bank for aviation fuel purchases. 3
Key Relationships
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS) (Profile 1.2): The primary Myanmar aviation fuel importer and licenced purchaser. NEPAS used UAB both directly and through its proxy Intraco to route payments to Thai fuel sellers in 2023. NEPAS's direct payments through UAB to CB Energy exceeded USD 40 million, and Intraco's payments through UAB to Tawan-Oil Trading exceeded USD 25 million. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Swan Energy Company Limited (Profile 1.5): An Asia Sun Group proxy purchaser that routed more than USD 15 million in aviation fuel payments through UAB to CB Energy in 2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Myan-Oil Company Limited and Rich Ray Trading Company Limited (Profile 1.6): Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers documented in the UN Special Rapporteur's Figure 7. Together with Swan Energy, they are grouped in a combined total of more than USD 15 million; no per-buyer or per-seller breakdown is given in the source. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil paid CB Energy; Rich Ray Trading paid Tawan-Oil Trading. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank (Profile 8.2): The Thailand-based receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payment receipts from NEPAS and Swan Energy, routed through UAB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Kasikorn Bank (Profile 8.5): The Thailand-based receiving bank for Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel payment receipts from Intraco and Rich Ray Trading, routed through UAB. The UN Special Rapporteur noted that effective due diligence by Kasikorn Bank, including a review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. (UNSR Jun 2024)
4
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report names UAB, documents its USD 80 million role, and explicitly calls on financial institutions to freeze existing relationships with UAB and A-Bank. No government acts on the recommendation. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•17 July 2025. Justice for Myanmar, Mekong Watch, and five Japanese civil society organisations write to JCB Co. Ltd. requesting confirmation of whether it has conducted human rights due diligence on its card partnership with UAB and what steps it has taken to assess the risk of contributing to international crimes. As of November 2025, JCB had not responded. (JFM Nov 2025)
5
Recommended Actions
•Designate UAB under Myanmar sanctions in all five jurisdictions. The UNSR's Figure 7 transaction table provides the evidentiary basis. Designation would trigger asset freezes and prohibit transactions with UAB across all five major financial jurisdictions, directly disrupting the documented aviation fuel payment channel. UAB's SWIFT BIC (UABMMMMY) provides the reference identifier for financial institution screening. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Press JCB Co. Ltd. to respond to the July 2025 civil society letter and implement the UNSR's freeze recommendation. JCB has maintained its card partnership with UAB despite direct civil society engagement and the UNSR's explicit freeze call. A formal follow-up citing the UNSR recommendation and requesting a public response should be sent. Visa, Mastercard, and other international card schemes maintaining acquiring or issuing relationships with UAB through the Myanmar banking system should be engaged with the same request. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Nov 2025)
•Engage correspondent banks maintaining relationships with UAB. Any international bank that processed USD-denominated transactions through UAB in connection with aviation fuel purchases should assess its OFAC exposure under the August 2023 sector determination. Civil society should identify and engage the correspondent banking relationships through which UAB's international payments cleared. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Investigate whether UAB continued to process aviation fuel payments in 2024 and 2025. The UNSR documentation covers 2023 only. Whether the UAB payment channel remained active after the June 2024 publication, or shifted to another Myanmar bank, has not been established. This is a priority research question. (UNSR Jun 2024)
