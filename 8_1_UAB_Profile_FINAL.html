United Amara Bank (UAB)
Profile 8.1
Summary Assessment
United Amara Bank (UAB) is a private Myanmar bank identified by the United Nations Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in Myanmar in June 2024 as the domestic financial intermediary through which Myanmar's aviation fuel purchasers made payments to Thai fuel sellers in calendar year 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur documented that UAB facilitated approximately USD 80 million in aviation fuel purchases in 2023, representing a near-30 per cent increase over 2022, through a series of transactions in which National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS) and Asia Sun Group proxy companies used UAB to route payments to two Thailand-registered fuel companies: CB Energy Co. Ltd. and Tawan-Oil Trading Co. Ltd. UAB processed payments across three documented amount lines from five Myanmar-side purchasers: NEPAS directly (more than USD 40 million to CB Energy), Intraco as a NEPAS proxy (more than USD 25 million to Tawan-Oil Trading), and three Asia Sun Group proxies (Swan Energy, Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading) whose combined payments of more than USD 15 million are not broken down per buyer in the source, with Swan Energy and Myan-Oil paying CB Energy and Rich Ray Trading paying Tawan-Oil Trading. The receiving banks on the Thailand side were Krung Thai Bank (for CB Energy payments) and Kasikorn Bank (for Tawan-Oil Trading payments). The UN Special Rapporteur recommended that financial institutions freeze existing relationships with UAB. UAB has not been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026.

1
Role in the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

UAB is identified by the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 Banking on the Death Trade report as the private Myanmar bank through which aviation fuel payment transactions were routed in calendar year 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur reviewed trade records relating to all USD 80 million in aviation fuel purchases in 2023 that passed through the international financial system. All transactions involved Myanmar-registered companies controlled by NEPAS (Profile 1.2) and the Asia Sun Group purchasing from two Thailand-registered companies: CB Energy Co. Ltd. and Tawan-Oil Trading Co. Ltd. UAB was used on the Myanmar side to make these outgoing international payments. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Key Relationships
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS) (Profile 1.2): The primary Myanmar aviation fuel importer and licenced purchaser. NEPAS used UAB both directly and through its proxy Intraco to route payments to Thai fuel sellers in 2023. NEPAS's direct payments through UAB to CB Energy exceeded USD 40 million, and Intraco's payments through UAB to Tawan-Oil Trading exceeded USD 25 million. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Swan Energy Company Limited (Profile 1.5): An Asia Sun Group proxy purchaser that routed more than USD 15 million in aviation fuel payments through UAB to CB Energy in 2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Myan-Oil Company Limited and Rich Ray Trading Company Limited (Profile 1.6): Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers documented in the UN Special Rapporteur's Figure 7. Together with Swan Energy, they are grouped in a combined total of more than USD 15 million; no per-buyer or per-seller breakdown is given in the source. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil paid CB Energy; Rich Ray Trading paid Tawan-Oil Trading. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank (Profile 8.2): The Thailand-based receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payment receipts from NEPAS and Swan Energy, routed through UAB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Kasikorn Bank (Profile 8.5): The Thailand-based receiving bank for Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel payment receipts from Intraco and Rich Ray Trading, routed through UAB. The UN Special Rapporteur noted that effective due diligence by Kasikorn Bank, including a review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. (UNSR Jun 2024)
4
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo investigation recommended that financial institutions "thoroughly assess and if necessary, block or suspend payments that are directed for transactions related to the supply of aviation fuel import to Myanmar." This recommendation, directed at financial institutions generally, was not UAB-specific but established the framework under which UAB's later-documented role in aviation fuel financing is assessed. (AI Nov 2022)
•June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report names UAB as the Myanmar-side bank through which approximately USD 80 million in aviation fuel payments were processed in calendar year 2023. The report documents the payment flows through UAB involving five Myanmar-side purchasers across three amount lines, totalling approximately USD 80 million, and explicitly calls on financial institutions to "freeze existing relationships with UAB and A-Bank." This is the first and only named identification of UAB as an aviation fuel supply chain financial facilitator in any reviewed source. No government has acted on the recommendation as of April 2026. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•July 2025. Justice For Myanmar, Mekong Watch and five Japanese civil society organisations sent a joint letter to JCB Co. Ltd. on 17 July 2025 raising human rights concerns over JCB's continued business relationship with UAB. JCB maintains a card partnership with UAB, which acts as the local acquirer for JCB cards in Myanmar. The letter asked JCB to confirm whether it had conducted human rights due diligence on its Myanmar partnership, what steps it had taken to assess and mitigate the risk of contributing to international crimes through its ongoing relationship with UAB, and whether it intended to end its partnership in light of the UN Special Rapporteur's explicit call for financial institutions to freeze relationships with UAB. As of November 2025, JCB had not responded to any of these questions. This is the only documented instance of a named payment card scheme being directly engaged by civil society over its relationship with UAB. (JFM, November 2025)
5
Recommended Actions
•Implement the UN Special Rapporteur's recommendation to freeze existing relationships with UAB: The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report explicitly called on financial institutions to freeze existing relationships with UAB. Any bank, payment network, card scheme or correspondent banking partner currently maintaining a relationship with UAB should conduct enhanced due diligence in light of UAB's documented role as the domestic payment intermediary for approximately USD 80 million in Myanmar military aviation fuel purchases in 2023. The recommendation applies to international correspondent banks, card scheme operators and payment networks with UAB as a member or partner. JCB Co. Ltd. is a documented example of a card scheme that has maintained its partnership with UAB despite civil society engagement in July 2025 and has not publicly responded to human rights concerns raised about that relationship. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM, November 2025)
•Designate UAB under Myanmar-related sanctions authorities: The US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia each maintain Myanmar-specific sanctions authorities under which UAB could be designated for its role as the financial intermediary in documented aviation fuel procurement for Myanmar's military. The UN Special Rapporteur's documentation of USD 80 million in aviation fuel payments processed by UAB in 2023 provides a documented evidentiary basis for designation submissions to all five jurisdictions. Designation would trigger asset freezes and prohibit transactions with UAB across five major financial jurisdictions, materially disrupting the financing channel for Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Engage correspondent banks and payment network partners of UAB to request enhanced due diligence or severance of the relationship: UAB's SWIFT BIC (UABMMMMY) provides a reference point for screening against correspondent banking relationships. Financial institutions that maintain UAB as a correspondent or hold UAB as a member of their payment network should be engaged by civil society, institutional investors and regulatory authorities with the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 findings. Any institution that processed USD-denominated transactions through UAB in connection with Myanmar aviation fuel purchases in 2023 should assess its potential OFAC exposure under the August 2023 determination on Myanmar's petroleum sector. JCB Co. Ltd. is a named card scheme that civil society engaged in July 2025 over its local card acquirer relationship with UAB; as of November 2025 JCB had not responded. Visa, Mastercard and other international payment card schemes that may maintain acquiring or issuing relationships with UAB through the Myanmar banking system should be engaged with the same human rights concerns. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM, November 2025)
•Investigate UAB's aviation fuel payment activity beyond calendar year 2023: The UN Special Rapporteur's documentation covers calendar year 2023. Whether UAB continued to process aviation fuel payments in 2024 and 2025 is not established in any reviewed source. Civil society, investigative journalists and financial intelligence units should investigate whether the payment channel through UAB remained active following the June 2024 publication of the Special Rapporteur's findings and whether the procurement structure shifted to other Myanmar banks following that disclosure. (UNSR Jun 2024)
