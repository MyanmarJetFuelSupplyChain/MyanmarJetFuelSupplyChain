Singapore Petroleum Company Limited and Singapore Refining Company Private Limited
Profile 5.8
Summary Assessment
Singapore Petroleum Company Limited (SPC) is a Singapore-incorporated petroleum refining, trading and distribution company that has been a wholly owned subsidiary of PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. since 2009, making it an indirect subsidiary of PetroChina Company Limited and ultimately of China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC), a Chinese state-owned enterprise. SPC holds a 50% interest in Singapore Refining Company Private Limited (SRC), a 290,000-barrel-per-day refinery on Jurong Island, Singapore, jointly owned with Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. SRC's terminal at Jurong Island served as the load port for four of the eight aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar documented in the Amnesty International and Justice For Myanmar November 2022 Deadly Cargo investigation: the April 2021 YU DONG shipment (row 1, 13,319 MT), the June 2021 SANTYA shipment (row 2, 17,328 MT), the December 2021 SYNERGY shipment (row 4, 20,270 MT), and the February 2022 SUPER HERO shipment (row 5, 13,310 MT). SPC is the confirmed seller for the December 2021 SYNERGY shipment (row 4): Amnesty International obtained a proforma invoice dated 18 November 2021 between SPC and Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. for the sale of Jet A-1 aviation fuel valued at approximately USD 18 million, as well as a letter from Myanmar's Ministry of Defence to the Myanma Port Authority dated 16 December 2021 stating that the fuel sold by SPC to Asia Sun Trading was "required for use in Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s Aircraft and Helicopters." The sellers for the other three SRC-load-port shipments (rows 1, 2 and 5) were not confirmed as SPC; Reuters separately identified the April 2021 YU DONG seller (row 1) as PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd, the Singapore-based trading unit of PetroChina Company Limited, based on Myanmar government import data; and the February 2022 SUPER HERO shipment was separately confirmed as having been supplied by Chevron. SPC did not respond to Amnesty International's letters. Neither SPC, PetroChina, nor SRC has been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain
SRC as load port: four shipments

Singapore Refining Company Private Limited (SRC) on Jurong Island served as the load port for four of the eight aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar documented in the Amnesty International and Justice For Myanmar November 2022 Deadly Cargo investigation, all of which arrived at the PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, during the NEPAS-era supply chain. The four shipments are: YU DONG (row 1, arrived 15 April 2021, 13,319 MT), SANTYA (row 2, arrived 27 June 2021, 17,328 MT), SYNERGY (row 4, arrived 21 December 2021, 20,270 MT), and SUPER HERO (row 5, arrived 22 February 2022, 13,310 MT). The combined volume of these four shipments is 64,227 MT of Jet A-1 aviation fuel. (AI Nov 2022)

SPC as confirmed seller: the December 2021 SYNERGY shipment

Singapore Petroleum Company Limited (SPC) is the confirmed seller of aviation fuel in the December 2021 SYNERGY shipment (20,270 MT). Amnesty International obtained two documents linking SPC directly to this transaction. First, a proforma invoice dated 18 November 2021, showing a USD 18 million sale of Jet A-1 aviation fuel from SPC to Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd., a Myanmar-based entity in the Asia Sun Group. Second, a letter dated 16 December 2021 from Myanmar's Ministry of Defence to the Myanma Port Authority, stating that the fuel sold by Singapore Petroleum Company to Asia Sun Trading in December 2021 was "required for use in Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s Aircraft and Helicopters." These two documents together establish that SPC sold aviation fuel to the military's procurement network with documentary evidence that the fuel was intended for military use. (AI Nov 2022)

SPC did not respond to Amnesty International's contact letters dated 25 August 2022 and 12 October 2022. Amnesty International stated that SPC's lack of response "raises significant questions, including whether the company knew that the fuel it sold was to be delivered to the Myanmar military." SRC also did not respond to Amnesty International's contact letter dated 24 August 2022. (AI Nov 2022)

Supplier attribution for rows 1, 2 and 5

For the three other shipments that loaded at SRC (rows 1, 2 and 5), SPC is not confirmed as the seller. For the April 2021 YU DONG shipment (row 1), Reuters reported on 20 May 2021 that the seller was PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd, the Singapore-based trading unit of PetroChina Company Limited, based on Myanmar government import data reviewed by Reuters. Reuters documented the customer as NEPAS and the volume as 13,300 MT; Amnesty International's independent vessel tracking and Myanma Port Authority records document the discharged volume as 13,319 MT. PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd is a distinct entity from SPC. For the June 2021 SANTYA shipment (row 2), the supplier is not identified in any reviewed source. The February 2022 SUPER HERO shipment (row 5, 13,310 MT) was separately confirmed by Amnesty International as having been supplied by Chevron, the other 50% shareholder of SRC; Chevron stated it believed it had provided aviation fuel for civilian purposes only. SRC produces and sells products under the brand labels of each of its shareholders (SPC and Chevron), and both shareholders traded aviation fuel that loaded at the SRC terminal during the documented period. (AI Nov 2022; Reuters May 2021)

Key Relationships
•Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. (Myanmar): The direct purchaser in the December 2021 SPC transaction (row 4). Asia Sun Trading was the formal consignee named on the proforma invoice between SPC and the Myanmar side. Asia Sun Trading is part of the Asia Sun Group, which served as the procurement and distribution network for NEPAS. The Ministry of Defence letter confirms Asia Sun Trading was the purchaser of the fuel that was designated for Tatmadaw Air Force use. Asia Sun Trading is sanctioned by the UK (January 2023), US (March 2023) and Australia. See Profile 1.4. (AI Nov 2022)
•PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd: The Singapore-based trading unit of PetroChina Company Limited identified by Reuters in May 2021 as the seller of 13,300 MT of Jet A-1 to NEPAS via the YU DONG in April 2021, based on Myanmar government import data reviewed by Reuters. This entity is distinct from both SPC and from PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (the holding company that owns SPC). See the note in the ID section on three distinct PetroChina Singapore entities. (Reuters May 2021)
•PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. / PetroChina Company Limited / CNPC: SPC's immediate parent is PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. The chain runs upward to PetroChina International Company Ltd., then to PetroChina Company Limited (HKEX: 00857; SSE: 601857), and ultimately to China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC), a Chinese state-owned enterprise. SPC's role as a confirmed seller to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply chain is a function of the PetroChina group's downstream trading and distribution operations through SPC. Neither PetroChina nor CNPC has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. PetroChina's current Chairman is Dai Houliang and its President is Ren Lixin (appointed September 2025). (AI Nov 2022)
•Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd.: The other 50% shareholder of SRC, the load port terminal. Chevron was separately confirmed by Amnesty International as the supplier for the February 2022 SUPER HERO shipment (row 5, 13,310 MT). Chevron is profiled separately. See Profile 5.11. SRC products are sold under the brand labels of its shareholders, so both SPC and Chevron were trading aviation fuel that loaded at the SRC terminal during the documented period. (AI Nov 2022)
•NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.): The NEPAS joint venture held Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence. NEPAS's role as the licensed importer and distributor of aviation fuel from the Thilawa terminal, and the deliveries of aviation fuel from NEPAS storage facilities to Myanmar Air Force bases, are documented by Amnesty International. The Ministry of Defence letter obtained by AI confirms the fuel sold by SPC to Asia Sun Trading was intended for Tatmadaw Air Force use, which was the ultimate destination within the NEPAS distribution network. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022)
•Former PEAS terminal, Thilawa, Yangon (now Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited): The destination terminal for all four SRC-loaded shipments. During the period of rows 1 through 5 (April 2021 to February 2022), the terminal operated as Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS) under Puma Energy's management. All four shipments were offloaded at the PEAS terminal berth at Thilawa. Following Puma Energy's exit in December 2022, the terminal passed to the Shoon Group and is now operated by Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited. See Profile 1.3. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy Holdings Pte. Ltd.: During the period of the four SRC-loaded shipments, the PEAS terminal and the NEPAS joint venture were under Puma Energy's effective management and control. SPC's sale in December 2021 was to Asia Sun Trading as the formal consignee, with NEPAS as the licensed importer. Puma Energy exited Myanmar in December 2022. See Profile 5.1. (AI Nov 2022)

5
Recommended Actions
•Seek formal disclosure from SPC and PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd on their respective Myanmar transactions: The proforma invoice and the Ministry of Defence letter obtained by Amnesty International constitute unusually direct documentary evidence linking SPC to a confirmed sale for Tatmadaw Air Force use. Civil society should engage SPC directly, requesting: confirmation of the circumstances in which the proforma invoice with Asia Sun Trading was issued; what end-use declarations SPC obtained before executing the sale; what SPC's response is to the Ministry of Defence letter establishing military end use; and what policies SPC has adopted since November 2022 to prevent recurrence of sales to purchasers connected to Myanmar's military. Civil society should separately engage PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd regarding the April 2021 YU DONG sale of 13,300 MT of jet fuel to NEPAS documented by Reuters, requesting what due diligence PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd applied before executing that sale, what end-use assurances it received from NEPAS, and whether any further sales to Myanmar were made after April 2021. SPC's parent company, PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd., and its ultimate parent PetroChina Company Limited (listed on HKEX and SSE), should be copied in any formal correspondence to either entity. (AI Nov 2022; Reuters May 2021)
•Assess designation submissions to OFAC, OTSI and equivalent bodies: The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the legal basis for designation of any entity that operates in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy; this determination remains in force following Executive Order 14014's renewal on 3 February 2026. While SPC's confirmed sale predates the sector determination, the documentary evidence of military end use is among the strongest in the reviewed record for any supplier entity. Civil society should assess whether a designation submission to OFAC, the UK Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation (OTSI), and equivalent bodies in the EU, Canada and Australia is supported by the available evidence. The Ministry of Defence letter in particular is a primary source document establishing that the Myanmar military itself identified SPC's fuel as destined for Air Force use. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage institutional investors in PetroChina through HKEX and SSE disclosure mechanisms: PetroChina Company Limited (HKEX: 00857; SSE: 601857) is the ultimate listed parent of SPC. PetroChina's current Chairman is Dai Houliang and its President is Ren Lixin (appointed September 2025). Institutional investor engagement should reference the Deadly Cargo report's documentary evidence and highlight the OFAC jet fuel sector determination as creating prospective designation risk for any future PetroChina-group supply to Myanmar. Any engagement should address all three relevant Singapore entities: SPC, PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd, and PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage prospective buyers of Chevron's SRC stake on the terminal's Myanmar supply history: Since mid-2025, Chevron has been conducting a sale of its 50% stake in SRC, with Morgan Stanley appointed to manage the process. As of January 2026, Chevron was in final-round negotiations with Japan's Eneos and Glencore, with the full Singapore asset package (including the Penjuru terminal and Caltex retail networks in Singapore, Cambodia and Malaysia) valued at over USD 1 billion. The sale had not been confirmed as completed as of April 2026. Advocacy organisations should ensure that the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply evidence, including SRC's role as the load port for four shipments and SPC's confirmed December 2021 sale, reaches prospective buyers before completion. Any incoming buyer of Chevron's stake will become co-owner of the SRC terminal alongside SPC and should conduct specific Myanmar-related due diligence as part of the acquisition process. (Reuters May 2021; AI Nov 2022)
•Monitor SRC for any renewed involvement in Myanmar-bound aviation fuel supply chains: SRC's Jurong Island terminal loaded four shipments in the period April 2021 to February 2022 and served as the primary Singapore load port for the early post-coup NEPAS supply chain. While the documented supply chain shifted away from direct Singapore departures after mid-2022, the SRC terminal remains an active and high-capacity petroleum products facility. If any future Myanmar-bound shipment is traced to the SRC terminal at Jurong Island, or if SPC or PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd is identified as a trader in any subsequent supply chain, that finding should trigger immediate re-engagement and designation submissions. (AI Nov 2022)
