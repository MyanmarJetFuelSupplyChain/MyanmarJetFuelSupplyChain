Singapore Petroleum Company Limited and Singapore Refining Company Private Limited
Profile 5.8
Summary Assessment
Singapore Petroleum Company Limited (SPC) is a Singapore-incorporated petroleum company wholly owned by PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd., itself a subsidiary of Chinese state-owned PetroChina and ultimately CNPC. SPC holds a 50 percent stake in Singapore Refining Company Private Limited (SRC) on Jurong Island, which served as the load port for four of the eight aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar documented in Deadly Cargo. SPC is the confirmed seller for the December 2021 SYNERGY shipment of 20,270 MT: Amnesty International obtained both a proforma invoice between SPC and Asia Sun Trading for a USD 18 million Jet A-1 sale and a Myanmar Ministry of Defence letter confirming the fuel was "required for use in Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s Aircraft and Helicopters." This is the strongest direct documentary evidence linking any named supplier to military end-use in the reviewed record. PetroChina International Singapore Pte. Ltd. was separately identified by Reuters as the seller in the first post-coup shipment in April 2021. SPC did not respond to Amnesty International. Neither SPC, SRC, nor PetroChina has been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

SRC as load port: four shipments

The SRC terminal on Jurong Island loaded four of the eight Deadly Cargo shipments: YU DONG (row 1, 13,319 MT, April 2021), SANTYA (row 2, 17,328 MT, June 2021), SYNERGY (row 4, 20,270 MT, December 2021), and SUPER HERO (row 5, 13,310 MT, February 2022), a combined 64,227 MT. SPC and Chevron each hold 50 percent of SRC. (AI Nov 2022)

SPC as confirmed seller: the December 2021 SYNERGY shipment

Amnesty International obtained a proforma invoice dated 18 November 2021 showing a USD 18 million Jet A-1 sale from SPC to Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd., and a Myanmar Ministry of Defence letter dated 16 December 2021 confirming the fuel was "required for use in Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s Aircraft and Helicopters." These two documents are the most direct primary evidence of confirmed military end-use in the reviewed record. SPC did not respond to Amnesty International's letters of 25 August and 12 October 2022. (AI Nov 2022)

Seller attribution for the other three SRC shipments

For the April 2021 YU DONG shipment, Reuters identified PetroChina International Singapore Pte. Ltd. as the seller based on Myanmar government import data. For the February 2022 SUPER HERO shipment, the seller was Chevron. The seller for the June 2021 SANTYA shipment has not been identified in any reviewed source. (AI Nov 2022; Reuters May 2021)

2
Key Relationships
•Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. (Myanmar): The direct purchaser in the December 2021 SPC transaction (row 4). Asia Sun Trading was the formal consignee named on the proforma invoice between SPC and the Myanmar side. Asia Sun Trading is part of the Asia Sun Group, which served as the procurement and distribution network for NEPAS. The Ministry of Defence letter confirms Asia Sun Trading was the purchaser of the fuel that was designated for Tatmadaw Air Force use. Asia Sun Trading is sanctioned by the UK (January 2023), US (March 2023) and Australia. See Profile 1.4. (AI Nov 2022)
•PetroChina International Singapore Pte Ltd: The Singapore-based trading unit of PetroChina Company Limited identified by Reuters in May 2021 as the seller of 13,300 MT of Jet A-1 to NEPAS via the YU DONG in April 2021, based on Myanmar government import data reviewed by Reuters. This entity is distinct from both SPC and from PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (the holding company that owns SPC). See the note in the ID section on three distinct PetroChina Singapore entities. (Reuters May 2021)
•PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. / PetroChina Company Limited / CNPC: SPC's immediate parent is PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. The chain runs upward to PetroChina International Company Ltd., then to PetroChina Company Limited (HKEX: 00857; SSE: 601857), and ultimately to China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC), a Chinese state-owned enterprise. SPC's role as a confirmed seller to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply chain is a function of the PetroChina group's downstream trading and distribution operations through SPC. Neither PetroChina nor CNPC has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. PetroChina's current Chairman is Dai Houliang and its President is Ren Lixin (appointed September 2025). (AI Nov 2022)
•Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd.: The other 50% shareholder of SRC, the load port terminal. Chevron was separately confirmed by Amnesty International as the supplier for the February 2022 SUPER HERO shipment (row 5, 13,310 MT). Chevron is profiled separately. See Profile 5.11. SRC products are sold under the brand labels of its shareholders, so both SPC and Chevron were trading aviation fuel that loaded at the SRC terminal during the documented period. (AI Nov 2022)
•NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.): The NEPAS joint venture held Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence. NEPAS's role as the licensed importer and distributor of aviation fuel from the Thilawa terminal, and the deliveries of aviation fuel from NEPAS storage facilities to Myanmar Air Force bases, are documented by Amnesty International. The Ministry of Defence letter obtained by AI confirms the fuel sold by SPC to Asia Sun Trading was intended for Tatmadaw Air Force use, which was the ultimate destination within the NEPAS distribution network. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022)
•Former PEAS terminal, Thilawa, Yangon (now Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited): The destination terminal for all four SRC-loaded shipments. During the period of rows 1 through 5 (April 2021 to February 2022), the terminal operated as Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS) under Puma Energy's management. All four shipments were offloaded at the PEAS terminal berth at Thilawa. Following Puma Energy's exit in December 2022, the terminal passed to the Shoon Group and is now operated by Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited. See Profile 1.3. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy Holdings Pte. Ltd.: During the period of the four SRC-loaded shipments, the PEAS terminal and the NEPAS joint venture were under Puma Energy's effective management and control. SPC's sale in December 2021 was to Asia Sun Trading as the formal consignee, with NEPAS as the licensed importer. Puma Energy exited Myanmar in December 2022. See Profile 5.1. (AI Nov 2022)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•20 May 2021. Reuters identifies PetroChina International Singapore as the seller in the April 2021 YU DONG shipment. PetroChina does not respond. Justice for Myanmar condemns the shipment. (Reuters May 2021)
•3 November 2022. Deadly Cargo identifies SPC as the confirmed SYNERGY seller and SRC as the load port for four shipments, publishing the proforma invoice and Ministry of Defence letter. SPC and SRC had not responded to pre-publication contact. No designation follows. (AI Nov 2022)
5
Recommended Actions
•Submit designation referrals for SPC and PetroChina International Singapore to OFAC and other jurisdictions. The proforma invoice and Ministry of Defence letter are among the strongest pieces of direct documentary evidence in the reviewed record. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination provides the legal authority. Equivalent frameworks in the UK, EU, Canada, and Australia should be assessed in parallel. Any OFAC submission should also address PetroChina International Singapore's April 2021 sale given the Reuters documentation. (AI Nov 2022; Reuters May 2021; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage SPC, PetroChina International Singapore, and PetroChina Company Limited for disclosure. SPC should be asked to respond to the proforma invoice and Ministry of Defence letter specifically. PetroChina International Singapore should be asked what due diligence it applied before the April 2021 NEPAS sale and whether further sales to Myanmar followed. Parent entities PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. and PetroChina Company Limited (HKEX 00857; SSE 601857) should be copied. Institutional investor engagement with PetroChina should reference the OFAC sector determination as creating prospective designation risk for any future group supply to Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022; Reuters May 2021; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage prospective buyers of Chevron's SRC stake on the terminal's Myanmar supply history. Since mid-2025, Chevron has been selling its 50 percent stake in SRC. As of April 2026 the sale had not been confirmed as completed. Any incoming buyer becomes co-owner of the terminal that loaded four documented Myanmar aviation fuel shipments and should conduct specific Myanmar-related due diligence as part of the acquisition. Advocacy organisations should ensure the Deadly Cargo evidence reaches prospective buyers before completion. (AI Nov 2022)
