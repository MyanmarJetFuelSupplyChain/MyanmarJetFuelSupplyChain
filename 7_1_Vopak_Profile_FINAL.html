Royal Vopak: Pengerang Independent Terminals and Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal
Profile 7.1
Summary Assessment
Royal Vopak NV is the world's largest independent tank storage operator, headquartered in the Netherlands. Two of its terminals appear in four documented Myanmar aviation fuel shipments across three investigations: Pengerang Independent Terminals (PITSB) in Malaysia served as direct load port for the August 2022 Super Hero delivery and as a staging stop for the April and May 2023 deliveries; the Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal served as a staging stop for the January 2024 shipment. Vopak responded to all three investigations, consistently describing itself as a storage-only provider and declining to name its customers. It denied that any vessel had berthed at Pengerang for the April and May 2023 shipments, a claim Amnesty International stated conflicted with its own evidence. Amnesty International named Royal Vopak as a company whose value chain due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles are engaged across all four shipments. Vopak has not been sanctioned by any government.

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Vopak's terminals served two distinct roles. PITSB was the direct load port for the August 2022 Super Hero shipment, from which fuel was delivered directly to Thilawa; Vopak confirmed departure but declined to name the fuel supplier, leaving that shipment's origin unconfirmed. For the April 2023 (YIDA 8) and May 2023 (HUITONG 78) shipments, PITSB was a staging stop before transfer to the Cai Mep terminal in Vietnam; Vopak denied the vessel calls, contradicting Amnesty International's evidence. The Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal staged the January 2024 HUITONG 78 shipment; Amnesty International traced the full chain from Banyan Terminal through Sahara Energy International to Hai Linh at Cai Mep and then to Myanmar. In no documented case was Vopak identified as the fuel supplier or cargo owner. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)

Vopak's Responses to Amnesty International Investigations

November 2022 (PITSB, Super Hero). Vopak responded by letter on 11 October 2022, confirming departure and describing itself as "an independent storage service provider" that does not "produce, transport, own, buy or sell products." It declined to name its customer. The fuel supplier for shipment 1 remains unconfirmed. (AI Nov 2022)

January 2024 (PITSB, YIDA 8 and HUITONG 78). Vopak was the only company to respond. It "claimed to have no record of a vessel berthing at Pengerang storage terminal on or about the dates we identified." Amnesty International stated this conflicted with its own evidence. Amnesty International named Vopak alongside BB Energy, CNOOC Trading, and Hai Linh as companies whose "entire value chain due diligence obligations are engaged" under the UN Guiding Principles. (AI Jan 2024)

July 2024 (Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal, HUITONG 78). Vopak confirmed the Banyan Terminal departure, reiterated its storage-only position, and declined to name its customer. It and the West of England P&I Club were the only two companies to respond to this investigation. (AI Jul 2024)

Key Relationships
•Dialog Group Berhad (PITSB co-shareholder, approximately 46% effective stake): Dialog Group Berhad is a Malaysian integrated technical services company listed on Bursa Malaysia. Through its subsidiary Dialog Pengerang Sdn Bhd, Dialog holds a 51% stake in Pengerang Terminals Sdn Bhd, the managing joint venture company that in turn holds 90% of PITSB, giving Dialog an effective stake of approximately 46% in PITSB. Dialog is the largest single effective stakeholder in PITSB and the majority shareholder in the managing entity. (AI Nov 2022; Vopak corporate disclosures)
•Johor State S.S.I. (PITSB co-shareholder, 10% stake): The State Secretary, Johor (Incorporated), representing the State Government of Johor Darul Ta'zim, holds the remaining 10% direct stake in PITSB. (AI Nov 2022; Vopak corporate disclosures)
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1): The Hai Linh Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam is the onward terminal for three of the four documented shipments. In all three cases (shipments 2, 3 and 4), fuel stored at a Vopak-linked terminal was subsequently transferred to Cai Mep before final delivery to Myanmar. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (Profile 6.1): HUITONG 78 (IMO 9646479) is the delivery vessel for two of the four documented shipments: the May 2023 PITSB staging delivery (shipment 3) and the January 2024 Banyan Terminal staging delivery (shipment 4). (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.3): Sahara Energy is the identified fuel trader for the January 2024 shipment (shipment 4). Amnesty International traced the full supply chain for that shipment from the Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal through Sahara Energy, which sold the fuel to a Vietnamese company before it was loaded at Cai Mep. (AI Jul 2024)
•BB Energy (Profile 5.2) and CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.4): In its January 2024 investigation, Amnesty International named Royal Vopak alongside BB Energy, CNOOC Trading and Hai Linh Co. Ltd. as companies whose entire value chain due diligence obligations are engaged under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. Neither BB Energy nor CNOOC Trading is identified as a fuel supplier for any of the four shipments passing through Vopak terminals; the connection is through Amnesty International's collective due diligence framing. (AI Jan 2024)

6
Recommended Actions
•Engage Royal Vopak to disclose the customer identities for all four documented shipments. Vopak declined to name its customers in all four cases. Identifying them would fill a significant evidentiary gap, particularly for shipment 1 where the fuel supplier remains unknown. Amnesty International, institutional shareholders, and investigative journalists should formally request voluntary disclosure. Vopak is Euronext-listed and publishes annual sustainability reporting; the request should be framed explicitly against its stated UN Guiding Principles commitments and its denial of the January 2024 Pengerang vessel calls, which Amnesty International found conflicted with its own evidence. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Engage Royal Vopak on enhanced customer screening and end-use declarations for high-risk cargo routes. Amnesty International's assessment across three investigations is that Vopak's storage and handling role engaged its value chain due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles. Civil society should formally request that Vopak implement enhanced customer due diligence and mandatory end-use declarations for petroleum products transiting PITSB and the Banyan Terminal toward high-risk destinations or onward to terminals associated with documented sanctions evasion. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Engage Malaysian and Singaporean port authorities on cargo screening at PITSB and the Banyan Terminal. The Malaysian Ministry of Transport and the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore should be engaged on end-use declaration requirements for petroleum products transiting these terminals toward known evasion routes. The documented pattern across three investigations and four shipments provides the evidentiary basis. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
