CNOOC Terminal, Huizhou, China
Profile 7.4
Summary Assessment
China National Offshore Oil Corporation (CNOOC) is China's third-largest national oil company and a state-owned enterprise directly owned by the State Council through the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission (SASAC). Two distinct CNOOC group entities are documented in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain in separate capacities. The CNOOC terminal in Huizhou, Guangdong Province, operated by the parent SOE, was identified by Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation as the upstream load point for the August 2023 aviation fuel shipment. Fuel loaded at Huizhou was transported by an unidentified vessel to the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. in southern Vietnam, then transferred to HUITONG 78 and delivered to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 15 August 2023 with a cargo of 9.91 kilotonnes. It is not established in any reviewed source whether the parent SOE sold the August 2023 cargo or provided only terminal storage or transit services. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd., a Singapore-incorporated trading subsidiary of the CNOOC group, is separately documented as the second-to-last seller of the February 2024 shipment, valued at approximately USD 8 million; Vietnamese customs data shows CNOOC Trading (Singapore) should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Vietnam for transit purposes only before onward delivery to Myanmar. Amnesty International contacted CNOOC for comment in connection with its January 2024 investigation. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) did not respond to Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation. It is one of two terminals identified so far in the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain located in mainland China, alongside the BP Hua Dong terminal in Ningbo (Profile 7.3). Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation named CNOOC alongside BB Energy, Hai Linh and Royal Vopak as a company whose entire value chain due diligence obligations are engaged under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. CNOOC has been designated by the United States under the Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies (NS-CMIC) program (OFAC, 3 December 2020), a designation that is not Myanmar-specific and predates the August 2023 shipment by nearly three years. Neither CNOOC nor CNOOC Trading (Singapore) has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar as of April 2026.

Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

The CNOOC terminal at Huizhou is the upstream origin point for the August 2023 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar, as documented by Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation. The full supply chain for this shipment ran as follows: fuel was loaded at the CNOOC terminal in Huizhou onto an unidentified vessel, which Amnesty International described via satellite imagery as being of the same approximate size, colour and layout as the Super Infinity (IMO 9341342); that vessel transported the fuel to the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1) in southern Vietnam; the fuel was then transferred to HUITONG 78 (Profile 6.1), which delivered it to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 15 August 2023, with a confirmed cargo volume of 9.91 kilotonnes. Amnesty International was unable to confirm the identity of the vessel that carried the fuel from Huizhou to Cai Mep. It is not established in any reviewed source whether the parent SOE sold the August 2023 cargo or provided only terminal storage or transit services. Amnesty International contacted CNOOC for comment in connection with its January 2024 investigation. For cross-reference with all documented jet fuel shipments, see the master shipment table. (AI Jan 2024)

The August 2023 shipment is the only documented connection between the CNOOC Huizhou terminal and the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain in any reviewed source. The CNOOC Huizhou terminal does not appear in any subsequent investigated supply chain. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.4), a separate CNOOC group entity, appears as the fuel trader in the February 2024 shipment documented by Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation, establishing a pattern of CNOOC group involvement across two consecutive supply chain iterations in 2023 and 2024. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)

Key Relationships
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1): The operator of the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam, which served as the intermediate staging point between the CNOOC Huizhou terminal and the final Myanmar delivery. An unidentified vessel transported the fuel from Huizhou to the Cai Mep terminal, where it was transferred to HUITONG 78 for the final leg to Thilawa. Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation named Hai Linh alongside CNOOC for engaged UNGP due diligence obligations. (AI Jan 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (Profile 6.1): The vessel that transported the August 2023 shipment from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 15 August 2023 with a cargo of 9.91 kilotonnes. HUITONG 78 was not the vessel that loaded at Huizhou; the Huizhou-to-Cai Mep leg was carried by a separate, unidentified vessel. (AI Jan 2024)
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.4): A separate CNOOC group entity that appears in the February 2024 shipment as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel, documented by Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation. The Huizhou terminal and CNOOC Trading Singapore involve distinct entities with distinct roles, but together establish that CNOOC group infrastructure appeared in the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain in consecutive years: 2023 via the Huizhou terminal and 2024 via CNOOC Trading Singapore. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•BB Energy (Profile 5.2) and Royal Vopak (Profile 7.1): Named by Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation alongside CNOOC and Hai Linh as companies whose entire value chain due diligence obligations are engaged under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. The grouping reflects their respective roles across the 2023 shipment series rather than any direct commercial relationship among them. (AI Jan 2024)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•January 2024. Amnesty International's investigation of 2023 shipments traces the August 2023 aviation fuel shipment upstream to the CNOOC terminal in Huizhou, China. The investigation describes the terminal as belonging to "the third-largest national oil company in China" and uses satellite imagery to identify an unidentified vessel at the CNOOC port in Huizhou consistent in size, colour and layout with the Super Infinity. Amnesty International names CNOOC alongside BB Energy (Profile 5.2), Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1) and Royal Vopak (Profile 7.1) as companies whose entire value chain due diligence obligations are engaged under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, and confirms it contacted CNOOC for comment. Two questions remain unresolved in any reviewed source: whether the parent SOE sold the August 2023 cargo or provided only terminal storage or transit services; and whether CNOOC Trading (Singapore) has taken any steps to exit the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain following Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation. No advocacy action, government response or civil society designation call directed at CNOOC or the Huizhou terminal has been documented as of April 2026. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•July 2024. Amnesty International's Reckless Shipments investigation names CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller in the February 2024 aviation fuel shipment, documenting that Vietnamese customs data showed it should have been aware the fuel was transported to Vietnam for transit purposes only before onward delivery to Myanmar. The shipment was valued at approximately USD 8 million. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) did not respond to Amnesty International. This is the only documented post-January 2024 reference to a CNOOC group entity in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain in any reviewed source. No advocacy action or government response directed at CNOOC Trading (Singapore) in connection with this investigation has been documented as of April 2026. (AI Jul 2024)

Recommended Actions
•Pursue the UN Guiding Principles responsibility framework with both CNOOC entities: Amnesty International has already established the UNGP basis for engaging both entities: the parent SOE was named in January 2024 and CNOOC Trading (Singapore) in July 2024. The UNGP framework does not require establishing knowledge of the ultimate end use, making it the more immediately actionable advocacy pathway while the seller-versus-terminal-operator question for the parent SOE remains unresolved. Both entities should be engaged on what steps they have taken since those reports to implement the responsibility to respect human rights, including what due diligence they now conduct on petroleum sales and terminal access decisions. The appropriate correspondence target for the parent SOE is Zhang Chuanjiang, appointed Chairman of China National Offshore Oil Corporation from June 2025. CNOOC's listings on the Hong Kong and New York stock exchanges provide additional channels: institutional investors and civil society organisations with shareholdings in CNOOC can raise these concerns through shareholder engagement mechanisms. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Write directly to CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. requesting confirmation of its current supply chain position: Write to CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. at its registered address, 12 Marina Boulevard, #34-02, Marina Bay Financial Centre, Singapore 018982, requesting confirmation of whether any further fuel sales have been made by CNOOC Trading (Singapore) to Vietnamese or other intermediary companies for transit to Myanmar since February 2024, and what due diligence measures CNOOC Trading (Singapore) applies to sales of fuel destined for transit. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) did not respond to Amnesty International's July 2024 request for comment. Any response or non-response should be documented for the evidentiary record and, where a non-response continues, referenced in future advocacy materials directed at Singapore regulatory authorities. (AI Jul 2024)
•Engage Singapore authorities with the Amnesty International July 2024 findings: Engage the Monetary Authority of Singapore, Singapore Customs and the Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority with the findings of Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation, noting that a Singapore-incorporated CNOOC group subsidiary has been documented selling aviation fuel in a supply chain delivering to the Myanmar military. MAS has jurisdiction over Singapore-based financial transactions including the power to give effect to UN Security Council sanctions. Singapore Customs enforces the Strategic Goods (Control) Act. ACRA maintains corporate compliance jurisdiction. All three authorities have distinct and complementary regulatory bases for investigating CNOOC Trading (Singapore)'s February 2024 transaction. Separately, US correspondent banks that may have processed USD-denominated payments for the February 2024 shipment should be engaged about their potential OFAC exposure under the August 2023 sector determination for any transactions transiting US banking channels in connection with Myanmar petroleum supply. (AI Jul 2024)
•Identify the vessel that transported fuel from Huizhou to Cai Mep and investigate its ownership and management: The vessel that loaded at the CNOOC Huizhou terminal and carried the fuel to the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam has not been confirmed in any reviewed source. Amnesty International described it via satellite imagery as being of the same approximate size, colour and layout as the Super Infinity (IMO 9341342). Identifying this vessel would complete the supply chain picture for the August 2023 shipment and could reveal additional entities warranting due diligence engagement or sanctions advocacy. Investigative journalists, civil society researchers and port state authorities with access to vessel movement records for Huizhou and Cai Mep should prioritise resolving this evidentiary gap. (AI Jan 2024)
