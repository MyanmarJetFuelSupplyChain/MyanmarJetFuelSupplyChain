CNOOC Terminal, Huizhou, China
Profile 7.4
Summary Assessment
The CNOOC terminal in Huizhou, Guangdong, operated by China National Offshore Oil Corporation, was the upstream load point for the August 2023 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar. Fuel loaded at Huizhou was carried by an unidentified vessel to Hai Linh's Cai Mep terminal, transferred to HUITONG 78, and delivered to Thilawa on 15 August 2023, carrying 9.91 kilotonnes. Whether the parent CNOOC SOE sold the fuel or provided only terminal storage has not been established. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd., a separate CNOOC group entity, was the second-to-last seller in the consecutive February 2024 shipment, establishing CNOOC group involvement across two consecutive supply chain iterations. Amnesty International named CNOOC alongside BB Energy, Hai Linh, and Royal Vopak as companies whose UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations are engaged. CNOOC has been designated by OFAC under the NS-CMIC programme (December 2020), but not in connection with Myanmar. No Myanmar-specific designation of any CNOOC entity has been made by any government.

Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Fuel loaded at the CNOOC Huizhou terminal was carried by an unidentified vessel to the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, transferred to HUITONG 78, and delivered to Thilawa on 15 August 2023. Amnesty International identified the vessel via satellite imagery as consistent in size, colour, and layout with the Super Infinity (IMO 9341342) but could not confirm its identity. Whether CNOOC acted as seller or as terminal operator only has not been established. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.4), a separate CNOOC group entity, was the second-to-last seller in the consecutive February 2024 shipment, confirming a pattern of CNOOC group infrastructure involvement across two supply chain iterations. This is the only reviewed source connecting the Huizhou terminal to the Myanmar supply chain. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)

Key Relationships
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1): The operator of the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam, which served as the intermediate staging point between the CNOOC Huizhou terminal and the final Myanmar delivery. An unidentified vessel transported the fuel from Huizhou to the Cai Mep terminal, where it was transferred to HUITONG 78 for the final leg to Thilawa. Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation named Hai Linh alongside CNOOC for engaged UNGP due diligence obligations. (AI Jan 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (Profile 6.1): The vessel that transported the August 2023 shipment from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 15 August 2023 with a cargo of 9.91 kilotonnes. HUITONG 78 was not the vessel that loaded at Huizhou; the Huizhou-to-Cai Mep leg was carried by a separate, unidentified vessel. (AI Jan 2024)
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.4): A separate CNOOC group entity that appears in the February 2024 shipment as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel, documented by Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation. The Huizhou terminal and CNOOC Trading Singapore involve distinct entities with distinct roles, but together establish that CNOOC group infrastructure appeared in the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain in consecutive years: 2023 via the Huizhou terminal and 2024 via CNOOC Trading Singapore. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•BB Energy (Profile 5.2) and Royal Vopak (Profile 7.1): Named by Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation alongside CNOOC and Hai Linh as companies whose entire value chain due diligence obligations are engaged under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. The grouping reflects their respective roles across the 2023 shipment series rather than any direct commercial relationship among them. (AI Jan 2024)

Recommended Actions
•Engage CNOOC and CNOOC Trading (Singapore) on UN Guiding Principles obligations. Amnesty International established the UNGP basis for both entities across two investigations. The UNGP framework does not require proof of knowledge of the ultimate end use, making it the more immediately actionable pathway while the seller-versus-terminal-operator question remains unresolved. Correspondence to the parent SOE should target Zhang Chuanjiang, appointed Chairman from June 2025. CNOOC's HKEX and NYSE listings provide institutional investor channels. CNOOC Trading (Singapore)'s registered address is 12 Marina Boulevard, #34-02, Marina Bay Financial Centre, Singapore 018982. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Engage Singapore authorities on CNOOC Trading (Singapore)'s February 2024 transaction. MAS, Singapore Customs, and ACRA each have distinct regulatory jurisdiction. MAS has power to give effect to UN Security Council sanctions; Singapore Customs enforces the Strategic Goods (Control) Act; ACRA maintains corporate compliance jurisdiction. US correspondent banks that processed USD-denominated payments in the February 2024 chain should also be engaged on OFAC exposure under the August 2023 sector determination. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Identify the vessel that transported fuel from Huizhou to Cai Mep. The vessel has not been confirmed in any reviewed source. Identifying it would complete the August 2023 supply chain picture and may reveal additional entities. Port state authorities with access to vessel movement records for Huizhou and Cai Mep are the most direct investigative route. (AI Jan 2024)
