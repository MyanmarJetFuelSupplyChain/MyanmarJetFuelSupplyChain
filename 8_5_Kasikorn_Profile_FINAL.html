Kasikorn Bank Public Company Limited
Also known as KBank  ·  Publicly listed commercial bank, Bangkok, Thailand  ·  Not designated by any government as of April 2026  ·  Profile 8.5
Summary Assessment
Kasikorn Bank Public Company Limited (KBank) is a Thai publicly listed commercial bank headquartered in Bangkok. It appears in two distinct but related capacities in the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain. First, and most directly, Kasikorn Bank held the Thai-side accounts through which Tawan-Oil Trading received aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report documented that aviation fuel payments of over USD 25 million from Intraco, a NEPAS proxy, passed through United Amara Bank (UAB) in Myanmar and were received in Tawan-Oil Trading accounts held at Kasikorn Bank. Payments from Rich Ray Trading, an Asia Sun Group proxy, were also received in Tawan-Oil Trading accounts at Kasikorn Bank, though the amount attributable to Rich Ray Trading was not separately specified in the UN Special Rapporteur's documentation. The UN Special Rapporteur found that effective due diligence by Kasikorn Bank, including a basic review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. Second, Kasikorn Bank held nostro accounts for Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) (Profile 8.4) since the coup, and facilitated over USD 35 million in Myanmar military procurement in FY2022. Following the imposition of US sanctions on MFTB and MICB in June 2023, Kasikorn Bank confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it had terminated its relationships with those banks. Kasikorn Bank has not been designated by any government as of April 2026.

1
Role in the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Payment Chain

Kasikorn Bank is directly documented by the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report as the Thai-side receiving bank for a portion of Myanmar's aviation fuel payments in calendar year 2023. Specifically, Kasikorn Bank held the accounts of Tawan-Oil Trading, a Thailand-registered company that sold aviation fuel to Myanmar purchasers. Through those accounts, Kasikorn Bank received payments routed from Myanmar via United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1). (UNSR Jun 2024)

The payment flows documented in the UN Special Rapporteur's Figure 7 show two streams terminating at Kasikorn Bank via Tawan-Oil Trading. First, Intraco, a Myanmar-registered company established on behalf of NEPAS (Profile 1.2) on 20 September 2023, made aviation fuel payments of over USD 25 million through UAB to Tawan-Oil Trading accounts at Kasikorn Bank. Second, Rich Ray Trading (Profile 1.6), an Asia Sun Group (Profile 1.4) proxy, made payments to Tawan-Oil Trading accounts at Kasikorn Bank; the amount attributable specifically to Rich Ray Trading was not separately specified in the UN Special Rapporteur's documentation, as it was included within the broader Asia Sun Group payment total of over USD 15 million across Swan Energy, Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading combined. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The UN Special Rapporteur's report identified a failure of due diligence on Kasikorn Bank's part. The report found that effective due diligence by Kasikorn Bank, including a basic review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. As a specific example, the UNSR noted that Swan Energy's website openly states that it sells 10,000 metric tons of aviation fuel to Myanmar every month, which it claims represents 100 percent of Myanmar's market share. This publicly available information was accessible to Kasikorn Bank through standard know-your-customer and correspondent banking procedures. (UNSR Jun 2024)

3
Role as Nostro Account Provider for MFTB and MICB

Separately from its role in the aviation fuel payment chain, Kasikorn Bank held nostro accounts for Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) (Profile 8.4) since the coup, as documented in the UN Special Rapporteur's Figure 5. Through those nostro accounts, Kasikorn Bank facilitated military procurement transactions on behalf of the Myanmar junta. The UN Special Rapporteur's report documents that Kasikorn Bank facilitated over USD 35 million in Myanmar military procurement in FY2022 (April 2021 to March 2022). By FY2023 (April 2022 to March 2023), this figure had declined to less than USD 5 million, with all FY2023 transactions occurring before the imposition of US sanctions on MFTB and MICB in June 2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Following the US designation of MFTB and MICB on 21 June 2023, Kasikorn Bank confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it had terminated its relationships with both banks. This was a documented positive action that contrasted with the response of other Thai banks, notably Siam Commercial Bank (SCB), which as of the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report had not confirmed termination of its MFTB and MICB nostro accounts despite a sharp increase in documented military procurement volumes through those accounts. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

4
Designation Status

Kasikorn Bank has not been designated by the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union, Canada or Australia as of April 2026. No designation call has been made by any government in connection with Kasikorn Bank's documented role in the Myanmar aviation fuel payment chain or military procurement network. The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report identified Kasikorn Bank's failure to conduct adequate due diligence on the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade but did not call for Kasikorn Bank's designation. (UNSR Jun 2024)

5
Key Relationships
•Tawan-Oil Trading: The Thailand-registered company whose accounts at Kasikorn Bank received aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023. Tawan-Oil Trading sold aviation fuel to NEPAS proxy Intraco and to Asia Sun Group proxy Rich Ray Trading. Tawan-Oil Trading has not been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) (Profile 8.4): Myanmar state-owned banks for which Kasikorn Bank held nostro accounts since the coup. Kasikorn Bank confirmed termination of both relationships following US sanctions on MFTB and MICB in June 2023. MFTB and MICB are designated by the United States, Canada and Australia. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1): The private Myanmar bank through which aviation fuel payments were routed from the Myanmar side before reaching Kasikorn Bank on the Thai side via Tawan-Oil Trading. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank (Profile 8.2): The other Thai bank documented in the 2023 aviation fuel payment chain, serving as the receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payment streams from NEPAS and from Swan Energy and Myan-Oil. Krung Thai Bank and Kasikorn Bank together received the entirety of the approximately USD 80 million in documented aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur's due diligence finding applied equally to both banks. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•NEPAS (Profile 1.2) and Asia Sun Group (Profile 1.4): The two Myanmar-side procurement networks whose proxy companies made the aviation fuel payments that terminated in Kasikorn Bank accounts. Intraco acted as a NEPAS proxy; Rich Ray Trading acted as an Asia Sun Group proxy. Both proxy companies transacted via UAB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
6
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•June 2023. Following US sanctions on MFTB and MICB, Kasikorn Bank confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it had terminated its nostro account relationships with both banks. This was the only proactive compliance action confirmed by a Thai bank in the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report, and was cited as a positive contrast to the inaction of other banks including Siam Commercial Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report publicly documents Kasikorn Bank's role as the Thai receiving bank for Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel accounts and identifies the bank's failure of due diligence in calendar year 2023. The UNSR finds that Kasikorn Bank could have detected the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade through basic review of corporate records and publicly available information. The report does not call for designation of Kasikorn Bank. In July 2024, Thai bank representatives told a parliamentary committee that they lacked the capacity to investigate Myanmar-linked transactions; the Bank of Thailand and AMLO subsequently announced the formation of a task force to investigate the transactions and enhance Thai banks' due diligence capabilities. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•December 2024. The BOT/AMLO joint directive took effect on 27 December 2024, mandating enhanced due diligence by all Thai banks on Myanmar-linked transactions, including verification of ultimate beneficial owners using third-party data sources. The Thai task force investigation into the transactions identified in the UNSR's June 2024 report concluded that while some Thai financial institutions had conducted transactions with individuals named in the report, no evidence was found linking those transactions to arms procurement. The task force's conclusion addressed military arms procurement broadly; it did not separately examine whether aviation fuel payments received at Kasikorn Bank and Krung Thai Bank through Tawan-Oil Trading and CB Energy served Myanmar military end-users, which remains the distinct and unresolved question identified by the UNSR. (Open source, December 2024)
•May 2025. Justice For Myanmar's Fuelling Atrocities report confirms Kasikorn Bank's termination of its MFTB and MICB relationships and presents this as a positive example to be contrasted with the continued inaction of other Thai banks. As of April 2026, Kasikorn Bank has not been designated by any government, and no government has publicly called for its designation in connection with its documented role in the aviation fuel payment chain. (JFM May 2025)
7
Recommended Actions
•Establish whether Kasikorn Bank continues to hold accounts for Tawan-Oil Trading and whether those accounts remain active for aviation fuel payments: The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report documents Kasikorn Bank's role as the Thai receiving bank for Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel accounts in calendar year 2023. It is not established in the reviewed sources whether Kasikorn Bank has since terminated or restricted those accounts, or whether Tawan-Oil Trading continues to receive aviation fuel payments from Myanmar entities through Kasikorn Bank. Civil society should request that Kasikorn Bank publicly confirm the current status of its relationship with Tawan-Oil Trading, and whether it has implemented enhanced due diligence on transactions connected to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Engage Kasikorn Bank and Thai authorities on the specific question of aviation fuel due diligence: The Thai task force investigation concluded in December 2024 that no evidence was found linking Thai bank transactions to arms procurement. However, this conclusion does not address the distinct question of whether aviation fuel payments received at Kasikorn Bank via Tawan-Oil Trading were adequately screened for connections to Myanmar military end-users. The UNSR's June 2024 report establishes that the information required to identify those connections was publicly available, and that Kasikorn Bank did not act on it. Civil society should engage Kasikorn Bank directly with the UNSR's aviation fuel-specific due diligence finding, and should work with the BOT/AMLO December 2024 directive on enhanced due diligence as a regulatory basis for requesting a written commitment from Kasikorn Bank on its Myanmar aviation fuel screening procedures. The directive's requirement to verify ultimate beneficial owners using third-party data rather than client-supplied documents is directly relevant to the type of Myanmar corporate records review the UNSR identified as necessary. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Investigate the current routing of Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel revenues: Even if Kasikorn Bank has restricted Tawan-Oil Trading's accounts, aviation fuel payments from Myanmar may continue to reach Tawan-Oil Trading through other Thai or regional banks. The BOT/AMLO December 2024 directive on enhanced due diligence for Myanmar-linked transactions applies to all Thai banks and creates a regulatory basis for investigating whether aviation fuel payments have shifted to alternative Thai banking channels. Investigative journalists and financial intelligence units should map the current financial flows associated with Tawan-Oil Trading to determine whether the aviation fuel payment structure documented in 2023 remains in operation. (UNSR Jun 2024)
