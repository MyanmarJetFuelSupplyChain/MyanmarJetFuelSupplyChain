Kasikorn Bank Public Company Limited
Profile 8.5
Summary Assessment
Kasikorn Bank (KBank) is a Thai publicly listed commercial bank. Tawan-Oil Trading, the second Thai seller in Myanmar's 2023 aviation fuel supply chain, held accounts at Kasikorn Bank into which it received over USD 25 million from Intraco (a NEPAS proxy) and a portion of the combined USD 15 million from Asia Sun Group proxies, all routed through United Amara Bank. The UN Special Rapporteur found that effective due diligence by Kasikorn Bank, including a basic review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. Separately, Kasikorn Bank held nostro accounts for MFTB and MICB, facilitating over USD 35 million in Myanmar military procurement in FY2022. Following June 2023 US sanctions on MFTB and MICB, Kasikorn confirmed termination of those nostro relationships, the only Thai bank to proactively do so. Whether its Tawan-Oil Trading accounts remain active has not been confirmed. Kasikorn Bank has not been sanctioned by any government.

Role in the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Payment Chain

Kasikorn Bank held the accounts of Tawan-Oil Trading through which it received aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in 2023. Intraco paid over USD 25 million and Rich Ray Trading paid an unspecified portion of the combined USD 15 million paid by Asia Sun Group proxies, all routed through United Amara Bank to Tawan-Oil Trading at Kasikorn Bank. The UNSR found that Swan Energy's website openly claiming 100 percent of Myanmar's aviation fuel market was publicly available information Kasikorn Bank could have reviewed as part of standard customer due diligence on Tawan-Oil Trading's counterparties. (UNSR Jun 2024)

3
Role as Nostro Account Provider for MFTB and MICB

Kasikorn Bank held nostro accounts for MFTB and MICB and facilitated over USD 35 million in Myanmar military procurement in FY2022, declining to under USD 5 million in FY2023 before US sanctions. Following the June 2023 OFAC designations, Kasikorn confirmed to the UNSR that it had terminated both relationships. This was the only proactive termination confirmed by a Thai bank in the UNSR report, contrasting with Siam Commercial Bank, which neither confirmed termination nor explained a sharp increase in documented military procurement volumes through its accounts. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

5
Key Relationships
•Tawan-Oil Trading: The Thailand-registered company whose accounts at Kasikorn Bank received aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023. Tawan-Oil Trading sold aviation fuel to NEPAS proxy Intraco and to Asia Sun Group proxy Rich Ray Trading. Tawan-Oil Trading has not been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) (Profile 8.4): Myanmar state-owned banks for which Kasikorn Bank held nostro accounts since the coup. Kasikorn Bank confirmed termination of both relationships following US sanctions on MFTB and MICB in June 2023. MFTB and MICB are designated by the United States, Canada and Australia. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1): The private Myanmar bank through which aviation fuel payments were routed from the Myanmar side before reaching Kasikorn Bank on the Thai side via Tawan-Oil Trading. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank (Profile 8.2): The other Thai bank documented in the 2023 aviation fuel payment chain, serving as the receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payment streams from NEPAS and from Swan Energy and Myan-Oil. Krung Thai Bank and Kasikorn Bank together received the entirety of the approximately USD 80 million in documented aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur's due diligence finding applied equally to both banks. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•NEPAS (Profile 1.2) and Asia Sun Group (Profile 1.4): The two Myanmar-side procurement networks whose proxy companies made the aviation fuel payments that terminated in Kasikorn Bank accounts. Intraco acted as a NEPAS proxy; Rich Ray Trading acted as an Asia Sun Group proxy. Both proxy companies transacted via UAB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
6
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•June 2023. Following US sanctions on MFTB and MICB, Kasikorn confirms termination of both nostro accounts to the UNSR. This is the only proactive confirmed compliance action by a Thai bank documented in the UNSR report. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•June 2024. The UNSR's Banking on the Death Trade report names Kasikorn Bank as the receiving bank for Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel accounts and identifies its due diligence failure in 2023. Thai bank representatives tell a parliamentary committee they lacked capacity to investigate indirect Myanmar transactions. The Bank of Thailand and AMLO announce a task force. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•27 December 2024. The Bank of Thailand (BOT) and Anti-Money Laundering Office (AMLO) joint directive takes effect, mandating enhanced due diligence on Myanmar-linked transactions and UBO verification using third-party data. The task force concluded no evidence linked Thai bank transactions to arms procurement, but did not specifically address the aviation fuel payment question identified by the UNSR. (BOT/AMLO Dec 2024)
•May 2025. Justice for Myanmar confirms Kasikorn's MFTB and MICB termination as a positive example. No government has called for Kasikorn's designation. Whether its Tawan-Oil Trading accounts remain active is unconfirmed. (JFM May 2025)
7
Recommended Actions
•Request that Kasikorn Bank publicly confirm the current status of its Tawan-Oil Trading accounts. The UNSR documented Kasikorn's role as Tawan-Oil Trading's receiving bank in 2023. No reviewed source confirms whether those accounts remain active or have been subject to enhanced screening. Civil society should write to Kasikorn Bank requesting confirmation and a written commitment on Myanmar aviation fuel screening procedures. The BOT/AMLO December 2024 directive provides the regulatory basis. (UNSR Jun 2024; BOT/AMLO Dec 2024)
•Engage Kasikorn Bank and the BOT specifically on the aviation fuel due diligence question. The Thai task force's December 2024 conclusion addressed arms procurement broadly and did not separately examine whether aviation fuel payments at Kasikorn Bank were adequately screened. The UNSR established that the information needed to identify the Myanmar purchasers' connections was publicly available. Civil society should formally submit the UNSR's aviation fuel-specific findings to both Kasikorn Bank and the BOT, distinguishing the aviation fuel question from the arms procurement conclusion. (UNSR Jun 2024; BOT/AMLO Dec 2024)
•Investigate whether Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel payment flows have shifted to another bank. Even if Kasikorn has restricted its Tawan-Oil Trading accounts, payments may have moved to other Thai or regional banks. The BOT/AMLO directive's enhanced Myanmar due diligence requirement applies to all Thai banks and creates a regulatory basis for investigating alternative channels. (UNSR Jun 2024)
