CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.
Profile 5.4
Summary Assessment
CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. is the Singapore-registered trading subsidiary of China National Offshore Oil Corporation (CNOOC), the third-largest national oil company in China, which is owned by the Government of the People's Republic of China. Vietnamese customs data, reviewed by Amnesty International and published in July 2024, identified CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel in one documented shipment that transited Vietnam before reaching Myanmar: the February 2024 shipment aboard HUITONG 78, which arrived at the Thilawa terminal in Yangon on 29 February 2024 and was valued at approximately USD 8 million. In that transaction, CNOOC Trading sold fuel to a Vietnamese company, which received it at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. before it was sold to the Myanmar purchaser and transported to Thilawa. Amnesty International determined, on the basis of Vietnamese customs data, that CNOOC Trading "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." The identical "should have been aware" language was applied to Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. for the immediately preceding January 2024 shipment, making the two Singapore-based traders together the clearest documented cases of constructive awareness of the transit supply chain. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. did not respond to Amnesty International's contact. Separately, the CNOOC terminal in Huizhou, China served as the upstream load point for one of the 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments (row 16, August 2023), establishing a broader pattern of CNOOC group infrastructure in the supply chain across two distinct periods. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. has not been sanctioned by any government and does not appear as a specifically designated entity in the opensanctions database.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain
The February 2024 shipment

CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. is identified by Vietnamese customs data, as reviewed and published by Amnesty International in July 2024, as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel in the February 2024 shipment to Myanmar. The full supply chain for that shipment, as documented by Amnesty International, ran as follows: fuel was sourced from the BP Hua Dong terminal at Ningbo port, Hangzhou Bay, China; CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. sold it to a Vietnamese company; that Vietnamese company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. in southern Vietnam; after storage at Hai Linh's terminal, the fuel was sold on to the Myanmar purchaser and loaded aboard HUITONG 78; the vessel then transported the fuel to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 29 February 2024. The shipment was valued at approximately USD 8 million. Amnesty International stated it was unable to identify the company that controls the BP Hua Dong terminal. (AI Jul 2024)

Amnesty International determined, on the basis of Vietnamese customs data, that CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." This constructive awareness finding, applied identically to Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. for the immediately preceding January 2024 shipment, establishes that the customs record for both Singapore-based traders independently supports a finding of constructive knowledge of the transit nature of the respective transactions. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. did not respond to Amnesty International's contact in connection with the July 2024 investigation. (AI Jul 2024)

The broader CNOOC group supply chain connection: August 2023

Separately from CNOOC Trading's role as second-to-last seller in the February 2024 shipment, the CNOOC terminal in Huizhou, China served as the upstream load point for one of the seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments: the August 2023 HUITONG 78 shipment (9.91 kilotonnes, arrived Thilawa 15 August 2023). The fuel loaded at the Huizhou CNOOC terminal was described by Amnesty International as originating at "the terminal of the third-largest national oil company in China." Amnesty International's January 2024 report named CNOOC alongside BB Energy, Hai Linh and Royal Vopak as companies with engaged UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations and confirmed it had contacted CNOOC for comment in connection with that investigation. This establishes that CNOOC group infrastructure appeared in the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain in both 2023 (Huizhou terminal) and 2024 (CNOOC Trading Singapore as second-to-last seller). These two connections involve distinct CNOOC group entities and distinct roles, but together indicate a pattern of CNOOC group involvement across consecutive supply chain iterations. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)

The state-ownership dimension: CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. is ultimately owned by the Chinese state. Unlike BB Energy and Sahara Energy, which are privately owned commercial traders, CNOOC Trading's ultimate beneficial owner is the Government of the People's Republic of China via SASAC. This creates a distinct diplomatic and political dimension to any advocacy or designation strategy: engagement with CNOOC Trading is simultaneously engagement with an instrumentality of the Chinese state. Designation of CNOOC Trading (Singapore) by a Western government would carry significant bilateral implications beyond the immediate sanctions objective. This does not change the evidentiary basis for designation, which rests on Vietnamese customs data and the August 2023 OFAC sector determination, but it materially affects the diplomatic context in which such a designation would operate. Note that the parent CNOOC group entity already appears on the OFAC non-SDN Consolidated Sanctions List and the US DoD Section 1260H Chinese military companies list; a separate China-incorporated CNOOC group trading subsidiary, CNOOC International Trading Co., Ltd., is named as a distinct entity on the January 2025 1260H list. None of those entries constitute a specific designation of CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

Key Relationships
•BP Hua Dong terminal, Ningbo, China: The upstream storage terminal from which the fuel in the February 2024 shipment was traced before CNOOC Trading sold it onward. Amnesty International identified this terminal as the immediately preceding stop before CNOOC Trading's transaction in the supply chain. Amnesty International stated it was unable to identify the company that controls the BP Hua Dong terminal. (AI Jul 2024)
•CNOOC terminal, Huizhou, China (August 2023 connection): A separate CNOOC group terminal that served as the upstream load point for the August 2023 shipment (row 16, 9.91 kt). Amnesty International identified this as the terminal of "the third-largest national oil company in China." This is a distinct role from CNOOC Trading's February 2024 seller role and involves different CNOOC group infrastructure. Amnesty International named CNOOC in its January 2024 investigation as a company with engaged UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations. See Profile 7.4. (AI Jan 2024)
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, Vietnam): The terminal operator in Vietnam that received the fuel purchased from CNOOC Trading by the Vietnamese intermediary company. The Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal served as the transit hub for all nine documented 2023 and 2024 Vietnam-transited shipments to Myanmar, including the February 2024 shipment in which CNOOC Trading participated. Hai Linh is unsanctioned. See Profile 4.1. (AI Jul 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (IMO 9646479): The vessel that transported the February 2024 shipment from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon, arriving 29 February 2024. HUITONG 78 was subsequently renamed BARAAWE 1. Per the West of England P&I Club, the vessel is owned by an affiliate of Chinese state-owned defence company China Aerospace Science and Industry Corp (CASIC). See Profile 6.1. (AI Jul 2024)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited / Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal (Thilawa, Yangon): The destination terminal in Myanmar to which the February 2024 shipment was delivered. During early 2024 the terminal was operating under the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited before arriving at its current name. See Profile 1.3. (AI Jul 2024)
•Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd.: The Singapore-based privately owned trading company identified by Amnesty International as the second-to-last seller in the immediately preceding January 2024 shipment (row 18). Vietnamese customs data applied the identical "should have been aware" standard to Sahara Energy as to CNOOC Trading, making the two transactions the strongest documented cases of constructive awareness in the reviewed supply chain. The parallel treatment of both Singapore-based traders in consecutive 2024 shipments is directly relevant to any joint designation submissions. See Profile 5.3. (AI Jul 2024)
•Unnamed Vietnamese intermediary company: The direct purchaser from CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. in the February 2024 shipment. This company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal and then sold it to the Myanmar-side purchaser. Its identity is not disclosed in any reviewed source. (AI Jul 2024)

Recommended Actions
•Submit a joint designation submission to OFAC covering CNOOC Trading and Sahara Energy: The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 authorises designation of any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy. CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. and Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. were identified by Vietnamese customs data in consecutive shipments (February and January 2024 respectively) using identical "should have been aware" language, making them the strongest available joint designation candidates from the documented 2024 supply chain. A joint submission reinforces the systematic nature of the transit supply chain. The submission should note that the CNOOC parent group already appears on the OFAC non-SDN Consolidated Sanctions List and the US DoD Section 1260H Chinese military companies list, and that CNOOC International Trading Co., Ltd. (a separate China-incorporated CNOOC group subsidiary) is named as a distinct entity on the January 2025 1260H list. These pre-existing US Government determinations provide relevant context for a designation submission targeting CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. specifically under the OFAC sector determination. Care should be taken in any submission to clearly identify CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. by its ACRA number (200412252W) and Singapore address to distinguish it from the China-incorporated entity on the 1260H list. Designation submissions should also be pursued with sanctions authorities in the UK, EU, Canada and Australia under their respective Myanmar sanctions frameworks. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage CNOOC Trading and the CNOOC parent at the diplomatic and corporate level: Given that CNOOC Trading (Singapore) is ultimately owned by the Chinese state, civil society engagement with CNOOC Trading itself should be accompanied by engagement through diplomatic channels. Governments with Myanmar sanctions programmes, particularly the United States, United Kingdom and European Union, should raise with the Government of the People's Republic of China the documented role of CNOOC group infrastructure in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain in both 2023 and 2024. CNOOC Trading's own corporate management in Singapore should simultaneously receive a formal disclosure request, asking for: confirmation of whether CNOOC Trading had knowledge that the fuel it sold to a Vietnamese company in February 2024 would be transported onward to Myanmar; disclosure of what human rights due diligence it applied to the Vietnamese purchaser; and confirmation of what controls it has put in place to prevent recurrence. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Engage the UK Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation and EU competent authorities: Civil society should assess whether the Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021 and equivalent EU sanctions frameworks apply to CNOOC Trading's documented conduct and submit referrals to the UK Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation (OTSI) and, where applicable, EU competent authorities. These submissions should reference the July 2024 Amnesty International findings and the constructive awareness standard derived from Vietnamese customs data. (AI Jul 2024)
•Monitor for renewed CNOOC group involvement: CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. is documented in one shipment as second-to-last seller (February 2024), but the CNOOC Huizhou terminal's role in the August 2023 supply chain indicates that different CNOOC group entities can appear at different points in the same supply chain across different time periods. The January 2026 Amnesty International investigation confirmed that the Vietnam transit route had been superseded, but CNOOC group infrastructure in China remains available to future supply chain configurations. Any renewed involvement by any CNOOC group entity in a Myanmar-bound aviation fuel supply chain should trigger immediate re-engagement on all the above fronts. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
