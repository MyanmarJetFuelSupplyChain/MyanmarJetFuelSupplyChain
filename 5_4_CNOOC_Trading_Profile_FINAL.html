CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.
Profile 5.4
Summary Assessment
CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. is the Singapore-registered trading subsidiary of China National Offshore Oil Corporation, owned by the Chinese government. Vietnamese customs data reviewed by Amnesty International identified it as the second-to-last seller in the February 2024 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar, valued at approximately USD 8 million, which transited the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal before delivery to Thilawa aboard HUITONG 78. Amnesty International found that CNOOC Trading "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." The same finding was applied to Sahara Energy for the immediately preceding January 2024 shipment. Separately, the CNOOC terminal in Huizhou served as the upstream load point for the August 2023 shipment, establishing CNOOC group infrastructure in the supply chain across two consecutive iterations. CNOOC Trading did not respond to Amnesty International. It has not been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Vietnamese customs data identified CNOOC Trading as the second-to-last seller in the February 2024 shipment. Fuel from the BP Hua Dong terminal in Ningbo, China was sold by CNOOC Trading to a Vietnamese company, received at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, and loaded aboard HUITONG 78, which arrived at Thilawa on 29 February 2024. The shipment was valued at approximately USD 8 million. Amnesty International found CNOOC Trading "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only," applying the same constructive awareness standard as it did to Sahara Energy for the immediately preceding January 2024 shipment. CNOOC Trading did not respond to Amnesty International. (AI Jul 2024)

The CNOOC terminal in Huizhou, China also served as the upstream load point for the August 2023 HUITONG 78 shipment of 9.91 kilotonnes. Amnesty International named CNOOC alongside BB Energy, Hai Linh, and Royal Vopak as companies with engaged UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations in connection with that 2023 role, and contacted CNOOC for comment. These two connections involve distinct CNOOC group entities and distinct roles but together indicate a pattern of CNOOC group involvement across consecutive supply chain iterations. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)

Unlike BB Energy and Sahara Energy, which are privately owned, CNOOC Trading's ultimate beneficial owner is the Chinese government via SASAC. Designation would carry significant bilateral implications beyond the immediate sanctions objective. The parent CNOOC group entity appears on the OFAC non-SDN Consolidated Sanctions List and the US DoD Section 1260H Chinese military companies list. A separate China-incorporated CNOOC group subsidiary, CNOOC International Trading Co. Ltd., appears on the January 2025 1260H list. None of those entries constitute a designation of CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (ACRA 200412252W) specifically. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

2
Key Relationships
•BP Hua Dong terminal, Ningbo, China: The upstream storage terminal from which the fuel in the February 2024 shipment was traced before CNOOC Trading sold it onward. Amnesty International identified this terminal as the immediately preceding stop before CNOOC Trading's transaction in the supply chain. Amnesty International stated it was unable to identify the company that controls the BP Hua Dong terminal. (AI Jul 2024)
•CNOOC terminal, Huizhou, China (August 2023 connection): A separate CNOOC group terminal that served as the upstream load point for the August 2023 shipment (row 16, 9.91 kt). Amnesty International identified this as the terminal of "the third-largest national oil company in China." This is a distinct role from CNOOC Trading's February 2024 seller role and involves different CNOOC group infrastructure. Amnesty International named CNOOC in its January 2024 investigation as a company with engaged UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations. See Profile 7.4. (AI Jan 2024)
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, Vietnam): The terminal operator in Vietnam that received the fuel purchased from CNOOC Trading by the Vietnamese intermediary company. The Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal served as the transit hub for all nine documented 2023 and 2024 Vietnam-transited shipments to Myanmar, including the February 2024 shipment in which CNOOC Trading participated. Hai Linh is unsanctioned. See Profile 4.1. (AI Jul 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (IMO 9646479): The vessel that transported the February 2024 shipment from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon, arriving 29 February 2024. HUITONG 78 was subsequently renamed BARAAWE 1. Per the West of England P&I Club, the vessel is owned by an affiliate of Chinese state-owned defence company China Aerospace Science and Industry Corp (CASIC). See Profile 6.1. (AI Jul 2024)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited / Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal (Thilawa, Yangon): The destination terminal in Myanmar to which the February 2024 shipment was delivered. During early 2024 the terminal was operating under the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited before arriving at its current name. See Profile 1.3. (AI Jul 2024)
•Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd.: The Singapore-based privately owned trading company identified by Amnesty International as the second-to-last seller in the immediately preceding January 2024 shipment (row 18). Vietnamese customs data applied the identical "should have been aware" standard to Sahara Energy as to CNOOC Trading, making the two transactions the strongest documented cases of constructive awareness in the reviewed supply chain. The parallel treatment of both Singapore-based traders in consecutive 2024 shipments is directly relevant to any joint designation submissions. See Profile 5.3. (AI Jul 2024)
•Unnamed Vietnamese intermediary company: The direct purchaser from CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. in the February 2024 shipment. This company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal and then sold it to the Myanmar-side purchaser. Its identity is not disclosed in any reviewed source. (AI Jul 2024)

Sanctions gap: CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. has not been specifically sanctioned by any government, notwithstanding the July 2024 constructive awareness finding derived from Vietnamese customs data. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the primary legal basis for a potential US designation, covering any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy without requiring proof of actual knowledge. The parent CNOOC group entity appears on the OFAC non-SDN Consolidated Sanctions List (from August 2021) and the US DoD Section 1260H Chinese military companies list (from June 2021); CNOOC International Trading Co., Ltd., a separate China-incorporated CNOOC group subsidiary, is named as a distinct entity on the January 2025 1260H list. None of these designations constitute a specific designation of CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. A joint OFAC submission covering both CNOOC Trading (Singapore) and Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd., based on Vietnamese customs data applying the same evidentiary standard to both, is the most coherent advocacy approach. The Chinese state-ownership of CNOOC Trading creates significant diplomatic complexity for any Western government considering designation. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

5
Recommended Actions
•Submit a joint designation referral for CNOOC Trading and Sahara Energy to OFAC and other jurisdictions. Both were identified in consecutive January and February 2024 shipments with identical "should have been aware" findings. A joint submission reinforces the systematic pattern. Any OFAC submission should identify CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. by ACRA number 200412252W and Singapore address to distinguish it clearly from the China-incorporated entity on the 1260H list. The August 2023 sector determination provides the legal authority. UK, EU, Canada, and Australia should be assessed in parallel. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage CNOOC Trading and raise the matter diplomatically with Beijing. Because CNOOC Trading is a state-owned entity, civil society engagement with the Singapore subsidiary should be accompanied by diplomatic engagement. Governments with Myanmar sanctions programmes should raise the documented CNOOC group infrastructure in the Myanmar supply chain with the Chinese government. CNOOC Trading's Singapore management should simultaneously receive a formal disclosure request asking whether it knew the fuel would reach Myanmar, what due diligence it applied to the Vietnamese purchaser, and what controls it has since put in place. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Submit a referral to UK OTSI and EU competent authorities. The Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021 and equivalent EU frameworks cover entities making economic resources available to Myanmar's security forces. A referral should present the July 2024 Amnesty International findings and the constructive awareness standard from Vietnamese customs data. (AI Jul 2024)
