Tawan-Oil Trading
Profile 3.2
Summary Assessment
Tawan-Oil Trading is a Thailand-registered company identified by the UN Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in Myanmar as one of two Thailand-registered aviation fuel trading companies that together supplied all documented aviation fuel purchased by Myanmar military-connected entities through the international banking system in calendar year 2023. Based on confidential trade records reviewed by the Special Rapporteur, Tawan-Oil Trading received payments of more than USD 25 million from Intraco, a Myanmar-registered company established on behalf of NEPAS, and an unspecified portion of a combined more than USD 15 million paid by Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers, with Rich Ray Trading Company Limited as the proxy whose payments were directed to Tawan-Oil Trading's accounts at Kasikorn Bank. All payments from the Myanmar side were processed through United Amara Bank and received at Tawan-Oil Trading's accounts at Kasikorn Bank in Thailand. No corporate registration number, ownership details, individuals, or address for Tawan-Oil Trading appear in any source reviewed for this database. Tawan-Oil Trading has not been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026. The UN Special Rapporteur explicitly calls for governments to holistically sanction trading companies providing aviation fuel to the Myanmar military, a category into which Tawan-Oil Trading squarely falls.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Tawan-Oil Trading is a Thailand-registered company that sold aviation fuel to Myanmar military-connected purchasers in calendar year 2023. It operated on the supply side of the transaction structure documented by the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 Banking on the Death Trade report, selling fuel to two Myanmar-registered proxy entities: Intraco, established on behalf of NEPAS, and Rich Ray Trading Company Limited, operating as a proxy for Asia Sun Group. Tawan-Oil Trading operated in parallel with CB Energy (Profile 3.1), the only other documented Thai seller in 2023. Together, CB Energy and Tawan-Oil Trading accounted for all documented aviation fuel purchased by Myanmar military-connected entities through the international banking system in that year. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Sales to Intraco (NEPAS proxy)

Tawan-Oil Trading's largest documented customer in 2023 was Intraco, a Myanmar-registered company confirmed by the UN Special Rapporteur as having been established on behalf of NEPAS on 20 September 2023. Evidence from Myanmar's corporate directory indicates that Intraco subsequently changed its name to International Green Agrotrade Company Limited. NEPAS is the joint venture between the junta-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (Singapore) that holds Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence. The UNSR's Figure 7 transaction table, based on confidential trade records, documents that Intraco paid more than USD 25 million to Tawan-Oil Trading in calendar year 2023. Payments were processed from Myanmar through United Amara Bank to Tawan-Oil Trading's accounts at Kasikorn Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Sales to Rich Ray Trading (Asia Sun Group proxy)

Tawan-Oil Trading also sold aviation fuel to Rich Ray Trading Company Limited, a Myanmar-registered company described by the UN Special Rapporteur as an Asia Sun Group proxy purchaser of aviation fuel. Rich Ray Trading, together with Swan Energy Company Limited and Myan-Oil Company Limited, formed the Asia Sun Group's parallel proxy purchasing structure alongside NEPAS's own direct and proxy procurement. The combined payments from Swan Energy, Myan-Oil, and Rich Ray Trading to Thai sellers in 2023 totalled more than USD 15 million. Within that combined figure, Rich Ray Trading purchased from Tawan-Oil Trading with payments processed through United Amara Bank to Kasikorn Bank. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil purchased from CB Energy (Profile 3.1). No per-entity breakdown for Rich Ray Trading's individual payment to Tawan-Oil Trading is provided in the source record. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Thailand's position in the supply chain

The UNSR's June 2024 report documented a structural shift in Myanmar's military procurement from Singapore-registered companies to Thailand-registered companies between fiscal year 2022 and fiscal year 2023. Thai-registered companies went from supplying more than USD 60 million in military goods of all types in the UNSR's fiscal year 2022 (April 2022 to March 2023) to more than USD 120 million in fiscal year 2023 (April 2023 to March 2024), a figure encompassing weapons, military equipment, and aviation fuel. This shift was driven in part by the collapse of Singapore-based supply following a Singaporean government investigation. Tawan-Oil Trading is one of the two Thailand-registered aviation fuel trading companies identified by the UNSR as the primary sellers in 2023, alongside CB Energy. The UNSR noted that Thailand has no explicit public policy opposing weapons or fuel transfers to Myanmar, in contrast to Singapore. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The due diligence failure: The UN Special Rapporteur noted that the Thai banks processing these transactions, including Kasikorn Bank, were among the 16 banks that facilitated SAC military procurement in FY2023 and held nostro accounts for Myanmar state banks. The UNSR observed that Swan Energy's own website publicly stated that it controlled 100 percent of Myanmar's aviation fuel market, meaning that any bank reviewing transactions to and from Myanmar aviation fuel purchasing entities should have identified the likely end-use without difficulty. Kasikorn Bank subsequently confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it had terminated its relationships with Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) following US sanctions; however, this confirmation addresses the bank's nostro account relationships with Myanmar state banks and does not establish that Tawan-Oil Trading's commercial accounts at Kasikorn Bank were also closed. The UNSR assessed that effective due diligence by Kasikorn Bank, including a review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchaser's connections to the aviation fuel trade. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The total value of aviation fuel purchased from all Thai sellers in 2023 was at least USD 80 million, a nearly 30 percent increase over 2022. Tawan-Oil Trading's documented share of this total comprises the more than USD 25 million received from Intraco and the portion of the combined more than USD 15 million Asia Sun Group proxy payments attributable to Rich Ray Trading's purchases. The exact amount paid by Rich Ray Trading to Tawan-Oil Trading is not separately specified in the source record. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Methodological note

The UNSR's report states that figures are rounded, not exact, to protect confidential sources. The evidence base for Tawan-Oil Trading's role therefore rests entirely on this single primary source. No corporate registry records, corporate filings, company website, shipping manifests, or other documentation relating to Tawan-Oil Trading appear in any source reviewed for this database. The UNSR confirmed its findings to Thailand's government in writing prior to publication. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Kasikorn Bank's role

Tawan-Oil Trading received payments at accounts held at Kasikorn Bank, a privately owned Thai commercial bank. The UNSR identified Kasikorn Bank as one of 16 banks that facilitated SAC military procurement in FY2023 and as a bank that held nostro accounts for both Myanmar Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB). Kasikorn Bank subsequently confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it had terminated its nostro account relationships with Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) following US sanctions in June 2023. This confirmation covers the bank's state-bank nostro relationships, not its commercial account relationships with Thai trading companies. The specific Tawan-Oil Trading accounts at Kasikorn Bank are identified in the UNSR's transaction table as the destination for payments from Myanmar originating at United Amara Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

Key Relationships
•Intraco / International Green Agrotrade Company Limited: Tawan-Oil Trading's largest documented customer in 2023. Intraco is a Myanmar-registered company confirmed by the UN Special Rapporteur as having been established on behalf of NEPAS on 20 September 2023. Evidence from Myanmar's corporate directory indicates Intraco subsequently changed its name to International Green Agrotrade Company Limited. Intraco paid more than USD 25 million to Tawan-Oil Trading in 2023, with payments processed through United Amara Bank to Kasikorn Bank. See Profile 1.2 for NEPAS and its proxy purchasing structure. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Rich Ray Trading Company Limited: An Asia Sun Group proxy purchaser confirmed as a buyer from Tawan-Oil Trading in 2023. Rich Ray Trading is documented in Figure 7 of the UNSR report as the Asia Sun Group proxy that purchased from Tawan-Oil Trading, as distinct from Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, which purchased from CB Energy. Rich Ray Trading's payments to Tawan-Oil Trading formed part of the combined more than USD 15 million in Asia Sun Group proxy purchases from Thai sellers in 2023. Rich Ray Trading was sanctioned by the UK in October 2024. See Profile 1.6. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•United Amara Bank (UAB): The Myanmar private bank through which all Myanmar-side payments to Tawan-Oil Trading were processed in 2023. UAB handled payments from both Intraco (on behalf of NEPAS) and Rich Ray Trading (on behalf of Asia Sun Group). The UN Special Rapporteur explicitly recommended that financial institutions freeze existing relationships with UAB. See Profile 8.1. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Kasikorn Bank Public Company Limited: The Thai bank at which Tawan-Oil Trading held the accounts into which all Myanmar payments were received in 2023. The UNSR identified Kasikorn Bank as one of 16 banks facilitating SAC military procurement in FY2023, confirmed it held nostro accounts for MFTB and MICB, and assessed that effective due diligence by the bank could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. Kasikorn Bank subsequently confirmed to the UNSR that it terminated its nostro account relationships with MFTB and MICB following US sanctions. This confirmation covers state-bank nostro relationships; no confirmation has been made regarding the commercial account relationship with Tawan-Oil Trading specifically. See Profile 8.5. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•CB Energy (Profile 3.1): The other Thailand-registered aviation fuel trading company documented in the UNSR's 2023 transaction table, operating in parallel with Tawan-Oil Trading. CB Energy supplied NEPAS directly, and also supplied Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, two Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers, receiving payments at Krung Thai Bank. Together, CB Energy and Tawan-Oil Trading accounted for all documented aviation fuel sold to Myanmar military-connected entities through the international banking system in 2023. See Profile 3.1. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Asia Sun Group Company Limited / Shoon Group: The parent conglomerate behind Rich Ray Trading, Tawan-Oil Trading's documented customer on the Asia Sun Group side of the 2023 transaction structure. Asia Sun Group is sanctioned by the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia. See Profile 1.4. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The Tawan-Oil Trading sanctions gap: Tawan-Oil Trading received more than USD 25 million directly from a NEPAS proxy in calendar year 2023 as documented by the UN Special Rapporteur's confidential trade record review. It operated alongside CB Energy as one of the only two Thailand-registered companies through which Myanmar's military-connected entities purchased all of their documented aviation fuel in 2023. Like CB Energy, it has no apparent physical infrastructure in Myanmar; its only documented Myanmar connection is through United Amara Bank, which processed payments from Myanmar to Tawan-Oil Trading's accounts at Kasikorn Bank. Tawan-Oil Trading has not been sanctioned by any government. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the legal authority in the United States to designate any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy. Tawan-Oil Trading's documented role in Figure 7 of the UNSR report provides the evidentiary basis for designation submissions across all five jurisdictions. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•23 August 2023. OFAC issues a determination under Executive Order 14014 that the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy is subject to sanctions, allowing the designation of any foreign person operating in that sector without requiring proof of direct military end-use. This determination is the primary legal basis for a potential US designation of Tawan-Oil Trading. (OFAC Aug 2023)
•27 October 2023. Canada enacts a ban on the provision of maritime shipping insurance for vessels transporting aviation fuel to Myanmar under Canada Gazette SOR/2023-228, which came into force on 27 October 2023. Canada is the first and, as of April 2026, the only jurisdiction to impose such a ban. The ban addresses the insurance dimension of the supply chain through which Tawan-Oil Trading's sales are shipped to Myanmar. (Canada Gazette SOR/2023-228)
•26 June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report names Tawan-Oil Trading in Figure 7 as the Thai seller receiving payments from Intraco (more than USD 25 million) and Rich Ray Trading (portion of combined more than USD 15 million in Asia Sun Group proxy purchases). The UNSR calls for governments to holistically sanction aviation fuel supply chain networks, including trading companies. Findings were communicated to Thailand's government in advance of publication. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•17 July 2024. Fair Finance Thailand publishes an open letter to five Thai banks named in the Banking on the Death Trade report, including Kasikorn Bank, calling for enhanced due diligence for all Myanmar-related transactions, transparent procedures for rejecting high-risk transactions, and closure of accounts held by Myanmar military-controlled banks. This is the first documented civil society action in Thailand directly targeting the banking pathway through which Tawan-Oil Trading received payments. (Fair Finance Thailand Jul 2024)
•July 2024. Thai banks are called before a parliamentary committee following publication of the Banking on the Death Trade report. The Thai government announces the formation of a task force to investigate Myanmar military procurement transactions through Thai banks, with the Bank of Thailand and the Anti-Money Laundering Office agreeing to establish the task force. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•29 October 2024. The UK designates Rich Ray Trading Company Limited (MYA0084) for making available aviation fuel to Myanmar's security forces. Rich Ray Trading is a confirmed buyer of Tawan-Oil Trading in calendar year 2023. (UK Oct 2024; JFM Oct 2024)
•May 2025. Justice for Myanmar's ASEAN companies report confirms Kasikorn Bank terminated its nostro account relationships with MFTB and MICB following US sanctions, consistent with the UNSR's findings. (JFM May 2025)
•February 2026. Justice for Myanmar calls for new and coordinated sanctions on the Myanmar junta's aviation fuel supply chain, naming MPE, Hai Linh Co. Ltd., and the ghost fleet vessels and their owners and managers, and describing the absence of sanctions on key supply chain entities as an ongoing gap. (JFM Feb 2026)

Recommended Actions
•Designate Tawan-Oil Trading in all five jurisdictions: Tawan-Oil Trading is the documented supplier of more than USD 25 million in aviation fuel directly to a NEPAS proxy in calendar year 2023, and is one of only two Thailand-registered companies that together supplied all documented Myanmar military aviation fuel purchases through the international banking system in 2023. For submissions to OFAC, the 23 August 2023 jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the legal authority: Tawan-Oil Trading is a company operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy, and no proof of direct military end-use is required. The UNSR's Figure 7 transaction table, based on confidential trade records with findings communicated to Thailand's government prior to publication, constitutes a substantial evidentiary basis for designation submissions to OFAC, UK OFSI, EU EEAS, Canadian Global Affairs and Australian DFAT. Coordinated designation alongside CB Energy across all five jurisdictions would maximise the disruptive effect and reduce the risk of either company shifting its banking pathway. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage Kasikorn Bank directly on the status of Tawan-Oil Trading's accounts: Kasikorn Bank confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it terminated its nostro account relationships with Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) following US sanctions. This confirmation addresses the bank's state-bank nostro relationships, not its commercial account relationships with Thai trading companies such as Tawan-Oil Trading. The UN Special Rapporteur documented that military procurement transactions through Kasikorn Bank fell from over USD 35 million in fiscal year 2022 to less than USD 5 million in fiscal year 2023 after the bank terminated its nostro account relationships with MFTB and MICB following US sanctions, demonstrating that Kasikorn's decisions have a measurable effect on the flow of funds to the Myanmar military. Civil society should engage Kasikorn Bank directly, referencing the UNSR's Figure 7 naming Tawan-Oil Trading as the recipient of aviation fuel payments from UAB at Kasikorn Bank, and requesting confirmation that those specific accounts have been closed and that the bank has filed any relevant Suspicious Transaction Reports with Thai financial intelligence authorities under the AML/CTPF measures mandated by the Anti-Money Laundering Office. This engagement should coordinate with Fair Finance Thailand's existing campaign. (UNSR Jun 2024; Fair Finance Thailand Jul 2024)
•Engage the Thai government on the conclusions of the July 2024 task force and compliance with the December 2024 joint directive: The task force established in July 2024 by the Bank of Thailand and the Anti-Money Laundering Office (AMLO) concluded its investigation and published its findings in December 2024. The Bank of Thailand and AMLO stated that some Thai financial institutions had conducted transactions with individuals named in the UNSR report, but that no evidence was found linking those transactions to arms procurement. The agencies noted that Thai financial institutions had "varying levels of rigor in their operations." In response, the AMLO and the Bank of Thailand issued a Joint Policy Statement on Sanctions-related Risks Oversight, effective 27 December 2024, requiring all Thai financial institutions to implement risk-proportionate enhanced due diligence on transactions connected to high-risk countries including Myanmar, with mandatory verification of Ultimate Beneficial Ownership and systems to assess and detect sanctions-related risks. Civil society should engage the Bank of Thailand and AMLO to press for confirmation that the December 2024 directive has been applied specifically to Thai companies operating in the jet fuel sector, including the account relationship between Kasikorn Bank and any entity identified in the UNSR's Figure 7 as a seller of aviation fuel to Myanmar-connected purchasers. The outcome of this engagement should be coordinated with Fair Finance Thailand. (UNSR Jun 2024; BOT Dec 2024)
•Hold the Bank of Thailand and AMLO accountable for enforcement of the December 2024 joint directive in respect of aviation fuel trade: The Bank of Thailand and AMLO's Joint Policy Statement of 27 December 2024 is the most comprehensive binding regulatory directive issued in Thailand in response to the Myanmar military procurement risk in the banking sector. The directive requires Thai financial institutions to implement UBO verification, risk-proportionate due diligence on high-risk country transactions, and systems to detect sanctions-related irregularities. However, the directive does not explicitly name the aviation fuel sector, Tawan-Oil Trading, CB Energy, or UAB, and no enforcement action has been publicly reported following its entry into force. The December 2024 joint directive, the FATF blacklisting of Myanmar since 21 October 2022, and the UN Special Rapporteur's explicit call to freeze UAB's international banking relationships together constitute a legal and regulatory framework under which Kasikorn Bank is already required to apply enhanced scrutiny to accounts connected to Myanmar-based entities including UAB. Civil society should formally request that the Bank of Thailand confirm whether enforcement of the December 2024 directive has been applied to the specific account relationships identified in the UNSR's Figure 7 transaction table, including accounts held at Kasikorn Bank by Thai aviation fuel sellers receiving payments from UAB. This engagement should be framed within Thailand's FATF obligations and coordinated with Fair Finance Thailand and international advocacy partners. (UNSR Jun 2024; BOT Dec 2024; FATF Oct 2022)
