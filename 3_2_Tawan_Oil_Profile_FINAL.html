Tawan-Oil Trading
Profile 3.2
Summary Assessment
Tawan-Oil Trading is a Thailand-registered company with no publicly available ownership, registration, or address. In calendar year 2023 it received over USD 25 million from Intraco, a NEPAS proxy, and a share of approximately USD 15 million from Rich Ray Trading, an Asia Sun Group proxy, all processed through United Amara Bank to its accounts at Kasikorn Bank. Together with CB Energy it accounted for all documented Myanmar military aviation fuel purchases through the international banking system in 2023. Tawan-Oil Trading has not been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Tawan-Oil sold aviation fuel to two Myanmar purchasers in 2023: Intraco, a proxy established on behalf of NEPAS on 20 September 2023 (subsequently renamed International Green Agrotrade Company Limited), and Rich Ray Trading, an Asia Sun Group proxy. The UNSR's Figure 7 records Intraco paying over USD 25 million to Tawan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading paying an unspecified portion of a combined USD 15 million paid by Asia Sun Group proxies to Thai sellers. All payments cleared through United Amara Bank to Kasikorn Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The UN Special Rapporteur found that a basic review of Myanmar corporate records by Kasikorn Bank would have been sufficient to identify the purchasers' connection to the aviation fuel trade. Swan Energy's own website claimed 100 percent of Myanmar's aviation fuel market, and any bank reviewing transactions connected to Myanmar aviation fuel purchasers was therefore on notice of the likely end-use. Kasikorn confirmed to the UNSR that it terminated its nostro account relationships with MFTB and MICB after US sanctions in June 2023, but that confirmation addresses state-bank relationships, not the commercial accounts through which Tawan-Oil received payments. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The total value of aviation fuel purchased from all Thai sellers in 2023 was at least USD 80 million, a nearly 30 percent increase over 2022. Tawan-Oil Trading's documented share of this total comprises the more than USD 25 million received from Intraco and the portion of the combined more than USD 15 million Asia Sun Group proxy payments attributable to Rich Ray Trading's purchases. The exact amount paid by Rich Ray Trading to Tawan-Oil Trading is not separately specified in the source record. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Kasikorn Bank's role

Tawan-Oil Trading received payments at accounts held at Kasikorn Bank, a privately owned Thai commercial bank. The UNSR identified Kasikorn Bank as one of 16 banks that facilitated SAC military procurement in FY2023 and as a bank that held nostro accounts for both Myanmar Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB). Kasikorn Bank subsequently confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it had terminated its nostro account relationships with Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Investment and Commercial Bank (MICB) following US sanctions in June 2023. This confirmation covers the bank's state-bank nostro relationships, not its commercial account relationships with Thai trading companies. The specific Tawan-Oil Trading accounts at Kasikorn Bank are identified in the UNSR's transaction table as the destination for payments from Myanmar originating at United Amara Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

3
Key Relationships
•Intraco / International Green Agrotrade Company Limited: Tawan-Oil Trading's largest documented customer in 2023. Intraco is a Myanmar-registered company confirmed by the UN Special Rapporteur as having been established on behalf of NEPAS on 20 September 2023. Evidence from Myanmar's corporate directory indicates Intraco subsequently changed its name to International Green Agrotrade Company Limited. Intraco paid more than USD 25 million to Tawan-Oil Trading in 2023, with payments processed through United Amara Bank to Kasikorn Bank. See Profile 1.2 for NEPAS and its proxy purchasing structure. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Rich Ray Trading Company Limited: An Asia Sun Group proxy purchaser confirmed as a buyer from Tawan-Oil Trading in 2023. Rich Ray Trading is documented in Figure 7 of the UNSR report as the Asia Sun Group proxy that purchased from Tawan-Oil Trading, as distinct from Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, which purchased from CB Energy. Rich Ray Trading's payments to Tawan-Oil Trading formed part of the combined more than USD 15 million in Asia Sun Group proxy purchases from Thai sellers in 2023. Rich Ray Trading was sanctioned by the UK in October 2024. See Profile 1.6. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•United Amara Bank (UAB): The Myanmar private bank through which all Myanmar-side payments to Tawan-Oil Trading were processed in 2023. UAB handled payments from both Intraco (on behalf of NEPAS) and Rich Ray Trading (on behalf of Asia Sun Group). The UN Special Rapporteur explicitly recommended that financial institutions freeze existing relationships with UAB. See Profile 8.1. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Kasikorn Bank Public Company Limited: The Thai bank at which Tawan-Oil Trading held the accounts into which all Myanmar payments were received in 2023. The UNSR identified Kasikorn Bank as one of 16 banks facilitating SAC military procurement in FY2023, confirmed it held nostro accounts for MFTB and MICB, and assessed that effective due diligence by the bank could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. Kasikorn Bank subsequently confirmed to the UNSR that it terminated its nostro account relationships with MFTB and MICB following US sanctions. This confirmation covers state-bank nostro relationships; no confirmation has been made regarding the commercial account relationship with Tawan-Oil Trading specifically. See Profile 8.5. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•CB Energy (Profile 3.1): The other Thailand-registered aviation fuel trading company documented in the UNSR's 2023 transaction table, operating in parallel with Tawan-Oil Trading. CB Energy supplied NEPAS directly, and also supplied Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, two Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers, receiving payments at Krung Thai Bank. Together, CB Energy and Tawan-Oil Trading accounted for all documented aviation fuel sold to Myanmar military-connected entities through the international banking system in 2023. See Profile 3.1. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Asia Sun Group Company Limited / Shoon Group: The parent conglomerate behind Rich Ray Trading, Tawan-Oil Trading's documented customer on the Asia Sun Group side of the 2023 transaction structure. Asia Sun Group is sanctioned by the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia. See Profile 1.4. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The Tawan-Oil Trading sanctions gap: Tawan-Oil Trading received more than USD 25 million directly from a NEPAS proxy in calendar year 2023 as documented by the UN Special Rapporteur's confidential trade record review. It operated alongside CB Energy as one of the only two Thailand-registered companies through which Myanmar's military-connected entities purchased all of their documented aviation fuel in 2023. Like CB Energy, it has no apparent physical infrastructure in Myanmar; its only documented Myanmar connection is through United Amara Bank, which processed payments from Myanmar to Tawan-Oil Trading's accounts at Kasikorn Bank. Tawan-Oil Trading has not been sanctioned by any government. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the legal authority in the United States to designate any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy. Tawan-Oil Trading's documented role in Figure 7 of the UNSR report provides the evidentiary basis for designation submissions across all five jurisdictions. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

6
Recommended Actions
•Designate Tawan-Oil Trading in all five jurisdictions alongside CB Energy. The UNSR's Figure 7 provides the evidentiary basis. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination removes the need to prove direct military end-use for the US submission. Coordinated designation of both Thai sellers simultaneously reduces the risk of either shifting its banking pathway. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage Kasikorn Bank directly on the status of Tawan-Oil Trading's accounts. Kasikorn's confirmation that it terminated state-bank nostro relationships after June 2023 US sanctions does not establish that the commercial accounts through which Tawan-Oil received payments have been closed. Civil society should formally request that Kasikorn confirm the status of those specific accounts, reference the UNSR's Figure 7 naming Tawan-Oil as the recipient of UAB payments, and ask whether Suspicious Transaction Reports were filed. This engagement should be coordinated with Fair Finance Thailand. (UNSR Jun 2024; Fair Finance Thailand Jul 2024)
•Press the Bank of Thailand and AMLO to apply the December 2024 directive specifically to the aviation fuel sector. The December 2024 joint directive requires enhanced due diligence on Myanmar-connected transactions but does not name the aviation fuel sector, Tawan-Oil Trading, or UAB. Civil society should formally request that the Bank of Thailand confirm whether enforcement has been applied to accounts identified in the UNSR's Figure 7 table, including Kasikorn accounts receiving payments from UAB. This request should reference Myanmar's FATF blacklisting since October 2022, which already requires enhanced due diligence on all Myanmar-connected transactions. (UNSR Jun 2024; BOT Dec 2024)
