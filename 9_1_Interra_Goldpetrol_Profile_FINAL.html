Interra Resources Ltd and Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc.
Profile 9.1
Summary Assessment
Interra Resources Ltd is a Singapore Exchange-listed petroleum company holding 60 percent of Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. (GJOC), which operates the Chauk and Yenangyaung oilfields in Magway Region under contracts with MOGE. From January 2021 to end of 2024, Goldpetrol supplied over three million barrels of oil to MOGE worth over USD 200 million, generating more than USD 72 million in revenues for Interra. All oil goes to MOGE, which transfers it to MPE for refining at Mann Thanbayarkan, where it is processed into aviation fuel and diesel for military use. The remaining 40 percent of Goldpetrol is held by North Petroleum International Co. Ltd., a subsidiary of China ZhenHua Oil, itself a subsidiary of NORINCO, the Chinese state arms conglomerate that also supplies weapons to the Myanmar military. Interra suspended SGX trading on 6 February 2025 following a Justice for Myanmar and Finance Uncovered investigation. A legal review subsequently found no breach of US or EU sanctions, though the full opinion has not been made public. SGX RegCo continues to review Interra's trading resumption proposal as of April 2026. Neither Interra nor Goldpetrol has been designated by any government.

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Every barrel Goldpetrol extracts is sold to MOGE under contractual obligation. MOGE transfers the crude to MPE, which refines it at Mann Thanbayarkan. A source with direct supply chain knowledge confirmed to Justice for Myanmar that Mann's petroleum products, including aviation turbine fuel, primarily supply military bases in Magway Region. Industry expert night-time light analysis cited in the JFM January 2025 investigation showed significant production increases at Mann from 2021 to mid-2023, consistent with junta statistics recording a 174 percent increase in domestic aviation turbine fuel output in early 2022. Junta statistics record that output reached two million gallons in 2021 to 2022. Mann sits between Magway Air Force Base and KaPaSa 13, a Tatmadaw gunpowder production facility, and leaked documents document military trucks transporting Jet A-1 from Mann to air bases at Meikhtila, Hmawbi, Nay Pyi Taw, Taungoo, and Monywa. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM May 2025; AI Nov 2022)

2
Contract Structure and Oil Payment Flows

The current IPRC was signed in April 2017 and expires in April 2028, predating the coup but running eleven years beyond it. The baseline payment is 39 percent of total production delivered in crude oil directly to MOGE; the remaining 61 percent is Goldpetrol's shareable production, from which the junta takes further taxes, royalties, domestic market obligation, and income taxes. MOGE pays Goldpetrol in USD at international market prices for its share. Goldpetrol signed its first Myanmar production contracts in 1996 under the previous military government. Interra told SGX that payments are made to Goldpetrol's bank in Myanmar, which it stated was not subject to any sanctions, without disclosing the bank's name. JFM noted the risk of payment channel shifts given the junta's pattern of moving transactions between banks to evade sanctions. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)

3
Production Volumes and Revenues

From January 2021 to end of 2024, Goldpetrol supplied over three million barrels of oil to MOGE worth over USD 200 million, generating more than USD 72 million in revenues for Interra Resources (JFM May 2025). Earlier figures from January 2025 covering January 2021 to end of 2023 showed over 2.3 million barrels and more than USD 55 million in revenues; the May 2025 figures supersede these. In 2022, Goldpetrol extracted approximately 800,000 barrels from both fields combined. Despite night-time light data showing Mann ceased emitting after mid-2023, Interra's FY2025 production figures show continued Goldpetrol output, with gross production of 753,730 barrels, down 13 percent from FY2024. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM May 2025; Interra FY2025)

4
Corporate Structure and Ownership

Interra channels its 60 percent Goldpetrol stake through Goldwater Company Limited, a wholly-owned British Virgin Islands holding company. Goldpetrol is registered in the Bahamas and operates in Myanmar as a branch office. The remaining 40 percent is held by North Petroleum International Co. Ltd., a subsidiary of China ZhenHua Oil Co. Ltd., itself a subsidiary of NORINCO. NORINCO is both a weapons supplier to the Myanmar military and a co-owner of Myanmar's primary crude-to-aviation-fuel supply chain. North Petroleum was a major Interra shareholder until at least September 2023. (JFM Jan 2025)

5
Designation Status

Neither Interra Resources nor Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company has been designated by the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Canada or Australia as of April 2026. As a Singapore-based company, Interra is not subject to western sanctions on Myanmar-related entities. No government has called for the designation of Interra Resources or Goldpetrol in connection with their documented role in the domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain, though Justice For Myanmar has called for coordinated international sanctions on MOGE, MPE and junta-controlled banks that facilitate Interra's transactions with MOGE. (JFM Jan 2025)

6
Key Relationships
•Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE): The junta-controlled state oil enterprise that is Goldpetrol's sole contractual counterparty and the mandatory purchaser of all oil extracted from Chauk and Yenangyaung. Designated by the EU on 21 February 2022, subject to a US financial services directive issued on 31 October 2023 (effective 15 December 2023), and added to Switzerland's SECO sanctions list on 11 March 2025. Interra acknowledges awareness that MOGE is a military junta-controlled entity. MOGE is the junta's single-biggest source of foreign revenue through fossil fuel sales. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE): The junta-controlled state enterprise responsible for refining, storage and distribution of domestically extracted oil. Receives Goldpetrol's crude oil from MOGE for processing at the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex, where it is refined into aviation fuel, diesel and other petroleum products that primarily supply military bases. Interra states it has no direct dealings with MPE. MPE has not been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM May 2025)
•North Petroleum International Co. Ltd. / NORINCO: The 40 percent co-owner of Goldpetrol through North Petroleum International, which is a subsidiary of China ZhenHua Oil Co. Ltd., itself a subsidiary of the Chinese state-owned arms conglomerate NORINCO. NORINCO is a major weapons supplier to the Myanmar military. The presence of a NORINCO subsidiary as co-owner of Goldpetrol creates a structural link between the same entity supplying weapons to the junta and the domestic oil supply chain supporting its military aviation. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) (Profile 8.4): Used by MOGE to pay Goldpetrol more than USD 11 million between February and May 2022, prior to US sanctions on MFTB in June 2023. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3): Justice For Myanmar flagged the risk that following US sanctions on MFTB, MOGE could route Goldpetrol payments through MEB, which was not sanctioned in any jurisdiction at the time of the January 2025 report. Interra acknowledged that MOGE payments are made through a Myanmar bank it stated was not subject to sanctions, but declined to identify the bank. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)

Advocacy Interventions to Date

•6 February 2025. Interra suspends trading on SGX. SGX RegCo requires Interra to respond to queries. JFM publishes a reply to Interra's initial response, challenging its claims. (JFM Feb 2025)

8
Recommended Actions
•Press Interra to cease oil supply to MOGE and wind down Goldpetrol operations. The IPRC expires in April 2028. Interra has acknowledged MOGE is junta-controlled and has constructive knowledge that oil supplied to MOGE reaches Mann and from there military bases. The contractual obligation to supply does not extinguish human rights responsibilities. Civil society, investors, and legal experts should maintain engagement with Interra's board and shareholders on exit, including by making clear that any legal opinion finding no current sanctions breach does not address the UN Guiding Principles obligations or the reputational and political risk of continued operation. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM May 2025)
•Designate Interra Resources and Goldpetrol under Myanmar sanctions in all five jurisdictions. JFM has formally called for designation. The evidentiary basis is the full chain from Goldpetrol extraction to MOGE delivery to Mann refining to military base supply, documented across AI Nov 2022, JFM Jan 2025, and JFM May 2025. For the US, the August 2023 OFAC sector determination is the most directly applicable legal authority. (JFM May 2025; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Press SGX RegCo to delist Interra Resources if trading resumption conditions are not met. JFM has called for delisting. SGX RegCo has the regulatory authority to require disclosure, impose conditions, and ultimately delist. Civil society should formally submit to SGX the full evidence chain connecting Interra's crude oil to Mann's aviation fuel output and military distribution, and request that trading not resume without full public disclosure of the bank receiving MOGE payments and an independent assessment of Interra's UNGP obligations. (JFM May 2025)
•Engage NORINCO and Chinese authorities on the dual arms-and-oil supply relationship. NORINCO both supplies weapons to the Myanmar military and, through North Petroleum, co-owns the primary domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain. This dual relationship should be raised diplomatically by governments engaging China on Myanmar. Institutional investors in NORINCO-connected entities should be alerted to the documented dual role. (JFM Jan 2025)
