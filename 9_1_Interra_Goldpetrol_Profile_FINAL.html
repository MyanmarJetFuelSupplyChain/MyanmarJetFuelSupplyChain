Interra Resources Ltd and Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc.
Profile 9.1
Summary Assessment
Interra Resources Ltd is a Singapore-listed petroleum exploration company that holds a 60 percent stake in Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. (GJOC), one of the few remaining onshore oil producers still operating in Myanmar since the February 2021 coup. GJOC operates the Chauk and Yenangyaung oil fields in Magway Region under Improved Petroleum Recovery Contracts (IPRCs) with Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE), a junta-controlled entity designated by the EU (21 February 2022), subject to a US financial services directive (31 October 2023), and added to Switzerland's SECO sanctions list (11 March 2025). From January 2021 to the end of 2024, Goldpetrol supplied over three million barrels of oil to MOGE, worth over USD 200 million, generating more than USD 72 million in reported revenues for Interra Resources. All oil extracted by Goldpetrol is delivered to MOGE, which transfers it to Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) for refining. According to a source with direct knowledge of the supply chain and consistent with the EITI Commodity Trading Report (2020), Goldpetrol's oil is transported along the Ayeyarwady River to the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex, where it is refined into petroleum products including aviation fuel and diesel that primarily supply military bases in Magway Region. Goldpetrol's oil thus forms part of the upstream domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain that sustains the Myanmar military's aerial campaign against civilians. The remaining 40 percent of GJOC is held by North Petroleum International Co. Ltd., a subsidiary of China ZhenHua Oil Co. Ltd., which is itself a subsidiary of NORINCO, a Chinese state-owned arms conglomerate that also supplies weapons to the Myanmar military. Interra Resources suspended trading on the Singapore Stock Exchange on 6 February 2025 following a Justice For Myanmar and Finance Uncovered investigation, and announced it would seek legal advice on potential sanctions exposure. A legal adviser subsequently found no breach of US or EU sanctions, though the full opinion has not been made public. Neither Interra Resources nor Goldpetrol has been designated by any government as of April 2026.

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Goldpetrol's role in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain arises from its position at the upstream end of the domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain. Under its IPRCs with MOGE, Goldpetrol is contractually obliged to sell all oil it produces to MOGE, the junta-controlled state oil enterprise designated by the EU (21 February 2022), subject to a US financial services directive (31 October 2023, effective 15 December 2023), and added to Switzerland's SECO sanctions list (11 March 2025). MOGE is the designated holder of oil and gas reserves in Myanmar. MOGE then transfers the crude oil to Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE), which controls the downstream petroleum sector including refining, storage and distribution. According to a source with direct knowledge of the Goldpetrol operation, and consistent with the EITI Commodity Trading Report (2020) which states that all oil produced from Chauk and Yenangyaung is supplied to Mann for domestic use, the oil is transported along the Ayeyarwady River by boat to the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex in Magway Region. (JFM Jan 2025)

The Mann refinery is located in an area of exceptional strategic importance along the Ayeyarwady River, between Magway air force base and KaPaSa 13, a Tatmadaw defence industry facility that produces priming materials and gunpowder for explosives. Magway is described as the centre of the military's domestic weapons production, which requires a significant supply of energy to operate. The junta-controlled energy ministry states that the Mann refinery has the capacity to produce various types of fuel, including aviation fuel. Two independent sources with direct knowledge of the supply chain confirm that the primary beneficiaries of petroleum products refined at Mann are military bases; fuel is also supplied to government departments and excess petroleum is auctioned to private companies by the junta. Domestic aviation turbine fuel production was 174 percent higher in the first quarter of 2022 than a year earlier, with output reaching two million gallons in the 2021 to 2022 period according to junta-controlled national statistics. Night-time light analysis by an industry expert confirms significant increases in Mann's activity from 2021 to mid-2023, while showing a significant decrease in activity at the No. 2 Chauk refinery over the same period, confirming Mann as the primary active destination for Goldpetrol's output during that period and the key source of the documented surge in domestic aviation fuel production. Mann stopped emitting night lights after mid-2023; this requires verification before drawing conclusions about the refinery's current operational status and Goldpetrol's current supply chain destination. Interra Resources has stated it has no dealings with MPE, noting that GJOC's contract is with MOGE, not MPE. For cross-reference with all documented jet fuel shipments, see the master shipment table. (JFM Jan 2025)

2
Contract Structure and Oil Payment Flows

Goldpetrol's oil is extracted under IPRCs with MOGE. The current contract was signed in April 2017 and expires in April 2028; Interra notes it was signed during the civilian government that the junta subsequently ousted. Goldpetrol signed its first production contracts with the previous military junta in 1996. The baseline payment for the Chauk and Yenangyaung fields under the IPRC was 39 percent of total production in the form of crude oil, paid directly to MOGE. The remaining 61 percent constituted Goldpetrol's shareable production, from which the junta also took taxes, royalties, domestic market obligations and a signature bonus. The combined effect is that the junta received more than half of total oil extracted before Goldpetrol received any cash compensation. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)

MOGE paid Goldpetrol more than USD 11 million through the junta-controlled Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) (Profile 8.4) between February and May 2022, according to leaked financial documents. The US sanctioned MFTB in June 2023 for facilitating the regime's profiteering from Burma's extractive industries and its use of foreign currency to procure arms and jet fuel abroad. Following the sanctions on MFTB, MOGE payments to GJOC were made through an undisclosed Myanmar bank that Interra stated was not subject to any sanctions; the company declined to name the bank. Justice For Myanmar noted the risk that MOGE could have switched to the unsanctioned Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3) to route payments. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)

3
Production Volumes and Revenues

The most current figures, reported by Justice For Myanmar in May 2025, show that from January 2021 to the end of 2024, Goldpetrol supplied over three million barrels of oil to MOGE, worth over USD 200 million in total, during which Interra Resources reported more than USD 72 million in revenues from its Myanmar operations. Earlier figures from the January 2025 report, covering January 2021 to end of 2023, showed over 2.3 million barrels delivered and more than USD 55 million in revenues; the May 2025 figures supersede these where they differ. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM May 2025)

In 2022, Goldpetrol extracted just under 855,000 barrels of oil, representing approximately 40 percent of Myanmar's entire production for that year. The value of Goldpetrol's production in 2022 reached more than USD 67 million, driven primarily by a surge in international oil prices. Interra Resources reported crude oil sales to MOGE of USD 24.5 million in 2022 and USD 19.1 million in 2023, substantially above the average annual profits of USD 12.9 million for the preceding five years. As of the end of 2023, Interra had close to 3.1 million barrels of proven reserves and as much as 12.27 million barrels of contingent resources, which at 2024 prices would be worth approximately USD 993.9 million. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)

In terms of field-level production, shareable oil production at Chauk and Yenangyaung increased 2.4 percent from 312,323 barrels in FY2022 to 319,909 barrels in FY2023, with Chauk accounting for approximately 75 percent and Yenangyaung for approximately 25 percent in both years. Production was suspended for approximately two months in early 2021 following the coup, driven by the civil disobedience movement, before Goldpetrol reached an agreement with MOGE to resume operations on 16 July 2021. New wells were drilled in 2021, 2022 and 2023, including six new wells completed in the first half of 2023. Interra's 2023 annual report noted that Myanmar operations had "maintained the strong momentum from the previous year" with oil production having "recovered to the FY2017 baseline level." (JFM Feb 2025)

4
Corporate Structure and Ownership

Interra Resources channels its 60 percent interest in Goldpetrol through Goldwater Company Limited (GCL), a wholly-owned British Virgin Islands holding company. Goldpetrol itself is registered in the Bahamas and operates in Myanmar as a branch office. Interra acquired GCL in July 2003, predating the current IPRC contract. The remaining 40 percent of Goldpetrol is held by North Petroleum International Co. Ltd., a subsidiary of China ZhenHua Oil Co. Ltd., which is itself a subsidiary of China North Industries Group Corporation (NORINCO). NORINCO is a Chinese state-owned arms conglomerate and a major weapons supplier to the Myanmar military. Interra has stated it has no direct dealings with NORINCO, noting that NORINCO acquired its interest in the Myanmar oil fields from another company. NORINCO did not respond to questions from Justice For Myanmar. (JFM Jan 2025)

Designation Status

Neither Interra Resources nor Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company has been designated by the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Canada or Australia as of April 2026. As a Singapore-based company, Interra is not subject to western sanctions on Myanmar-related entities. No government has called for the designation of Interra Resources or Goldpetrol in connection with their documented role in the domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain, though Justice For Myanmar has called for coordinated international sanctions on MOGE, MPE and junta-controlled banks that facilitate Interra's transactions with MOGE. (JFM Jan 2025)

Key Relationships
•Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE): The junta-controlled state oil enterprise that is Goldpetrol's sole contractual counterparty and the mandatory purchaser of all oil extracted from Chauk and Yenangyaung. Designated by the EU on 21 February 2022, subject to a US financial services directive issued on 31 October 2023 (effective 15 December 2023), and added to Switzerland's SECO sanctions list on 11 March 2025. Interra acknowledges awareness that MOGE is a military junta-controlled entity. MOGE is the junta's single-biggest source of foreign revenue through fossil fuel sales. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE): The junta-controlled state enterprise responsible for refining, storage and distribution of domestically extracted oil. Receives Goldpetrol's crude oil from MOGE for processing at the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex, where it is refined into aviation fuel, diesel and other petroleum products that primarily supply military bases. Interra states it has no direct dealings with MPE. MPE has not been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM May 2025)
•North Petroleum International Co. Ltd. / NORINCO: The 40 percent co-owner of Goldpetrol through North Petroleum International, which is a subsidiary of China ZhenHua Oil Co. Ltd., itself a subsidiary of the Chinese state-owned arms conglomerate NORINCO. NORINCO is a major weapons supplier to the Myanmar military. The presence of a NORINCO subsidiary as co-owner of Goldpetrol creates a structural link between the same entity supplying weapons to the junta and the domestic oil supply chain supporting its military aviation. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) (Profile 8.4): Used by MOGE to pay Goldpetrol more than USD 11 million between February and May 2022, prior to US sanctions on MFTB in June 2023. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3): Justice For Myanmar flagged the risk that following US sanctions on MFTB, MOGE could route Goldpetrol payments through MEB, which was not sanctioned in any jurisdiction at the time of the January 2025 report. Interra acknowledged that MOGE payments are made through a Myanmar bank it stated was not subject to sanctions, but declined to identify the bank. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
7
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•29 January 2025. Justice For Myanmar and Finance Uncovered published a joint investigation documenting Goldpetrol's supply of over 2.3 million barrels of oil to MOGE from January 2021 to end of 2023, generating more than USD 55 million in revenues for Interra Resources, and showing the oil's route to the Mann refinery where it is processed into aviation fuel and diesel for military use. JFM simultaneously made a whistleblower submission to SGX. Within two days of publication, Singapore Exchange Regulation contacted Interra with queries about its Myanmar operations. JFM called on Interra to immediately halt oil supply to the junta, on Singapore and SGX to take regulatory action, and on governments to impose coordinated sanctions on MOGE, MPE and junta-controlled banks. (JFM Jan 2025)
•6 February 2025. Singapore Exchange Regulation imposed a trading suspension on Interra Resources following regulatory queries about the company's Myanmar operations and their compliance with international sanctions. SGX RegCo's action represented the most direct regulatory intervention to date by a state-linked body specifically in response to the JFM investigation. (JFM Feb 2025)
•12 February 2025. Justice For Myanmar published a detailed rebuttal of Interra Resources' response to SGX queries, arguing that Interra had inadvertently confirmed many of the facts in the January 2025 investigation, that the company had both actual and constructive knowledge that oil supplied to the military junta would be used to commit war crimes, and that Interra had made misleading statements in its SGX response. JFM argued potential civil and criminal liability for Interra's senior leadership, identified Interra's failure to address human rights law obligations beyond narrow sanctions compliance, and cited the significance of CDM participation among Goldpetrol's own field staff as evidence of actual knowledge. (JFM Feb 2025)
•11 March 2025. Switzerland's State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) designated MOGE, implementing EU sanctions through the Embargo Act. This was the first time Switzerland had sanctioned MOGE, more than three years after the EU designation of 21 February 2022. The designation followed a sustained JFM advocacy campaign including a freedom of information request to SECO submitted on 23 March 2024, a JFM press release on 7 March 2025, and reporting by Tages-Anzeiger. JFM welcomed the designation and called on Canada, the United Kingdom and Australia to follow Switzerland and impose sanctions on MOGE. (Open source, March 2025)
•24 May 2025. Justice For Myanmar published Fuelling Atrocities, updating the total oil supply figure to over three million barrels worth more than USD 200 million (January 2021 to end 2024) and Myanmar revenues to more than USD 72 million. (JFM May 2025)
•June 2025. Dr. Felicity Gerry KC warned publicly, in response to questions from Myanmar Now, that Interra's continued listing on SGX could undermine Singapore's credibility and global reputation, and that SGX as a key financial institution had a responsibility to uphold human rights through stronger oversight. (Open source, June 2025)
•October 2025 to April 2026. On 29 October 2025, SGX RegCo abolished the entire SGX financial watch-list, a regulatory step that removed Interra's watch-list status but left the trading suspension, a separate instrument imposed for compliance reasons, intact. As of April 2026, SGX RegCo was still reviewing Interra's trading resumption proposal. The suspension remained in force while Goldpetrol's operations continued under the IPRC contract. (Open source, 2025 to 2026)
8
Recommended Actions
•Call on Interra Resources to cease oil supply to MOGE and wind down Goldpetrol operations: Interra Resources has acknowledged that MOGE is controlled by the military junta, that the current IPRC contract expires in April 2028, and that the company has constructive knowledge that oil supplied to the junta is used in military operations. The contractual obligation to supply oil to MOGE does not extinguish Interra's international human rights responsibilities. Civil society and legal experts should continue to engage Interra Resources, its board and its shareholders with the full scope of its legal and ethical obligations, including the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, and call on the company to suspend operations at Chauk and Yenangyaung pending a comprehensive legal and human rights review. Interra has stated its strategy is to exit Myanmar; civil society should hold the company to that commitment and press for an accelerated timeline that does not wait until the IPRC expires in April 2028. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•File regulatory complaints with SGX RegCo and MAS: Justice For Myanmar made a whistleblower submission to SGX in January 2025; civil society should supplement this with formal regulatory complaints to both Singapore Exchange Regulation (SGX RegCo) and the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS). Complaints to SGX RegCo should cite: Interra's documented transactions with MOGE, which is designated by the EU and Switzerland and subject to a US financial services directive; Interra's own admission that it knew MOGE was under junta control when it resumed and expanded operations; the ongoing failure to disclose the current payment bank despite JFM's identification of MEB as the probable successor to MFTB; the legal adviser's narrow sanctions-compliance framing that Dr. Felicity Gerry KC and others have criticised as failing to engage with international human rights law obligations; and the argument, advanced by JFM, that Interra's failure to disclose MOGE's EU designation status in its SGX listing disclosures may constitute an independent breach of listing obligations separate from any substantive sanctions violation. The trading suspension already in place is the most immediate regulatory lever: SGX RegCo should refuse to approve Interra's pending trading resumption proposal while the company continues to supply oil to MOGE, and should launch a full investigation into whether Interra's transactions constitute a violation of SGX's own listing rules. MAS should separately investigate whether Interra's payments to or through an undisclosed Myanmar bank implicate any sanctioned financial institution. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•Submit designation evidence to OFAC, UK OFSI, the EU, Canada, and Australia: Civil society should submit evidence packages to the sanctions authorities of the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia for consideration of designating Interra Resources Ltd and Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. under their respective Myanmar sanctions frameworks. Evidence should cite the fully documented post-coup supply of crude oil to MOGE; the oil's confirmed route to the Mann complex for refining into aviation fuel and diesel that primarily supplies military bases; the 174 percent surge in domestic aviation turbine fuel production in the first quarter of 2022 consistent with Mann's documented increased activity; and the UNSR's extensive documentation of the Myanmar military's use of aviation fuel in war crimes against civilians. For OFAC, two legal bases are available under EO 14014. First, Section 1(a)(i) as applied to the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy under the August 2023 sector determination: Goldpetrol's crude oil is extracted for, and delivered exclusively to, a supply chain whose documented endpoint is aviation fuel production at the Mann refinery for military use. Second, Section 1(a)(vii), which permits designation of persons acting for or on behalf of the military or security forces of Burma, given Goldpetrol's exclusive, contractually bound supply of crude oil to junta-controlled MOGE for use in the military's fuel supply chain. Civil society should note that submission of evidence does not guarantee designation, and that the current US political environment may affect OFAC's prioritisation of new Myanmar designations. The UK, EU, Canada and Australia offer parallel tracks whose designations are not contingent on US action. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025; JFM May 2025)
•Demand public disclosure of IPRC terms, the 2021 resumption agreement, the current payment bank, and the May 2025 legal opinion: Civil society should formally demand that Interra Resources publicly disclose: the complete text of the IPRCs; the nature and terms of the agreement it reached with MOGE to resume operations on 16 July 2021; the identity of the Myanmar bank currently used by MOGE to pay Goldpetrol; and the full legal opinion obtained in May 2025. Each of these refusals represents a specific gap in public accountability. The refusal to disclose the resumption agreement is particularly significant given that GJOC field staff had publicly joined the Civil Disobedience Movement and the junta had already committed widespread atrocities in Magway Region at the time the agreement was made, and Interra's own 2021 annual report confirmed the civil disobedience movement had not been removed when operations resumed. The refusal to name the payment bank is directly relevant to sanctions compliance by the bank providing the service and by Interra itself. SGX RegCo should require disclosure of the payment bank identity as a condition of any trading resumption approval. Independent legal experts should be given access to the full legal opinion to assess whether it adequately engages with international human rights law and the UN Guiding Principles. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•Engage institutional investors and ESG-rated financial institutions: Civil society should engage institutional investors and ESG-rated financial institutions with exposure to Interra Resources to press for immediate contract termination and divestment from Myanmar operations. Interra has acknowledged constructive knowledge that oil supplied to junta-controlled MOGE is used in the commission of war crimes. The pending trading resumption proposal before SGX RegCo creates a specific near-term decision point at which institutional investor pressure, publicly expressed, could strengthen the public and regulatory case against resumption while Interra continues to supply MOGE. Institutional engagement should draw on the UNSR's documentation of aviation fuel's role in the military's aerial campaign, the legal critique by Dr. Felicity Gerry KC of the narrow sanctions compliance opinion, and the UNSR's documentation that the aviation fuel supply chain supports airstrikes that have killed civilians and destroyed schools, hospitals and displacement camps. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM May 2025)
•Investigate current banking channel and call on all correspondent banks to act: Interra Resources refused to identify the Myanmar bank used by MOGE to pay Goldpetrol following the US sanctions on MFTB in June 2023. Justice For Myanmar identified MEB as the probable successor given the junta's documented shift of procurement transactions to MEB after MFTB's designation. This question has direct implications for sanctions compliance: if payments are being routed through MEB, the international correspondent banks holding nostro accounts for MEB could be implicated. Investigative journalists, financial intelligence units, SGX RegCo and MAS should require Interra to disclose the banking channel. Governments should investigate whether MOGE-to-Goldpetrol payments are being made through a sanctioned institution. Any bank identified as holding the relevant accounts should be engaged directly with the JFM evidence base and urged to terminate those accounts. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•Submit evidence to Singapore's Attorney General and Ministry of Home Affairs under Singapore AML and proceeds of crime law: Civil society should submit evidence to Singapore's Attorney General's Chambers and Ministry of Home Affairs for investigation of potential exposure under Singapore's anti-money laundering and proceeds of crime framework, including the Corruption, Drug Trafficking and Other Serious Crimes (Confiscation of Benefits) Act (CDSA) and MAS guidelines on AML/CFT. Interra's transactions with junta-controlled MOGE, which the EU, Switzerland and the US have taken restrictive measures against, create potential exposure under Singapore's domestic AML framework, which applies to proceeds of serious crimes regardless of where the predicate offence occurred. The undisclosed Myanmar bank facilitating MOGE-to-Goldpetrol payments may additionally be implicated. Singapore's credibility as a responsible financial centre following its 2023 crackdown on Myanmar arms-related transactions creates both a legal basis and a reputational incentive for Singapore authorities to investigate. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
