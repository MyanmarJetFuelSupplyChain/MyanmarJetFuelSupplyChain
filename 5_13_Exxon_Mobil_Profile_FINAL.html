Exxon Mobil Corporation
Profile 5.13
Summary Assessment
Exxon Mobil Corporation (NYSE: XOM) is a US-headquartered multinational integrated oil and gas company. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo investigation implicates ExxonMobil's Singapore refinery berth as the load point for one post-coup aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar: approximately 13,311 MT of Jet A-1 loaded at the ExxonMobil berth (OM5) on Jurong Island in June 2022 and delivered to the Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, aboard the Super Infinity (IMO 9341342), a vessel owned by Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10). Vessel tracking data places the Super Infinity at ExxonMobil's berth on 11 and 12 June 2022. ExxonMobil did not respond to multiple letters from Amnesty International during the pre-publication investigation. When it did respond, ExxonMobil did not deny that the shipment had departed from its refinery but stated only that any potential aviation fuel sale related to Myanmar would be conducted in accordance with applicable law. Amnesty International's findings implicate ExxonMobil in this shipment; the investigation does not confirm ExxonMobil as the supplier in the same manner as Chevron Corporation (Profile 5.11), which provided written confirmation of its involvement. ExxonMobil made no documented commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel sales and has not appeared in any of the four Amnesty International investigations into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain published after November 2022. Neither Exxon Mobil Corporation nor ExxonMobil Asia Pacific Pte. Ltd. has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar.

Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo investigation implicates ExxonMobil Asia Pacific Pte. Ltd.'s Jurong Island refinery as the load point for one post-coup aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar. The basis for this is vessel tracking data: the Super Infinity (IMO 9341342), a chemical and oil product tanker owned by Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10) and operated from the ExxonMobil berth (OM5) on Jurong Island, Singapore, on 11 and 12 June 2022. The vessel then offloaded approximately 13,311 MT of Jet A-1 at the PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, on or about 16 to 17 June 2022. See the master shipment table for the confirmed details of this shipment. (AI Nov 2022)

The evidential basis for implicating ExxonMobil is vessel tracking data, not documentary confirmation. This distinguishes ExxonMobil's position from that of Chevron Corporation (Profile 5.11), which provided written confirmation of its involvement in the February 2022 Super Hero shipment from the SRC terminal. Amnesty International's own language consistently uses the word "implicate" rather than "confirm" when describing ExxonMobil's connection to the June 2022 shipment. EMAPPL's Singapore complex comprises the former Mobil refinery at Pioneer Road, Jurong, and the former Esso refinery at Pulau Ayer Chawan, integrated as a single facility following the 1999 Exxon-Mobil merger. The complex produces jet fuel among its primary outputs, and ExxonMobil's product range includes aviation lubricants, jet oils, hydraulic fluids and greases marketed across the Asia-Pacific region. (AI Nov 2022)

The June 2022 shipment involved Jet A-1, the most widely used commercial aviation fuel worldwide. In Myanmar, the military uses Jet A-1 for the vast majority of its aircraft rather than a separately formulated military fuel. Amnesty International confirmed this through interviews with former Myanmar Air Force officers and leaked procurement documents obtained during the Deadly Cargo investigation. Because commercial Jet A-1 and military aviation fuel in Myanmar are the same product, commercial supply chains for aviation fuel directly supply the military's air strikes on civilians. This is the reason why the June 2022 ExxonMobil-implicated shipment is relevant to Myanmar's military operations, regardless of any civilian-use warranties provided by commercial counterparties. (AI Nov 2022)

Amnesty International sent multiple letters to ExxonMobil during its pre-publication investigation. ExxonMobil did not respond to any of these letters. Following publication of the Deadly Cargo report, ExxonMobil issued a statement that did not deny that the shipment had departed from its refinery: the company stated only that any potential sale of aviation fuel related to Myanmar would be conducted in accordance with applicable law. ExxonMobil did not address whether it had supplied the June 2022 shipment, did not disclose the identity of any customer, and made no commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel sales. (AI Nov 2022)

ExxonMobil has not been identified in any of the four Amnesty International investigations into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain published after November 2022. The supply chain documented in post-2022 investigations shifted progressively toward Vietnamese intermediary storage routes and subsequently toward Iran-linked ghost ships and AIS manipulation, moving away from the direct-load model under which the June 2022 shipment from the ExxonMobil berth occurred. (AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)

Key Relationships
•Super Infinity (IMO 9341342) and Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10): The Super Infinity is the vessel whose tracking data implicates ExxonMobil's Jurong Island berth as the load point for the June 2022 Myanmar shipment. Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. is the beneficial owner and commercial manager of the Super Infinity; POS Maritime UC S.A. is the registered owner. The Super Infinity is a chemical and oil product tanker, built 2009, flag of Panama, 14,999 DWT, 150 metres LOA. Vessel tracking places the Super Infinity at ExxonMobil's berth OM5 on 11 and 12 June 2022 before its departure for Thilawa. Pan Ocean committed following the Deadly Cargo investigation that it would no longer allow its vessels to transport aviation fuel to Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
•PEAS terminal, Thilawa / NEPAS (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The PEAS terminal at Thilawa was the delivery point for the June 2022 shipment. At the time of delivery, the terminal was operated by Puma Energy through the National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS) joint venture, the principal aviation fuel importer for Myanmar's military. Puma Energy completed the sale of the terminal to the Shoon Group in December 2022. The terminal now operates under the Shoon Energy and Myan Oil Terminal network. See the master shipment table and Profiles 5.1 and 2.1 for the NEPAS and PEAS terminal network. (AI Nov 2022)
•Chevron Corporation (Profile 5.11): ExxonMobil's Jurong Island refinery and Chevron's Singapore Refining Company (SRC) stake are both located on Jurong Island and are the two Jurong Island load points implicated or confirmed in the 2022 Myanmar aviation fuel shipments documented in the Deadly Cargo investigation. The evidential basis differs: Chevron provided written confirmation of its involvement in the February 2022 Super Hero shipment; ExxonMobil's involvement in the June 2022 Super Infinity shipment rests on vessel tracking data alone, and ExxonMobil neither confirmed nor denied the shipment. Neither company has been designated by any government in connection with Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022)

Advocacy Interventions
•11 to 12 June 2022. Vessel tracking data places the Super Infinity (IMO 9341342), owned by Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10), at ExxonMobil berth OM5 on Jurong Island, Singapore. This is the evidential basis for Amnesty International's finding that ExxonMobil's Jurong Island refinery was the load point for this shipment. (AI Nov 2022)
•16 to 17 June 2022. The Super Infinity offloads approximately 13,311 MT of Jet A-1 at the PEAS terminal, Thilawa, Yangon. See the master shipment table. (AI Nov 2022)
•Pre-November 2022. Amnesty International sends multiple letters to ExxonMobil during its pre-publication investigation into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. ExxonMobil does not respond to any of these letters prior to publication. (AI Nov 2022)
•3 November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo report, produced in research collaboration with Justice for Myanmar, is published. It documents the vessel tracking evidence linking the Super Infinity's departure from ExxonMobil's Jurong Island berth to the June 2022 delivery at the PEAS terminal. Following publication, ExxonMobil issues a statement that does not deny the shipment departed from its refinery, stating that any potential aviation fuel sale related to Myanmar would be conducted in accordance with applicable law. ExxonMobil does not confirm it was the supplier, does not identify any customer, and makes no commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel sales. Unlike Thai Oil (Profile 5.12) and Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar (Profile 6.9), which had committed to cease before publication, and Pan Ocean (Profile 6.10), which committed after publication, ExxonMobil makes no documented cessation commitment at any stage. (AI Nov 2022)
•23 August 2023. OFAC issues a determination under Executive Order 14014 that the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy falls within the scope of Burma-related sanctions, creating legal authority to designate any foreign person operating in that sector without requiring proof of direct military end-use. The June 2022 ExxonMobil-implicated shipment predated this determination; any future shipment from the ExxonMobil berth to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain would take place in a materially different legal environment.
•March 2023, January 2024, July 2024 and 27 January 2026. Four subsequent Amnesty International investigations document Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. ExxonMobil does not appear in any of these investigations. The supply chain has shifted toward Vietnamese intermediary storage routes and Iran-linked ghost ships. ExxonMobil's Jurong Island berth has not been identified as a load point in any documented post-2022 Myanmar aviation fuel shipment. (AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Engage ExxonMobil directly to obtain a response to the vessel tracking evidence and a formal statement of its current Myanmar aviation fuel policy: ExxonMobil did not respond to Amnesty International's multiple pre-publication letters and has not addressed the evidential basis for its implication in the June 2022 shipment in any reviewed source. Its post-publication statement neither confirmed nor denied the shipment and contained no disclosure of due diligence practices, customer identity, or future supply policy. Civil society should engage ExxonMobil's compliance and human rights teams to request a formal response to the vessel tracking evidence, disclosure of any customer or charterer arrangement associated with the June 2022 shipment, and a public statement of ExxonMobil's current policy regarding aviation fuel sales to Myanmar or to any entity operating in Myanmar's military-linked aviation fuel supply chain. The August 2023 OFAC jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the current legal frame: any future documented supply from ExxonMobil's Jurong Island berth to Myanmar's jet fuel sector would expose ExxonMobil to designation under BURMA-EO14014 without requiring proof of direct military end-use. (AI Nov 2022)
•Monitor ExxonMobil's Jurong Island berth activity and engage institutional investors if further supply to Myanmar is documented: ExxonMobil has not been identified as a load point in any post-2022 documented Myanmar aviation fuel shipment, and the supply chain documented in post-2022 investigations has shifted substantially toward Iranian-linked ghost ships with disabled AIS transponders and ship-to-ship transfers in international waters, making direct commercial loading at named refinery berths less likely in the near term. However, this shift is not irreversible: commercial suppliers could re-enter the chain if sanctions enforcement weakens or if demand conditions change, and any such re-entry by a named load point would represent a regression to a more transparent and legally exposed supply model. ExxonMobil made no cessation commitment and has not publicly addressed whether its customer screening has changed since the June 2022 shipment. Civil society should maintain monitoring of vessel movements at ExxonMobil's Jurong Island berths using shipping data tools and treat any departure of a vessel subsequently confirmed to have delivered aviation fuel to Myanmar as a matter for immediate engagement with ExxonMobil, referral to OFAC, and engagement with UK and Australian sanctions authorities. As a NYSE-listed company, ExxonMobil is also an appropriate target for institutional investor engagement on the adequacy of its due diligence for aviation fuel sales in Southeast Asia. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2026)
