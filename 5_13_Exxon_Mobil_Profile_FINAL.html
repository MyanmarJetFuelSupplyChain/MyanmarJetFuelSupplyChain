Exxon Mobil Corporation
Profile 5.13
Summary Assessment
ExxonMobil's Singapore berth (OM5) on Jurong Island is implicated by vessel tracking data as the load point for one post-coup Myanmar aviation fuel shipment: approximately 13,311 MT aboard the Super Infinity in June 2022, delivered to Thilawa. ExxonMobil did not respond to multiple pre-publication letters from Amnesty International. After publication it neither confirmed nor denied the shipment, stating only that any potential sale would be conducted in accordance with applicable law. It disclosed no customer, no due diligence approach, and made no commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel sales. This distinguishes it from Chevron, which confirmed its involvement in writing, and from Thai Oil, Wilhelmsen, and Pan Ocean, which each made cessation commitments. ExxonMobil has not appeared in any subsequent investigated shipment. It has not been sanctioned in connection with Myanmar.

Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Vessel tracking data places the Super Infinity (IMO 9341342), owned by Pan Ocean Co. Ltd., at ExxonMobil's Jurong Island berth OM5 on 11 and 12 June 2022. The vessel offloaded approximately 13,311 MT of Jet A-1 at Thilawa on 16 to 17 June 2022. Amnesty International uses the word "implicate" rather than "confirm" throughout; the evidentiary basis is vessel tracking, not documentary confirmation such as an invoice or written admission. ExxonMobil did not respond to multiple pre-publication letters. After publication it stated only that any potential sale would be conducted in accordance with applicable law, neither confirming nor denying the shipment, disclosing no customer, and making no cessation commitment. (AI Nov 2022)

The June 2022 shipment predated the August 2023 US OFAC jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014. As a US person, Exxon Mobil Corporation is directly subject to the Burma Sanctions Regulations (31 CFR Part 525) under Executive Order 14014; any future documented supply from ExxonMobil or its subsidiaries to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain would fall within this regulatory framework. No sanctions authority has taken action against ExxonMobil for the prior shipment, and ExxonMobil has not been identified as a supplier in any post-2022 investigation. (AI Nov 2022)

Key Relationships
•Super Infinity (IMO 9341342) and Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10): The Super Infinity is the vessel whose tracking data implicates ExxonMobil's Jurong Island berth as the load point for the June 2022 Myanmar shipment. Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. is the beneficial owner and commercial manager of the Super Infinity; POS Maritime UC S.A. is the registered owner. The Super Infinity is a chemical and oil product tanker, built 2009, flag of Panama, 14,999 DWT, 150 metres LOA. Vessel tracking places the Super Infinity at ExxonMobil's berth OM5 on 11 and 12 June 2022 before its departure for Thilawa. Pan Ocean committed following the Deadly Cargo investigation that it would no longer allow its vessels to transport aviation fuel to Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
•PEAS terminal, Thilawa / NEPAS (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The PEAS terminal at Thilawa was the delivery point for the June 2022 shipment. At the time of delivery, the terminal was operated by Puma Energy through the National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS) joint venture, the principal aviation fuel importer for Myanmar's military. Puma Energy completed the sale of the terminal to the Shoon Group in December 2022. The terminal now operates under the Shoon Energy and Myan Oil Terminal network. See the master shipment table and Profiles 5.1 and 2.1 for the NEPAS and PEAS terminal network. (AI Nov 2022)
•Chevron Corporation (Profile 5.11): ExxonMobil's Jurong Island refinery and Chevron's Singapore Refining Company (SRC) stake are both located on Jurong Island and are the two Jurong Island load points implicated or confirmed in the 2022 Myanmar aviation fuel shipments documented in the Deadly Cargo investigation. The evidential basis differs: Chevron provided written confirmation of its involvement in the February 2022 Super Hero shipment; ExxonMobil's involvement in the June 2022 Super Infinity shipment rests on vessel tracking data alone, and ExxonMobil neither confirmed nor denied the shipment. Neither company has been designated by any government in connection with Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022)
4
Advocacy Interventions
•Pre-November 2022. Amnesty International sends multiple letters to ExxonMobil during its pre-publication investigation. ExxonMobil does not respond to any of them. (AI Nov 2022)
•3 November 2022. Deadly Cargo is published, documenting the vessel tracking evidence. ExxonMobil issues a statement that does not confirm or deny the shipment and makes no cessation commitment. Unlike Thai Oil, Wilhelmsen, and Pan Ocean, no documented commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel supply is made at any stage. (AI Nov 2022)
•23 August 2023. OFAC issues the jet fuel sector determination under EO 14014. Any future documented ExxonMobil supply to Myanmar's jet fuel supply chain would now be subject to designation authority without proof of military end-use. (OFAC Aug 2023)
5
Recommended Actions
•Engage ExxonMobil directly to obtain a response to the vessel tracking evidence and a formal statement of its Myanmar aviation fuel policy. ExxonMobil has never responded to the specific evidential basis for its implication and has made no public disclosure of its due diligence approach, customer identity, or current supply policy. Civil society should request a formal response to the vessel tracking evidence, disclosure of any customer or charterer arrangement for the June 2022 shipment, and a public commitment not to supply aviation fuel to Myanmar's military or any entity in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination means any future documented supply would expose ExxonMobil to designation without proof of military end-use. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Submit the vessel tracking evidence to OFAC for a formal assessment of whether it meets the threshold for designation. ExxonMobil has not confirmed the shipment, which creates an evidentiary gap. However, OFAC is equipped to assess vessel tracking evidence and may be able to obtain additional information through intelligence channels. A formal referral requesting OFAC's assessment of whether the evidence meets the designation threshold under the Burma EO and sector determination would either produce a designation or a formal determination of insufficiency, both of which would advance the accountability record. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Aug 2023)
