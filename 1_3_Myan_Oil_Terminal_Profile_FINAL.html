Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited
Profile 1.3
Summary Assessment
Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited operates the petroleum products terminal at Thilawa Port, Yangon, where every documented international aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar since the coup has been offloaded. The terminal was built by Puma Energy between 2013 and 2017 at a cost of approximately USD 92 million and passed to the Asia Sun / Shoon Group network in December 2022. It is owned by P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (Singapore), which has been sanctioned by the United States, and co-managed with MPE. The terminal operating company itself has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. It is the sole large-scale aviation fuel import facility in Myanmar and without it no international shipment to Myanmar would be possible at its documented scale.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

The Thilawa terminal is the physical entry point for all imported aviation fuel into Myanmar. Vessels dock at the Thilawa berth, offload Jet A-1 into storage tanks, and fuel is loaded onto tanker trucks for distribution to NEPAS airport facilities and Myanmar Air Force bases. All 30 shipments documented since the coup were received here.

Puma Energy built the terminal between 2013 and 2017, investing approximately USD 92 million. It operated as Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS), a joint venture between Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd. (80 percent) and Asia Sun Energy Co. Ltd. (20 percent), with a storage capacity of 91,000 cubic metres including three Jet A-1 tanks of 7,000 m³ each. Soldiers arrived at Thilawa on the morning of the coup and have maintained a military presence there since. Puma briefly suspended operations, then resumed. Following a Human Rights Impact Assessment and sustained public pressure, Puma completed the sale of its Myanmar assets in December 2022. (AI Nov 2022)

Puma did not publicly disclose the buyer. Amnesty International established it was the Asia Sun / Shoon Group network. The terminal passed through the interim name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited before being registered under its current name, Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited. Its Singapore owner, P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd.), was sanctioned by the United States in August 2023. The terminal is co-managed with MPE. Every aviation fuel shipment since December 2022 has arrived at the same berth under the new ownership. (AI Mar 2023; JFM May 2025; OFAC Aug 2023)

An archived version of the terminal's corporate website stated it handled 100 percent of Myanmar's market for jet fuel. This confirmed position as the country's sole large-scale import facility means that designation of the terminal operating company would create legal exposure for every vessel operator, maritime insurer, flag state, and port service provider involved in any future delivery. (Reuters Jan 2026)

2
Evidence
Physical infrastructure documented by Amnesty International

Amnesty International's November 2022 investigation describes the terminal's physical layout and operational function in detail. Aviation fuel arriving by vessel at the Thilawa berth is stored in the terminal's tanks. It is then loaded onto tanker trucks at a loading bay within the terminal compound, and transported by road to NEPAS storage facilities at civilian airports across Myanmar and to Myanmar Air Force bases. Satellite imagery obtained by Amnesty International confirmed vessel activity at the Thilawa berth corresponding to the documented shipment dates. The main distributor of fuel from the PEAS/NEPAS network was identified as Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited, which operated the tanker truck fleet. (AI Nov 2022)

Eight shipments confirmed at the terminal, April 2021 to August 2022

Amnesty International documented eight separate shipments of aviation fuel offloaded at the Thilawa terminal between April 2021 and 25 August 2022, carried by seven tankers: the YU DONG, the SANTYA (later renamed VOLTA), the SWARNA MALA, the SYNERGY (formerly OCEAN LILY), the SUPER HERO (formerly STX HERO), the BHUREEMAS, and the SUPER INFINITY (formerly STX INFINITY). The confirmed suppliers for four of these shipments were PetroChina's Singapore Petroleum Company (December 2021), Rosneft (December 2021), Chevron Singapore (February 2022) and Thai Oil (June 2022). A fifth shipment in June 2022 was linked to ExxonMobil. Documents obtained by Amnesty International showed that the Thai Oil and SPC shipments were intended for Myanmar Air Force use. (AI Nov 2022)

Continued operation confirmed post-transfer, 2023 to 2025

All shipments documented by Amnesty International in January 2024 (seven shipments in 2023 via Vietnam: one by YIDA 8 and six by HUITONG 78) and July 2024 (two confirmed 2024 shipments, with a likely third) were offloaded at the Thilawa terminal. The January 2026 reports by Amnesty International and Reuters confirmed three additional 2024 shipments (two by HUITONG 78 and YONG SHENG 56 in July 2024, and one by REEF in October 2024), bringing the total confirmed 2024 shipments to six. Shipments in 2025 by REEF and NOBLE were also received at the Thilawa terminal, now operating under the name Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited and consigned to Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited as confirmed by Myanma Port Authority import data. The 2025 total documented by Amnesty International was 109,604 metric tonnes, representing a near-doubling over the 2024 figure of 64,562 metric tonnes. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)

Ownership confirmed through Singapore corporate records

JFM's May 2025 report confirms that P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd., Singapore, US-sanctioned) is the owner of Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited. JFM describes the terminal as operating major fuel storage tanks in Yangon. The corporate lineage from Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd. to P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. is confirmed in the same report, establishing the connection between the sanctioned Singapore holding company and the Myanmar terminal operating entity. Singapore corporate registry records confirm all three Shoon Group Singapore companies, including P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd., remain active despite sanctions as of May 2025. (JFM May 2025)

Terminal operational role: supporting evidence

Wilhelmsen Ships Service (WSS) Myanmar, a subsidiary of Norwegian maritime group Wilhelmsen, handled vessels and fuel at the PEAS terminal on arrival at port alongside PEAS itself. Between October 2020 and June 2021, Wilhelmsen Myanmar received at least MMK 15.7 million (approximately USD 7,500) to manage NEPAS and Puma shipments at the terminal. (AI Nov 2022)

The Myanma Port Authority ceased publishing berthing list information for ships transporting oil and gas products as of January 2022. Prior to that date, the berthing lists, published once or twice a week, included vessel name, cargo type, quantity or weight, and berth. This cessation explains why subsequent investigation relied on AIS tracking, satellite imagery and customs data to document shipments rather than official port records. (AI Nov 2022)

An archived website of the terminal's former corporate owner stated that it "handled 100% of Myanmar's market for jet fuel, which spoils easily and requires specialized storage and transport." This confirms the terminal's position as the country's sole large-scale aviation fuel import facility and establishes that its role as an exclusive entry point predates the coup. (Reuters Jan 2026)

Key Relationships
•P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd., Singapore): The Singapore-registered holding company that owns Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited. P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. is US-sanctioned under OFAC's August 2023 designation. It is owned by Khin Phyu Win, who is sanctioned by the US, UK and Canada. The corporate name change from Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd. to P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. mirrors the pattern of renaming across the Shoon Group following Puma Energy's exit and the onset of sanctions pressure. See Joint Profile 2.1. (JFM May 2025; OFAC Aug 2023)
•NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.): The joint venture between MPE and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. that holds Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence. NEPAS is the formal purchasing entity for imported aviation fuel; Myan Oil Terminal is the physical infrastructure through which NEPAS receives and stores that fuel. The two entities are operationally inseparable: NEPAS issues the purchase orders and the terminal receives the cargo. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022; JFM May 2025)
•Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE): The junta-controlled state enterprise that is NEPAS's joint venture partner and co-operates the aviation fuel distribution network with the Shoon Group. MPE's involvement gives the terminal and its operations the backing of state authority. See Profile 1.1. (AI Nov 2022)
•Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd., Singapore): The Singapore-registered holding company, sanctioned by the US, UK and Canada, which is the parent of NEPAS. Shoon Energy is the commercial entity through which the Shoon Group coordinates aviation fuel procurement; Myan Oil Terminal is the physical asset through which that fuel is received. See Joint Profile 2.1. (AI Mar 2023; JFM May 2025)
•Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited: The Asia Sun Group subsidiary that operated the tanker truck fleet distributing fuel from the Thilawa terminal to NEPAS airport storage facilities and Myanmar Air Force bases. Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics is sanctioned by the US, Canada and Australia. Cargo Link Company Limited (its parent) is sanctioned by the UK and Canada. See Joint Profile 1.7. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy / Trafigura (exited): The Singapore-registered, Geneva-based multinational that built the terminal and operated it from 2014 to 2022 through the PEAS joint venture. Puma Energy held 80 percent of PEAS at incorporation; local company Asia Sun Energy held 20 percent. Three days after the coup, Puma bought out Asia Sun Energy's stake, making PEAS wholly owned by Puma until the December 2022 sale. Puma did not publicly disclose the identity of the buyer when it exited. Puma Energy is majority-owned by global commodity trading group Trafigura (approximately 92 percent at the time of the report). Advocacy groups called on Puma to disclose the identity of the buyer and to take steps to prevent the terminal's use for military aviation fuel supply. See Reference Entry (Puma Energy / Trafigura). (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1, YONG SHENG 56 / LS MERCURY, REEF, NOBLE and other vessels (2021 to 2025): All vessels documented delivering aviation fuel to Myanmar in the database berthed at the Thilawa terminal. The terminal's ability to receive product tankers of the size used in the documented shipments is a necessary precondition for the entire import supply chain. See Group 6 vessel profiles. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2024; AI Jan 2026)

The terminal sanctions gap: Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited is the single physical infrastructure asset through which every documented international aviation fuel shipment has passed. Sanctioning the three Singapore holding companies that control it (P.E.I. Energy, PEIA, Shoon Energy, of which P.E.I. Energy is US-sanctioned only) without sanctioning the terminal operating company itself leaves the Myanmar-registered legal entity free to continue receiving fuel deliveries. A direct designation of Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited would create legal exposure for any vessel operator, insurer, or port service provider handling shipments to the Thilawa berth, regardless of which ownership layer sits above it. JFM called in February 2026 for sanctions on MPE and vessel owners as the most direct intervention targets; the terminal operating entity itself has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Nov 2022)
Note on Canada / Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.: Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. does not appear as a named entry in Canada's Special Economic Measures (Burma) Regulations (SEMA) sanctions list. Canada is marked as sanctioned here because Canada's SEMA regulations include Asia Sun Group Company Limited, of which Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. is a subsidiary and affiliated company. Under Canadian sanctions law, entities owned or controlled by a designated person or entity are subject to the same prohibitions as the designated entity. Canada's October 2023 aviation fuel ban (SOR/2023-228, effective 31 October 2023) also prohibits the export, sale, supply and shipment of aviation fuel, and the provision of shipping insurance, to Myanmar, a prohibition that applies to all actors in the supply chain regardless of individual designation status. Advocates should verify the precise legal basis for any enforcement action under Canadian law.

6
Recommended Actions
•Designate Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited in all five jurisdictions. Designation of the terminal operating entity would create legal exposure for vessel operators, flag states, maritime insurers, and port service providers engaged with any shipment destined for the Thilawa berth, regardless of the ownership layer above. The operational link between the terminal and Myanmar Air Force fuel supply is documented through Amnesty International's satellite imagery, distribution records, and delivery data to air bases. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination provides the legal authority for a US designation without requiring proof of direct military end-use. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Press UK and EU to adopt Canada's maritime insurance ban. Canada's ban on shipping insurance for vessels transporting aviation fuel to Myanmar is the only measure that directly targets the vessel-to-terminal link. The UK and EU should be urged to adopt equivalent bans. P&I insurers known to have provided cover for vessels calling at Thilawa include UK P&I, Steamship Mutual, Japan P&I, QBE, Britannia, Shipowners' Club, and the West of England P&I Club. A terminal designation, combined with an insurance ban, would significantly constrain future deliveries. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jul 2024)
•Engage Singapore on P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd.'s continued activity. Singapore's corporate registry confirms P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. remains active despite US sanctions. Singapore's Ministry of Finance and Monetary Authority should be pressed to enforce compliance with OFAC designations affecting Singapore-registered entities and to consider domestic designation under Singapore's own targeted financial sanctions framework. (JFM May 2025)
