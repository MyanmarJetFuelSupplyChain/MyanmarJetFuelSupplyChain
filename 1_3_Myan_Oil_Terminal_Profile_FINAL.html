Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited
Profile 1.3
Summary Assessment
Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited is the operator of Myanmar's largest petroleum products terminal, located at Thilawa Port, Yangon. The terminal was built from 2014 to 2017 under the name Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS) as a joint venture between Puma Energy (80%) and Asia Sun Energy (20%), representing an investment of approximately USD 92 million and providing 91,000 cubic metres of storage capacity. Three days after the February 2021 coup, Puma bought out Asia Sun Energy's stake, making PEAS wholly owned by Puma until the December 2022 sale. Following Puma Energy's exit from Myanmar in December 2022, the terminal passed into the ownership of P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (Singapore, formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd.), a Singapore-registered holding company owned by Khin Phyu Win and sanctioned by the United States. The company is also referred to in advocacy sources under its interim name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited, though Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited is confirmed as the current name by JFM May 2025. Every international aviation fuel shipment documented in this database between February 2021 and the present was offloaded at this terminal. The terminal operating company itself has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. Its continued operation as the sole large-scale aviation fuel import terminal in Myanmar makes it the single point of entry for every documented international aviation fuel shipment into Myanmar.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited operates Myanmar's largest petroleum products terminal at Thilawa Port in Yangon. It is the physical infrastructure through which every documented international aviation fuel shipment has been received since April 2021. Vessels carrying Jet A-1 aviation fuel dock at the Thilawa berth, offload their cargo into the terminal's storage tanks, and the fuel is then loaded onto tanker trucks for distribution to NEPAS storage facilities, military air bases and civilian airports across the country. Without access to this terminal, no international aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar would be possible at its current scale.

Establishment and construction under Puma Energy

In June 2013, Puma Energy was awarded a tender to lease Plot 3 of the Thilawa area port by Myanmar's Ministry of Transport and the Myanma Port Authority, to construct and operate storage tanks for petroleum products. Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS) was incorporated on 25 June 2014 as a joint venture between Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd. (80%) and local company Asia Sun Energy Co. Ltd. (20%). The joint venture constructed Myanmar's largest fuel storage terminal at Thilawa Port at a cost of approximately USD 92 million. The terminal opened in May 2017 with a storage capacity of 91,000 cubic metres, including three tanks dedicated to Jet A-1 aviation fuel at 7,000 m³ each. Through PEAS and its linked joint venture NEPAS, Puma Energy operated as Myanmar's principal aviation fuel supplier from 2015 until its exit in December 2022. (AI Nov 2022)

Three days after the military coup, on 3 February 2021, Puma Energy bought out Asia Sun Energy Co. Ltd.'s 20 percent stake in PEAS, making PEAS wholly owned by Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd. from that date until the December 2022 sale. On the morning of the coup itself, soldiers arrived at Thilawa port and did not let anyone leave. The military has maintained a presence at the Thilawa terminal since that day. On 10 February 2021, Puma Energy announced suspension of operations to protect the safety and security of employees and partners, but shortly thereafter resumed operations. (AI Nov 2022)

Transfer to Shoon Group ownership following Puma's exit

On 5 October 2022, Puma Energy signed an agreement to sell its stake in PEAS and its minority share in NEPAS to a locally owned private company, which it did not publicly identify. The sale completed in December 2022. Amnesty International established that the buyer was Shoon Energy (formerly Asia Sun Aviation), a Singapore-registered entity owned by Khin Phyu Win and part of the Asia Sun Group. Management of the Thilawa terminal passed to a joint venture between Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Co. Ltd. and the junta-controlled state enterprise MPE. JFM's May 2025 report confirms P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd., Singapore), owned by Khin Phyu Win, as the terminal's Singapore-registered owner. The terminal entity was renamed, passing through the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited before arriving at its current registered name, Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited. (AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; JFM May 2025; Puma Energy Dec 2022)

Function as the offloading point for all documented shipments

Amnesty International's November 2022 investigation documented that the PEAS terminal at Thilawa is the entry point for all imported aviation fuel into Myanmar. Between February 2021 and 17 September 2022, at least seven oil tankers offloaded eight shipments of aviation fuel at the Thilawa terminal berth. From the terminal, Jet A-1 was loaded onto tanker trucks operated by Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics and distributed to NEPAS storage facilities at civilian airports and directly to Myanmar Air Force bases. The same offloading function continued under the terminal's new ownership following the December 2022 transfer, with subsequent shipments documented arriving at Thilawa through 2023, 2024 and 2025. All 30 shipments recorded in this database's shipment table were received at this terminal. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)

The naming chain: The terminal has operated under three names. It was built and operated as Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS) from 2014 to 2022. Following Puma's exit and the transfer to Asia Sun / Shoon Group ownership, sources variously refer to it as Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited (used in some advocacy and media sources, including JFM press releases from 2023) and Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited, confirmed as the current registered name by JFM's May 2025 report. The UNSR June 2024 report references the terminal by its physical location (Thilawa) without using a company name. Amnesty International's January 2024 report confirms the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited as the entity managing the terminal following the ownership change (describing it as formerly Asia Sun Aviation). For consistency, this profile uses the current name throughout and refers to the historical name PEAS where chronologically appropriate.
2
Evidence
Physical infrastructure documented by Amnesty International

Amnesty International's November 2022 investigation describes the terminal's physical layout and operational function in detail. Aviation fuel arriving by vessel at the Thilawa berth is stored in the terminal's tanks. It is then loaded onto tanker trucks at a loading bay within the terminal compound, and transported by road to NEPAS storage facilities at civilian airports across Myanmar and to Myanmar Air Force bases. Satellite imagery obtained by Amnesty International confirmed vessel activity at the Thilawa berth corresponding to the documented shipment dates. The main distributor of fuel from the PEAS/NEPAS network was identified as Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited, which operated the tanker truck fleet. (AI Nov 2022)

Eight shipments confirmed at the terminal, April 2021 to August 2022

Amnesty International documented eight separate shipments of aviation fuel offloaded at the Thilawa terminal between April 2021 and 25 August 2022, carried by seven tankers: the YU DONG, the SANTYA (later renamed VOLTA), the SWARNA MALA, the SYNERGY (formerly OCEAN LILY), the SUPER HERO (formerly STX HERO), the BHUREEMAS, and the SUPER INFINITY (formerly STX INFINITY). The confirmed suppliers for four of these shipments were PetroChina's Singapore Petroleum Company (December 2021), Rosneft (December 2021), Chevron Singapore (February 2022) and Thai Oil (June 2022). A fifth shipment in June 2022 was linked to ExxonMobil. Documents obtained by Amnesty International showed that the Thai Oil and SPC shipments were intended for Myanmar Air Force use. (AI Nov 2022)

Continued operation confirmed post-transfer, 2023 to 2025

All shipments documented by Amnesty International in January 2024 (seven shipments in 2023 via Vietnam: one by YIDA 8 and six by HUITONG 78) and July 2024 (two confirmed 2024 shipments, with a likely third) were offloaded at the Thilawa terminal. The January 2026 reports by Amnesty International and Reuters confirmed three additional 2024 shipments (two by HUITONG 78 and YONG SHENG 56 in July 2024, and one by REEF in October 2024), bringing the total confirmed 2024 shipments to six. Shipments in 2025 by REEF and NOBLE were also received at the Thilawa terminal, now operating under the name Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited and consigned to Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited as confirmed by Myanma Port Authority import data. The 2025 total documented by Amnesty International was 109,604 metric tonnes, representing a near-doubling over the 2024 figure of 64,562 metric tonnes. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)

Ownership confirmed through Singapore corporate records

JFM's May 2025 report confirms that P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd., Singapore, US-sanctioned) is the owner of Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited. JFM describes the terminal as operating major fuel storage tanks in Yangon. The corporate lineage from Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd. to P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. is confirmed in the same report, establishing the connection between the sanctioned Singapore holding company and the Myanmar terminal operating entity. Singapore corporate registry records confirm all three Shoon Group Singapore companies, including P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd., remain active despite sanctions as of May 2025. (JFM May 2025)

Terminal operational role: supporting evidence

Wilhelmsen Ships Service (WSS) Myanmar, a subsidiary of Norwegian maritime group Wilhelmsen, handled vessels and fuel at the PEAS terminal on arrival at port alongside PEAS itself. Between October 2020 and June 2021, Wilhelmsen Myanmar received at least MMK 15.7 million (approximately USD 7,500) to manage NEPAS and Puma shipments at the terminal. (AI Nov 2022)

The Myanma Port Authority ceased publishing berthing list information for ships transporting oil and gas products as of January 2022. Prior to that date, the berthing lists, published once or twice a week, included vessel name, cargo type, quantity or weight, and berth. This cessation explains why subsequent investigation relied on AIS tracking, satellite imagery and customs data to document shipments rather than official port records. (AI Nov 2022)

An archived website of the terminal's former corporate owner stated that it "handled 100% of Myanmar's market for jet fuel, which spoils easily and requires specialized storage and transport." This confirms the terminal's position as the country's sole large-scale aviation fuel import facility and establishes that its role as an exclusive entry point predates the coup. (Reuters Jan 2026)

Key Relationships
•P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd., Singapore): The Singapore-registered holding company that owns Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited. P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. is US-sanctioned under OFAC's August 2023 designation. It is owned by Khin Phyu Win, who is sanctioned by the US, UK and Canada. The corporate name change from Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd. to P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. mirrors the pattern of renaming across the Shoon Group following Puma Energy's exit and the onset of sanctions pressure. See Joint Profile 2.1. (JFM May 2025; OFAC Aug 2023)
•NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.): The joint venture between MPE and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. that holds Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence. NEPAS is the formal purchasing entity for imported aviation fuel; Myan Oil Terminal is the physical infrastructure through which NEPAS receives and stores that fuel. The two entities are operationally inseparable: NEPAS issues the purchase orders and the terminal receives the cargo. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022; JFM May 2025)
•Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE): The junta-controlled state enterprise that is NEPAS's joint venture partner and co-operates the aviation fuel distribution network with the Shoon Group. MPE's involvement gives the terminal and its operations the backing of state authority. See Profile 1.1. (AI Nov 2022)
•Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd., Singapore): The Singapore-registered holding company, sanctioned by the US, UK and Canada, which is the parent of NEPAS. Shoon Energy is the commercial entity through which the Shoon Group coordinates aviation fuel procurement; Myan Oil Terminal is the physical asset through which that fuel is received. See Joint Profile 2.1. (AI Mar 2023; JFM May 2025)
•Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited: The Asia Sun Group subsidiary that operated the tanker truck fleet distributing fuel from the Thilawa terminal to NEPAS airport storage facilities and Myanmar Air Force bases. Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics is sanctioned by the US, Canada and Australia. Cargo Link Company Limited (its parent) is sanctioned by the UK and Canada. See Joint Profile 1.7. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy / Trafigura (exited): The Singapore-registered, Geneva-based multinational that built the terminal and operated it from 2014 to 2022 through the PEAS joint venture. Puma Energy held 80 percent of PEAS at incorporation; local company Asia Sun Energy held 20 percent. Three days after the coup, Puma bought out Asia Sun Energy's stake, making PEAS wholly owned by Puma until the December 2022 sale. Puma did not publicly disclose the identity of the buyer when it exited. Puma Energy is majority-owned by global commodity trading group Trafigura (approximately 92 percent at the time of the report). Advocacy groups called on Puma to disclose the identity of the buyer and to take steps to prevent the terminal's use for military aviation fuel supply. See Reference Entry (Puma Energy / Trafigura). (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1, YONG SHENG 56 / LS MERCURY, REEF, NOBLE and other vessels (2021 to 2025): All vessels documented delivering aviation fuel to Myanmar in the database berthed at the Thilawa terminal. The terminal's ability to receive product tankers of the size used in the documented shipments is a necessary precondition for the entire import supply chain. See Group 6 vessel profiles. (AI Nov 2022; AI Jan 2024; AI Jan 2026)

The terminal sanctions gap: Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited is the single physical infrastructure asset through which every documented international aviation fuel shipment has passed. Sanctioning the three Singapore holding companies that control it (P.E.I. Energy, PEIA, Shoon Energy, of which P.E.I. Energy is US-sanctioned only) without sanctioning the terminal operating company itself leaves the Myanmar-registered legal entity free to continue receiving fuel deliveries. A direct designation of Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited would create legal exposure for any vessel operator, insurer, or port service provider handling shipments to the Thilawa berth, regardless of which ownership layer sits above it. JFM called in February 2026 for sanctions on MPE and vessel owners as the most direct intervention targets; the terminal operating entity itself has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Nov 2022)
Note on Canada / Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.: Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. does not appear as a named entry in Canada's Special Economic Measures (Burma) Regulations (SEMA) sanctions list. Canada is marked as sanctioned here because Canada's SEMA regulations include Asia Sun Group Company Limited, of which Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. is a subsidiary and affiliated company. Under Canadian sanctions law, entities owned or controlled by a designated person or entity are subject to the same prohibitions as the designated entity. Canada's October 2023 aviation fuel ban (SOR/2023-228, effective 31 October 2023) also prohibits the export, sale, supply and shipment of aviation fuel, and the provision of shipping insurance, to Myanmar, a prohibition that applies to all actors in the supply chain regardless of individual designation status. Advocates should verify the precise legal basis for any enforcement action under Canadian law.

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•October 2022. Amnesty International and Justice for Myanmar respond to Puma Energy's announced exit. Both organisations call on Puma to disclose the identity of the buyer of its PEAS and NEPAS stakes, and urge governments to prevent the terminal from continuing to supply aviation fuel to the Myanmar military. Puma does not disclose the buyer publicly. (AI Oct 2022; JFM Oct 2022)
•November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo report documents the terminal's function in full, including satellite imagery of vessels at the Thilawa berth, the loading bay infrastructure and the tanker truck distribution network. The report calls for sanctions on the PEAS and NEPAS structure. No designation of the terminal operating entity follows. (AI Nov 2022)
•March 2023. Amnesty International, Global Witness and Burma Campaign UK confirm that Puma transferred its PEAS stake to a locally owned company (subsequently identified as the Asia Sun / Shoon Group network) and call for sanctions targeting the terminal. JFM notes that the terminal has been renamed Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited and that its Singapore owner P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. remains active. US designation of P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. follows in August 2023 under the OFAC jet fuel sector determination, but the terminal operating entity itself is not designated. (AI Mar 2023; JFM Mar 2023; OFAC Aug 2023)
•August 2023. OFAC designates P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (the Singapore owner of Myan Oil Terminal) alongside Khin Phyu Win, PEIA Pte. Ltd. and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. under the jet fuel sector determination. The Myanmar terminal operating company is not designated. JFM welcomes the designation but reiterates the call for sanctions on the terminal itself and on MPE. (OFAC Aug 2023; JFM Aug 2023)
•February 2026. JFM calls for sanctions on MPE as the entity that controls the aviation fuel supply chain and jointly owns businesses that import, store and distribute aviation fuel alongside the Myan Oil group (formerly Shoon and Asia Sun), alongside vessel owners and managers. The press release does not call for direct designation of the terminal operating company by name. No designation follows as of April 2026. (JFM Feb 2026)
6
Recommended Actions
•Designate Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited directly in all five jurisdictions: The terminal operating company is the physical chokepoint of the entire import supply chain. Its designation would create legal exposure for vessel operators, flag states, maritime insurers, port service providers and bunker suppliers engaged with any shipment destined for the Thilawa berth, regardless of the ownership layer above the terminal. Submissions to OFAC can rely on the documented operational link between the terminal and Myanmar Air Force fuel supply established by Amnesty International's satellite imagery and distribution records, combined with the documented distribution of jet fuel from NEPAS facilities to Myanmar Air Force bases. For UK OFSI, EU EEAS, Canadian Global Affairs and Australian DFAT, the same operational evidence base applies.
•Target vessel P&I insurers with reference to the terminal: Canada's ban on maritime shipping insurance for vessels transporting aviation fuel to Myanmar is the only jurisdiction-level measure that directly addresses the vessel-to-terminal link. Advocacy should press the UK and EU to adopt equivalent bans, citing the terminal's documented role as the sole large-scale offloading point. P&I insurers that have previously provided cover for vessels calling at Thilawa include UK P&I, Steamship Mutual, Japan P&I, QBE, Britannia and Shipowners' Club (documented for the 2021 to 2022 phase). Subsequent shipments have used the West of England P&I Club and others. Formal correspondence to these clubs referencing terminal designation, where achieved, would significantly strengthen the case for withdrawal of cover. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023; AI Jul 2024)
•Engage Singapore authorities on the continued activity of P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd.: Singapore's corporate registry confirms P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. remains active despite US sanctions. Advocacy should press Singapore's Ministry of Finance and the Monetary Authority of Singapore to enforce compliance with OFAC designations affecting Singapore-registered entities under US secondary sanctions risk frameworks, and to consider domestic designation of P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. under Singapore's own targeted financial sanctions regulations. JFM has noted that Singapore has thus far imposed no sanctions on the Myanmar junta or its associated companies. (JFM May 2025; JFM Aug 2023)
