Indian Oil Corporation Limited
Profile 5.7
Summary Assessment
Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL) is India's largest government-owned oil company, listed on the BSE and NSE and under the administrative control of India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas. Its connection to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply chain runs on two distinct tracks. Before the coup, IOCL shipped nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. and its parent Trafigura between 2017 and 2020. Puma Energy was Myanmar's main foreign aviation fuel supplier until its December 2022 exit, placing IOCL as a documented upstream contributor to that supply chain in the pre-coup period. After the coup, IOCL shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel directly to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel, with the majority of Servo-branded lubricant and fuel exports directed to Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM), a designated arms broker for Myanmar's military. IOCL does not appear in any of the thirty documented aviation fuel shipments to Thilawa. It has not been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Pre-coup upstream supply to Puma Energy (2017 to 2020)

Frontier Myanmar's January 2024 investigation, confirmed by Justice for Myanmar in March 2024, documented that IOCL supplied nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020. This falls entirely before the February 2021 coup. Puma Energy was the principal foreign entity in Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain through its NEPAS joint venture and PEAS terminal until its December 2022 exit. IOCL had no direct contractual relationship with Myanmar-based entities in the documented record; its transactions were with Puma's Singapore trading entity. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024; AI Nov 2022)

Post-coup direct Myanmar shipments

Since the coup, IOCL has shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel directly to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel. In the first eighteen months following the coup, the majority of shipments consisted of Servo-branded engine oil, motor spirits, lubricants, and brake fluids directed to MCM, a known arms broker designated by OFAC and the UK from 25 March 2022. Following Frontier Myanmar's January 2024 publication, IOCL's trade continued: Justice for Myanmar documented at least three additional shipments in late January 2024 and five more in February 2024. IOCL does not appear in any of the thirty documented aviation fuel deliveries to the Thilawa terminal. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

2
Key Relationships
•Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. (Singapore): The direct purchaser of jet fuel from IOCL in the 2017 to 2020 period. Puma Energy Supply and Trading is Puma Energy's Singapore-based petroleum products trading entity, distinct from the Myanmar-based joint venture entities (PEAS and NEPAS) through which Puma's Myanmar aviation fuel operations were conducted. Both Frontier Myanmar and JFM Mar 2024 identify Puma Energy Supply and Trading as the recipient of IOCL's fuel alongside Puma's parent Trafigura. See Profile 5.1. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. (Singapore): The parent company of Puma Energy and co-recipient with Puma Energy Supply and Trading of IOCL's jet fuel deliveries between 2017 and 2020. Trafigura held approximately 92% of Puma Energy during the relevant period. The JFM Mar 2024 investigation explicitly identifies IOCL's fuel supply as confirming Trafigura's role as a jet fuel supplier to Puma Energy's Myanmar operations in the pre-coup period. See Profile 5.1. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM): The primary confirmed recipient of IOCL's post-coup Servo brand fuel shipments. MCM is described by Justice For Myanmar as a known arms broker for the Myanmar military acting as a key source of weapons and commercial support for the junta. MCM was designated by OFAC under Executive Order 14014 from 25 March 2022 until its removal on 24 July 2025. MCM retains UK designation (MYA0043, asset freeze, from 25 March 2022) and Canada designation, both currently active as of April 2026. On 9 April 2025, the UK imposed an additional Director Disqualification Sanction on MCM under the Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021. MCM's chairman, Aung Hlaing Oo, was designated by OFAC from 25 March 2022 until 24 July 2025 and retains UK designation (MYA0042) and Canada designation as of April 2026. IOCL's post-coup fuel shipments to MCM occurred while MCM was an active OFAC-designated and UK-designated entity. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•IOCL Singapore Pte. Ltd.: IOCL's Singapore-registered subsidiary. The source record does not specify whether the jet fuel transactions with Puma Energy were conducted through IOCL's Singapore subsidiary or directly by the Indian parent entity. This distinction is relevant to any assessment of the applicable legal framework and jurisdiction for potential advocacy or disclosure requests. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, Government of India: The oversight ministry with direct administrative responsibility for IOCL as a Maharatna PSU. The Government of India directly holds 51.50% of IOCL's shares. Because IOCL is majority-owned by the Indian government, India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas is the primary addressee for diplomatic pressure targeting IOCL's Myanmar fuel supply relationships. (JFM Mar 2024)

Indian Oil Corporation Limited has not been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. Regarding the pre-coup period: IOCL's transactions with Puma Energy Supply and Trading and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020 predate both Myanmar's February 2021 coup and the post-coup international sanctions framework targeting Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain, and were commercial petroleum product sales conducted before any Myanmar aviation fuel sanctions regime existed. Regarding the post-coup period: IOCL's documented direct fuel shipments to Myanmar, including Servo brand products to MCM, occurred while MCM was an active OFAC-designated and UK-designated entity, a materially different evidentiary position from the pre-coup Puma Energy transactions. India has not joined the Myanmar sanctions coalition and has no documented policy restricting fuel exports to Myanmar. As a Maharatna PSU under the administrative control of India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, any engagement or advocacy targeting IOCL simultaneously engages the Government of India. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

5
Recommended Actions
•Request disclosure from IOCL on both documented tracks. On the pre-coup Puma relationship: the nature and volume of jet fuel supplied to Puma Energy Supply and Trading and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020, what end-use representations IOCL received, and what due diligence it applied. On the post-coup direct Myanmar shipments: the identity of all post-coup Myanmar recipients, what screening IOCL applied before shipping to MCM while MCM was designated by OFAC and the UK, and what steps it took after Frontier Myanmar's January 2024 publication. IOCL's BSE and NSE listing and its status as a Ministry of Petroleum PSU both provide grounds for this engagement. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Engage India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas. As IOCL's administrative authority, the Ministry is the appropriate state-level engagement point. Governments with Myanmar sanctions programmes should raise the Frontier Myanmar and JFM findings and press for end-use screening policies governing Indian state-owned enterprise fuel sales to purchasers connected to Myanmar's military or to designated entities. This is particularly important given that India has not joined any Myanmar sanctions coalition. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Flag IOCL's shipments to MCM to OFAC and UK OTSI for compliance review. IOCL shipped Servo-branded products to MCM after MCM was designated by OFAC and the UK on 25 March 2022. Whether these shipments constituted a sanctions violation depends on whether the products qualify as "goods" under the applicable Myanmar sanctions regulations and whether any exemptions apply. Civil society should refer the documented post-designation shipments to OFAC and UK OTSI for assessment, providing the Frontier Myanmar January 2024 trade data as the evidentiary basis. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
