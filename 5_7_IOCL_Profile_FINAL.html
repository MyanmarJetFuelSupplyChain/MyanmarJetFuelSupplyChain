Indian Oil Corporation Limited
Profile 5.7
Summary Assessment
Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL, also trading as IndianOil) is a Maharatna Public Sector Undertaking listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE: 530965) and National Stock Exchange of India (NSE: IOC), under the ownership and administrative control of India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas. It is the largest government-owned oil company in India. Frontier Myanmar's investigation published on 15 January 2024, confirmed by Justice For Myanmar on 27 March 2024, documented that IOCL sent nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. in Singapore and its parent Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. between 2017 and 2020. This period falls entirely before Myanmar's February 2021 military coup. Before Puma Energy's exit from Myanmar in 2022, Puma was Myanmar's military's main source of jet fuel, making IOCL a documented upstream contributor to that supply chain in the pre-coup period. Separately, since the February 2021 coup, IOCL has shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel directly to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel. In the first 18 months following the coup, many shipments consisted of engine oil, motor spirits, lubricants and brake fluids manufactured under the Servo brand, with the majority shipped to Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM), a known arms broker for the Myanmar military. IOCL does not appear in any of the thirty documented aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar and has not been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Indian Oil Corporation Limited was first identified in connection with Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain in Frontier Myanmar's investigation published on 15 January 2024, which documented that IOCL had shipped nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. in Singapore and its parent Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. between 2017 and 2020. Justice For Myanmar confirmed this finding in its investigation published on 27 March 2024 into India's role in facilitating aviation fuel supply to Myanmar's military, describing IOCL as a Public Sector Undertaking under India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas. Both investigations noted that before Puma Energy's exit from Myanmar in 2022, Puma was Myanmar's military's main source of jet fuel. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

IOCL's upstream pre-coup role in this supply chain is distinct from the load port connections documented for Profile 5.5 (Bangchak Corporation) and Profile 5.6 (Reliance Industries). The 2017 to 2020 period falls entirely before Myanmar's February 2021 military coup. IOCL supplied jet fuel commercially to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd., Puma Energy's Singapore-based petroleum trading entity, and to Puma's parent Trafigura. The downstream pathway from Puma Energy to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply was documented by Amnesty International in its November 2022 Deadly Cargo investigation, which established that Puma Energy was the main foreign business involved in handling, storage and distribution of aviation fuel in Myanmar through its NEPAS joint venture with the state-owned Myanmar Petrochemical Enterprise. IOCL's commercial transactions with Puma Energy in Singapore thus formed part of the upstream supply chain that ultimately supported Puma's Myanmar operations, though IOCL had no direct contractual relationship with Myanmar-based entities in the documented record. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024; AI Nov 2022)

Separately from the pre-coup Puma Energy supply relationship, Frontier Myanmar's investigation documented that since the February 2021 coup, IOCL has shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel directly to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel. In the first 18 months following the coup, many of the shipments consisted of engine oil, motor spirits, lubricants and brake fluids manufactured under the Servo brand, an IOCL oil and lubricant brand. The majority of the Servo exports were shipped to Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM), described by Justice For Myanmar as a known arms broker for the Myanmar military. At the time of these deliveries, MCM and its chairman Aung Hlaing Oo were designated by OFAC (from 25 March 2022) and the UK (from 25 March 2022). Justice For Myanmar's reporting confirmed that following Frontier Myanmar's publication on 15 January 2024, IOCL's trade with Myanmar continued, with at least three additional shipments documented in late January 2024 and five more in February 2024. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

IOCL does not appear in any of the thirty documented aviation fuel deliveries to the Thilawa terminal from February 2021 onwards. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

2
Key Relationships
•Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. (Singapore): The direct purchaser of jet fuel from IOCL in the 2017 to 2020 period. Puma Energy Supply and Trading is Puma Energy's Singapore-based petroleum products trading entity, distinct from the Myanmar-based joint venture entities (PEAS and NEPAS) through which Puma's Myanmar aviation fuel operations were conducted. Both Frontier Myanmar and JFM Mar 2024 identify Puma Energy Supply and Trading as the recipient of IOCL's fuel alongside Puma's parent Trafigura. See Profile 5.1. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. (Singapore): The parent company of Puma Energy and co-recipient with Puma Energy Supply and Trading of IOCL's jet fuel deliveries between 2017 and 2020. Trafigura held approximately 92% of Puma Energy during the relevant period. The JFM Mar 2024 investigation explicitly identifies IOCL's fuel supply as confirming Trafigura's role as a jet fuel supplier to Puma Energy's Myanmar operations in the pre-coup period. See Profile 5.1. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM): The primary confirmed recipient of IOCL's post-coup Servo brand fuel shipments. MCM is described by Justice For Myanmar as a known arms broker for the Myanmar military acting as a key source of weapons and commercial support for the junta. MCM was designated by OFAC under Executive Order 14014 from 25 March 2022 until its removal on 24 July 2025. MCM retains UK designation (MYA0043, asset freeze, from 25 March 2022) and Canada designation, both currently active as of April 2026. On 9 April 2025, the UK imposed an additional Director Disqualification Sanction on MCM under the Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021. MCM's chairman, Aung Hlaing Oo, was designated by OFAC from 25 March 2022 until 24 July 2025 and retains UK designation (MYA0042) and Canada designation as of April 2026. IOCL's post-coup fuel shipments to MCM occurred while MCM was an active OFAC-designated and UK-designated entity. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•IOCL Singapore Pte. Ltd.: IOCL's Singapore-registered subsidiary. The source record does not specify whether the jet fuel transactions with Puma Energy were conducted through IOCL's Singapore subsidiary or directly by the Indian parent entity. This distinction is relevant to any assessment of the applicable legal framework and jurisdiction for potential advocacy or disclosure requests. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, Government of India: The oversight ministry with direct administrative responsibility for IOCL as a Maharatna PSU. The Government of India directly holds 51.50% of IOCL's shares. Because IOCL is majority-owned by the Indian government, India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas is the primary addressee for diplomatic pressure targeting IOCL's Myanmar fuel supply relationships. (JFM Mar 2024)

Indian Oil Corporation Limited has not been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. Regarding the pre-coup period: IOCL's transactions with Puma Energy Supply and Trading and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020 predate both Myanmar's February 2021 coup and the post-coup international sanctions framework targeting Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain, and were commercial petroleum product sales conducted before any Myanmar aviation fuel sanctions regime existed. Regarding the post-coup period: IOCL's documented direct fuel shipments to Myanmar, including Servo brand products to MCM, occurred while MCM was an active OFAC-designated and UK-designated entity, a materially different evidentiary position from the pre-coup Puma Energy transactions. India has not joined the Myanmar sanctions coalition and has no documented policy restricting fuel exports to Myanmar. As a Maharatna PSU under the administrative control of India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, any engagement or advocacy targeting IOCL simultaneously engages the Government of India. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•15 January 2024. Frontier Myanmar publishes its investigation documenting that since the February 2021 coup IOCL has shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel, and that in the first 18 months following the coup many shipments of Servo brand engine oil, motor spirits, lubricants and brake fluids were directed primarily to the then-OFAC-designated and UK-designated MCM. The investigation also documents IOCL's pre-coup supply of nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020. (Frontier Jan 2024)
•27 March 2024. Justice For Myanmar publishes its investigation into India's role in facilitating aviation fuel supply to Myanmar's military, confirming Frontier Myanmar's findings on the Puma Energy and Trafigura jet fuel supply relationship and documenting that following Frontier Myanmar's reporting on 15 January 2024, IOCL's trade with Myanmar continued, with at least three additional shipments in late January 2024 and five more in February 2024. No advocacy contact with IOCL is recorded in either report, and no response from IOCL has been documented as of April 2026. (JFM Mar 2024)
5
Recommended Actions
•Request disclosure from IOCL regarding the full scope of its Myanmar-related fuel transactions: Civil society should engage IOCL directly, requesting disclosure covering both documented connections. On the pre-coup Puma Energy relationship: the nature and volume of jet fuel supplied to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020; whether IOCL Singapore Pte. Ltd. was the contracting entity or whether transactions were conducted by the Indian parent; what end-use representations IOCL received from Puma Energy; and what due diligence IOCL applied to buyers of bulk aviation fuel. On the post-coup direct Myanmar shipments: the identity of recipients of IOCL's post-coup fuel exports to Myanmar; what screening IOCL applied before shipping to MCM while MCM was OFAC- and UK-designated; and what steps, if any, IOCL took to cease or curtail its direct Myanmar fuel trade following Frontier Myanmar's reporting on 15 January 2024. As a BSE/NSE-listed company and a PSU accountable to the Government of India, IOCL has both corporate governance disclosure obligations and public interest accountability obligations that support this engagement. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Engage India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: As IOCL's administrative authority, India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas is the appropriate state-level engagement point. Civil society and governments with Myanmar sanctions programmes should engage the Ministry to raise the Frontier Myanmar and JFM Mar 2024 findings and to encourage the adoption of end-use screening policies for fuel sales by Indian state-owned enterprises to purchasers connected to Myanmar's military or to sanctioned entities. This engagement is particularly important given that India has not joined the Myanmar sanctions coalition. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Monitor IOCL's ongoing direct Myanmar fuel trade: The profile documents two distinct connections: pre-coup upstream supply to Puma Energy (2017-2020) and post-coup direct fuel shipments to Myanmar including to the then-designated MCM. JFM Mar 2024 confirmed that IOCL's direct Myanmar trade continued after Frontier Myanmar's 15 January 2024 publication, with at least three additional shipments in late January 2024 and five more in February 2024. IOCL does not appear in any reviewed investigation after March 2024. However, given the documented pattern of continued trade following public exposure, civil society and institutional investors should monitor shipping and trade data for any further IOCL fuel exports to Myanmar. If renewed or continuing direct Myanmar shipments are identified, or if any IOCL entity is identified in connection with a Myanmar aviation fuel shipment, that finding should trigger immediate re-engagement, formal disclosure requests and escalation to the Indian government. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
