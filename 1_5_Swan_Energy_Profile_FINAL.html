Swan Energy Company Limited
Profile 1.5
Summary Assessment
Swan Energy Company Limited is a Myanmar-registered Asia Sun Group proxy that purchased aviation fuel from Thai trading company CB Energy in 2023 as part of a combined approximately USD 15 million in Asia Sun Group proxy purchases. Swan Energy's own website claims it supplies 10,000 metric tonnes of aviation fuel to Myanmar per month, representing 100 percent of the market. Win Kyaw Kyaw Aung, sanctioned by the UK and Canada, is reportedly a co-owner and represented Swan Energy in a junta-linked petroleum committee. Canada and the UK designated Swan Energy in October 2024. It remains unsanctioned by the US, EU, and Australia.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Swan Energy is one of three Myanmar-registered Asia Sun Group proxy companies that purchased aviation fuel from Thai trading companies in 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur's 2024 report records Swan Energy and Myan-Oil as buying from CB Energy, with payments processed through United Amara Bank to Krung Thai Bank, as part of a combined total of approximately USD 15 million paid by all three proxies. No per-entity breakdown is given. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Swan Energy's own website (basc.com.mm, captured 30 May 2024) states it sells 10,000 metric tonnes of aviation fuel to Myanmar per month and holds 100 percent of the market. The UN Special Rapporteur cited this as evidence that the Thai banks handling the transactions were on notice of Swan Energy's role: a company publicly claiming 100 percent of Myanmar's aviation fuel market was not an obscure counterparty. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Win Kyaw Kyaw Aung, sanctioned by the UK and Canada as a director and former shareholder of Asia Sun Trading, is reportedly a co-owner of Swan Energy and represented it in a junta-linked petroleum committee chaired from October 2023 by former Lieutenant General Nyo Saw of Myanmar Economic Corporation. The company name derives from reported co-owner Sein Wan, a crony businessman with military ties who is related by marriage to a current junta minister. (Irrawaddy Jan 2025)

2
Evidence
UNSR transaction documentation, calendar year 2023

The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report documents Swan Energy's purchasing role from confidential trade records. Figure 7 of the report lists Swan Energy, Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading as the three Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers in calendar year 2023, with a combined total of approximately USD 15 million. No per-entity breakdown is provided. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil are shown as purchasing from CB Energy (Thailand), processed through United Amara Bank to CB Energy's account at Krung Thai Bank. Rich Ray Trading is shown as purchasing from Tawan-Oil Trading (Thailand), processed through United Amara Bank to Tawan-Oil's account at Kasikorn Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Market share claim from company website

The UN Special Rapporteur's report includes a screenshot (Figure 8) of Swan Energy's company website (basc.com.mm, captured 30 May 2024) showing the company's stated claim to sell 10,000 metric tonnes of aviation fuel per month and to hold 100 percent of the Myanmar aviation fuel market share. The UNSR noted: "Swan Energy's website openly states that it sells 10,000 metric tons of aviation fuel to Myanmar every month, which it claims is 100 percent of the Myanmar market share." This claim is sourced to the company itself. The profile treats this as evidence of the company's self-declared role; it is not independently verified against Port Authority import data. (UNSR Jun 2024)

UK designation basis

The UK designated Swan Energy Company Limited on 29 October 2024 on the basis that it is "associated with Asia Sun Trading Company Limited and by making available economic resources (aviation fuel) directly or indirectly to or for the benefit of the Myanmar security forces." This is the first official government designation that identifies Swan Energy as directly supplying aviation fuel to Myanmar's security forces. (UK Oct 2024)

Reported ownership and military connections

The Irrawaddy reported in January 2025 that the company name "Swan" derives from Sein Wan, a crony businessman from Mon State with long-standing ties to the Myanmar military who is related by marriage to a current junta minister. Win Kyaw Kyaw Aung, who is sanctioned by the UK (January 2023) and Canada (October 2024) as a director and former shareholder of Asia Sun Trading Company Limited, is also reportedly a co-owner of Swan Energy. Win Kyaw Kyaw Aung represented Swan Energy in a junta-linked petroleum committee chaired by former Lieutenant General Nyo Saw of MEC. These ownership details are drawn from Irrawaddy reporting and are described as reported rather than confirmed through corporate registry records. (Irrawaddy Jan 2025)

Key Relationships
•Asia Sun Group Company Limited / Shoon Group: Swan Energy's parent entity in the UNSR's 2023 transaction table. Swan Energy is described by the UK as associated with Asia Sun Trading Company Limited, part of the Asia Sun Group network. Asia Sun Group is sanctioned in all five jurisdictions. See Profile 1.4. (UNSR Jun 2024; UK Oct 2024)
•CB Energy (Thailand): Thailand-registered company from which Swan Energy and Myan-Oil purchased aviation fuel in 2023 as part of a combined Asia Sun Group proxy total of approximately USD 15 million, paid via United Amara Bank to CB Energy's account at Krung Thai Bank. CB Energy is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 3.1. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•United Amara Bank (UAB): The Myanmar private bank through which Swan Energy processed its aviation fuel purchase payments in 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur explicitly calls for UAB's international banking relationships to be frozen. Unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 8.1. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Win Kyaw Kyaw Aung: Reportedly a co-owner of Swan Energy; confirmed as having represented Swan Energy in a junta-linked petroleum committee in October 2023. Win Kyaw Kyaw Aung is sanctioned by the UK (January 2023) and Canada (October 2024) as a director and former shareholder of Asia Sun Trading Company Limited. (Irrawaddy Jan 2025; UK Jan 2023; JFM Oct 2024)
•Sein Wan: Reportedly the individual after whom Swan Energy is named and a reported co-owner. Described by the Irrawaddy as a crony businessman from Mon State with long-standing military ties who is related by marriage to a current junta minister. Not sanctioned in any jurisdiction as of April 2026. (Irrawaddy Jan 2025)
•Myan-Oil Company Limited and Rich Ray Trading Company Limited: The other Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers documented in the UNSR's 2023 transaction table alongside Swan Energy. Both sanctioned by the UK in October 2024. See Joint Profile 1.6. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Oct 2024)
•Myanmar Economic Corporation (MEC) committee: Win Kyaw Kyaw Aung represented Swan Energy in a junta-linked petroleum committee chaired by former Lieutenant General Nyo Saw, chairman of MEC. MEC is sanctioned by the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia. This committee link establishes a direct organisational connection between Swan Energy and the military's commercial apparatus. (Irrawaddy Jan 2025)

The Swan Energy sanctions gap: Swan Energy publicly claims 100 percent of the Myanmar aviation fuel market. It is documented in the UNSR's confidential trade records as a direct purchaser from a Thai supplier. It was unsanctioned in all five jurisdictions for nearly two years after the supply chain was publicly documented by Amnesty International (November 2022), and for four months after the UNSR specifically confirmed Swan Energy's purchasing role in confidential trade records (June 2024). It remains unsanctioned by the United States, European Union and Australia as of April 2026. Swan Energy falls squarely within the OFAC jet fuel sector determination of August 2023: any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy is susceptible to designation. Its own website constitutes public evidence of its operating role. The UNSR's transaction documentation provides the evidentiary basis for designation submissions. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•26 June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report documents Swan Energy as a proxy purchaser, publishes a screenshot of its 100 percent market share claim, and states it has not been sanctioned by any government. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•29 October 2024. Canada and the UK designate Swan Energy. The UK basis is association with Asia Sun Trading and making aviation fuel available to Myanmar's security forces. Swan Energy remains unsanctioned by the US, EU, and Australia. (UK Oct 2024; JFM Oct 2024)
•February 2026. Justice for Myanmar calls for coordinated sanctions on the supply chain. Swan Energy is not named explicitly. It remains unsanctioned by the US, EU, and Australia as of April 2026. (JFM Feb 2026)
6
Recommended Actions
•Designate Swan Energy in the US, EU, and Australia. Swan Energy's own website is public evidence of operating in Myanmar's jet fuel sector under the August 2023 OFAC sector determination. The UNSR's Figure 7 provides the transactional evidentiary basis. Canada and the UK have already acted; the remaining three jurisdictions should follow. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Investigate and designate Sein Wan. Sein Wan is the reported namesake and co-owner of Swan Energy with long-standing military ties. He has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. Civil society should request that sanctions authorities investigate his ownership stake as grounds for individual designation. (Irrawaddy Jan 2025)
•Notify CB Energy and Krung Thai Bank of the Swan Energy designations. Swan Energy's 100 percent market share claim means any counterparty handling its transactions was on notice of its role. Civil society should formally notify CB Energy and Krung Thai Bank of the October 2024 UK and Canada designations and request confirmation that all Swan Energy transactions have ceased. (UNSR Jun 2024)
