YONG SHENG 56 (now LS MERCURY)
Profile 6.3
Summary Assessment
The vessel known during its documented delivery as YONG SHENG 56, now renamed LS MERCURY, is a Chinese-flagged oil products tanker (IMO 9657507) that made one confirmed delivery of aviation fuel to Myanmar's military junta on 30-31 July 2024. The vessel loaded at the Fujairah Offshore Anchorage Area (FOAA) in the UAE, where AIS data places it between 15 and 29 June 2024, before travelling to the Myan Oil Terminal at Thilawa, Yangon. The delivery was documented solely by Amnesty International's January 2026 Ghost Ships investigation, which identified YONG SHENG 56 as one of four ghost fleet vessels involved in Myanmar aviation fuel deliveries from mid-2024 onward. The cargo volume was not confirmed in the source. The vessel made its single documented Myanmar delivery within days of HUITONG 78's final Myanmar delivery (12 July 2024), with both vessels having loaded at FOAA in overlapping timeframes. Legendsea Co. Ltd., domiciled in Vietnam, is the registered owner, and Allsea Management JSC, domiciled in Vietnam, is both the commercial manager and ISM manager, as identified by Justice For Myanmar in February 2026. Neither the vessel nor either of its managing entities has been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026, though Justice For Myanmar called for coordinated designations across five jurisdictions in February 2026.

Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation established through AIS data analysis that YONG SHENG 56 was at the Fujairah Offshore Anchorage Area (FOAA) between 15 and 29 June 2024, then travelled to Myanmar to offload. The cargo volume was not confirmed in the source. Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release characterises this as a delivery of aviation fuel. The vessel's AIS data and loading pattern at FOAA are consistent with those of HUITONG 78, which was also sighted at FOAA in an overlapping window (13-26 June 2024) before its final Myanmar delivery on 12 July 2024. The two vessels appear to have loaded at FOAA concurrently and departed for Myanmar within weeks of each other. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)

2
Ghost Fleet Context

Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation identified YONG SHENG 56 as one of four ghost fleet vessels responsible for delivering aviation fuel to Myanmar from mid-2024 onward. The other three are HUITONG 78 (now BARAAWE 1, IMO 9646479, Profile 6.1), REEF (IMO 9263382, Profile 6.5) and NOBLE (IMO 9162928, Profile 6.6). The four-vessel framing reflects a documented shift in the supply chain following the public exposure of HUITONG 78 in Amnesty International's January 2024 investigation: the supply chain broadened, with additional vessels loading at open-water anchorages rather than fixed terminals, before delivering to the Myan Oil Terminal at Thilawa. (AI Jan 2026)

Amnesty International documented the following ghost fleet behaviours in connection with this supply chain as a whole: turning off AIS transponders during loading and offloading; broadcasting false or manipulated vessel positions; repeatedly changing vessel name, flag or ownership; and loading fuel through open-water ship-to-ship transfers rather than in ports. YONG SHENG 56's loading at FOAA, an offshore bunkering and STS transfer area, is consistent with the ship-to-ship transfer methodology described for this supply chain. The source does not confirm AIS deactivation or spoofing specifically by YONG SHENG 56, though these behaviours are documented for HUITONG 78 and the later Iranian supply vessels. See Profile 7.2 for the FOAA anchorage area. (AI Jan 2026)

The vessel was renamed to LS MERCURY following the Amnesty International January 2026 exposure. Renaming after public identification is a documented ghost fleet evasion tactic and mirrors the renaming of HUITONG 78 to BARAAWE 1 following its exposure in the same investigation. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)

3
Ownership and Management

Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation did not identify the owner or manager of YONG SHENG 56. Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release, drawing on publicly available information, identified the following corporate entities in connection with LS MERCURY (formerly YONG SHENG 56): Legendsea Co. Ltd., domiciled in Vietnam, as the registered owner; and Allsea Management JSC, domiciled in Vietnam, as both the commercial manager and ISM manager. Both entities are Vietnamese-domiciled, which is notable given the broader Vietnamese dimension of the earlier 2023 supply chain routed through Hai Linh Co. Ltd.'s Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, though no direct connection between these entities has been established in any reviewed source. See Profile 6.4 for Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC. (JFM Feb 2026)

7
Recommended Actions
•Designate LS MERCURY (IMO 9657507) and its owner and manager across all five jurisdictions: Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 call for coordinated sanctions on the vessel, Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC remains unimplemented. The OFAC jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 and its equivalents in the UK, EU, Canada and Australia provide the applicable designation authority. Designation of the corporate entities alongside the vessel is necessary to prevent evasion through renaming and re-flagging; the vessel's renaming from YONG SHENG 56 to LS MERCURY after the January 2026 exposure demonstrates that this risk is already being acted upon. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026)
•Investigate the connection between YONG SHENG 56 and the broader ghost fleet coordination: The concurrent FOAA loading of YONG SHENG 56 and HUITONG 78 in June-July 2024, followed by deliveries to the same terminal within days of each other, raises the question of whether the two vessels were coordinated by a common operator or network. HUITONG 78 was owned by a CASIC affiliate during its deliveries (Profile 6.1). Whether YONG SHENG 56 shares any beneficial ownership connection with that network, or whether Legendsea and Allsea represent a separate and independent operation, has not been established. Investigators should seek shipping records, AIS path data and corporate registry information to trace whether a common coordinator existed. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage Vietnam on its domestic companies' roles as vessel owner and manager: Legendsea Co. Ltd. and Allsea Management JSC are both Vietnamese-domiciled entities. Vietnam has not designated any of the entities in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain. Civil society and governments should raise with Vietnamese authorities both the specific roles of Legendsea and Allsea in respect of LS MERCURY and the broader pattern of Vietnam-based entities serving as transshipment and management nodes in the supply chain, including Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1), which operated the Cai Mep terminal through which earlier deliveries by HUITONG 78 were routed. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2024)
