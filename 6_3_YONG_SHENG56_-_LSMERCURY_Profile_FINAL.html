YONG SHENG 56 (now LS MERCURY)
Profile 6.3
Summary Assessment
YONG SHENG 56 (IMO 9657507), now renamed LS MERCURY, made one confirmed aviation fuel delivery to Myanmar on 30 to 31 July 2024, loading at the FOAA anchorage in Fujairah, UAE, before offloading at the Myan Oil Terminal at Thilawa. The cargo volume was not confirmed. The vessel was documented by Amnesty International's January 2026 Ghost Ships investigation as one of four ghost fleet vessels delivering aviation fuel to Myanmar from mid-2024. It was renamed LS MERCURY following that exposure. Legendsea Co. Ltd. (Vietnam) is the registered owner; Allsea Management JSC (Vietnam) is both the commercial manager and ISM manager. Neither the vessel nor either company has been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026.

Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation established through AIS data analysis that YONG SHENG 56 was at the Fujairah Offshore Anchorage Area (FOAA) between 15 and 29 June 2024, then travelled to Myanmar to offload. The cargo volume was not confirmed in the source. Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release characterises this as a delivery of aviation fuel. The vessel's AIS data and loading pattern at FOAA are consistent with those of HUITONG 78, which was also sighted at FOAA in an overlapping window (13-26 June 2024) before its final Myanmar delivery on 12 July 2024. The two vessels appear to have loaded at FOAA concurrently and departed for Myanmar within weeks of each other. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)

2
Ghost Fleet Context

Amnesty International identified YONG SHENG 56 as one of four ghost fleet vessels in the Myanmar supply chain from mid-2024, alongside HUITONG 78 (now BARAAWE 1), REEF, and NOBLE. The four-vessel framing reflects the supply chain's expansion after HUITONG 78's public exposure in January 2024, with vessels now loading at open-water anchorages such as FOAA rather than fixed terminals. YONG SHENG 56 and HUITONG 78 were both sighted at FOAA in overlapping windows in June 2024 (YONG SHENG 56: 15 to 29 June; HUITONG 78: 13 to 26 June) and both delivered to Thilawa within weeks of each other, suggesting coordinated loading. The source does not confirm AIS deactivation or spoofing specifically for YONG SHENG 56, though these behaviours are documented for HUITONG 78 and the Iranian supply vessels. The renaming to LS MERCURY following the January 2026 exposure mirrors HUITONG 78's renaming to BARAAWE 1. (AI Jan 2026)

3
Ownership and Management

Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation did not identify the owner or manager. Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 press release identified Legendsea Co. Ltd. (Vietnam) as registered owner and Allsea Management JSC (Vietnam) as both commercial manager and ISM manager. Both are Vietnamese-domiciled, notable given the broader Vietnamese dimension of the 2023 supply chain routed through Hai Linh's Cai Mep terminal, though no direct connection between these entities has been established. See Profile 6.4. (JFM Feb 2026)

Recommended Actions
•Designate LS MERCURY, Legendsea Co. Ltd., and Allsea Management JSC across all five jurisdictions. The vessel's renaming after the January 2026 exposure confirms active evasion. Designating owner and manager alongside the vessel is necessary to prevent further name-change evasion. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination and equivalents provide the legal authority. (JFM Feb 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Investigate whether YONG SHENG 56 and HUITONG 78 shared a common coordinator. The concurrent FOAA loading and sequential Thilawa deliveries suggest possible coordination. Whether Legendsea and Allsea are connected to the CASIC affiliate network identified for HUITONG 78 has not been established. AIS path data, shipping records, and Vietnamese corporate registry searches are the most direct investigative tools. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage Vietnam on Legendsea and Allsea alongside the Hai Linh engagement. Both are Vietnamese-domiciled entities. Any engagement with Vietnamese authorities on Hai Linh's role as transit hub (Profile 4.1) should be extended to include Legendsea and Allsea, presenting the full picture of Vietnamese entities across two phases of Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2024)
