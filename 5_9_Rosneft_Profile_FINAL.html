Rosneft Oil Company
Profile 5.9
Summary Assessment
Rosneft Oil Company (PJSC Rosneft) is a Russian state-controlled oil company. Amnesty International's November 2022 Deadly Cargo investigation identified Rosneft as the confirmed supplier of 15,710 MT of Jet A-1 delivered to Myanmar via the SWARNA MALA in December 2021, loading at the Vadinar Oil Terminal, India. The consignee was NEPAS, confirmed by Rosneft in correspondence with Amnesty International. Rosneft asserted civilian end-use but declined to provide any evidentiary basis when pressed. It made no mention of due diligence in any of its responses. Rosneft has not been designated in connection with Myanmar. It has since been sanctioned by the US, UK, Canada, and Australia in connection with Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and is subject to an EU transaction ban under Regulation 833/2014.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

SWARNA MALA (IMO 9421403) loaded 15,710 MT of Jet A-1 at Essar Jetty B, Vadinar Oil Terminal, Gujarat, India, on approximately 25 November 2021, arriving at Thilawa on 7 December 2021. The Vadinar terminal is owned by Nayara Energy, in which Rosneft's Singapore subsidiary holds a 49.13 percent stake. Rosneft confirmed the shipment and identified NEPAS as the consignee in correspondence with Amnesty International. Trafigura and Nayara Energy separately told Amnesty International they were not involved in the sale. (AI Nov 2022)

2
Rosneft's Response to Amnesty International

Rosneft engaged more substantively than most other named suppliers, but the substance of its responses does not hold. It asserted civilian end-use ("the shipment was used, exclusively and entirely, for civil aviation"), cited a contractual end-use restriction, and claimed Myanmar had domestic jet fuel production capacity of 1.1 million tonnes. When Amnesty International asked for the evidentiary basis for the civilian-use assertion, Rosneft did not respond. When Amnesty International asked for a source for the domestic production claim, Rosneft did not provide one. Amnesty International noted that claim is contradicted by media and industry reports. Unlike Chevron and Thai Oil, which referenced counterparty due diligence in their responses, Rosneft made no mention of any due diligence in any of its communications. A contractual end-use restriction, even if genuine, does not negate Rosneft's role as supplier where the evidence establishes that NEPAS aviation fuel was transported to Myanmar Air Force bases. (AI Nov 2022)

3
Key Relationships
•NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.): The formal consignee of the December 2021 Rosneft shipment, confirmed by Rosneft itself. NEPAS held the aviation fuel import licence in Myanmar. Rosneft stated it believed NEPAS was using the fuel for civilian purposes only. Amnesty International's investigation established that fuel delivered to NEPAS was transported to Myanmar Air Force bases. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022)
•Nayara Energy Limited (India): The owner of the Vadinar Oil Terminal, Essar Jetty B, from which the SWARNA MALA loaded. Nayara Energy is a Gujarat-based refinery company in which Rosneft Singapore Pte. Ltd. holds a 49.13% stake. Nayara Energy informed Amnesty International that it was not involved in the sale of the Jet A-1 shipment and separately committed, out of an abundance of caution, not to supply fuel to any parties in Myanmar. The EU designated Nayara Energy in its 18th sanctions package (18 July 2025) as an entity providing substantial revenue to the Russian government. The UK sanctioned Nayara Energy on 15 October 2025, in the same round as Rosneft. Both designations are in connection with Russia's energy revenues and the war in Ukraine, not with Myanmar. Rosneft is seeking to sell its 49.13% Nayara stake; Reliance Industries Ltd is reported as the frontrunner buyer, with the stake valued at approximately USD 17 billion. The EU and UK Nayara designations have significantly complicated the proposed sale. Rosneft publicly condemned the EU designation as "unjustified and illegal." (AI Nov 2022)
•Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. (Singapore), former investor: At the time of the December 2021 shipment, Trafigura was a co-investor alongside UCP Investment Group in Kesani Enterprises Co. Ltd., the consortium vehicle that held 49.13% of Nayara Energy. Trafigura's 2021 Annual Report stated it planned to sell its indirect stake in Nayara Energy; as of September 2022 the sale had not yet taken place. Trafigura separately informed Amnesty International that it was not involved in the sale of the SWARNA MALA Jet A-1 shipment. On 11 January 2023, Trafigura completed the sale of its 24.5% indirect stake in Nayara Energy (representing its share within Kesani) to Hara Capital Sarl, a subsidiary of Mareterra Group Holding, completing its exit from the Nayara shareholding. Kesani Enterprises is now owned by UCP Investment Group and Mareterra Group. See Profile 5.1. (AI Nov 2022)
•Former PEAS terminal, Thilawa, Yangon (now Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited): The destination terminal for the December 2021 Rosneft shipment. During the period of the shipment (December 2021), the terminal operated as Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS) under Puma Energy's management. See Profile 1.3. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy Holdings Pte. Ltd.: The PEAS terminal and the NEPAS joint venture were under Puma Energy's effective management at the time of the December 2021 shipment. Rosneft's own response to Amnesty International referenced "Puma Aviation" in connection with its civilian-use assurance, indicating that it regarded Puma Energy as the responsible operator for the end-use of the fuel. Puma Energy exited Myanmar in December 2022. See Profile 5.1. (AI Nov 2022)

Rosneft has not been designated by any government in connection with Myanmar. All current sanctions on Rosneft relate to Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Rosneft was added to the US OFAC SDN List on 22 October 2025 under Executive Order 14024, for operating in the energy sector of the Russian economy. The UK imposed asset-freezing sanctions on 15 October 2025 under Russia sanctions regulations (UK reference: RUS3064). Canada sanctioned Rosneft under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA) on 19 February 2026. Australia has also sanctioned Rosneft in connection with Russia. The EU imposed a transaction ban on Rosneft under Article 5aa of Regulation 833, adding it to Annex XIX of that regulation in March 2022 and tightening the ban in October 2025 when remaining energy import exemptions were removed. Switzerland has had Rosneft under sanctions measures since November 2014. New Zealand has sanctioned Rosneft since June 2022.

At the time of the December 2021 Myanmar shipment, Rosneft was subject to limited US sectoral sanctions under the Sectoral Sanctions Identifications (SSI) List (restricting certain debt and equity transactions), but had not been subjected to full blocking sanctions. The full SDN designation and UK asset freeze both postdate the Myanmar shipment by nearly four years. The OFAC jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 (23 August 2023), which extends designation risk to entities operating in Myanmar's jet fuel sector, applies prospectively and was issued after the period of Rosneft's documented involvement. (AI Nov 2022)

5
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•3 November 2022. Deadly Cargo identifies Rosneft as the confirmed supplier, publishes its substantive responses, and assesses the civilian-use assertion as unsupported. No Myanmar-specific designation or regulatory action follows. (AI Nov 2022)
•October 2025. The US (22 October) and UK (15 October) sanction Rosneft in connection with Russia's war in Ukraine. These designations are not Myanmar-related but fundamentally change the compliance landscape for any entity dealing with Rosneft. Canada and Australia had earlier sanctioned Rosneft on the same basis. The EU has imposed a transaction ban under Article 5aa of Regulation 833/2014, adding Rosneft to Annex XIX in March 2022, tightened in October 2025. Nayara Energy has been designated by the EU (July 2025) and UK (October 2025). (OFAC Oct 2025; UK Oct 2025)
6
Recommended Actions
•Use the Ukraine sanctions framework to re-open the Myanmar disclosure question. Rosneft's full SDN designation in October 2025 and the UK asset freeze create new leverage. Civil society should renew engagement with Rosneft requesting the evidentiary basis for its civilian-use assertion, the terms and enforcement record of any contractual end-use restriction in the December 2021 supply contract, and documentation of what due diligence it applied to NEPAS. Although Rosneft is now subject to broad sanctions, a formal documented response on the Myanmar shipment remains relevant for accountability purposes. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Oct 2025)
•Monitor the Nayara Energy stake sale. Rosneft is seeking to sell its 49.13 percent stake in Nayara Energy to Reliance Industries, a transaction complicated by Nayara's EU and UK designations. The Vadinar terminal loaded the December 2021 Myanmar shipment. Any new buyer of the Nayara stake should be engaged before completion to ensure Myanmar supply chain due diligence is part of the transaction. If the stake sale proceeds through a structure that obscures Rosneft's ongoing beneficial interest, that arrangement should be flagged to OFAC and UK OTSI. (AI Nov 2022)
