Rosneft Oil Company
Profile 5.9
Summary Assessment
Rosneft Oil Company (PJSC Rosneft) is a Russian state-controlled integrated energy company and one of the world's largest oil producers, listed on the Moscow Exchange (MCX: ROSN). Amnesty International's November 2022 Deadly Cargo investigation identified Rosneft as the confirmed supplier of 15,710 MT of Jet A-1 aviation fuel delivered to Myanmar's military supply chain via the SWARNA MALA (IMO 9421403) in December 2021. The fuel loaded at Essar Jetty B at the Vadinar Oil Terminal in Gujarat, India, a terminal owned by Nayara Energy Limited, in which Rosneft's Singapore subsidiary holds a 49.13% stake. The consignee was National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS), the Myanmar military's licensed aviation fuel importer, confirmed by Rosneft itself in correspondence with Amnesty International. Rosneft disputed that the fuel was used for military purposes, asserting civilian end-use, but declined to provide any evidentiary basis for this assertion when pressed. Amnesty International assessed Rosneft's civilian-use claim as contradicted by its own investigative findings and by the documented military distribution of aviation fuel through NEPAS. Rosneft has not been designated in connection with Myanmar. Rosneft has subsequently been sanctioned by the United States (October 2025), United Kingdom (October 2025), Canada, Australia and other jurisdictions in connection with Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and is subject to EU transaction restrictions.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Rosneft Oil Company is the confirmed supplier of the December 2021 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar. The SWARNA MALA (IMO 9421403), a chemical and oil products tanker built in 2010 and flying the flag of India, loaded 15,710 MT of Jet A-1 aviation fuel at Essar Jetty B at the Vadinar Oil Terminal in Jamnagar, Gujarat, India, on approximately 25 November 2021. The vessel arrived at the PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, on 7 December 2021 and offloaded its cargo. (AI Nov 2022)

Rosneft confirmed its involvement to Amnesty International. In correspondence with AI, Rosneft acknowledged that "the shipment had been offloaded at the Puma Energy Asia Sun Co. Ltd. terminal in Thilawa area port to the benefit of National Energy Puma Aviation Services," identifying NEPAS as the end user of the fuel. NEPAS, the consignee identified by Rosneft, was at the time the licensed aviation fuel importer in Myanmar, operating as a joint venture between Puma Energy and an affiliate of the Asia Sun Group. Amnesty International's investigation documented that aviation fuel delivered to NEPAS was transported to Myanmar Air Force bases and used in aerial attacks on civilians. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022)

The Vadinar Oil Terminal from which the shipment loaded is owned by Nayara Energy Limited, a Gujarat-based refinery company. Rosneft's Singapore subsidiary, Rosneft Singapore Pte. Ltd., holds a 49.13% stake in Nayara Energy. A further 49.13% of Nayara Energy is held through Kesani Enterprises Co. Ltd., a consortium special purpose vehicle that was jointly owned by Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. and UCP Investment Group at the time of the December 2021 shipment; Trafigura's 24.5% indirect stake in Nayara (representing its share within Kesani) was subsequently sold to Hara Capital Sarl (a subsidiary of Mareterra Group Holding) on 11 January 2023, completing Trafigura's exit from the Nayara shareholding. Both Trafigura and Nayara Energy separately informed Amnesty International that they were not involved in the sale of the December 2021 Jet A-1 shipment. The commercial transaction was between Rosneft as seller and NEPAS as consignee. See Profile 5.1 for Puma Energy and Trafigura. (AI Nov 2022)

2
Rosneft's Response to Amnesty International

Rosneft provided substantive responses to Amnesty International's inquiries. While Rosneft confirmed the shipment and the identity of the consignee (NEPAS), it asserted that the fuel was used exclusively for civilian aviation. Its specific statements, as documented by Amnesty International, are set out below. (AI Nov 2022)

•On civilian end-use: Rosneft stated: "We are confident of impeccable end-user reputation and we have no doubts about an accurate operation by Puma Aviation. The shipment you mentioned was used, exclusively and entirely, for civil aviation last year and bears no relation to your assumptions." When Amnesty International asked Rosneft to clarify the basis for this assertion, Rosneft did not respond to that question. (AI Nov 2022)
•On contractual prohibition: Rosneft stated that "the prohibition of the use of fuel for military purposes was stated in the supply contract." This assertion implies a contractual end-use restriction, but Rosneft provided no evidence that such a restriction was enforced or enforceable given the documented diversion of NEPAS fuel to Myanmar Air Force bases. (AI Nov 2022)
•On Myanmar's domestic fuel capacity: Rosneft stated that "Myanmar's aviation fuel consumption averages some 100,000 tonnes per year" and claimed that Myanmar had its own domestic production capacity of 1.1 million tonnes capable of producing all types of fuel including jet fuel. Amnesty International followed up requesting a source for this claim; Rosneft did not provide one. Amnesty International noted that this claim is contradicted by media and industry reports indicating that Myanmar imports all of its jet fuel. (AI Nov 2022)
•On its general trading standards: Rosneft reaffirmed that it "sells to credible market buyers who have an impeccable reputation, operate in line with global standards and adhere to all international legal requirements." (AI Nov 2022)
•On due diligence: Amnesty International documented that, unlike Chevron and Thai Oil, which each referenced counterparty due diligence in their responses, Rosneft made no mention of any due diligence conducted in any of its communications. In its first response, Rosneft urged Amnesty International to "conduct a detailed review of the information you received" but did not describe any steps it had itself taken to assess the human rights risks of the supply. (AI Nov 2022)
Rosneft confirmed the shipment and identified NEPAS as the consignee, which is the critical factual finding. The civilian-use assertion it offered in response is not independently supported. Rosneft refused to disclose the basis for that assertion when Amnesty International pressed the point directly. Its claim that Myanmar produces its own jet fuel is contradicted by available evidence and Rosneft did not provide a source when asked. Amnesty International's investigation independently established that NEPAS aviation fuel was transported to Myanmar Air Force bases. And unlike Chevron and Thai Oil, both of which referenced counterparty due diligence in their responses, Rosneft made no mention of any due diligence at any point in its communications. A contractual end-use restriction, even if genuine, does not negate Rosneft's role in supplying fuel that reached Myanmar's military aviation system.

Key Relationships
•NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.): The formal consignee of the December 2021 Rosneft shipment, confirmed by Rosneft itself. NEPAS held the aviation fuel import licence in Myanmar. Rosneft stated it believed NEPAS was using the fuel for civilian purposes only. Amnesty International's investigation established that fuel delivered to NEPAS was transported to Myanmar Air Force bases. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022)
•Nayara Energy Limited (India): The owner of the Vadinar Oil Terminal, Essar Jetty B, from which the SWARNA MALA loaded. Nayara Energy is a Gujarat-based refinery company in which Rosneft Singapore Pte. Ltd. holds a 49.13% stake. Nayara Energy informed Amnesty International that it was not involved in the sale of the Jet A-1 shipment and separately committed, out of an abundance of caution, not to supply fuel to any parties in Myanmar. The EU designated Nayara Energy in its 18th sanctions package (18 July 2025) as an entity providing substantial revenue to the Russian government. The UK sanctioned Nayara Energy on 15 October 2025, in the same round as Rosneft. Both designations are in connection with Russia's energy revenues and the war in Ukraine, not with Myanmar. Rosneft is seeking to sell its 49.13% Nayara stake; Reliance Industries Ltd is reported as the frontrunner buyer, with the stake valued at approximately USD 17 billion. The EU and UK Nayara designations have significantly complicated the proposed sale. Rosneft publicly condemned the EU designation as "unjustified and illegal." (AI Nov 2022)
•Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. (Singapore), former investor: At the time of the December 2021 shipment, Trafigura was a co-investor alongside UCP Investment Group in Kesani Enterprises Co. Ltd., the consortium vehicle that held 49.13% of Nayara Energy. Trafigura's 2021 Annual Report stated it planned to sell its indirect stake in Nayara Energy; as of September 2022 the sale had not yet taken place. Trafigura separately informed Amnesty International that it was not involved in the sale of the SWARNA MALA Jet A-1 shipment. On 11 January 2023, Trafigura completed the sale of its 24.5% indirect stake in Nayara Energy (representing its share within Kesani) to Hara Capital Sarl, a subsidiary of Mareterra Group Holding, completing its exit from the Nayara shareholding. Kesani Enterprises is now owned by UCP Investment Group and Mareterra Group. See Profile 5.1. (AI Nov 2022)
•Former PEAS terminal, Thilawa, Yangon (now Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited): The destination terminal for the December 2021 Rosneft shipment. During the period of the shipment (December 2021), the terminal operated as Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS) under Puma Energy's management. See Profile 1.3. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy Holdings Pte. Ltd.: The PEAS terminal and the NEPAS joint venture were under Puma Energy's effective management at the time of the December 2021 shipment. Rosneft's own response to Amnesty International referenced "Puma Aviation" in connection with its civilian-use assurance, indicating that it regarded Puma Energy as the responsible operator for the end-use of the fuel. Puma Energy exited Myanmar in December 2022. See Profile 5.1. (AI Nov 2022)

Designation Status

Rosneft Oil Company (PJSC Rosneft)Russian state-controlled oil company; confirmed seller December 2021 Myanmar shipment (row 3). All designations are Russia/Ukraine-related. ✓✓✓✓✓

Rosneft has not been designated by any government in connection with Myanmar. All current sanctions on Rosneft relate to Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Rosneft was added to the US OFAC SDN List on 22 October 2025 under Executive Order 14024, for operating in the energy sector of the Russian economy. The UK imposed asset-freezing sanctions on 15 October 2025 under Russia sanctions regulations (UK reference: RUS3064). Canada sanctioned Rosneft under the Special Economic Measures Act (SEMA) on 19 February 2026. Australia has also sanctioned Rosneft in connection with Russia. The EU imposed a transaction ban on Rosneft under Article 5aa of Regulation 833, adding it to Annex XIX of that regulation in March 2022 and tightening the ban in October 2025 when remaining energy import exemptions were removed. Switzerland has had Rosneft under sanctions measures since November 2014. New Zealand has sanctioned Rosneft since June 2022.

At the time of the December 2021 Myanmar shipment, Rosneft was subject to limited US sectoral sanctions under the Sectoral Sanctions Identifications (SSI) List (restricting certain debt and equity transactions), but had not been subjected to full blocking sanctions. The full SDN designation and UK asset freeze both postdate the Myanmar shipment by nearly four years. The OFAC jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 (23 August 2023), which extends designation risk to entities operating in Myanmar's jet fuel sector, applies prospectively and was issued after the period of Rosneft's documented involvement. (AI Nov 2022)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•3 November 2022. Amnesty International and Justice For Myanmar publish the Deadly Cargo investigation, identifying Rosneft as the confirmed supplier of 15,710 MT of Jet A-1 to NEPAS via the SWARNA MALA in December 2021, loading at the Vadinar Oil Terminal in Gujarat. The report publishes Rosneft's substantive responses asserting civilian end-use, and AI's assessment that those responses are not supported by the available evidence. The report notes that Rosneft declined to provide a source for its claim that Myanmar produces its own jet fuel, and that Rosneft failed to respond to the follow-up question about the basis for its civilian-use assertion. (AI Nov 2022)
•No Myanmar-specific subsequent advocacy documented. Rosneft does not appear in any reviewed investigation after the November 2022 Deadly Cargo report in connection with Myanmar. No designation, regulatory action, or further public engagement with Rosneft specifically targeting its Myanmar aviation fuel supply role has been documented as of April 2026. Rosneft's subsequent designation by the US (October 2025), UK (October 2025), Canada (February 2026), and Australia (May 2023) relates exclusively to its role in Russia's energy sector and the war in Ukraine, not to Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022)
6
Recommended Actions
•Assess whether Rosneft's full SDN designation creates new leverage for Myanmar advocacy: Rosneft's designation to the US SDN List on 22 October 2025 and UK asset freeze on 15 October 2025, while not Myanmar-related, significantly changes the risk and compliance environment for any entity dealing with Rosneft. Civil society and governments pursuing accountability for the December 2021 Myanmar shipment should assess whether the new sanctions framework creates additional leverage for securing disclosure or acknowledgement from Rosneft of its role in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain. Separately, the US OFAC jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 (23 August 2023) remains in force and applies prospectively to any future Rosneft involvement in Myanmar's jet fuel sector. (AI Nov 2022)
•Seek disclosure from Rosneft on the scope of its Myanmar supply relationship: Amnesty International's contact with Rosneft in 2022 established that the December 2021 SWARNA MALA shipment was a confirmed transaction. The questions left unanswered are: the evidentiary basis for Rosneft's civilian-use assertion; the terms and enforcement of any end-use restrictions in the supply contract; and whether Rosneft conducted any due diligence on NEPAS's documented links to the Myanmar military aviation network. Civil society should seek formal responses to these questions in any renewed engagement. (AI Nov 2022)
•Monitor Nayara Energy for any resumed connection to Myanmar aviation fuel supply chains: The Vadinar Oil Terminal was the load port for the December 2021 shipment. Nayara Energy has been sanctioned by the EU (18 July 2025) and the UK (15 October 2025), and Rosneft is seeking to sell its 49.13% Nayara stake, with the proposed transaction to Reliance Industries Ltd complicated by those designations. Given Rosneft's 49.13% shareholding in Nayara Energy and the terminal's established role as a load port in the Myanmar supply chain, any future Myanmar-bound aviation fuel shipment loading at Vadinar should be treated as warranting immediate investigation and re-engagement. Any change in Nayara's ownership structure resulting from the proposed stake sale should also be assessed for its implications for Myanmar supply chain risk. (AI Nov 2022)
