North Petroleum International Co. Ltd. and China North Industries Group Corporation Limited (NORINCO)
Profile 9.3
Summary Assessment
North Petroleum International Co. Ltd. is a subsidiary of China ZhenHua Oil Co. Ltd., which is itself a subsidiary of China North Industries Group Corporation Limited (NORINCO), a Chinese state-owned arms conglomerate ultimately owned by the Chinese government. North Petroleum International holds the 40 percent stake in Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. (Profile 9.1) that is not held by Interra Resources Ltd. Through this stake, North Petroleum International is a co-owner of the oil extraction operation whose output feeds the domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain documented across Profiles 9.1 and 9.2. NORINCO is a major arms supplier to the Myanmar military and separately operates copper mines in Myanmar with Myanma Economic Holdings Limited (MEHL), the junta's primary military conglomerate. North Petroleum International was also a major shareholder of Interra Resources on the Singapore Exchange until at least September 2023. Neither North Petroleum International nor China ZhenHua Oil has been designated by any government in connection with Myanmar. NORINCO has been listed on the United States Department of Defense list of Chinese military companies and the US OFAC Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex (NS-CMIC) list under authorities unrelated to Myanmar, and on Japan's METI End User List. This profile is a reference entry. The principal advocacy and recommended actions relating to Goldpetrol's crude oil supply are addressed in Profile 9.1 (Interra Resources / Goldpetrol) and Profile 9.2 (MOGE).

1
Corporate Structure and Connection to the Myanmar Supply Chain

North Petroleum International Co. Ltd. holds 40 percent of Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. through its position in the China ZhenHua Oil group. The remaining 60 percent is held by Interra Resources Ltd. via its British Virgin Islands holding company, Goldwater Company Limited (Profile 9.1). NORINCO, as the ultimate parent of North Petroleum International through China ZhenHua Oil, is therefore an indirect co-owner of the oil extraction operation whose crude oil forms the upstream input to the domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain in Magway Region. All crude oil extracted by Goldpetrol is sold exclusively to Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE) (Profile 9.2), which transfers it to Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) for refining at the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex, where it is processed into petroleum products including aviation fuel and diesel that primarily supply Myanmar military bases. (JFM Jan 2025)

North Petroleum International was also a major shareholder of Interra Resources on the Singapore Exchange (SGX) until at least September 2023. In August 2023, as a separate transaction, Edwin Soeryadjaya's Saratoga vehicle sold its own Interra stake to Ng Soon Kai. Beyond its position in Goldpetrol and its historical Interra shareholding, North Petroleum International has no documented independent operational role in the aviation fuel supply chain. A spokesperson for Interra Resources stated that Interra had no dealings with NORINCO, which bought its interest in the Myanmar oil fields from another company. NORINCO did not respond to questions from Justice For Myanmar. (JFM Jan 2025)

NORINCO, one of China's largest arms conglomerates and the world's largest specialist in land-based weapons systems, separately supplies weapons to the Myanmar military and operates copper mines in Myanmar in partnership with Myanma Economic Holdings Limited (MEHL), the junta's primary military conglomerate. The connection between NORINCO's arms supply role and its indirect co-ownership of Goldpetrol's crude oil production has not been the subject of formal advocacy specific to this profile, but it is a structural feature of the supply chain that reinforces the military nexus of the Goldpetrol operation. The corporate chain connecting North Petroleum International to NORINCO also implicates China ZhenHua Oil, which functions as the NORINCO group's oil trading and upstream investment vehicle. North Petroleum International, as a unit of China ZhenHua Oil, has been identified in trade reporting as loading Russian ESPO crude cargoes, confirming the group's active role in international oil trading beyond the Myanmar context. (JFM Jan 2025)

NORINCO was subsequently placed on the US OFAC Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies (NS-CMIC) list effective 2 August 2021, under Executive Order 13959 as amended by Executive Order 14032, which prohibits US persons from purchasing or selling NORINCO's publicly traded securities. NORINCO has not been designated by the EU, UK, Canada or Australia in connection with either Myanmar or China defence sector concerns as of April 2026.

North Petroleum International Co. Ltd. and China ZhenHua Oil Co. Ltd. have not been independently designated by any government as of April 2026. No designation specific to their co-ownership of Goldpetrol or their role in the Myanmar crude supply chain has been identified in any reviewed sanctions list.

Key Relationships
•Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. / Interra Resources Ltd (Profile 9.1): North Petroleum International holds 40 percent of Goldpetrol. All crude oil produced by Goldpetrol from the Chauk and Yenangyaung fields is sold to MOGE under the Improved Petroleum Recovery Contracts. North Petroleum International's co-ownership position means it benefits financially from Goldpetrol's revenues, which flow through the same payment structure that has been documented as generating more than USD 200 million in oil sales to MOGE between January 2021 and the end of 2024. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE) (Profile 9.2): As Goldpetrol's co-owner, North Petroleum International is indirectly party to the IPRC relationship with MOGE, the junta-controlled state oil enterprise that is the mandatory purchaser of all Goldpetrol crude. MOGE is designated by the EU (21 February 2022), subject to a US financial services directive (effective 15 December 2023), and designated by Switzerland (11 March 2025). (Profile 9.2; Open source, March 2025)
•Myanma Economic Holdings Limited (MEHL): NORINCO separately operates copper mines in Myanmar in partnership with MEHL, the junta's primary military conglomerate. MEHL is designated by the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia. This separate NORINCO-MEHL relationship runs alongside the North Petroleum International-Goldpetrol relationship and is part of the broader pattern of NORINCO's military and commercial engagement with the Myanmar junta. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Yang Peilin / Heilongjiang United Oil and Chemicals Co. Ltd. (HUOC): Yang Peilin, Interra Resources' second-largest shareholder at 5.7 percent as of January 2025, is the chairperson of Heilongjiang United Oil and Chemicals Co. Ltd. (HUOC), a Chinese commodity trading firm that imports crude oil from Russia and Southeast Asia. HUOC signed a strategic cooperation agreement with China ZhenHua Oil in 2015 to 2016, creating a documented commercial link between the Interra shareholder register and the NORINCO oil trading arm. (JFM Jan 2025)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•29 January 2025. Justice For Myanmar and Finance Uncovered published their joint investigation into Interra Resources, identifying the NORINCO corporate chain as the ultimate beneficial owner of the 40 percent North Petroleum International stake in Goldpetrol. The investigation documented NORINCO's dual role as a Myanmar arms supplier and indirect co-owner of Goldpetrol's crude oil production. NORINCO did not respond to JFM's questions. (JFM Jan 2025)
•12 February 2025. Justice For Myanmar's rebuttal of Interra Resources' SGX response reiterated the significance of the NORINCO chain in the Goldpetrol ownership structure, noting the structural connection between a designated Chinese military-industrial complex company and the co-ownership of oil fields whose output reaches Myanmar military bases. (JFM Feb 2025)

Recommended Actions
•Submit evidence to all five sanctions authorities for Myanmar-specific designation of NORINCO and North Petroleum International: NORINCO has not been designated under Myanmar-specific sanctions by any government. Its existing listings on the US DoD 1260H list and the US OFAC NS-CMIC list relate to China's military-industrial complex and do not create Myanmar sanctions exposure. The DoD 1260H listing is an identification instrument under the National Defense Authorization Act that names Chinese military companies but does not itself impose financial restrictions. The NS-CMIC listing under Executive Order 13959, as amended by Executive Order 14032, imposes securities investment restrictions, prohibiting US persons from purchasing or selling NORINCO's publicly traded securities. Neither constitutes a property-blocking designation under the Myanmar programme. A separate designation under EO 14014 would be required for NORINCO to be blocked under the Myanmar framework, and if obtained, OFAC's 50 percent rule would automatically extend that designation to North Petroleum International and China ZhenHua Oil as subsidiaries, provided majority ownership is confirmed throughout the chain, a question that itself warrants investigation given that exact ownership percentages within the NORINCO-ZhenHua Oil-NPI structure have not been publicly confirmed. Civil society should compile and submit evidence packages to OFAC, UK OFSI, the EU, Global Affairs Canada and Australia's DFAT. Evidence should address: NORINCO's documented arms supply to the Myanmar military; NORINCO's copper mining operations in Myanmar conducted in partnership with the designated military conglomerate MEHL; North Petroleum International's 40 percent co-ownership of Goldpetrol and its indirect financial benefit from oil sales to EU-designated MOGE; and the downstream connection between Goldpetrol's crude oil, MPE's Mann refinery, and aviation fuel production for Myanmar military bases. For OFAC, the strongest basis is EO 14014 Section 1(a)(vii), which permits designation of persons acting for or on behalf of the military or security forces of Burma. NORINCO's arms supply relationship with the Myanmar military and its indirect participation in the crude-to-aviation-fuel chain through North Petroleum International both bear directly on that criterion. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Engage institutional investors in NORINCO subsidiaries on the Myanmar arms supply and Goldpetrol co-ownership connection: US capital markets have invested in NORINCO subsidiaries despite the NS-CMIC listing. Institutional investors and asset managers holding exposure to any NORINCO-affiliated publicly traded entity should be engaged with the documented evidence of NORINCO's arms supply to the Myanmar military and North Petroleum International's co-ownership of Goldpetrol. The combination of an indirect financial stake in a junta-controlled crude oil supply chain and direct arms supply to a military committing atrocity crimes creates material reputational and regulatory risk for any investor exposed to the NORINCO corporate family. ESG-focused engagement and divestment pressure on NORINCO-affiliated securities should be pursued in parallel with sanctions designation advocacy. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Investigate the corporate structure and jurisdiction of North Petroleum International Co. Ltd.: North Petroleum International's jurisdiction of incorporation, directors, and corporate accounts have not been publicly confirmed in any reviewed source. Clarifying this information would substantially strengthen any formal designation submission and is necessary for assessing whether NPI's jurisdiction of incorporation carries Myanmar sanctions obligations. Investigative journalists and legal researchers should consult the Chinese corporate registry (SAMR), international shipping databases such as Equasis and Lloyd's List, and the Bahamas registry (given Goldpetrol's Bahamas registration) as starting points. Identifying NPI's directors and any overlap with China ZhenHua Oil or NORINCO governance would also strengthen a designation evidence package. (JFM Jan 2025)
