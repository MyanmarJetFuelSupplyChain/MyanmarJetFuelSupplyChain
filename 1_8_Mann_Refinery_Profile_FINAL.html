Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex
Profile 1.8
Summary Assessment
The Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex (Mann Refinery) in Magway Region is the only MPE refinery still operational after the coup and the junta's sole domestic source of aviation turbine fuel. MPE's own website lists ATF as a current Mann product. Between December 2021 and January 2022, leaked documents show 1.5 million litres of Jet A-1 were trucked from Mann to six air bases, two of which are directly linked to war crimes. Night-time light analysis indicates a surge in production activity from 2021 to mid-2023, consistent with junta statistics recording a 174 percent increase in domestic aviation turbine fuel output in early 2022. The refinery sits between Magway Air Force Base and KaPaSa 13, a military gunpowder production facility. Mann has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. Justice for Myanmar called for its designation in February 2026.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Mann's documented role has two dimensions. The first, established by Amnesty International's 2022 evidence, is as a storage and distribution node for imported Jet A-1. Leaked documents and delivery records show tanker trucks transporting 1,518,354 litres from Mann to Hmawbi, Meikhtila, Monywa, Nay Pyi Taw, Tada-U, and Taungoo air bases between December 2021 and January 2022. Twelve of the 54 NEPAS invoices to Cargo Link Petroleum cover Mann-related movements; truck routes in the delivery dataset include "TG-Mann-Taungoo-Thilawa" and "TG-Mann-TG", confirming Mann as a waypoint in the military distribution chain. Hmawbi and Taungoo are directly linked to attacks amounting to war crimes. NEPAS paid Cargo Link for these deliveries. (AI Nov 2022)

The second dimension is domestic production. MPE's own website lists Aviation Turbine Fuel as a current Mann product. Junta statistics recorded a 174 percent increase in domestic aviation turbine fuel output in the first quarter of 2022 compared with the prior year. Industry expert analysis of night-time light emissions cited by Justice for Myanmar showed significant activity increases at Mann from 2021 to mid-2023, consistent with the production surge, while the Chauk refinery showed a concurrent decrease confirming it was not the source. Two sources with supply chain knowledge told Justice for Myanmar that military bases are the primary beneficiaries of Mann's petroleum products. Night-time light activity at Mann stopped after mid-2023; current operational status is uncertain. (JFM Jan 2025)

Mann sits on the Ayeyarwady River between Magway Air Force Base and KaPaSa 13, a Tatmadaw gunpowder mill producing priming materials and explosives. Its crude feedstock from the Chauk and Yenangyaung oilfields is transported by barge from those fields, with Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. the documented supplier under a contract with MOGE. With Chauk offline, Mann is the sole refinery destination for that crude. (JFM Jan 2025)

2
Key Relationships
•Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE): State-owned enterprise that manages Mann Refinery under the junta's Ministry of Energy, and also manages the No. 2 Refinery (Chauk) and the closed No. 1 Refinery (Thanlyin), making it the operator of Myanmar's entire domestic refinery portfolio. MPE is under military control since February 2021. MPE is also the majority shareholder in NEPAS, connecting it to the imported Jet A-1 supply chain as well as to the domestic refinery operation. Any sanctions submission targeting MPE should note that its portfolio encompasses both Mann and Chauk, and that the crude routing decision that channels Chauk and Yenangyaung field output exclusively to Mann is an MPE-level supply chain decision. See Profile 1.1. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Jan 2025; MOEP Website)
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Company Limited (NEPAS): The primary documented payer for tanker truck deliveries of aviation fuel to and from Mann Refinery during the October 2021 to January 2022 period covered by the NEPAS invoice dataset. NEPAS contracted Cargo Link Petroleum for this distribution work. NEPAS is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022)
•Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited (CLPL): The tanker truck operator contracted by NEPAS and, according to Amnesty International, apparently contracted separately by the military for air base deliveries. CLPL's delivery datasets are the primary evidentiary basis for Mann's documented role in the aviation fuel distribution network. See Profile 1.7. (AI Nov 2022)
•Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. / Interra Resources Ltd.: Singapore-listed Interra Resources, through its subsidiary Goldpetrol, holds concessions for the Chauk and Yenangyaung oilfields that provide crude feedstock to Mann Refinery by barge along the Ayeyarwady River. The relationship between Goldpetrol's upstream crude production and Mann's refining and distribution function is documented in JFM's January 2025 report. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Myanmar military air bases supplied via Mann: Documented air bases to which Jet A-1 was distributed from Mann Refinery include Hmawbi (Yangon Region), Meikhtila, Monywa, Nay Pyi Taw, Tada-U (Mandalay Region), and Taungoo (Bago Region). Amnesty International linked Hmawbi and Taungoo directly to air strikes constituting war crimes in its Mann Refinery dataset, and linked four of the five air bases documented as departure points for attacks between March 2021 and August 2022 to attacks constituting war crimes: Hmawbi, Magway, Tada-U, and Taungoo. (AI Nov 2022)

Sanctions gap: Neither Mann Refinery nor its managing entity MPE has been designated by any jurisdiction. Mann's role as a military-controlled storage and distribution hub for Jet A-1, confirmed by leaked documents covering December 2021 to January 2022, and the evidence of a post-coup surge in refinery activity documented by JFM in 2025, provide a strong evidentiary basis for designation under the jet fuel sector criteria operative in the US, UK, and other jurisdictions. JFM called for new coordinated sanctions targeting Mann in its February 2026 press release. MPE's unsanctioned status is a separate and longstanding gap: MPE is the majority shareholder of NEPAS and the managing entity of the military's primary remaining refinery, yet faces no targeted financial measures. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2026)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo documents Mann's role as a military distribution node for the first time, presenting invoice and delivery dataset evidence. Amnesty calls on all states to suspend aviation fuel supplies to Myanmar. No designation follows. (AI Nov 2022)
•January 2025. Justice for Myanmar's Goldpetrol and Interra Resources investigation includes the most detailed assessment of Mann's post-coup activity to date, presenting night-time light analysis and source testimony on the production surge. No designation follows. (JFM Jan 2025)
•February 2026. Justice for Myanmar formally calls for coordinated designation of Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex across all five jurisdictions. No designation follows as of April 2026. (JFM Feb 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Designate Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex in all jurisdictions. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination provides the legal authority without requiring additional criteria. The evidentiary basis spans AI Nov 2022 (distribution to war-crime-linked bases), the MPE website (ATF listed as a current product), and JFM Jan 2025 (production surge and military beneficiary testimony). JFM's February 2026 call for coordinated designation should be treated as an actionable submission and supported with both evidence packages. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Jan 2025; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Designate MPE simultaneously. MPE manages Mann and holds the controlling stake in NEPAS. Designating Mann without MPE leaves the institutional operator intact. The two designations should be submitted together. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Feb 2026)
•Target the feedstock supply chain through the Goldpetrol and Interra Resources lever. With Chauk offline, Mann is the sole destination for crude from the Chauk and Yenangyaung fields. Disruption to Goldpetrol's crude deliveries would directly constrain Mann's throughput. The SGX inquiry into Interra Resources is the active pressure point. Submissions to SGX RegCo should explicitly connect Interra's crude deliveries to Mann's production of aviation fuel. The Chauk No. 2 Refinery, currently shutdown, should also be named in any designation submission to prevent reactivation as a fallback if Mann is designated. (JFM Jan 2025)

The No. 1 Refinery (Thanlyin) closed in March 2017 and the No. 2 Refinery (Chauk) closed in March 2019 due to local crude oil availability constraints, leaving Mann as Myanmar's sole operational MPE refinery before the coup. February 2021Military coup. MPE, including its management of Mann Refinery, comes under direct military control. (AI Nov 2022)

December 2021 to January 2022Leaked documents record 1,518,354 litres of Jet A-1 transported by tanker truck from Mann Refinery to Hmawbi, Meikhtila, Monywa, Nay Pyi Taw, Tada-U, and Taungoo air bases. (AI Nov 2022)
