Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex
Profile 1.8
Summary Assessment
The Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex (commonly: Mann Refinery), located in Minhla Township, Magway Region, is a military-controlled refinery managed by Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) and the only one of Myanmar's three MPE refineries still operational after the coup. The two others, the No. 1 Refinery at Thanlyin (closed March 2017) and the No. 2 Refinery at Chauk (closed March 2019), are both offline, leaving Mann as the junta's sole domestic refinery. The Ministry of Energy's own website confirms Mann's current product range includes Gasoline, Diesel, ATF (Aviation Turbine Fuel), LPG, Kerosene, Fuel Oil, and Petroleum Coke, listing ATF as a current output. Design capacity is 25,000 BPSD; current operating capacity is 8,500 BPSD (300,000 gallons per day), dependent on local crude oil availability. Amnesty International's November 2022 Deadly Cargo report documented Mann's role as a storage and distribution node for imported Jet A-1: NEPAS paid Cargo Link Petroleum for tanker truck deliveries of aviation fuel to and from the refinery, and leaked documents show the military then distributed that fuel from Mann to at least six air bases, two of which (Hmawbi and Taungoo) are directly linked to war crimes as documented by Amnesty International. Justice for Myanmar's January 2025 investigation found evidence that Mann functioned as the primary source of a post-coup surge in domestic aviation fuel production from 2021 to mid-2023, with crude from the Chauk and Yenangyaung oilfields routed entirely to Mann by barge. The refinery sits on the Ayeyarwady River between Magway air force base and the No. 13 Tatmadaw Defence Industry facility, making it a strategically central node in the military's domestic fuel and weapons production complex. Mann Refinery has not been sanctioned by any jurisdiction. Justice for Myanmar called for its designation in February 2026.

Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain
Storage and distribution node for imported Jet A-1

Amnesty International's November 2022 Deadly Cargo report documented Mann Refinery's role not as a production source but as a storage and distribution node for imported Jet A-1. Leaked documents covering December 2021 to January 2022 show that tanker trucks transported 1,518,354 litres of Jet A-1 from Mann Refinery to at least six air bases: Hmawbi, Meikhtila, Monywa, Nay Pyi Taw, Tada-U, and Taungoo. Two of these bases, Hmawbi and Taungoo, are directly linked to war crimes as documented by Amnesty International. (AI Nov 2022)

Among the 54 invoices issued by Cargo Link Petroleum (then Cargo Link Pongrawe Logistics) to NEPAS for the October to December 2021 period, 12 involve Mann Refinery: two cover the transport of empty trucks from Yangon to Mann, and ten cover deliveries of fuel from Mann to Mandalay and Yangon airports. The air base delivery dataset also records multiple trip routes through Mann, including "TG-Mann-Taungoo-Thilawa" and "TG[Taungoo]-Mann-TG." Destinations of trips departing from or stopping at Mann include Meikhtila, Hmawbi, Nay Pyi Taw, Taungoo, and Monywa air bases. Of these, Amnesty International directly linked Hmawbi and Taungoo to air strikes that amounted to war crimes. NEPAS paid Cargo Link Petroleum for these truck deliveries to and from Mann. Puma Energy declined to answer Amnesty International's questions on deliveries to and from the refinery. (AI Nov 2022)

Amnesty International assessed it as "very likely" that Mann also stores aviation fuel for military use, and described the refinery as functioning "most likely as a storage facility" given that the documentary evidence establishes distribution out of it rather than confirmed production within it. The relationship between NEPAS storage facilities, the military air bases, and Mann Refinery is, in Amnesty International's assessment, significant: it shows that civilian and military use of aviation fuel is inextricably linked. (AI Nov 2022)

Production capacity and post-coup activity

Myanmar has three MPE refineries: No. 1 at Thanlyin, Yangon (closed since March 2017); No. 2 at Chauk, Magway (closed since March 2019 due to local crude availability constraints); and the Mann Thanbayarkan complex, also in Magway, which is the only one of the three that remained operational after the coup. The Ministry of Energy's website confirms Mann's current product range as Gasoline, Diesel, ATF (Aviation Turbine Fuel), LPG, Kerosene, Fuel Oil (FO), and Petroleum Coke, with ATF listed as a current product output, not merely a design capacity. The same page states a design capacity of 25,000 BPSD and a current operating capacity of 8,500 BPSD (300,000 gallons per day), dependent on local crude oil availability, indicating the facility operates at approximately one-third of its design capacity. (MOEP Website; AI Nov 2022)

The closure of Chauk in March 2019 is directly relevant to Mann's post-coup role. JFM's January 2025 investigation found that crude from the Chauk and Yenangyaung oilfields was being transported by boat along the Ayeyarwady River to Mann for refining. Night-time light analysis conducted by an industry expert and cited by JFM found a significant increase in activity at Mann from 2021 to mid-2023, running concurrently with a decrease in activity at the already-offline Chauk refinery. The night-time light decrease at Chauk confirms no other MPE facility was absorbing the crude supply. JFM assessed Mann as the likely primary source of a reported 174% increase in domestic aviation turbine fuel production in the first quarter of 2022 compared with the prior year, with output reaching 2 million gallons in the 2021-2022 period per official junta statistics. Night-time light emissions at Mann stopped after mid-2023, leaving the refinery's current operational status uncertain. (JFM Jan 2025)

Two sources with knowledge of the supply chain confirmed to JFM that the primary beneficiaries of petroleum products refined at Mann are military bases, with fuel also supplied to government departments and excess auctioned to private companies. The feedstock supply chain linking those oilfields to Mann involves Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. and its Singapore-listed parent Interra Resources Ltd., documented separately. (JFM Jan 2025)

Strategic location

Mann Refinery sits on the Ayeyarwady River between Magway air force base and No. 13 Tatmadaw Defence Industry facility (KaPaSa 13), a gunpowder mill unit that produces priming materials and gunpowder for explosives. Magway is described by JFM as the centre of the military's domestic weapons production, requiring a significant and sustained energy supply. The refinery's position between an operational air base and a major munitions production site underscores its centrality to the junta's military-industrial infrastructure. (JFM Jan 2025)

Key Relationships
•Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE): State-owned enterprise that manages Mann Refinery under the junta's Ministry of Energy, and also manages the No. 2 Refinery (Chauk) and the closed No. 1 Refinery (Thanlyin), making it the operator of Myanmar's entire domestic refinery portfolio. MPE is under military control since February 2021. MPE is also the majority shareholder in NEPAS, connecting it to the imported Jet A-1 supply chain as well as to the domestic refinery operation. Any sanctions submission targeting MPE should note that its portfolio encompasses both Mann and Chauk, and that the crude routing decision that channels Chauk and Yenangyaung field output exclusively to Mann is an MPE-level supply chain decision. See Profile 1.1. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Jan 2025; MOEP Website)
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Company Limited (NEPAS): The primary documented payer for tanker truck deliveries of aviation fuel to and from Mann Refinery during the October 2021 to January 2022 period covered by the NEPAS invoice dataset. NEPAS contracted Cargo Link Petroleum for this distribution work. NEPAS is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022)
•Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited (CLPL): The tanker truck operator contracted by NEPAS and, according to Amnesty International, apparently contracted separately by the military for air base deliveries. CLPL's delivery datasets are the primary evidentiary basis for Mann's documented role in the aviation fuel distribution network. See Profile 1.7. (AI Nov 2022)
•Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. / Interra Resources Ltd.: Singapore-listed Interra Resources, through its subsidiary Goldpetrol, holds concessions for the Chauk and Yenangyaung oilfields that provide crude feedstock to Mann Refinery by barge along the Ayeyarwady River. The relationship between Goldpetrol's upstream crude production and Mann's refining and distribution function is documented in JFM's January 2025 report. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Myanmar military air bases supplied via Mann: Documented air bases to which Jet A-1 was distributed from Mann Refinery include Hmawbi (Yangon Region), Meikhtila, Monywa, Nay Pyi Taw, Tada-U (Mandalay Region), and Taungoo (Bago Region). Amnesty International linked Hmawbi and Taungoo directly to air strikes constituting war crimes in its Mann Refinery dataset, and linked four of the five air bases documented as departure points for attacks between March 2021 and August 2022 to attacks constituting war crimes: Hmawbi, Magway, Tada-U, and Taungoo. (AI Nov 2022)

Sanctions gap: Neither Mann Refinery nor its managing entity MPE has been designated by any jurisdiction. Mann's role as a military-controlled storage and distribution hub for Jet A-1, confirmed by leaked documents covering December 2021 to January 2022, and the evidence of a post-coup surge in refinery activity documented by JFM in 2025, provide a strong evidentiary basis for designation under the jet fuel sector criteria operative in the US, UK, and other jurisdictions. JFM called for new coordinated sanctions targeting Mann in its February 2026 press release. MPE's unsanctioned status is a separate and longstanding gap: MPE is the majority shareholder of NEPAS and the managing entity of the military's primary remaining refinery, yet faces no targeted financial measures. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2026)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo report publicly documents Mann Refinery's role in the Jet A-1 distribution network for the first time, presenting leaked invoice and delivery dataset evidence and identifying Mann as a military-controlled storage node through which fuel reaches war-crime-linked air bases. Amnesty International calls on all states to suspend aviation fuel supplies to Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022)
•January 2025. Justice for Myanmar publishes its investigation into Goldpetrol and Interra Resources, which includes the most detailed public assessment to date of Mann Refinery's post-coup operational activity. The report presents night-time light analysis, crude feedstock supply chain data, and source testimony pointing to Mann as the primary source of a post-coup surge in domestic aviation fuel production from 2021 to mid-2023. (JFM Jan 2025)
•February 2026. Justice for Myanmar calls for new and coordinated sanctions targeting the junta's aviation fuel supply chain, naming Mann Refinery (Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex) as one of the entities for which designation is sought. (JFM Feb 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Designate Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex in all jurisdictions: The documentary evidence, spanning leaked NEPAS invoices, tanker truck delivery datasets, and independent night-time light analysis, collectively establishes Mann Refinery as a military-controlled node through which aviation fuel is both stored and distributed to air bases, and potentially produced. The US jet fuel sector determination of August 2023, which authorises OFAC to sanction any entity operating in Myanmar's jet fuel sector, provides the direct legal authority for designation without requiring additional criteria to be met. UK, EU, Canadian, and Australian Myanmar sanctions frameworks similarly cover entities that make aviation fuel available to the Myanmar security forces. JFM's February 2026 call for coordinated designation should be treated as an actionable submission. Civil society organisations should support this call with the AI Nov 2022 and JFM Jan 2025 evidentiary packages addressed to OFAC, OFSI, the EU, Global Affairs Canada, and the Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Designate Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) in all jurisdictions: MPE manages Mann Refinery and is the majority shareholder of NEPAS, making it the single entity with the deepest structural involvement in both the imported and domestically refined aviation fuel supply chains. Its designation has been called for by civil society across multiple reporting cycles yet it remains unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. Designation of MPE would apply financial pressure to the institutional backbone of both supply chains simultaneously. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Feb 2026)
•Investigate current operational status and consider targeting the feedstock supply chain: Night-time light analysis indicates Mann's refinery activity fell sharply after mid-2023. Whether this reflects reduced output, a shift in operations, maintenance, or a deliberate effort to reduce its signature is unknown. Investigators and sanctions authorities should seek updated source intelligence on current activity at the refinery. Separately, the crude supply chain linking Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. and Interra Resources Ltd. to Mann via the Chauk and Yenangyaung fields provides an upstream pressure point: with the Chauk refinery offline, Mann is the sole destination for that crude, meaning disruption to the feedstock supply would directly constrain Mann's throughput. The No. 2 Refinery (Chauk), though currently shutdown, is also an MPE asset and should be named alongside Mann in any comprehensive sanctions submission targeting MPE's refinery portfolio, to prevent it being reactivated as a fallback if Mann is designated. (JFM Jan 2025; MOEP Website)
