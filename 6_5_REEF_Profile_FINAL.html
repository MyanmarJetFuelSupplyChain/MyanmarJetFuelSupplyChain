REEF (formerly BALTIC HORIZON / PETRA)
Profile 6.5
Summary Assessment
REEF (IMO 9263382), formerly known as BALTIC HORIZON and before that PETRA, is a Guinea-flagged chemical/oil products tanker that made four confirmed deliveries of aviation fuel to Myanmar's military junta between October 2024 and December 2025. The vessel is part of Iran's shadow fleet network and has a documented history of transporting sanctioned fuel from Iran, as confirmed by OFAC. Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation and a concurrent Reuters investigation established through satellite imagery, AIS data analysis and shipping documents that REEF loaded fuel at Iran's Bandar Abbas port before delivering it to the Myan Oil Terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, while simultaneously broadcasting falsified AIS positions placing the vessel hundreds of kilometres away. The two confirmed cargo volumes total more than 31,000 metric tonnes across the October and December 2025 deliveries. The United States designated REEF (then named BALTIC HORIZON) on 27 June 2024 under the Iran Sanctions program (Executive Order 13846) for its history of transporting Iranian petrochemical products. REEF and NOBLE (Profile 6.6) are the only two vessels in the Myanmar aviation fuel ghost fleet to have been sanctioned before making their documented Myanmar deliveries, with both designated by OFAC on the same date. The UK, EU, Canada and Australia have not designated REEF. Justice For Myanmar called for coordinated designation across all five jurisdictions in February 2026. Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA (UAE) is the registered owner and Sea Route Ship Management FZE (UAE) is the ISM and commercial manager. Sea Route Ship Management FZE was also designated by OFAC on 27 June 2024 for the same Iran sanctions violations. Reporters were unable to reach the owners of REEF and NOBLE; an email listed as contact was not valid.

2
Iranian Fuel Origin and AIS Evasion

All four of REEF's Myanmar deliveries are attributed to Iranian fuel sources. Kpler, a commodity intelligence platform that tracks global fuel flows using AIS data, satellite imagery, customs information and other sources, assessed all of REEF's Myanmar shipments as originating from Iran. Amnesty International's satellite imagery analysis and a concurrent Reuters investigation confirmed the Iranian origin for at least two of the four deliveries through direct observation of the vessel at Bandar Abbas, Iran's principal oil export terminal in Hormozgan province. (AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)

REEF employed systematic AIS position falsification across its documented delivery cycles. Amnesty International's January 2026 investigation found that REEF's AIS data placed the vessel near Kuwait and near the UAE at the time of its presumed loading, while satellite imagery simultaneously placed it at Bandar Abbas with high confidence. REEF's AIS data displayed what Amnesty International described as "unnaturally geometric 'box-shaped' loitering patterns" over 750 kilometres from Bandar Abbas port, in areas where no vessels of similar size were present, indicating deliberate AIS manipulation or spoofing. (AI Jan 2026)

Reuters' January 2026 investigation documented the AIS falsification sequence for the September 2025 loading and October 2025 delivery in specific detail. On 15 September 2025, REEF's AIS signal placed the vessel near Iraq's Basrah Oil Terminal while satellite imagery showed no such vessel there. Satellite imagery confirmed that REEF was at Bandar Abbas, loading fuel. At 07:28 on 16 September 2025, REEF's AIS showed it approximately 330 kilometres from Bandar Abbas; just three minutes later the same signal showed the vessel at port, a journey that would normally take at least ten hours. After loading, REEF transited the Strait of Hormuz and resumed accurate AIS reporting temporarily, before spoofing again after rounding the southern tip of India. On 2 October 2025, REEF's falsified AIS signal showed it heading toward Chittagong, Bangladesh; later the same day, its real location pinged approximately 800 kilometres away at the Myan Oil Terminal near Yangon. The December 2025 delivery followed the same spoofing pattern, with AIS falsely showing a Basrah-to-Chittagong voyage while satellite imagery and shipping documents confirmed loading at Bandar Abbas and offloading at the Myan Oil Terminal on 9 December 2025. (Reuters Jan 2026)

Some of REEF's visits to the Myan Oil Terminal were independently confirmed by Myanmar Witness, a project of the Centre for Information Resilience focused on documenting human rights violations. As of late January 2026, SynMax Intelligence data showed REEF had loaded again and was heading back toward Yangon, indicating a potential fifth delivery at the time of publication. (Reuters Jan 2026)

3
Prior Sanctions History and Iran Shadow Fleet Context

REEF was designated by OFAC on 27 June 2024 under the Iran Sanctions program (Executive Order 13846), while operating under the name BALTIC HORIZON and flagged to Panama. The designation was linked to Sea Route Ship Management FZE, which was simultaneously designated for the same Iran sanctions violations. OFAC documented both vessels and Sea Route as having a history of transporting sanctioned fuel from Iran. The vessel was designated before it made any of its documented Myanmar aviation fuel deliveries: the first Myanmar delivery (delivery 1 in Section 1 above) occurred in October 2024, four months after the OFAC designation. (AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)

REEF is described in Reuters' January 2026 investigation as part of Iran's shadow fleet: a network of vessels used to covertly transport illicit cargo. The IRGC is documented as dominating the fuel-smuggling networks of which REEF forms a part. REEF changed its name and flag of registration multiple times, consistent with common shadow fleet evasion tactics. Reuters documented that the vessel was sanctioned, renamed REEF, and reflagged to Guinea following the OFAC designation. The continued Myanmar deliveries by a US-sanctioned vessel between October 2024 and December 2025 demonstrate both the limits of vessel-level designation without complementary measures, and the centrality of REEF and NOBLE to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain in this period. See Profile 6.6 for NOBLE, which Reuters described as REEF's "larger sister ship" and which was also sanctioned by OFAC on 27 June 2024 alongside REEF and Sea Route. (Reuters Jan 2026; AI Jan 2026)

7
Recommended Actions
•Urgently designate REEF across the UK, EU, Canada and Australia: REEF and NOBLE are the only two vessels in the Myanmar aviation fuel ghost fleet to have been designated by any government, yet both continued to make Myanmar deliveries after their OFAC designations in June 2024. REEF alone made four documented Myanmar deliveries after its designation, including two in 2025 with confirmed cargo volumes exceeding 31,000 metric tonnes combined. This demonstrates that vessel-level US designation alone is insufficient without matching designations across other major jurisdictions. Without UK, EU, Canadian and Australian designations, the US sanction is easily evaded through flag changes, operator changes and continued access to non-US financial, insurance and port services. JFM's February 2026 call for coordinated designation across all four remaining jurisdictions remains unimplemented. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
•Designate Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Route Ship Management FZE across all jurisdictions where not already designated: OFAC designated Sea Route Ship Management FZE on 27 June 2024 alongside REEF, but Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA (the registered owner) has not been designated by any government. JFM's February 2026 call covers both entities. Designation of the owner and manager alongside the vessel is necessary to close the evasion pathway. The common Hamriyah Free Zone address shared by Amethyst and Sea Hill Maritime SA (the owner of NOBLE) is a further basis for investigative attention; the co-location suggests a common beneficial owner or operator, and any future designation action should assess whether the two entities are connected. (JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage Guinea's maritime registry on REEF's flag registration: REEF is currently Guinea-flagged, having reflagged from Panama following its OFAC designation. The Guinea flag registry should be formally notified of REEF's documented history as a US-sanctioned vessel delivering aviation fuel to Myanmar. Guinea is a member of the International Maritime Organization and is subject to the obligations of SOLAS and other IMO conventions. Formal notification of flag authorities has previously prompted registry action against shadow fleet vessels in other sanctions-evasion contexts. (AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
•Pursue diplomatic engagement with Iran on IRGC-linked fuel exports to Myanmar: Reuters' January 2026 investigation confirmed that REEF loaded fuel at Iran's Bandar Abbas Oil Refinery, overseen by NIORDC, a subsidiary of NIOC (which OFAC identified as an agent or affiliate of the IRGC in 2012) and designated by OFAC since October 2020. The IRGC dominates Iran's fuel-smuggling networks. UN Special Rapporteur Tom Andrews explicitly called for the Iranian government to be held accountable following the Reuters investigation. Governments imposing Iran sanctions and engaging Iran bilaterally should include Myanmar aviation fuel exports as a specific point of engagement, alongside existing Iran sanctions pressure on IRGC-linked entities. (Reuters Jan 2026; AI Jan 2026)
•Engage UAE authorities regarding Sea Route Ship Management FZE, Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Hill Maritime SA, all domiciled in the Hamriyah Free Zone, Sharjah: The UAE holds domestic jurisdiction over the ISM and commercial manager of both REEF and NOBLE (Sea Route Ship Management FZE) and the registered owners of both vessels (Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA for REEF; Sea Hill Maritime SA for NOBLE). All three entities share a Hamriyah Free Zone address. UAE engagement would simultaneously address the corporate infrastructure behind both primary ghost fleet vessels responsible for Myanmar's current aviation fuel imports. The UAE removed itself from the FATF grey list in February 2024 and from the EU's high-risk countries list in July 2025, demonstrating an active commitment to AML/CFT compliance that civil society can leverage. Engagement should target the Hamriyah Free Zone Authority in respect of the entities' free zone corporate compliance obligations, and the UAE Executive Office of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Terrorism Financing, which coordinates AML/CFT policy across all sectors including commercial free zones. The UAE's 2025 AML Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025), which entered into force on 14 October 2025, expanded sanctions-evasion offences and lowered the evidentiary threshold for establishing AML liability, providing stronger domestic legal grounds for action. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
