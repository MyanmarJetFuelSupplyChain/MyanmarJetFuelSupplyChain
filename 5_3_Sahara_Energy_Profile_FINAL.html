Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd.
Profile 5.3
Summary Assessment
Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. is the Singapore-registered trading subsidiary of Sahara Group. Vietnamese customs data reviewed by Amnesty International identified it as the second-to-last seller in the January 2024 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar, valued at approximately USD 8 million, which transited the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal before delivery to Thilawa aboard HUITONG 78. Amnesty International found that Sahara Energy "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." Sahara Group publicly denied any involvement in the January 2024 shipment and stated that its last Myanmar delivery was in August 2022, directly contradicting the customs data. Sahara Energy did not respond to Amnesty International. It has not been sanctioned by any government and has not appeared in any documented shipment since.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Vietnamese customs data identified Sahara Energy as the second-to-last seller in the January 2024 shipment. Fuel sourced from the Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal was sold by Sahara Energy to a Vietnamese company, received at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, stored for between a few hours and several days, sold to the Myanmar purchaser, and loaded aboard HUITONG 78, which arrived at Thilawa on 14 January 2024. The shipment was valued at approximately USD 8 million. Amnesty International found that Sahara Energy "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." The same "should have been aware" language was applied to CNOOC Trading for the consecutive February 2024 shipment. This is a stronger finding than Amnesty International's treatment of BB Energy for the 2023 shipments, where it acknowledged uncertainty about actual knowledge. Sahara Energy did not respond to Amnesty International. (AI Jul 2024)

Three days after the report was published, Sahara Group issued a public statement calling the allegation "utterly unfounded and false" and asserting that Sahara Energy's last Myanmar delivery was in August 2022. The statement did not address or dispute the specific Vietnamese customs data on which Amnesty International's identification rests. Sahara Group's denial is directly contradicted by the January 2024 customs records. The statement separately disclosed that Sahara Energy's pre-August 2022 deliveries went directly to NEPAS, confirming prior supply into Myanmar's military-linked distribution network. (Sahara Group statement, 11 July 2024; AI Jul 2024)

2
Key Relationships
•Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal: The upstream storage terminal from which the fuel in the January 2024 shipment was traced before Sahara Energy sold it onward. Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal is operated by Royal Vopak and is located on Jurong Island, Singapore. Amnesty International identified this terminal as the immediately preceding stop before Sahara Energy's transaction in the supply chain. Vopak was named by Amnesty International in its January 2024 investigation as one of four companies with engaged UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations. See Profile 7.1. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, Vietnam): The terminal operator in Vietnam that received the fuel purchased from Sahara Energy by the Vietnamese intermediary company. The Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal served as the transit hub for all nine documented 2023 and 2024 Vietnam-transited shipments to Myanmar, including the January 2024 shipment in which Sahara Energy participated. Hai Linh is unsanctioned. See Profile 4.1. (AI Jul 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (IMO 9646479): The vessel that transported the January 2024 shipment from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon, arriving 14 January 2024. HUITONG 78 was subsequently renamed BARAAWE 1. It transported six of the seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments and both confirmed 2024 shipments. Per the West of England P&I Club, the vessel is owned by an affiliate of Chinese state-owned defence company China Aerospace Science and Industry Corp (CASIC). See Profile 6.1. (AI Jul 2024)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited / Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal (Thilawa, Yangon): The destination terminal in Myanmar to which the January 2024 shipment was delivered. During early 2024 the terminal was operating under the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited before arriving at its current name. See Profile 1.3. (AI Jul 2024)
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.: The Singapore-based trading company identified by Amnesty International as the second-to-last seller in the February 2024 shipment (row 19), the month after the Sahara Energy transaction. Vietnamese customs data applied the same "should have been aware" standard to CNOOC Trading as to Sahara Energy. The parallel treatment of the two Singapore-based traders in consecutive 2024 shipments is directly relevant to any designation submissions for either entity. See Profile 5.4. (AI Jul 2024)
•Unnamed Vietnamese intermediary company: The direct purchaser from Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. in the January 2024 shipment. This company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal and then sold it to the Myanmar-side purchaser. Its identity is not disclosed in any reviewed source. (AI Jul 2024)

Sanctions gap: Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. has not been sanctioned by any government despite Amnesty International's July 2024 finding that it should have been aware the fuel it sold would be transported to Vietnam for transit purposes only. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides legal authority for a designation without requiring proof of actual knowledge of the Myanmar end-destination, and the constructive awareness finding drawn from Vietnamese customs data provides a stronger evidentiary basis for a designation submission than is available for the comparable 2023 BB Energy transactions. The same customs data methodology was applied to both Sahara Energy and CNOOC Trading in consecutive shipments, making a joint or parallel designation submission for both Singapore-based traders the most coherent advocacy strategy. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

•11 July 2024. Sahara Group publishes a public denial, calling the allegation "utterly unfounded and false" and asserting its last Myanmar delivery was August 2022. •No subsequent advocacy or designation. Sahara Energy does not appear in any reviewed source after July 2024. The supply chain shifted to direct Iranian deliveries from mid-2024, superseding the Vietnamese transit model. No designation or regulatory action has been documented as of April 2026. (AI Jan 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Submit a joint designation referral for Sahara Energy and CNOOC Trading to OFAC and other jurisdictions. Both were identified in consecutive January and February 2024 shipments using identical "should have been aware" language from Vietnamese customs data. A joint submission reinforces the systematic pattern. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination provides the legal authority without requiring proof of actual knowledge. Equivalent frameworks in the UK, EU, Canada, and Australia should be assessed simultaneously. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Press Sahara Group to address the Vietnamese customs data directly. Sahara Group's public denial did not engage with the specific customs records on which Amnesty International's findings rest. Civil society should write to Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. in Singapore and to the Sahara Group parent requesting a response to the specific Vietnamese customs data, disclosure of due diligence applied to the January 2024 Vietnamese purchaser, and confirmation of whether Sahara Energy has ceased business with the Cai Mep supply chain. The OFAC sector determination's sanctions risk should be explicitly referenced. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Refer the Geneva branch to SECO. Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. operates an active Geneva branch (cours de Rive 13-15, 1204 Genève; Swiss UID: CHE-115.078.589) registered for international oil products trading. Civil society should refer the matter to the Swiss State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) to assess whether any Swiss export controls or sanctions obligations were engaged by the Geneva branch's activities in connection with the documented Myanmar supply chain. (AI Jul 2024)
