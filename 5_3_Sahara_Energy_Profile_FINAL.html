Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd.
Profile 5.3
Summary Assessment
Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. is the Singapore-registered trading subsidiary of Sahara Group, a privately owned international energy and infrastructure conglomerate with operations across more than 40 countries in Africa, Asia, Europe and the Middle East. Vietnamese customs data, reviewed by Amnesty International and published in July 2024, identified Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel in one documented shipment that transited Vietnam before reaching Myanmar: the January 2024 shipment aboard HUITONG 78, which arrived at the Thilawa terminal in Yangon on 14 January 2024 and was valued at approximately USD 8 million. In that transaction, Sahara Energy sold fuel to a Vietnamese company, which received it at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. The fuel was then sold on to the Myanmar purchaser and transported directly to Thilawa. Amnesty International determined, on the basis of Vietnamese customs data, that Sahara Energy "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. did not respond to Amnesty International's contact. It has not been sanctioned by any government and does not appear in the opensanctions database. The January 2024 shipment is the only documented instance of Sahara Energy's involvement in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain
The January 2024 shipment

Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. is identified by Vietnamese customs data, as reviewed and published by Amnesty International in July 2024, as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel in the January 2024 shipment to Myanmar. The full supply chain for that shipment, as documented by Amnesty International, ran as follows: fuel was sourced from the Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal; Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. sold it to a Vietnamese company; that Vietnamese company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. in southern Vietnam; after storage of between a few hours and several days at Hai Linh's terminal, the fuel was sold on to the Myanmar purchaser and loaded aboard HUITONG 78; the vessel then transported the fuel directly to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 14 January 2024. The shipment was valued at approximately USD 8 million. (AI Jul 2024)

Constructive awareness finding

Amnesty International's assessment of Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. goes further than its treatment of BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. in the comparable 2023 supply chain. While Amnesty International acknowledged uncertainty about whether BB Energy knew the fuel it sold in 2023 would reach Myanmar, it applied a stronger standard to Sahara Energy for the January 2024 shipment: Vietnamese customs data showed that Sahara Energy "should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Viet Nam for transit purposes only." This constructive awareness finding, drawn directly from customs data rather than presented as a question, establishes Sahara Energy's awareness of the transit nature of the transaction as a matter of documented record. Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. did not respond to Amnesty International's contact in connection with the July 2024 investigation. (AI Jul 2024)

Significance of the "should have been aware" standard: The same "should have been aware" language was applied by Amnesty International to both Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. (January 2024 shipment) and CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (February 2024 shipment). This language, derived from Vietnamese customs data in both cases, establishes a higher level of documented culpability than the uncertainty acknowledged for the 2023 BB Energy transactions. Under the August 2023 OFAC sector determination, designation does not require proof of actual knowledge of the Myanmar end-destination. Under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, companies are expected to conduct due diligence to identify adverse human rights impacts throughout their value chains. A constructive awareness finding strengthens both the OFAC and UN Guiding Principles cases substantially. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
Sahara Group's public denial and the evidentiary conflict

On 11 July 2024, three days after Amnesty International published its report, Sahara Group published a public statement on its corporate website (available at: https://www.sahara-group.com/2024/07/11/re-shipment-of-jet-a1-to-myanmar-2/) calling the allegation "utterly unfounded and false." The statement asserted that Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. (referred to as SIL) "at no time, directly or indirectly supplied Jet A1 to the military in Myanmar." It further stated that the last supply of Jet A-1 to Myanmar by SIL was in August 2022, and that SIL made a business decision after that delivery to stop further deliveries of Jet A-1 and all petroleum products into Myanmar. The statement also disclosed that the last three deliveries by SIL prior to August 2022 were made directly to NEPAS (National Energy PUMA Aviation Services Ltd.), described as Trafigura's local trading arm in Myanmar. (Sahara Group statement, 11 July 2024)

Sahara Group's denial is directly contradicted by Amnesty International's Vietnamese customs data, which identifies Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller in the January 2024 shipment. The statement's claim that the last delivery was in August 2022 is flatly inconsistent with the January 2024 supply chain documentation. This conflict is a central feature of the evidentiary record. Amnesty International's findings rest on Vietnamese customs data reviewed independently of Sahara's accounts. The statement did not address or dispute the specific Vietnamese customs data on which Amnesty International's January 2024 identification is based. The NEPAS disclosure is separately significant: it confirms that Sahara Energy was supplying fuel into Myanmar's military-linked aviation fuel distribution network prior to August 2022, even if Sahara characterises those deliveries as legitimate commercial transactions with a licensed distributor. (AI Jul 2024; Sahara Group statement, 11 July 2024)

Key Relationships
•Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal: The upstream storage terminal from which the fuel in the January 2024 shipment was traced before Sahara Energy sold it onward. Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal is operated by Royal Vopak and is located on Jurong Island, Singapore. Amnesty International identified this terminal as the immediately preceding stop before Sahara Energy's transaction in the supply chain. Vopak was named by Amnesty International in its January 2024 investigation as one of four companies with engaged UN Guiding Principles due diligence obligations. See Profile 7.1. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, Vietnam): The terminal operator in Vietnam that received the fuel purchased from Sahara Energy by the Vietnamese intermediary company. The Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal served as the transit hub for all nine documented 2023 and 2024 Vietnam-transited shipments to Myanmar, including the January 2024 shipment in which Sahara Energy participated. Hai Linh is unsanctioned. See Profile 4.1. (AI Jul 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (IMO 9646479): The vessel that transported the January 2024 shipment from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon, arriving 14 January 2024. HUITONG 78 was subsequently renamed BARAAWE 1. It transported six of the seven 2023 Vietnam-transited shipments and both confirmed 2024 shipments. Per the West of England P&I Club, the vessel is owned by an affiliate of Chinese state-owned defence company China Aerospace Science and Industry Corp (CASIC). See Profile 6.1. (AI Jul 2024)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited / Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal (Thilawa, Yangon): The destination terminal in Myanmar to which the January 2024 shipment was delivered. During early 2024 the terminal was operating under the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited before arriving at its current name. See Profile 1.3. (AI Jul 2024)
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.: The Singapore-based trading company identified by Amnesty International as the second-to-last seller in the February 2024 shipment (row 19), the month after the Sahara Energy transaction. Vietnamese customs data applied the same "should have been aware" standard to CNOOC Trading as to Sahara Energy. The parallel treatment of the two Singapore-based traders in consecutive 2024 shipments is directly relevant to any designation submissions for either entity. See Profile 5.4. (AI Jul 2024)
•Unnamed Vietnamese intermediary company: The direct purchaser from Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. in the January 2024 shipment. This company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal and then sold it to the Myanmar-side purchaser. Its identity is not disclosed in any reviewed source. (AI Jul 2024)

Sanctions gap: Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. has not been sanctioned by any government despite Amnesty International's July 2024 finding that it should have been aware the fuel it sold would be transported to Vietnam for transit purposes only. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides legal authority for a designation without requiring proof of actual knowledge of the Myanmar end-destination, and the constructive awareness finding drawn from Vietnamese customs data provides a stronger evidentiary basis for a designation submission than is available for the comparable 2023 BB Energy transactions. The same customs data methodology was applied to both Sahara Energy and CNOOC Trading in consecutive shipments, making a joint or parallel designation submission for both Singapore-based traders the most coherent advocacy strategy. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)

5
Recommended Actions
•Request disclosure from Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. and Sahara Group: Civil society should write to Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. at its Singapore office (1 Temasek Avenue #23-01, Millenia Tower, Singapore 039192) and to the Sahara Group parent requesting: confirmation of whether Sahara Energy had knowledge that the fuel it sold to a Vietnamese company in January 2024 would be transported onward to Myanmar; disclosure of what human rights due diligence it applied to the Vietnamese purchaser before the transaction; confirmation of what policies and operational controls Sahara Energy now applies to sales where a subsequent purchaser may transfer fuel to Myanmar or to entities associated with the Myanmar military; and confirmation of whether it has ceased any business with Hai Linh Co. Ltd. or with the Cai Mep terminal supply chain since its public naming in July 2024. Engagement should reference the August 2023 OFAC sector determination, which creates material sanctions risk for any company determined to operate in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy, including through indirect supply chains. Sahara Group also has a Geneva branch of its Singapore entity, providing a European jurisdiction for parallel engagement. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Submit a joint designation submission to OFAC covering Sahara Energy and CNOOC Trading: The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 authorises designation of any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy. Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. and CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. were identified by Vietnamese customs data in consecutive shipments (January and February 2024) using identical "should have been aware" language, making them natural candidates for a joint submission. The constructive awareness finding derived from Vietnamese customs data is the primary evidentiary basis and directly addresses the knowledge question. A joint submission would reinforce the systematic nature of the transit supply chain and demonstrate that both entities operated in a pattern, not in isolated instances. Sanctions authorities in the UK, EU, Canada and Australia should also assess whether equivalent frameworks apply to both entities' documented conduct. (AI Jul 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage the UK Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation and EU competent authorities: Civil society should assess whether the Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021 and equivalent EU sanctions frameworks apply to Sahara Energy's documented conduct and submit referrals to the UK Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation (OTSI) and, where applicable, EU competent authorities. These submissions should reference the July 2024 Amnesty International findings and the constructive awareness standard derived from Vietnamese customs data. (AI Jul 2024)
•Refer the matter to SECO (the Swiss State Secretariat for Economic Affairs): The Geneva branch of Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. (Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd., Singapour, succursale à Genève; Swiss UID: CHE-115.078.589; cours de Rive 13-15, 1204 Genève) is registered for the purpose of international oil products trading, shipping, and hedging, and remains active as of its most recent Swiss Official Gazette of Commerce publication in May 2023. Civil society should refer the matter to SECO for assessment of whether the Geneva branch was involved in any transactions that may have engaged Swiss export controls or sanctions obligations, given its registered purpose and continued active status. Switzerland is not part of the five-jurisdiction Myanmar sanctions coalition, but SECO has authority over Swiss export controls and can assess whether any Swiss legal obligations apply to the Geneva branch's activities in connection with the documented Myanmar-related supply chain. (AI Jul 2024)
•Monitor for renewed involvement: Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. is documented in one shipment only: the January 2024 transit via Cai Mep. The January 2026 Amnesty International investigation confirmed that the Vietnam transit route was superseded by ghost fleet deliveries from Iran and the UAE, but this does not preclude Sahara Energy from resuming involvement in an alternative supply chain route. Any renewed involvement by Sahara Energy in a Myanmar-bound aviation fuel supply chain should trigger immediate re-engagement on all the above fronts. (AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
