Prima Marine Public Company Limited
Profile 6.11
Summary Assessment
Prima Marine Public Company Limited (SET: PRM) is a Thai marine transportation company headquartered in Bangkok, Thailand, and a subsidiary of Nathalin Group Co., Ltd. Prima Marine is the beneficial owner of Big Sea 104 (IMO 9635535), an oil products tanker that transported 12,592 MT of Jet A-1 aviation fuel to the former Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, in October 2022. The fuel was supplied by Bangchak Corporation Plc (Profile 5.5) and loaded at the Bangchak Oil Refinery at Bangkok Port, Thailand. Prima Marine did not respond to any letters from Amnesty International during the investigation that documented this shipment. Prima Marine made no public commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel transport and has not been identified as a vessel owner in any subsequent Amnesty International investigation into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. Neither Prima Marine Public Company Limited nor Big Sea 104 has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Prima Marine Public Company Limited is the beneficial owner of Big Sea 104 (IMO 9635535), a Thai-flagged oil products tanker that carried one documented post-coup Jet A-1 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar in October 2022. The Big Sea 104 departed the Bangchak Oil Refinery at Bangkok Port, Thailand, on or about 8 October 2022 and arrived at the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, on approximately 15 October 2022, offloading 12,592 MT of Jet A-1. The fuel supplier for this shipment was Bangchak Corporation Plc (Profile 5.5). See the master shipment table for the confirmed details of this shipment. (AI Mar 2023)

Prima Marine's role in this shipment is that of vessel owner: it owned and managed Big Sea 104 but did not supply the fuel. At the time of the October 2022 delivery, the PEAS terminal was still under the operational control of Puma Energy's Myanmar subsidiary. The sale of the terminal to the Shoon Group was completed in December 2022. Amnesty International sent letters to Prima Marine regarding its vessel's role in the October 2022 shipment. Prima Marine did not respond to any of these letters. Prima Marine made no public commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel transport at any point following the investigation. (AI Mar 2023)

Prima Marine has not been identified as a vessel owner in any of the three Amnesty International investigations into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain published after March 2023. The supply chain documented in post-2023 investigations shifted progressively toward Vietnamese intermediary storage routes and subsequently toward Iran-linked ghost ships and AIS manipulation, moving away from the direct-load model under which the October 2022 Big Sea 104 shipment occurred. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)

Key Relationships
•Big Sea 104 (IMO 9635535): Oil products tanker, built 2012, flag of Thailand, 6,424 DWT, 4,347 GT, approximately 112 metres LOA, 16.3 metres beam. Beneficial owner: Prima Marine Public Company Limited. P&I insurance: Shipowners' Mutual Protection and Indemnity Association (Luxembourg), affiliated with the Shipowners' Club. Amnesty International documented the Big Sea 104's departure from the Bangchak Oil Refinery at Bangkok Port on or about 8 October 2022 and its arrival at the former PEAS terminal, Thilawa, on approximately 15 October 2022. Neither Prima Marine nor the Shipowners' Club responded to Amnesty International's letters. Big Sea 104 is not identified in any reviewed source as a sanctioned or flagged entity in connection with Myanmar. (AI Mar 2023)
•Bangchak Corporation Plc (Profile 5.5): The confirmed fuel supplier for the October 2022 Big Sea 104 shipment. Bangchak Corporation Plc operates the Bangchak Oil Refinery at Bangkok Port, which was the load terminal for this shipment. Bangchak did not respond to Amnesty International's letters. The relationship between Prima Marine as vessel owner and Bangchak as fuel supplier illustrates the structure of the October 2022 transaction: both are Thai companies operating through Thai port infrastructure. (AI Mar 2023)
•Former PEAS terminal, Thilawa (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The delivery point for the October 2022 Big Sea 104 shipment. At the time of delivery, the terminal was still under Puma Energy's operational control through the NEPAS joint venture. Puma Energy completed the sale of the terminal to the Shoon Group in December 2022. The terminal now operates under the Shoon Energy and Myan Oil Terminal network and continues to receive aviation fuel deliveries. See the master shipment table and Profiles 5.1 and 2.1 for the NEPAS and PEAS terminal network. (AI Mar 2023)
•Thai Oil Public Company Limited (Profile 5.12): Prima Marine acquired Thaioil Marine Company Limited, the wholly-owned marine subsidiary of Thai Oil, through its subsidiary Phurich Marine Company Limited in April 2021 for approximately THB 1.6 billion. Thaioil Marine was subsequently renamed Truth Maritime Company Limited. This acquisition predated the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain investigations and is documented in Profile 5.12. The Truth Maritime / Thaioil Marine vessels are not implicated in any Myanmar aviation fuel shipment; the Big Sea 104 is a separate vessel in Prima Marine's tanker fleet. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)

•1 March 2023. Amnesty International, Global Witness and Burma Campaign UK publish their follow-up investigation, identifying Prima Marine as the beneficial owner of Big Sea 104 and documenting the October 2022 Bangchak-to-Thilawa shipment. The report notes that Prima Marine did not respond to any letters from Amnesty International. Prima Marine makes no public commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel transport following publication. (AI Mar 2023)
•23 August 2023. OFAC issues a determination under Executive Order 14014 that the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy falls within the scope of Burma-related sanctions, creating legal authority to designate any foreign person operating in that sector without requiring proof of direct military end-use. The October 2022 Big Sea 104 shipment predated this determination; any future Prima Marine vessel delivering aviation fuel to Myanmar's supply chain would take place in a materially different legal environment.
•January 2024, July 2024 and 27 January 2026. Three subsequent Amnesty International investigations document Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain: "Myanmar: New Data Suggests Military Still Importing Fuel for Deadly Air Strikes Despite Sanctions" (January 2024), "Myanmar: Reckless Shipments of Jet Fuel Continue as Air Strikes Multiply" (July 2024), and "Myanmar: Jet Fuel Used in Deadly Air Strikes Flowing in on Ghost Ships with Suspected Links to Iran" (27 January 2026). Prima Marine does not appear in any of these investigations. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Engage Prima Marine directly to obtain a formal statement of its current Myanmar aviation fuel policy and to disclose any due diligence measures applied when accepting the charter for the October 2022 shipment: Prima Marine did not respond to any of Amnesty International's letters during the investigation that documented the Big Sea 104 shipment. No public statement from Prima Marine addressing the October 2022 delivery, its due diligence practices as vessel owner when accepting charters for cargo routes involving conflict-affected jurisdictions, or its current position on Myanmar aviation fuel transport has been identified in any reviewed source. As a SET-listed company operating oil product tankers across Southeast Asia, Prima Marine is an appropriate target for civil society and institutional investor engagement on the human rights due diligence framework it applies when accepting charters for cargo routes that involve jurisdictions under active military conflict. The August 2023 OFAC jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the current legal frame: any future Prima Marine vessel delivering aviation fuel to Myanmar's supply chain would expose Prima Marine to designation under BURMA-EO14014 without requiring proof of direct military end-use. (AI Mar 2023)
•Monitor Prima Marine's vessel fleet for any future involvement in Myanmar aviation fuel delivery: Prima Marine operates a fleet of oil product tankers across Southeast Asia, the Gulf, and internationally and has made no documented commitment to exclude Myanmar from its operating areas for aviation fuel transport. The supply chain documented in post-2023 investigations has shifted toward ghost ships with disabled AIS transponders and ship-to-ship transfers in international waters, making vessel-level monitoring more difficult. However, any departure of a Prima Marine vessel from any regional load terminal subsequently confirmed to have delivered aviation fuel to Myanmar should be treated as a matter for immediate engagement with Prima Marine, referral to relevant sanctions authorities, and engagement with the SET and Thai Securities and Exchange Commission regarding Prima Marine's sustainability and human rights due diligence disclosures. Civil society should maintain monitoring of Prima Marine vessel AIS records using shipping data tools and cross-reference any Myanmar port calls or departures from known aviation fuel terminals in Thailand, Singapore, and Malaysia. (AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2026)
