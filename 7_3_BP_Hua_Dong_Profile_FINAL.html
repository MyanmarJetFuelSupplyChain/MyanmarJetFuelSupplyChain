BP Hua Dong Terminal, Ningbo, China
Profile 7.3
Summary Assessment
The BP Hua Dong terminal at Ningbo port, China, is the upstream load point for the February 2024 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar. Fuel sourced here was sold by CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. to a Vietnamese company, transited the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd., and was delivered to Thilawa aboard HUITONG 78 on 29 February 2024. The shipment was valued at approximately USD 8 million. Amnesty International stated it was unable to identify the company that controls this terminal. The name may suggest a connection to BP plc, which has had joint ventures in Ningbo, but no reviewed source confirms this. No controlling entity has been identified and no advocacy action has been possible as a result.

Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

The February 2024 supply chain ran from the BP Hua Dong terminal in Ningbo through CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. to a Vietnamese company at Hai Linh's Cai Mep terminal, then aboard HUITONG 78 to Thilawa. It is one of two mainland China terminals in the documented supply chain, alongside the CNOOC Huizhou terminal (Profile 7.4). The controlling entity of the BP Hua Dong terminal was not identified in any reviewed source. Without an identified operator, no corporate engagement, designation submission, or regulatory intervention can be directed at this terminal. Identifying the controlling entity is the primary outstanding investigative task for this supply chain node. (AI Jul 2024)

Key Relationships
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.4): The identified fuel trader for the February 2024 shipment. Vietnamese customs data reviewed by Amnesty International identifies CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel in this shipment, selling to a Vietnamese company after the fuel had been sourced at the BP Hua Dong terminal. Amnesty International found that customs data showed CNOOC Trading should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Vietnam for transit purposes only. CNOOC Trading is the only named entity in the February 2024 supply chain that can be connected to both the BP Hua Dong terminal and the downstream Vietnam-to-Myanmar delivery leg. (AI Jul 2024)
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1): The operator of the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam, which served as the intermediate staging point between the BP Hua Dong terminal and the final Myanmar delivery. The Vietnamese company that purchased fuel from CNOOC Trading received it at Hai Linh's terminal before HUITONG 78 loaded it for the final leg to Thilawa. (AI Jul 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (Profile 6.1): The vessel that transported the February 2024 shipment from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 29 February 2024. HUITONG 78 is the only vessel documented as having made a delivery with the BP Hua Dong terminal as its upstream origin. (AI Jul 2024)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•July 2024. Amnesty International traces the February 2024 shipment to the BP Hua Dong terminal and states it was unable to identify the controlling entity. This is the only documented reference to this terminal in any reviewed source. No advocacy action has followed. (AI Jul 2024)
4
Recommended Actions
•Identify the controlling entity of the BP Hua Dong terminal. This is the primary outstanding investigative gap. Chinese corporate registry data, Ningbo port authority records, and petroleum industry licensing databases are the most direct sources. If the terminal is controlled by BP plc, a BP joint venture, or CNOOC group, a specific entity becomes the target for due diligence engagement and, if warranted, sanctions advocacy. (AI Jul 2024)
•Engage Chinese authorities on end-use screening at Ningbo port. The terminal falls within the jurisdiction of the Ningbo Maritime Safety Administration and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce. Civil society should request that petroleum exports to intermediary destinations with documented Myanmar supply chain connections be subject to end-use declaration requirements. The CNOOC Trading "should have been aware" finding from the same shipment provides the evidentiary hook. (AI Jul 2024)
•Once the controlling entity is identified, engage it on UN Guiding Principles obligations. If connected to BP plc, engagement should proceed through BP's existing human rights and sustainability governance structures. If connected to CNOOC group, engagement should be directed to the relevant group entity and to Chinese authorities with oversight of state-owned enterprise human rights due diligence. (AI Jul 2024)
