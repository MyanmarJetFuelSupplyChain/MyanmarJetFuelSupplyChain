BP Hua Dong Terminal, Ningbo, China
Profile 7.3
Summary Assessment
The BP Hua Dong terminal at Ningbo port, Hangzhou Bay, China is the upstream load point for the February 2024 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar's military junta, as documented by Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation. It is one of two terminals identified so far in the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain located in mainland China, and the only reviewed source to document its connection to the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain is Amnesty International's July 2024 report. The full supply chain for the February 2024 shipment ran as follows: fuel was sourced at the BP Hua Dong terminal in Ningbo; CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. sold it to a Vietnamese company; that Vietnamese company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. in southern Vietnam; the fuel was then loaded aboard HUITONG 78 and transported to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 29 February 2024. The shipment was valued at approximately USD 8 million. Amnesty International stated it was unable to identify the company that controls the BP Hua Dong terminal. The name "BP Hua Dong" may suggest a connection to BP plc, whose Chinese operations have included joint ventures in Ningbo and elsewhere, but no reviewed source identifies BP plc or any specific entity as the owner or operator of this terminal. No entity associated with this terminal has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar, and no controlling company has been identified against whom any advocacy action could be directed as of April 2026.

Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

The BP Hua Dong terminal at Ningbo port is the upstream origin point for the February 2024 aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar, as documented by Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation. It is one of two terminals identified so far in the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain located in mainland China, alongside the CNOOC terminal in Huizhou (Profile 7.4). The full supply chain for this shipment, as documented by Amnesty International, ran as follows: fuel was sourced at the BP Hua Dong terminal in Ningbo; CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.4) sold it to a Vietnamese company; that Vietnamese company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1) in southern Vietnam; after storage at Hai Linh's terminal, the fuel was sold on to the Myanmar purchaser and loaded aboard HUITONG 78 (Profile 6.1); the vessel then transported the fuel directly to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 29 February 2024. The shipment was valued at approximately USD 8 million. (AI Jul 2024) For cross-reference with all documented jet fuel shipments, see the master shipment table.

Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation explicitly stated it was unable to identify the company that controls the BP Hua Dong terminal. This makes the BP Hua Dong terminal the only upstream terminal in the documented supply chain for which no controlling entity has been established in any reviewed source. The absence of an identified operator means that no corporate due diligence engagement, sanctions advocacy or regulatory intervention can be directed at a specific entity in connection with this terminal at this time. (AI Jul 2024)

Key Relationships
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.4): The identified fuel trader for the February 2024 shipment. Vietnamese customs data reviewed by Amnesty International identifies CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller of aviation fuel in this shipment, selling to a Vietnamese company after the fuel had been sourced at the BP Hua Dong terminal. Amnesty International found that customs data showed CNOOC Trading should have been aware that the fuel was transported to Vietnam for transit purposes only. CNOOC Trading is the only named entity in the February 2024 supply chain that can be connected to both the BP Hua Dong terminal and the downstream Vietnam-to-Myanmar delivery leg. (AI Jul 2024)
•Hai Linh Co. Ltd. (Profile 4.1): The operator of the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam, which served as the intermediate staging point between the BP Hua Dong terminal and the final Myanmar delivery. The Vietnamese company that purchased fuel from CNOOC Trading received it at Hai Linh's terminal before HUITONG 78 loaded it for the final leg to Thilawa. (AI Jul 2024)
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (Profile 6.1): The vessel that transported the February 2024 shipment from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam to the former PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, arriving on 29 February 2024. HUITONG 78 is the only vessel documented as having made a delivery with the BP Hua Dong terminal as its upstream origin. (AI Jul 2024)

Recommended Actions
•Investigate the ownership and control of the BP Hua Dong terminal as a priority research objective: The controlling entity of the BP Hua Dong terminal is the primary evidentiary gap in the February 2024 supply chain. Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation was unable to identify this entity. Investigative journalists, civil society researchers and government agencies with access to Chinese corporate registry data, port authority records and petroleum industry licensing databases should prioritise establishing whether the terminal is controlled by BP plc, a BP plc joint venture partner, CNOOC group, or another entity. Identifying the controlling company would enable targeted due diligence engagement and, if warranted, sanctions advocacy. (AI Jul 2024)
•Engage Chinese authorities on end-use screening for petroleum exports from Ningbo port: The BP Hua Dong terminal is within the jurisdiction of Chinese port and maritime authorities. Civil society should engage the Ningbo Maritime Safety Administration and the Chinese Ministry of Commerce to request that petroleum exports from Ningbo port to intermediary destinations with documented Myanmar supply chain connections be subject to end-use declaration requirements. Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation identified CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. as the trader that moved fuel from this terminal onward and found that customs data showed CNOOC Trading should have been aware that the fuel was destined for Vietnam as a transit point only. (AI Jul 2024)
•If a controlling entity is identified, engage it on its human rights due diligence obligations: Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation named China, alongside Vietnam and Singapore, as a state bearing responsibility for the continuing supply of aviation fuel to Myanmar's military. Once the controlling entity of the BP Hua Dong terminal is identified, it should be engaged on its obligations under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, including the implementation of enhanced customer screening and end-use declaration requirements for petroleum products exported through the terminal. If the controlling entity is found to be a subsidiary or joint venture of BP plc, engagement should proceed through BP plc's existing human rights and sustainability governance structures as a publicly listed company. (AI Jul 2024)
