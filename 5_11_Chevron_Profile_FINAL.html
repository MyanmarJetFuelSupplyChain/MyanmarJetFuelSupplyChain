Chevron Corporation
Profile 5.11
Summary Assessment
Chevron Corporation (NYSE: CVX) is a US-headquartered multinational integrated energy company. Chevron is the confirmed supplier of one post-coup aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar: 13,310 MT of Jet A-1 loaded at the Singapore Refining Company (SRC) terminal on Jurong Island in February 2022 and delivered to the Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal at Thilawa, Yangon, aboard the Super Hero (IMO 9341330), a vessel owned by Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10). The SRC terminal is a refinery jointly owned by Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. (50%) and Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd. (50%), which is itself a wholly-owned subsidiary of PetroChina. Chevron confirmed the shipment to Amnesty International, stated that its customer in Singapore had provided assurances the fuel was for commercial use only, and described its standard counterparty due diligence process. Chevron explicitly confirmed it was involved in no other post-coup Myanmar aviation fuel shipment. Unlike Thai Oil, Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar and Pan Ocean Co., Ltd., Chevron made no documented public commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel sales. However, Chevron has not been identified as a supplier in any Amnesty International investigation into Myanmar aviation fuel published after November 2022. Neither Chevron Corporation nor Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Chevron Corporation confirmed to Amnesty International that it had sold only a single parcel of Jet A-1 fuel, through a customer in Singapore, that was ultimately delivered to Myanmar. The shipment loaded at the SRC terminal on Jurong Island between 15 and 18 February 2022 and was carried to the PEAS terminal at Thilawa, Yangon aboard the Super Hero (IMO 9341330), a vessel owned by Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10). The Super Hero arrived at the PEAS terminal on or about 22 to 23 February 2022 and offloaded 13,310 MT of Jet A-1. See the master shipment table for the confirmed details of this shipment. (AI Nov 2022)

The SRC terminal from which the shipment loaded is a refinery on Jurong Island, Singapore, jointly owned by Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. (50%) and Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd. (50%). Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd. is a wholly-owned subsidiary of PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.8). SRC has a crude processing capacity of approximately 290,000 barrels per day and produces jet fuel as one of its primary outputs. At the time of the February 2022 shipment, Chevron held its 50% stake in SRC through Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. Chevron initiated a process to sell this stake in mid-2025, appointing Morgan Stanley to manage the sale. As of January 2026, Chevron was in final-round discussions with Glencore and Eneos; the transaction had not been confirmed as completed in reviewed sources as of April 2026. (AI Nov 2022)

Chevron's customer for the February 2022 shipment was not identified by name in Chevron's correspondence with Amnesty International, but Amnesty International's independently obtained evidence identifies the Myanmar importer as NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd., Profile 2.1). At the time of the February 2022 shipment, NEPAS was a joint venture in which Puma Energy Irrawaddy Aviation Pte. Ltd. (a subsidiary of Puma Energy, itself majority-owned by Trafigura) held 49%, and the military-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) held 51%. NEPAS was the documented import vehicle for Myanmar's military aviation fuel and managed storage facilities at ten airports across Myanmar. Chevron stated that its Singapore customer had provided assurance the fuel was for commercial purposes only and that it would be resold to a commercial airline under a term supply contract. Chevron did not address whether that commercial airline was connected to or controlled by the Myanmar military. (AI Nov 2022)

In its statement to Amnesty International, Chevron described its due diligence as comprising: counterparty screening against US, EU, UK and UN sanctions programmes to validate that the customer was not a denied party; and actively seeking contractual warranties from customers that the product was for commercial use only and would not be supplied to the Myanmar military. Amnesty International assessed that the contractual warranty and sanctions-screening approach Chevron described was inadequate as a mechanism for ensuring aviation fuel was not reaching Myanmar's military: the end-use of the fuel at the military level could not be excluded on the basis of assurances provided by an intermediate trader in Singapore. (AI Nov 2022)

Chevron explicitly confirmed that it was not involved in the April 2021 Yu Dong shipment (the first post-coup delivery to the PEAS terminal) or any other Myanmar aviation fuel shipment beyond the one February 2022 consignment. Multiple other shipments loaded at the SRC terminal (see the master shipment table) had suppliers that were not identified as Chevron. Chevron has not been identified as a supplier in any of the four Amnesty International investigations into Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain published after November 2022. (AI Nov 2022)

3
Key Relationships
•Singapore Refining Company Pte. Ltd. (SRC), Jurong Island, Singapore: The load terminal for the February 2022 Chevron Myanmar shipment. SRC is a 50/50 joint venture between Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. and Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd., the latter wholly owned by PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.8). SRC operates a refinery on Jurong Island with a crude processing capacity of approximately 290,000 barrels per day and produces jet fuel among its primary outputs. SRC was the load terminal for multiple documented Myanmar aviation fuel shipments across 2021 and 2022; Chevron confirmed it was the supplier for the February 2022 shipment only. See the master shipment table for all SRC-loaded deliveries. Importantly, Chevron's 50% joint venture partner at SRC, Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd., was itself the named seller in a December 2021 Jet A-1 shipment to Asia Sun Trading. Amnesty International obtained a Ministry of Defence letter sent to the Myanma Port Authority confirming that fuel sold by SPC to Asia Sun Trading was required for use in the Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s aircraft and helicopters. This means Chevron's JV partner at the same terminal was supplying Myanmar's military aviation fuel chain approximately two months before Chevron's own February 2022 shipment. Chevron initiated the sale of its 50% SRC stake in mid-2025 as part of a broader Asia downstream restructuring. (AI Nov 2022)
•Super Hero (IMO 9341330) and Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10): The Super Hero is the vessel that carried the February 2022 Chevron shipment from the SRC terminal to the PEAS terminal at Thilawa. Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. is the beneficial owner and commercial manager of the Super Hero; POS Maritime TC S.A. is the registered owner. Pan Ocean confirmed following the Deadly Cargo investigation that it would no longer allow its vessels to transport aviation fuel to Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
•PEAS terminal, Thilawa / NEPAS (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The PEAS terminal at Thilawa was the delivery point for the February 2022 shipment. At the time of delivery, the terminal was operated by Puma Energy through the NEPAS joint venture. Amnesty International's independently obtained evidence identifies NEPAS as the confirmed customer for the February 2022 shipment. At the time of the shipment, NEPAS was a joint venture in which Puma Energy Irrawaddy Aviation Pte. Ltd. held 49% and the military-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) held 51%. Chevron did not name its customer in its correspondence with Amnesty International. See the master shipment table and Profiles 5.1 and 2.1 for the NEPAS / PEAS terminal network. (AI Nov 2022)
•Yadana natural gas project, Myanmar: Separate from its aviation fuel supply chain role, Chevron held a participating interest in Myanmar's Yadana natural gas field through its subsidiary Unocal Myanmar Offshore Company (UMOC), alongside TotalEnergies, PTTEP and the military-controlled Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE, Profile 9.2). TotalEnergies withdrew from Yadana in July 2022, and its stake was redistributed proportionally among the remaining partners, increasing Chevron's interest to 41.1%. Chevron announced its intention to exit in January 2022 and agreed to relinquish its stake in February 2023. The withdrawal was effective 1 April 2024, with all processes completed by 5 April 2024 as confirmed by PTTEP to the Thai Stock Exchange. Chevron's redistributed stake was absorbed by the remaining shareholders: PTTEP's interest increased to 62.96% and MOGE received the balance. Chevron's Yadana exit is separate from and predates its SRC stake sale process.
•Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd. / PetroChina (Profile 5.8): SRC's co-owner. Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd. is wholly owned by PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. and holds the remaining 50% of SRC. PetroChina holds the right of first refusal on Chevron's SRC stake under the joint venture agreement. Profile 5.8 documents Singapore Petroleum Company's confirmed supply of a December 2021 Jet A-1 shipment to Asia Sun Trading, also loaded at SRC. A Ministry of Defence letter obtained by Amnesty International confirmed that fuel was for Tatmadaw (Air Force) use. SPC did not respond to Amnesty International's correspondence. (AI Nov 2022)
4
Advocacy Interventions
•February 2022. A shipment of 13,310 MT of Jet A-1 aviation fuel loads at the SRC terminal on Jurong Island and is delivered to the PEAS terminal at Thilawa aboard the Super Hero. The customer for the shipment is a Singapore entity; Amnesty International independently identifies NEPAS as the confirmed end-customer in Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022)
•5 September and 18 October 2022. Amnesty International contacts Chevron during its pre-publication investigation. In a letter dated 5 September 2022, Chevron responds, confirming the shipment in writing and providing its due diligence position. Chevron confirms it sold only a single Jet A-1 parcel through a Singapore customer that was ultimately delivered to Myanmar, and that its customer had warranted the fuel was for commercial use only. In a subsequent letter dated 18 October 2022, Chevron states it had established the fuel would be resold by the Myanmar importer to a commercial airline under a term supply contract. Chevron describes its standard counterparty due diligence process: screening counterparties against US, EU, UK and UN sanctions lists and seeking warranties that the product will not be supplied to the Myanmar military. Chevron does not commit to ceasing Myanmar aviation fuel sales. (AI Nov 2022)
•3 November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo report, produced in research collaboration with Justice for Myanmar, is published. It documents Chevron's confirmed supply of the February 2022 SRC shipment and reproduces Chevron's due diligence statement. Amnesty International assesses that counterparty warranties and sanctions-list screening are insufficient safeguards for Myanmar aviation fuel supply chains, using the Chevron case as one of several examples. Unlike Thai Oil (Profile 5.12), Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar (Profile 6.9) and Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10), Chevron does not make any documented commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel supply before or after publication. (AI Nov 2022)
•23 August 2023. The US Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) issues a determination under Executive Order 14014 that the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy falls within the scope of Burma-related sanctions. The determination establishes legal authority to designate any foreign person operating in that sector without requiring proof of direct military end-use. Activities covered include the importation, exportation, re-exportation, sale, supply or transport, directly or indirectly, of jet fuel in or involving Burma. The February 2022 Chevron shipment predated this determination; any future Chevron sale into the Myanmar jet fuel supply chain would take place in a materially different legal environment. (OFAC, 23 August 2023)
•28 May 2025. At Chevron's Annual Meeting of Stockholders in Houston, Texas, shareholder Proposal 5, filed by Investor Advocates for Social Justice (IASJ) on behalf of the Franciscan Sisters of Allegany, NY, calls on Chevron to commission a third-party report on the effectiveness of its human rights practices. The proposal receives approximately 11% of votes cast (approximately 89% voted against). The proposal was not framed specifically around Myanmar but drew on Chevron's documented involvement in regions where indigenous peoples and frontline communities have been affected by its operations. Separately, IASJ filed a further shareholder proposal for the 2026 proxy calling for a report on Chevron's policies and performance in respecting indigenous peoples' rights. (Chevron Annual Meeting Report, 28 May 2025; IASJ)
•March 2023, January 2024, July 2024 and 27 January 2026. Four subsequent Amnesty International investigations document Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain: the March 2023 follow-up (published with Global Witness and Burma Campaign UK), the January 2024 report, the July 2024 report, and the January 2026 report documenting Iran-linked ghost ships and AIS manipulation. Chevron Corporation and Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. do not appear in any of these four investigations as a supplier to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. The supply chain documented in post-2022 investigations has shifted toward Vietnamese intermediary storage routes and subsequently toward Iran-linked ghost ships, moving away from the direct-sale model under which the February 2022 Chevron shipment was structured. (AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Engage Chevron directly to obtain a formal written commitment not to supply aviation fuel to Myanmar's military or to any entity in Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain: Chevron responded substantively to Amnesty International's pre-publication engagement and confirmed its due diligence approach, but made no commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel sales. Unlike Thai Oil, Wilhelmsen and Pan Ocean, no documented commitment exists. Civil society should engage Chevron's compliance and human rights teams to request a formal public statement on its current Myanmar aviation fuel policy and to reference the OFAC jet fuel sector determination of 23 August 2023 as the changed legal context. Under that determination, any future documented supply to Myanmar's jet fuel sector would expose Chevron to designation under BURMA-EO14014 without requiring proof that the fuel reached a military aircraft. Chevron's stated due diligence in 2022, which comprised validation against sanctions lists and reliance on contractual warranties expressing the understanding that the product would not be supplied to the Myanmar military, was assessed by Amnesty International as insufficient to prevent supply to the military-linked supply chain, as Puma Energy had itself acknowledged that the military was forcibly demanding fuel at selected NEPAS airport facilities. (AI Nov 2022)
•Engage the incoming or new buyer of Chevron's SRC stake on terminal-level due diligence: The SRC terminal on Jurong Island was the load point for the February 2022 Myanmar aviation fuel shipment. It was also, separately, the terminal through which Chevron's 50% JV partner Singapore Petroleum Company sold Jet A-1 to Asia Sun Trading in December 2021 that a Myanmar Ministry of Defence letter confirmed was for Tatmadaw (Air Force) use. A change of ownership does not reduce the terminal's exposure to Myanmar supply chain risk. Chevron targeted completion of its SRC stake sale in Q1 2026; the transaction status is unconfirmed as of April 2026. If the sale has not yet completed, civil society should engage both Chevron and the incoming buyer simultaneously. If the sale has completed, engagement should be directed immediately at the new owner, whether Eneos, Glencore or any other acquiring entity, to confirm it has reviewed both the February 2022 Chevron shipment history and the December 2021 SPC transaction and has implemented measures to prevent future shipments from the SRC terminal to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. (AI Nov 2022)
•Submit evidence to OFAC if any further Chevron or SRC-terminal supply to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain is documented: The February 2022 Chevron shipment predated the August 2023 OFAC jet fuel sector determination and no designation action was taken. The legal threshold is now lower: a single documented post-determination sale to a Myanmar military-linked buyer would likely be sufficient basis for designation, and this applies equally to any SRC-terminal shipments during any transition period before ownership transfer completes. Civil society monitoring of shipping and trade data for any Chevron or SRC-linked deliveries to Myanmar should treat any documented supply as a matter for immediate referral to OFAC and to the UK, Australian and other active sanctions authorities. (AI Nov 2022)
•Engage institutional investors and responsible investment networks to explore a Myanmar-specific shareholder resolution at a future Chevron AGM: The IASJ-filed Proposal 5 at the May 2025 AGM, calling for a third-party report on human rights practices, was defeated with approximately 89% of votes cast against. A broad human rights mandate has limited institutional support. A narrower, Myanmar-specific resolution focused on the documented February 2022 shipment, Chevron's stated due diligence approach and its adequacy in light of the August 2023 OFAC determination, and Chevron's current policy on aviation fuel sales in Myanmar-relevant jurisdictions may have greater traction with specialist responsible investment institutions. Civil society should engage IASJ, the Interfaith Center on Corporate Responsibility, and other responsible investment networks to assess appetite for such a resolution ahead of the next annual meeting. (Chevron Annual Meeting Report, 28 May 2025; AI Nov 2022)
