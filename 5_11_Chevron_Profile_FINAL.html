Chevron Corporation
Profile 5.11
Summary Assessment
Chevron Corporation (NYSE: CVX) is the confirmed supplier of one post-coup aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar: 13,310 MT of Jet A-1 loaded at the Singapore Refining Company (SRC) terminal on Jurong Island in February 2022 and delivered to the PEAS terminal at Thilawa aboard the Super Hero. Chevron confirmed the shipment to Amnesty International, stated its Singapore customer had warranted the fuel was for commercial use only, and described counterparty screening and contractual warranties as its due diligence. It made no commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel sales and has not appeared in any subsequent investigated shipment. The SRC terminal is co-owned by Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. (50%) and SPC (50%). Chevron initiated a sale of its SRC stake in mid-2025; as of April 2026 the transaction had not been confirmed as completed. Neither Chevron Corporation nor Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. has been sanctioned in connection with Myanmar.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

The Super Hero (IMO 9341330), owned by Pan Ocean Co. Ltd., loaded 13,310 MT of Jet A-1 at the SRC terminal between 15 and 18 February 2022 and arrived at the PEAS terminal at Thilawa on or about 22 February 2022. Chevron confirmed the shipment in writing and stated the end-customer was a commercial airline in Myanmar. Amnesty International independently identified the Myanmar importer as NEPAS, the joint venture controlled by MPE. Chevron stated it had not been involved in any other post-coup Myanmar shipment, a statement confirmed by four subsequent Amnesty International investigations in which Chevron does not appear. (AI Nov 2022)

Chevron described its due diligence as counterparty screening against US, EU, UK, and UN sanctions lists and contractual warranties from its Singapore customer that the fuel would not be supplied to the Myanmar military. Amnesty International assessed this approach as inadequate: the end-use of fuel at the military level cannot be excluded on the basis of assurances from an intermediate Singapore trader, as Puma Energy itself had acknowledged the military was forcibly demanding fuel at NEPAS facilities. Unlike Thai Oil, Wilhelmsen, and Pan Ocean, Chevron made no public commitment to cease Myanmar aviation fuel sales. (AI Nov 2022)

3
Key Relationships
•Singapore Refining Company Pte. Ltd. (SRC), Jurong Island, Singapore: The load terminal for the February 2022 Chevron Myanmar shipment. SRC is a 50/50 joint venture between Chevron Singapore Pte. Ltd. and Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd., the latter wholly owned by PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.8). SRC operates a refinery on Jurong Island with a crude processing capacity of approximately 290,000 barrels per day and produces jet fuel among its primary outputs. SRC was the load terminal for multiple documented Myanmar aviation fuel shipments across 2021 and 2022; Chevron confirmed it was the supplier for the February 2022 shipment only. See the master shipment table for all SRC-loaded deliveries. Importantly, Chevron's 50% joint venture partner at SRC, Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd., was itself the named seller in a December 2021 Jet A-1 shipment to Asia Sun Trading. Amnesty International obtained a Ministry of Defence letter sent to the Myanma Port Authority confirming that fuel sold by SPC to Asia Sun Trading was required for use in the Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s aircraft and helicopters. This means Chevron's JV partner at the same terminal was supplying Myanmar's military aviation fuel chain approximately two months before Chevron's own February 2022 shipment. Chevron initiated the sale of its 50% SRC stake in mid-2025 as part of a broader Asia downstream restructuring. (AI Nov 2022)
•Super Hero (IMO 9341330) and Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. (Profile 6.10): The Super Hero is the vessel that carried the February 2022 Chevron shipment from the SRC terminal to the PEAS terminal at Thilawa. Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. is the beneficial owner and commercial manager of the Super Hero; POS Maritime TC S.A. is the registered owner. Pan Ocean confirmed following the Deadly Cargo investigation that it would no longer allow its vessels to transport aviation fuel to Myanmar. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
•PEAS terminal, Thilawa / NEPAS (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The PEAS terminal at Thilawa was the delivery point for the February 2022 shipment. At the time of delivery, the terminal was operated by Puma Energy through the NEPAS joint venture. Amnesty International's independently obtained evidence identifies NEPAS as the confirmed customer for the February 2022 shipment. At the time of the shipment, NEPAS was a joint venture in which Puma Energy Irrawaddy Aviation Pte. Ltd. held 49% and the military-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) held 51%. Chevron did not name its customer in its correspondence with Amnesty International. See the master shipment table and Profiles 5.1 and 2.1 for the NEPAS / PEAS terminal network. (AI Nov 2022)
•Yadana natural gas project, Myanmar: Separate from its aviation fuel supply chain role, Chevron held a participating interest in Myanmar's Yadana natural gas field through its subsidiary Unocal Myanmar Offshore Company (UMOC), alongside TotalEnergies, PTTEP and the military-controlled Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE, Profile 9.2). TotalEnergies withdrew from Yadana in July 2022, and its stake was redistributed proportionally among the remaining partners, increasing Chevron's interest to 41.1%. Chevron announced its intention to exit in January 2022 and agreed to relinquish its stake in February 2023. The withdrawal was effective 1 April 2024, with all processes completed by 5 April 2024 as confirmed by PTTEP to the Thai Stock Exchange. Chevron's redistributed stake was absorbed by the remaining shareholders: PTTEP's interest increased to 62.96% and MOGE received the balance. Chevron's Yadana exit is separate from and predates its SRC stake sale process.
•Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd. / PetroChina (Profile 5.8): SRC's co-owner. Singapore Petroleum Company Ltd. is wholly owned by PetroChina International (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. and holds the remaining 50% of SRC. PetroChina holds the right of first refusal on Chevron's SRC stake under the joint venture agreement. Profile 5.8 documents Singapore Petroleum Company's confirmed supply of a December 2021 Jet A-1 shipment to Asia Sun Trading, also loaded at SRC. A Ministry of Defence letter obtained by Amnesty International confirmed that fuel was for Tatmadaw (Air Force) use. SPC did not respond to Amnesty International's correspondence. (AI Nov 2022)
4
Advocacy Interventions
•September and October 2022. Amnesty International contacts Chevron before publication. Chevron confirms the February 2022 shipment in writing, provides its due diligence position, and states it has been involved in no other post-coup Myanmar aviation fuel shipment. No commitment to cease sales is made. (AI Nov 2022)
•3 November 2022. Deadly Cargo documents Chevron's confirmed supply and reproduces its due diligence statement. Amnesty International assesses warranty-based due diligence as insufficient. Chevron makes no public commitment. (AI Nov 2022)
•23 August 2023. OFAC issues the jet fuel sector determination under EO 14014. Any future documented Chevron supply to Myanmar's jet fuel supply chain would now be subject to designation authority without requiring proof of military end-use. (OFAC Aug 2023)
•28 May 2025. A shareholder proposal at Chevron's AGM, filed by Investor Advocates for Social Justice on behalf of the Franciscan Sisters of Allegany, calls for a third-party human rights report. It receives approximately 11 percent of votes cast. The proposal is not Myanmar-specific. (Chevron AGM May 2025)
5
Recommended Actions
•Obtain a formal written commitment from Chevron not to supply aviation fuel to Myanmar's military or any entity in Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. Chevron confirmed its supply, explained its due diligence, and went no further. The OFAC sector determination of August 2023 changed the legal landscape: any future supply documented in the sector would expose Chevron to designation without proof of military end-use. Civil society should engage Chevron's compliance and human rights teams requesting a formal public statement on its current Myanmar aviation fuel policy. (AI Nov 2022; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage the incoming buyer of Chevron's SRC stake before the transaction completes. The SRC terminal loaded the February 2022 Chevron shipment and the December 2021 SPC shipment for which a Myanmar Ministry of Defence letter confirmed Tatmadaw Air Force end-use. A change of ownership does not reduce the terminal's exposure. If the sale has not completed, both Chevron and the incoming buyer (reported as Eneos or Glencore) should be engaged simultaneously. If completed, the new owner should be engaged immediately to confirm it has reviewed the terminal's Myanmar supply history and implemented measures to prevent recurrence. (AI Nov 2022)
•Pursue a Myanmar-specific shareholder resolution at a future Chevron AGM. The broad human rights proposal at the May 2025 AGM received only 11 percent support. A narrower, Myanmar-specific resolution focused on the February 2022 shipment, the adequacy of warranty-based due diligence in light of the OFAC determination, and Chevron's current policy would be more precise and potentially more compelling for specialist responsible investment institutions. Civil society should engage Investor Advocates for Social Justice and the Interfaith Center on Corporate Responsibility to assess appetite for such a resolution. (Chevron AGM May 2025; AI Nov 2022)
