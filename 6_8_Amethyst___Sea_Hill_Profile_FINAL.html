Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Hill Maritime SA
Profile 6.8
Summary Assessment
Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Hill Maritime SA are UAE-domiciled entities registered in the Hamriyah Free Zone, Sharjah, that are the registered owners of REEF (IMO 9263382, Profile 6.5) and NOBLE (IMO 9162928, Profile 6.6) respectively. These two vessels are the only documented carriers of Iranian aviation fuel to Myanmar's military junta, together responsible for eight confirmed deliveries between October 2024 and December 2025. Both Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Hill Maritime SA were identified for the first time in Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release, which drew on publicly available information. Neither entity appears in any reviewed source prior to February 2026. Both are registered at the same Hamriyah Free Zone address as Sea Route Ship Management FZE (Profile 6.7), the ISM and commercial manager of both REEF and NOBLE, which has been designated by the United States since June 2024. The concentration of the owners and manager of both ghost fleet vessels at a single shared Hamriyah Free Zone address strongly suggests a common controlling party behind the structure, though no reviewed source has confirmed this. Neither Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA nor Sea Hill Maritime SA has been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026. Justice For Myanmar called for their coordinated designation across five jurisdictions in February 2026.

3
Co-location and Structural Analysis

Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA, Sea Hill Maritime SA and Sea Route Ship Management FZE (Profile 6.7) are all registered in the Hamriyah Free Zone, Sharjah, UAE at the same address. Sea Route Ship Management FZE is the ISM and commercial manager of both REEF and NOBLE, making it the operational layer that sits above both registered owners in the corporate structure. The concentration of the manager and the registered owners of both simultaneously US-sanctioned ghost fleet vessels at a single shared free zone address is a strong basis for investigative attention into whether the three entities share a common beneficial owner or controlling party. No reviewed source has confirmed the identity of any common controlling party. (JFM Feb 2026)

This structural pattern, in which vessel management and vessel ownership are concentrated in co-located free zone entities, is consistent with shadow fleet operational practice and makes tracing beneficial ownership substantially more difficult. Justice For Myanmar called for the designation of all three entities together, reflecting its assessment that addressing only the vessels or only their manager is insufficient without simultaneously targeting the registered owners. (JFM Feb 2026)

5
Recommended Actions
•Designate Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Hill Maritime SA across all five jurisdictions, and pursue network designation if common beneficial ownership is established: Both entities are the registered owners of US-sanctioned ghost fleet vessels that continued to make Myanmar deliveries after their OFAC designations in June 2024. The registered owners were not designated at the time of the vessel designations, creating a structural gap that has been exploited to sustain ongoing operations. JFM's February 2026 call for coordinated designation across the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia remains unimplemented across all five jurisdictions. Designation of both registered owners alongside their managed vessels and the co-located manager Sea Route Ship Management FZE would close the ownership layer of the ghost fleet corporate structure. If investigation of UAE corporate registry records, shipping databases and financial intelligence establishes that Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Hill Maritime SA share a common beneficial owner, a network designation naming both companies as entities under common control would be significantly more disruptive to the ghost fleet operation than piecemeal entity designations, and should be pursued as a priority. (JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage UAE authorities to pursue beneficial ownership disclosure for all three co-located Hamriyah Free Zone entities: Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA, Sea Hill Maritime SA and Sea Route Ship Management FZE share a Hamriyah Free Zone address. Identifying the common beneficial owner or controlling party behind this structure is a prerequisite for effective and durable sanctions targeting. UAE Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023 on beneficial ownership registers provides a domestic legal basis for compelling disclosure. Engagement should target the Hamriyah Free Zone Authority in respect of corporate compliance obligations, the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit to receive and act on suspicious activity reporting related to the three entities, and the UAE Executive Office of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Terrorism Financing, which coordinates AML/CFT policy across all sectors including commercial free zones. The UAE's 2025 AML Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025), in force since 14 October 2025, further strengthens the domestic legal framework for such action. The concentration of the two primary Myanmar aviation fuel vessels and their owner and manager entities within the Hamriyah Free Zone represents a pattern that UAE authorities have legal tools to investigate and disrupt. (JFM Feb 2026)
•Include beneficial ownership analysis in designation submissions: Designation submissions to UK, EU, Canadian, US and Australian authorities should include an explicit analysis of the structural connection between Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA, Sea Hill Maritime SA and Sea Route Ship Management FZE. The co-location of management and ownership of two simultaneously US-sanctioned vessels in a shared free zone address is material evidence of coordinated evasion that strengthens the designation case for all three entities. Civil society should engage relevant financial intelligence units to share this structural analysis and to request that any available beneficial ownership data be incorporated into designation decisions. (JFM Feb 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
•Raise the Hamriyah Free Zone network with FATF as a case study in shadow fleet beneficial ownership opacity: The three co-located Hamriyah Free Zone entities (Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA, Sea Hill Maritime SA and Sea Route Ship Management FZE) represent a documented example of Gulf free zone corporate structures being used to shield shadow fleet beneficial ownership from sanctions enforcement. Civil society and partner governments should raise this cluster formally with FATF as a case study, with the aim of prompting FATF to include free zone beneficial ownership disclosure requirements in its next mutual evaluation of the UAE and in its updated guidance on maritime sanctions evasion typologies. The UAE was removed from the FATF grey list in February 2024, and its commitment to maintaining that status provides ongoing leverage for advocacy partners seeking enforcement action. The UAE has a FATF mutual evaluation scheduled for 2026, which increases the timeliness of this intervention. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
•Seek designation of REEF under Myanmar-specific sanctions programmes in the UK, EU, Canada and Australia, alongside designation of Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA as its registered owner: REEF was designated by OFAC in June 2024 under Iran sanctions (Executive Order 13846) but has not been designated by any jurisdiction under Myanmar-specific sanctions programmes. The UK, EU, Canada and Australia each maintain Myanmar-specific sanctions instruments that address aviation fuel supply and military support. Designation of REEF by name under these Myanmar-specific instruments, alongside designation of Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA as its registered owner, would close the current jurisdictional enforcement gap and align cross-jurisdictional action on Myanmar grounds rather than relying solely on the existing Iran sanctions designation. Myanmar-specific designation would also provide a stronger legal basis for port and insurance denials in non-US jurisdictions that are not bound by the Iran sanctions programmes. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
