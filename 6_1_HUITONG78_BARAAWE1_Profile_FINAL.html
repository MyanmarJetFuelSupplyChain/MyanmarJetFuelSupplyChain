HUITONG 78 (now BARAAWE 1)
Profile 6.1
Summary Assessment
HUITONG 78 (IMO 9646479), now renamed BARAAWE 1, carried nine confirmed and one assessed Jet A-1 deliveries to Myanmar between May 2023 and July 2024. Eight loaded at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal in Vietnam; the final confirmed delivery staged at the FOAA anchorage in Fujairah, UAE. Chinese-flagged throughout the documented period, it continued operating after being publicly identified by Amnesty International in January 2024. The West of England P&I Club confirmed ownership by a CASIC affiliate and stated no insurance cover applied to Myanmar jet fuel trades. The vessel was subsequently renamed BARAAWE 1, re-flagged to St Kitts and Nevis, and its AIS was deactivated during offloading at Thilawa. Abu Marine Co. Ltd. (Marshall Islands) is the registered owner and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management (UAE) is the ISM manager. Neither the vessel, its owner, nor its manager has been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar as of April 2026.

2
Supply Chain Role and Methodology

HUITONG 78 was the primary delivery vessel for the Vietnamese transit supply chain, carrying six of the seven 2023 shipments and both confirmed early 2024 shipments. Every 2023 delivery loaded at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd., sometimes preceded by a stop at Pengerang (Malaysia), the CNOOC Huizhou terminal (China), the Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal, or the BP Hua Dong terminal in Ningbo. The multi-hop routing was designed to obscure the fuel's origin and diffuse liability among multiple intermediaries. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)

After being named publicly in January 2024 as the primary vessel in the supply chain, HUITONG 78 continued making deliveries. The July 2024 delivery staged at the FOAA anchorage in Fujairah rather than Vietnam, marking the transition to Phase 3 ghost fleet tactics. AIS was deactivated during offloading at Thilawa. Amnesty International's Secretary General stated: "The Myanmar military is relying on the very same Chinese vessel and Vietnamese companies to import its aviation fuel, despite Amnesty International having already exposed that reckless supply chain." (AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)

3
Ownership and Insurance

At the time of Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation, the West of England P&I Club confirmed the vessel was owned by an affiliate of China Aerospace Science and Industry Corp (CASIC), a Chinese state-owned defence company. The Club stated that if the vessel was carrying jet fuel to Myanmar "then there was no cover for those trades." Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 call identified Abu Marine Co. Ltd. (Marshall Islands) as the registered owner and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management (UAE) as the ISM manager. The relationship between Abu Marine and the CASIC affiliate identified by the West of England Club has not been established in any reviewed source. (AI Jul 2024; JFM Feb 2026)

4
Sanctions Evasion Behaviours
•Continued operation after public exposure. Named publicly in January 2024, the vessel made four further documented deliveries before ceasing its documented Myanmar role. (AI Jul 2024)
•AIS deactivation during offloading. AIS was turned off as the vessel entered Thilawa during the May and July 2024 deliveries. (AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
•Renaming and re-flagging. Following exposure across multiple investigations, the vessel was renamed HUITONG 78 to BARAAWE 1 and re-flagged from China to St Kitts and Nevis. Its IMO number (9646479) is unchanged. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)
•Multi-hop routing. Fuel was routed through storage terminals in Malaysia, Singapore, and China before the Cai Mep terminal in Vietnam to mask its origin and diffuse legal liability. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)

•Abu Marine Co. Ltd. (Marshall Islands) and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management (UAE): Identified by Justice For Myanmar in February 2026 as the registered owner and ISM manager respectively. See Profile 6.2. (JFM Feb 2026)
•West of England P&I Club: The vessel's Protection and Indemnity insurer at the time of Amnesty International's July 2024 investigation. The Club disclosed the CASIC affiliate ownership and stated there would be no cover for trades involving jet fuel to Myanmar. (AI Jul 2024)
•Former PEAS terminal / Myan Oil Terminal, Thilawa, Yangon: The destination terminal for all ten documented deliveries. Shipments 1-9 arrived when the terminal was under Shoon Energy and Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise joint management (former PEAS terminal). •FOAA anchorage area, Fujairah, UAE: Intermediate staging point for the July 2024 delivery (shipment 10), where HUITONG 78 was sighted from 13 to 26 June 2024, likely loading fuel via ship-to-ship transfer. See Profile 7.2. (AI Jan 2026)

Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release called for coordinated sanctions by the EU, UK, Canada, US, and Australia on BARAAWE 1 and on its registered owner and ISM manager. 8
Recommended Actions
•Designate BARAAWE 1, Abu Marine Co. Ltd., and Pacific Light Operation and Ship Management in all five jurisdictions. The vessel has nine confirmed Jet A-1 deliveries to Myanmar across 2023 and 2024. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination and its equivalents in the UK, EU, Canada, and Australia provide the legal authority. The renaming, re-flagging, and AIS deactivation behaviours provide additional grounds under vessel sanctions frameworks. JFM's February 2026 call provides the submission framework. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Investigate the relationship between Abu Marine Co. Ltd. and the CASIC affiliate identified by the West of England P&I Club. The Club confirmed a CASIC affiliate owned the vessel in July 2024; JFM identified Abu Marine as the registered owner in February 2026. Whether these are the same entity, related entities, or represent a subsequent ownership transfer has not been established. If CASIC or its affiliates retain a beneficial interest, that warrants diplomatic engagement with China alongside any designation submissions. (AI Jul 2024; JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage the St Kitts and Nevis flag administration and alert port state control. The vessel's re-flagging to St Kitts and Nevis following public exposure follows a documented pattern across the Myanmar ghost fleet. The flag state should be formally notified of the vessel's documented history and requested to confirm whether it maintains the flag registration, and if so, what due diligence it applied. Port state control bodies in jurisdictions the vessel transits should be alerted to the IMO number (9646479) regardless of any further name changes. (AI Jan 2026; JFM Feb 2026)
