Pan Ocean Co., Ltd.
Profile 6.10
Summary Assessment
Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. is a South Korean shipping company listed on the KRX and SGX and a subsidiary of Harim Holdings. Two Pan Ocean vessels carried three Jet A-1 deliveries to the PEAS terminal at Thilawa in 2022: Super Hero (IMO 9341330) carried two shipments (February and August 2022) and Super Infinity (IMO 9341342) carried one (June 2022). Pan Ocean did not respond to any correspondence from Amnesty International before publication. Following publication of Deadly Cargo in November 2022, Pan Ocean committed it would no longer allow its vessels to transport aviation fuel to Myanmar. The commitment was confirmed in March 2023 and Pan Ocean has not appeared in any subsequent investigated shipment. Neither Pan Ocean nor its vessels has been sanctioned in connection with Myanmar.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Pan Ocean was the beneficial owner and commercial manager of both vessels; the registered owners are Panamanian special purpose subsidiaries (POS Maritime TC S.A. for Super Hero, POS Maritime UC S.A. for Super Infinity). Pan Ocean was not the fuel supplier and was not the shipping agent; its role was as owner of the vessels used to carry the deliveries. (AI Nov 2022)

Super Hero loaded 13,310 MT at the SRC terminal on Jurong Island in February 2022 (supplier: Chevron, confirmed) and an unconfirmed quantity at Pengerang Independent Terminals, Malaysia, in August 2022 (supplier not identified; Vopak confirmed departure but declined to name its customer). Super Infinity loaded approximately 13,311 MT at the ExxonMobil berth on Jurong Island in June 2022 (supplier: ExxonMobil implicated by vessel tracking; ExxonMobil did not respond to Amnesty International). Pan Ocean did not respond to any pre-publication correspondence, making it impossible to establish what due diligence it had conducted before leasing its vessels for these cargoes. (AI Nov 2022)

Key Relationships
•Super Hero (IMO 9341330): Chemical and oil product tanker, built 2009, flag of Panama. 14,984 DWT; 10,549 GRT; 149 metres LOA; 22 metres beam. Formerly named STX HERO (until June 2014), reflecting the vessel's earlier designation under Pan Ocean's prior corporate identity as STX Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. Registered owner: POS Maritime TC S.A. (Panamanian special purpose vehicle). Beneficial owner and commercial manager: Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. Documented carrying two Jet A-1 shipments to the PEAS terminal, Thilawa, in February and August 2022; see the master shipment table. Neither the Super Hero nor POS Maritime TC S.A. is identified in reviewed sources as a sanctioned or subsequently flagged entity. (AI Nov 2022)
•Super Infinity (IMO 9341342): Chemical and oil product tanker, built 2009, flag of Panama. 14,999 DWT; 10,549 GRT; 150 metres LOA; 22 metres beam. Formerly named STX INFINITY (until July 2014). Registered owner: POS Maritime UC S.A. (Panamanian special purpose vehicle). Beneficial owner and commercial manager: Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. Documented carrying one Jet A-1 shipment to the PEAS terminal, Thilawa, in June 2022; see the master shipment table. Neither the Super Infinity nor POS Maritime UC S.A. is identified in reviewed sources as a sanctioned or subsequently flagged entity. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy Asia Sun (PEAS) terminal, Thilawa (Profiles 5.1 and 2.1): The delivery terminal for all three Pan Ocean vessel shipments. At the time of the 2022 deliveries, the terminal was operated under Puma Energy's management through the NEPAS joint venture. Puma Energy completed the sale of the terminal to the Shoon Group in December 2022. The terminal now operates under the Shoon Energy and Myan Oil Terminal network and continues to receive aviation fuel deliveries. See the master shipment table for post-2022 deliveries to the former PEAS terminal. (AI Nov 2022)
•Chevron Corporation (Profile 5.11): Confirmed supplier of the February 2022 Super Hero shipment (13,310 MT). Chevron stated that its customer in Singapore provided assurance that the Jet A-1 was for commercial purposes only and that the fuel was supplied to a commercial airline under a term supply contract. Chevron confirmed it was not involved in the August 2022 Super Hero shipment or any other Myanmar aviation fuel shipment. (AI Nov 2022)
•Thai Oil Public Company Limited (Profile 5.12) and Wilhelmsen Ships Service Myanmar Ltd. (Profile 6.9): The two other named entities that committed to exit Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain following the Deadly Cargo investigation. Thai Oil and Wilhelmsen both committed before the report was published; Pan Ocean committed after publication. All three commitments were confirmed in the March 2023 follow-up investigation. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)
4
Advocacy Interventions and Outcome
•February to August 2022. Super Hero and Super Infinity carry three Jet A-1 deliveries to Thilawa. Pan Ocean does not respond to any pre-publication letters from Amnesty International. (AI Nov 2022)
•3 November 2022. Deadly Cargo is published. Pan Ocean's non-response is documented. Amnesty International calls on Pan Ocean to ensure none of its other vessels are used for shipments that can contribute to human rights harm. (AI Nov 2022)
•Post-November 2022. Pan Ocean commits it will no longer allow its vessels to transport aviation fuel to Myanmar. Unlike Thai Oil and Wilhelmsen, this commitment was made after publication. (AI Mar 2023)
•March 2023 to January 2026. Four subsequent Amnesty International investigations confirm Pan Ocean vessels do not appear in any further documented shipments. (AI Mar 2023; AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
5
Recommended Actions
•Confirm the current status of the commitment and seek a formal public policy statement. Pan Ocean's post-publication commitment was communicated to Amnesty International but no formal public policy statement has been issued. Its scope (whether it covers all Pan Ocean vessels and Harim Holdings affiliates) has not been publicly defined. Civil society should engage Pan Ocean to confirm the commitment remains in effect, clarify its fleet-wide scope, and request a public statement committing not to transport aviation fuel to Myanmar's military or any entity in Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. As a KRX and SGX-listed company, Pan Ocean is subject to institutional investor engagement on this point. (AI Nov 2022; AI Mar 2023)

Amnesty International states it is impossible to ascertain whether Pan Ocean conducted human rights due diligence prior to leasing its vessels for the Myanmar deliveries. (AI Nov 2022)

The report calls on Pan Ocean to ensure no other vessel it owns is involved in shipments that can lead to human rights harm. (AI Nov 2022)

1 March 2023The follow-up investigation by Amnesty International, Global Witness and Burma Campaign UK confirms Pan Ocean's commitment. Pan Ocean vessels do not appear in the newly identified shipments. The report cites Pan Ocean's commitment as one of three significant advocacy outcomes of the Deadly Cargo investigation, alongside those of Thai Oil and Wilhelmsen. (AI Mar 2023)
January 2024Amnesty International publishes its third Myanmar aviation fuel investigation, documenting continued supply through Vietnamese storage intermediaries and evasion of sanctions. Pan Ocean vessels do not appear. (AI Jan 2024)
July 2024Amnesty International publishes its fourth Myanmar aviation fuel investigation. Pan Ocean vessels do not appear. (AI Jul 2024)
27 January 2026Amnesty International publishes its fifth Myanmar aviation fuel investigation, documenting Iran-linked ghost ships and AIS manipulation. Pan Ocean vessels do not appear. Across all four post-2022 investigations, Pan Ocean vessels have not been identified as parties to any documented aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar. (AI Jan 2026)
April 2026Pan Ocean Co., Ltd. is not designated by any government in connection with Myanmar. The post-publication commitment has not been rescinded in any reviewed source. Both vessels remain active in the Pan Ocean fleet under POS Maritime ownership. As of April 2026, the Super Hero is underway in Southeast Asian waters and the Super Infinity has conducted recent port calls in Singapore and Southeast Asia. Neither vessel has visited any Myanmar port in the preceding twelve months, and neither is sanctioned or flagged in connection with Myanmar. (MagicPort, April 2026)
