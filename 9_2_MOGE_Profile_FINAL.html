Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE)
Profile 9.2
Summary Assessment
Myanma Oil and Gas Enterprise (MOGE) is the junta-controlled state oil enterprise that sits at the centre of Myanmar's domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain. As the designated holder of all oil and gas reserves in Myanmar, MOGE holds exclusive rights to carry out exploration and production with private contractors, and is the mandatory purchaser of all crude oil extracted from the Chauk and Yenangyaung fields by Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. (Profile 9.1). MOGE transfers that crude oil to Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) for refining at the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex, where it is processed into petroleum products including aviation fuel and diesel that primarily supply Myanmar military bases. Beyond the domestic crude chain, MOGE is also described by the UN Special Rapporteur as the junta's single-biggest source of foreign revenue through the sale of fossil fuels, earning at least USD 55 million per month in Thai Baht payments from gas revenues as of end of 2023. MOGE was designated by the European Union on 21 February 2022. The United States issued a financial services directive against MOGE on 31 October 2023, effective 15 December 2023, prohibiting US persons from providing financial services to MOGE; this is a directive rather than a full asset-blocking designation. Switzerland designated MOGE under its SECO sanctions list on 11 March 2025. The United Kingdom, Canada and Australia had not designated MOGE as of April 2026. The UN Special Rapporteur explicitly recommended in June 2024 that the United States upgrade its directive to full sanctions and that the UN Security Council freeze MOGE's assets.

1
Role in the Domestic Crude-to-Aviation-Fuel Chain

MOGE is the mandatory recipient of all crude oil produced by Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. (Profile 9.1) from the Chauk and Yenangyaung oil fields in Magway Region. Under the IPRCs between Goldpetrol and MOGE, the baseline payment of 39 percent of total production is delivered directly to MOGE in the form of crude oil, with additional oil reaching MOGE through taxes, royalties, domestic market obligations and a signature bonus. The combined effect is that MOGE receives more than half of all oil extracted before Goldpetrol receives any cash compensation. MOGE then transfers that crude oil to Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE), which processes it at the Mann Thanbayarkan petrochemical complex. According to sources with knowledge of the supply chain and the 2020 EITI Commodity Trading Report, all crude oil from Chauk and Yenangyaung is transported along the Ayeyarwady River to Mann for refining. Two independent sources with direct knowledge confirm that the primary beneficiaries of petroleum products refined at Mann are military bases. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)

In addition to its role in the domestic crude chain, MOGE administers all of Myanmar's oil and gas reserves and is the entity through which all private exploration and production contracts are structured. MOGE provides the junta with its single-biggest source of foreign revenue through the sale of fossil fuels. As of end of 2023, MOGE earned at least USD 55 million per month in Thai Baht revenue payments from gas sales, including revenues from the Shwe gas field. These payments were processed through Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3). During Q3 FY2023 (October to December 2023), MEB processed approximately USD 165 million in Thai Baht revenues on behalf of MOGE. (UNSR Jun 2024)

MOGE's role also extends to the junta's ability to pay private contractors. Between February and May 2022, MOGE paid Goldpetrol more than USD 11 million through the junta-controlled Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) (Profile 8.4), according to leaked financial documents. Following US sanctions on MFTB in June 2023, MOGE shifted its payments to an undisclosed Myanmar bank. Interra Resources, Goldpetrol's majority owner, acknowledged that payments continue through a Myanmar bank not subject to sanctions but declined to name the institution. Justice For Myanmar identified the risk that MOGE may have shifted to MEB. For cross-reference with all documented jet fuel shipments, see the master shipment table. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)

The European Union designated MOGE on 21 February 2022, finding that MOGE contributes to the Myanmar military's capabilities to carry out activities undermining democracy and the rule of law in Myanmar. Switzerland designated MOGE under its SECO sanctions list on 11 March 2025, following a sustained advocacy campaign by Justice For Myanmar including a freedom of information request to SECO in March 2024 and reporting by Tages-Anzeiger. The United States issued a financial services directive against MOGE on 31 October 2023, taking effect on 15 December 2023. The directive prohibits US persons from providing financial services to or for the benefit of MOGE or its property or interests in property; it is a sectoral measure that places MOGE on the US OFAC Consolidated Non-SDN list rather than the Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons list. The United Kingdom, Canada and Australia had not designated MOGE as of April 2026. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025; UNSR Jun 2024)

The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report explicitly recommended that the United States upgrade its financial services directive to full sanctions on MOGE, and called on the UN Security Council to sanction MOGE and freeze assets that rightfully belong to the people of Myanmar, including billions in foreign currency reserves. The UNSR also noted that under current UK and EU law, all Myanmar state-owned enterprises should be considered sanctioned given EU and UK sanctions on the SAC itself, but that both jurisdictions have failed to treat MOGE as fully sanctioned in practice. The UNSR described this as a significant oversight creating confusion for financial institutions. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The EU designation had an immediate practical consequence: Bank of China reportedly stopped transmitting euro-denominated payments to MOGE for gas exports from the Shwe gas field following the February 2022 designation. Payments reportedly totalling over USD 500 million were placed into escrow accounts and remained outside the junta's reach as of the UNSR's writing in June 2024. This represents a direct financial cost to the junta from the EU designation. (UNSR Jun 2024)

3
Sanctions Evasion

Following the US financial services directive of October 2023, the SAC's energy ministry attempted to disguise MOGE revenue flows through Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3) by instructing MEB to open new accounts for MOGE revenues under the name "Kant Kaw" rather than MOGE, according to a leaked letter to the Central Bank of Myanmar cited by the UN Special Rapporteur. This evasion attempt was documented in the UNSR's June 2024 report. The "Kant Kaw" manoeuvre illustrates the junta's active use of account renaming to circumvent the financial services directive, and underscores the importance of financial institutions conducting enhanced due diligence on accounts associated with MOGE even where MOGE is not directly named. (UNSR Jun 2024)

In the domestic crude payment chain, MOGE shifted payments to Goldpetrol's Myanmar bank account following US sanctions on MFTB in June 2023. Interra Resources declined to identify which bank now handles these payments, stating only that the bank is not subject to sanctions. Justice For Myanmar noted the risk that MOGE could have shifted to MEB. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)

Key Relationships
•Goldpetrol Joint Operating Company Inc. / Interra Resources Ltd (Profile 9.1): Goldpetrol's sole contractual counterparty under its IPRCs. All crude oil extracted by Goldpetrol from Chauk and Yenangyaung is sold exclusively to MOGE. MOGE paid Goldpetrol more than USD 11 million through MFTB between February and May 2022. Current payment bank is undisclosed. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE): The downstream state enterprise to which MOGE transfers crude oil for refining at Mann Thanbayarkan. MPE processes Goldpetrol's crude into aviation fuel, diesel and other petroleum products that primarily supply military bases in Magway Region. MPE has not been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM May 2025)
•Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3): The state bank through which MOGE channels its foreign gas revenues. MEB processed approximately USD 165 million in Thai Baht revenues on behalf of MOGE during Q3 FY2023 (approximately USD 55 million per month). The SAC's energy ministry attempted to route these revenues under the alias "Kant Kaw" to evade the US financial services directive. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) (Profile 8.4): Used by MOGE to pay Goldpetrol more than USD 11 million between February and May 2022. The US sanctioned MFTB in June 2023, prompting MOGE to shift Goldpetrol payments to an undisclosed bank. MFTB is designated by the US, Canada and Australia. (JFM Jan 2025)
•Ministry of Electricity and Energy: MOGE operates as an operational department of the Ministry of Electricity and Energy, which the junta seized control of following the February 2021 coup by appointing new ministers and managing directors. MOGE is described as functioning as an operational department of the ministry rather than as an autonomous corporate entity. (AI Nov 2022; JFM Jan 2025)
5
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•21 February 2022. The European Union designated MOGE under its Myanmar restrictive measures regime, stating that MOGE contributes to the Myanmar military's capabilities to carry out activities undermining democracy and the rule of law. The designation was the first by a major sanctions jurisdiction and had immediate practical consequences for MOGE's access to euro-denominated payments. Bank of China reportedly stopped transmitting euro payments to MOGE for Shwe gas field exports, placing over USD 500 million into escrow outside the junta's reach. (JFM Feb 2025; UNSR Jun 2024)
•July 2022. EarthRights International criticised the TotalEnergies exit from the Yadana gas project as irresponsible, arguing that the proportional redistribution of TotalEnergies' 31.2 percent stake to Chevron, PTTEP and MOGE ensured continued revenue flows to the junta rather than redirecting the stake away from junta-controlled entities. (Open source, July 2022)
•21 June 2023. The United States sanctioned MFTB and MICB for facilitating the junta's profiteering from Burma's extractive industries and its use of foreign currency to procure arms and jet fuel. The OFAC press release explicitly cited MOGE's role in the extractives sector. Following the designations, the junta shifted MOGE-related banking transactions to Myanma Economic Bank (MEB) (Profile 8.3), which was not then designated by the US, UK, EU or Australia. (JFM Jan 2025; UNSR Jun 2024)
•24 August 2023. Justice For Myanmar welcomed the US determination that the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy is subject to EO 14014 sanctions, and called on the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia to impose coordinated sanctions on MOGE, MPE and junta-controlled banks. (JFM Aug 2023)
•31 October 2023. The United States issued a financial services directive against MOGE under Executive Order 14014, taking effect 15 December 2023. OFAC confirmed in the directive that MOGE is a political subdivision of the Government of Burma and the largest single source of foreign revenue for the military regime. The directive prohibits US persons from providing financial services to or for the benefit of MOGE but does not block MOGE's assets or prohibit all transactions. Justice For Myanmar welcomed the action but noted it fell short of full sanctions that would freeze MOGE's assets and block all trade. (JFM Jan 2025; UNSR Jun 2024)
•April 2024. EarthRights International criticised the Chevron exit from the Yadana gas project, stating that by redistributing rather than selling its 41.1 percent stake, Chevron had exited in an irresponsible manner that ensured the junta would continue misappropriating tens of millions of dollars in gas revenues each month. Justice For Myanmar simultaneously called for Canadian and UK sanctions on MOGE in response to the stake redistribution. (Open source, April 2024)
•April 2024. Justice For Myanmar filed legal submissions to the governments of Bermuda and Cayman Islands, alleging that two PTTEP joint ventures with MOGE registered in those territories, Moattama Gas Transportation Company (MGTC, Bermuda, 63 percent PTTEPO and 37 percent MOGE) and Andaman Transportation Limited (ATL, Cayman Islands), are operating in breach of UK Myanmar sanctions extended to UK Overseas Territories through the Myanmar (Sanctions) (Overseas Territories) Order 2021. Both island governments acknowledged receipt of the submissions but did not confirm whether investigations were launched. (JFM Nov 2025)
•26 June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report explicitly recommended that the United States upgrade its directive to full sanctions on MOGE, and called on the UN Security Council to sanction MOGE and freeze its foreign currency reserves. The report documented MEB's role in routing MOGE's Thai Baht gas revenues and the "Kant Kaw" evasion attempt. The UNSR described the UK's and EU's failure to treat all Myanmar SOEs as sanctioned as a "significant oversight." (UNSR Jun 2024)
•15 July 2024. A joint statement by 240 Myanmar and international organisations called for coordinated sanctions on MOGE and MEB simultaneously, citing the UNSR's June 2024 report documenting MEB as MOGE's primary post-MFTB transaction vehicle and gas revenues as the junta's largest revenue stream. The statement explicitly called on the US to fully sanction MOGE and on the UK, EU, Canada and Australia to follow the EU's lead in designating MOGE. (Open source, July 2024)
•29 January 2025. Justice For Myanmar and Finance Uncovered published their joint investigation into Interra Resources, documenting Goldpetrol's supply of crude oil to MOGE and the oil's subsequent route to the Mann refinery. The report renewed calls for coordinated sanctions on MOGE, MPE and junta-controlled banks to cut off the domestic crude-to-aviation-fuel chain. (JFM Jan 2025)
•11 March 2025. Switzerland designated MOGE under its SECO measures against Myanmar, implementing the EU sanctions through the Embargo Act. The designation followed a sustained JFM campaign including a freedom of information request to SECO submitted in March 2024, reporting by Tages-Anzeiger, and a JFM press release on 7 March 2025. JFM welcomed the designation and called on Canada, the United Kingdom and Australia to follow Switzerland and impose sanctions on MOGE. (Open source, March 2025)
•24 May 2025. Justice For Myanmar's ASEAN report updated the total oil supply figure to over three million barrels worth more than USD 200 million since January 2021, all delivered to MOGE. The report also named PetroVietnam and AlphaECC as providing technical services to MOGE and PTTEP in the offshore gas sector, and called on ASEAN governments to cut junta access to funds and aviation fuel. The report was published to coincide with the 46th ASEAN Summit in Kuala Lumpur (26 to 27 May 2025). JFM continued to call for coordinated designation of MOGE across the UK, Canada and Australia. (JFM May 2025)
•3 November 2025. Justice For Myanmar and Finance Uncovered published an investigation documenting that MGTC and ATL, pipeline companies jointly owned by PTTEP and MOGE and registered in UK Overseas Territories, generated over USD 1 billion in pipeline revenues between 2021 and 2024, of which MOGE's minority share was approximately USD 239 million. Labour peer Baroness Hodge described the failure to act as an "outrage." The UK Foreign Office stated it was supporting the Overseas Territories to investigate and act on any allegations of possible sanction breaches. As of April 2026, neither territory had confirmed whether investigations were launched. JFM reiterated calls for Bermuda and Cayman Islands authorities to enforce UK sanctions. (JFM Nov 2025)
6
Recommended Actions
•Call on the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia to designate MOGE independently and by name: As of April 2026, the UK, Canada and Australia have not designated MOGE. Canada and Australia have designated MFTB and MICB (Canada since 2012; Australia since 2023); the UK has not designated MFTB, MICB or MOGE. The UK's only current basis for treating MOGE as sanctioned derives from its designation of the SAC, which the junta dissolved and rebranded as the State Security and Peace Commission (SSPC) on 31 July 2025. This rebrand creates legal uncertainty about whether the UK's derivative designation of MOGE still applies. An explicit UK designation of MOGE by name under the Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021 would remove that ambiguity and directly protect UK Overseas Territories from sanctions evasion through MOGE-linked structures such as MGTC and ATL. The EU designation of February 2022 and Switzerland's designation of March 2025 demonstrate that the legal basis and political will exist in allied jurisdictions. Civil society should submit evidence packages to UK OFSI, Global Affairs Canada, and Australia's DFAT citing the EU designation, the Swiss designation, the US directive's own characterisation of MOGE, the UNSR's June 2024 documentation of MOGE gas revenues as the junta's largest foreign currency income stream, and the JFM evidence of MOGE's role in the crude-to-aviation-fuel chain. (JFM Jan 2025; UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Nov 2025)
•Call on the United States to upgrade the financial services directive to full blocking sanctions on MOGE: The US financial services directive of October 2023 prohibits US persons from providing financial services to MOGE but does not block MOGE's property and interests in property. OFAC's own October 2023 action confirmed that MOGE is a political subdivision of the Government of Burma and the largest single source of foreign revenue for the military regime, a finding that is directly applicable to a full SDN designation under EO 14014 Section 1(a)(iv). The UNSR explicitly recommended in June 2024 that the US upgrade the directive to full sanctions. Advocacy submissions should cite: OFAC's own characterisation of MOGE; the EU designation of February 2022; the MFTB June 2023 sanctions rationale documenting MOGE's extractives profiteering; the JFM evidence of MOGE's role in the aviation fuel supply chain via Goldpetrol crude and MPE refining; and the UNSR's documentation of MOGE gas revenues as the junta's largest revenue stream routed through MEB. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Jan 2025)
•Advocate for simultaneous designation of MOGE and MEB to prevent transaction shifting: The July 2024 joint statement by 240 Myanmar and international organisations specifically called for coordinated sanctions on MOGE and MEB simultaneously, arguing that isolated MOGE designation without accompanying MEB designation will be insufficient. As MFTB's June 2023 US designation demonstrated, single-jurisdiction action enables the junta to shift to undesignated banking channels. Advocacy submissions to all five sanctions jurisdictions should make the explicit argument for simultaneous MOGE and MEB designation, citing the UNSR's documentation of MEB as MOGE's primary post-MFTB gas revenue conduit and the "Kant Kaw" evasion attempt as evidence that the junta actively prepares to circumvent partial measures. (UNSR Jun 2024; Open source, July 2024)
•Call on Bermuda and Cayman Islands to enforce UK Myanmar sanctions against MGTC and ATL: Justice For Myanmar filed legal submissions to the Bermuda and Cayman Islands governments in April 2024 alleging that MGTC (Bermuda, 37 percent MOGE-owned) and ATL (Cayman Islands), which generated an estimated USD 1.078 billion in pipeline revenues between 2021 and 2024 with MOGE's share approximately USD 239 million, are operating in breach of UK Myanmar sanctions extended to UK Overseas Territories. As of April 2026, neither territory has confirmed whether investigations were launched. Civil society should press the UK Foreign Office to require the Overseas Territories to provide a public update on the status of those submissions, and should engage the UK Parliament with the Finance Uncovered and JFM November 2025 investigation as a basis for parliamentary scrutiny of UK Overseas Territories sanctions enforcement. (JFM Nov 2025)
•Investigate and disclose the current payment bank for MOGE-to-Goldpetrol transactions: MOGE shifted payments to Goldpetrol to an undisclosed Myanmar bank following US sanctions on MFTB in June 2023. Interra Resources declined to identify the bank. Justice For Myanmar identified the risk that MOGE may have shifted to MEB. If MOGE is routing Goldpetrol payments through MEB, the international correspondent banks holding nostro accounts for MEB are implicated. Investigative journalists, financial intelligence units and the Monetary Authority of Singapore should require Interra to disclose the identity of the payment bank, and governments should investigate whether MOGE-to-Goldpetrol payments involve any sanctioned financial institution. (JFM Jan 2025; JFM Feb 2025)
•Engage financial institutions on the "Kant Kaw" evasion tactic and enhanced due diligence for MOGE-linked accounts: The UNSR's June 2024 report documented the SAC energy ministry's attempt to route MOGE revenues through MEB under the alias "Kant Kaw" to evade the US financial services directive. Financial institutions with Myanmar-related correspondent banking exposure should be engaged with this specific evasion tactic and urged to implement name-screening that covers known MOGE aliases and associated account names. The UNSR's recommendation to conduct enhanced due diligence on all Myanmar-related transactions should be operationalised through direct engagement with financial institutions. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Call on the UN Security Council to freeze MOGE's foreign currency reserves: The UNSR explicitly called on the UN Security Council to sanction MOGE and freeze assets that rightfully belong to the people of Myanmar, including billions in foreign currency reserves. Over USD 500 million in Shwe gas field euro payments placed into escrow following the EU designation remains outside the junta's reach, demonstrating that international designation has concrete financial consequences for MOGE. Civil society and member states sympathetic to the Myanmar people's cause should raise the UNSR's recommendation at the Security Council and support any resolution extending sanctions to MOGE. (UNSR Jun 2024)
