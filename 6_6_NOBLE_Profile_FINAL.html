NOBLE (formerly ASTRA / TRAA3 / HURON)
Profile 6.6
Summary Assessment
NOBLE (IMO 9162928), formerly HURON, TRAA3, and ASTRA, is a Guinea-flagged tanker that made four confirmed aviation fuel deliveries to Myanmar between January and June 2025, loading at Bandar Abbas, Iran via the FOAA anchorage in Fujairah. No cargo volumes have been confirmed for any delivery. OFAC designated NOBLE (then ASTRA) on 27 June 2024 under Iran sanctions, before it made any documented Myanmar delivery. It continued operating through all four 2025 deliveries. As of late January 2026, SynMax data showed NOBLE feigning an anchorage position off Iraq while loitering near Bandar Abbas, indicating preparation for a further delivery. Sea Hill Maritime SA (UAE, Hamriyah Free Zone) is the registered owner; Sea Route Ship Management FZE (UAE, Hamriyah Free Zone), also OFAC-designated, is the ISM and commercial manager. The UK, EU, Canada, and Australia have not designated NOBLE. Note: IMO 9162928 must be specified in all screening and monitoring to avoid confusion with a separate vessel named NOBLE (IMO 9333400) sanctioned under Russia-related programmes.

2
Iranian Fuel Origin and AIS Evasion

Kpler assessed all four deliveries as originating from Iran. Satellite imagery confirmed NOBLE at Bandar Abbas before its June 2025 delivery, after staging at FOAA. AIS was deactivated during offloading at the Myan Oil Terminal. For the other three deliveries, Kpler commodity intelligence and FOAA staging provide the Iranian attribution. As of late January 2026, SynMax showed NOBLE loitering near Bandar Abbas while its AIS falsely placed it off southern Iraq, indicating preparation for a fifth delivery at time of reporting. (AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)

3
Prior Sanctions History and Iran Shadow Fleet Context

NOBLE was designated by OFAC on 27 June 2024 as ASTRA under Executive Order 13846 for transporting sanctioned Iranian petrochemical products, alongside Sea Route Ship Management FZE and REEF. All four Myanmar deliveries occurred in 2025, at least six months after designation. The vessel was renamed NOBLE and reflagged to Guinea. Reuters described NOBLE as REEF's "larger sister ship." The continued deliveries by a US-sanctioned vessel across an entire year demonstrate that single-jurisdiction designation without complementary measures in other jurisdictions is insufficient to disrupt this supply chain. (AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)

7
Recommended Actions
•Urgently designate NOBLE across the UK, EU, Canada, and Australia. NOBLE made four Myanmar deliveries through all of 2025 after its June 2024 OFAC designation, and was preparing a fifth as of late January 2026. Single-jurisdiction designation is easily evaded through flag changes and access to non-US financial and insurance services. JFM's February 2026 call for coordinated designation by the remaining four jurisdictions remains unimplemented. All submissions should specify IMO 9162928 to distinguish NOBLE from the separately sanctioned vessel of the same name (IMO 9333400). (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
•Designate Sea Hill Maritime SA across all jurisdictions. Sea Route Ship Management FZE was designated alongside NOBLE by OFAC, but Sea Hill Maritime SA, the registered owner, has not been designated by any government. Sea Hill shares its Hamriyah Free Zone address with Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA, the owner of REEF, suggesting a common beneficial owner behind both primary ghost fleet vessels. Any designation action should investigate and address both owner entities simultaneously. (JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage UAE authorities and Guinea's flag registry. Sea Route Ship Management FZE, Sea Hill Maritime SA, and Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA all share a Hamriyah Free Zone address, placing UAE authorities in jurisdiction over the manager and both owners of REEF and NOBLE simultaneously. The Hamriyah Free Zone Authority and the UAE Executive Office of AML/CFT should be formally engaged. Guinea should be notified of NOBLE's documented history as a US-sanctioned vessel. The UAE's 2025 AML Law provides stronger domestic legal grounds for action than existed at the time of the original OFAC designation. (JFM Feb 2026; AI Jan 2026)
•Include Myanmar aviation fuel exports in diplomatic engagement with Iran. Satellite imagery confirmed NOBLE loading at Bandar Abbas before its June 2025 delivery. Kpler assessed all four deliveries as Iranian-sourced. Governments engaging Iran bilaterally should raise Myanmar aviation fuel exports alongside existing IRGC-linked sanctions pressure, consistent with the UN Special Rapporteur's call to hold the Iranian government accountable. (Reuters Jan 2026; AI Jan 2026)
