Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL)
Profile 9.4
Summary Assessment
Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL) is a majority government-owned Indian energy company under the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, listed on the BSE and NSE. It has two documented connections to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. Before the coup, IOCL supplied nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. and its parent Trafigura between 2017 and 2020, entities that were central to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply chain. After the coup, IOCL shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel directly to Myanmar including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel, with Servo-branded products shipped primarily to MCM, a designated Myanmar military arms broker, at a time when MCM was sanctioned by the US and UK. IOCL does not appear in any of the thirty documented aviation fuel shipments to Thilawa. It has not been sanctioned in connection with Myanmar. This is a reference entry cross-referenced from Profile 5.7; primary documentation is in Profiles 5.1 and 5.7.

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Pre-coup upstream supply (2017 to 2020). Between 2017 and 2020 IOCL supplied nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. and Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. Puma Energy built and operated the Thilawa terminal and held a 49 percent stake in NEPAS, Myanmar's licensed aviation fuel importer, throughout the period. This connection is entirely pre-coup and predates any Myanmar military end-use documentation. IOCL had no direct contractual relationship with Myanmar-based entities; its transactions were with Puma's Singapore trading entity. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024; AI Nov 2022)

Post-coup direct Myanmar fuel trade. Since the coup, IOCL shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel directly to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel. In the first eighteen months following the coup, many shipments of Servo-branded engine oil, motor spirits, lubricants, and brake fluids were directed to MCM, which was designated by OFAC and the UK from 25 March 2022. Following Frontier Myanmar's January 2024 publication, IOCL's trade continued: JFM documented at least three additional shipments in late January 2024 and five more in February 2024. MCM was subsequently removed from the OFAC SDN list on 24 July 2025; the UK designation remains active. IOCL does not appear in any documented aviation fuel delivery to Thilawa. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

Indian Oil Corporation Limited has not been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. Its pre-coup transactions with Puma Energy Supply and Trading and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020 predate both Myanmar's February 2021 coup and the post-coup international sanctions framework targeting Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. Its post-coup direct fuel shipments to Myanmar, including Servo brand products to MCM, occurred while MCM was an active OFAC-designated and UK-designated entity, a materially different evidentiary position from the pre-coup supply chain involvement. India has not joined the Myanmar sanctions coalition and has no documented policy restricting fuel exports to Myanmar. As a Maharatna PSU under the administrative control of India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, any advocacy targeting IOCL simultaneously engages the Government of India. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM) is currently designated by the UK (OFSI/FCDO, MYA0043, from 25 March 2022, ongoing as of April 2026; additionally subject to a Director Disqualification Sanction imposed 9 April 2025) and by Canada (Global Affairs Canada, SEMA, ongoing as of April 2026). MCM's US OFAC designation under Executive Order 14014 was removed on 24 July 2025. MCM is not designated by the EU or Australia. MCM's chairman, Aung Hlaing Oo, retains UK designation (MYA0042) and designations by the EU (from 20 February 2023), Canada and Switzerland (from 2 March 2023) as of April 2026. His US OFAC individual designation was also removed.

3
Key Relationships
•Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. / Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.1): The direct purchasers of jet fuel from IOCL in the 2017 to 2020 period. Puma Energy Supply and Trading is Puma Energy's Singapore-based petroleum trading entity. Trafigura held approximately 92% of Puma Energy during the relevant period. Both entities are identified in Frontier Myanmar's January 2024 investigation and the JFM March 2024 report as the recipients of IOCL's pre-coup jet fuel supply. The downstream connection from these entities to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply chain is documented in Profile 5.1. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM): The primary confirmed recipient of IOCL's post-coup Servo brand fuel shipments. MCM is described by Justice For Myanmar as a known arms broker for the Myanmar military. At the time of IOCL's post-coup deliveries, MCM was designated by OFAC (from 25 March 2022) and by the UK (from 25 March 2022). MCM's OFAC designation was removed 24 July 2025. MCM retains UK designation (MYA0043) and Canada designation as of April 2026, and was additionally subject to a UK Director Disqualification Sanction from 9 April 2025. MCM's chairman, Aung Hlaing Oo, retains UK (MYA0042), EU, Canada and Switzerland designations as of April 2026; his US OFAC individual designation was also removed. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•IOCL Singapore Pte. Ltd.: IOCL's Singapore-registered subsidiary. The source record does not specify whether the jet fuel transactions with Puma Energy Supply and Trading were conducted through the Singapore subsidiary or directly by the Indian parent entity. This distinction is relevant to any assessment of the applicable legal framework and jurisdiction for potential advocacy or disclosure requests. (Frontier Jan 2024)
•Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, Government of India: The oversight ministry with direct administrative responsibility for IOCL as a Maharatna PSU. The Government of India holds approximately 51.50% of IOCL's shares. Because IOCL is majority-owned by the Indian government, India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas is the primary addressee for any government-to-government engagement targeting IOCL's Myanmar fuel relationships. (JFM Mar 2024)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•15 January 2024. Frontier Myanmar documents IOCL's pre-coup USD 140 million Puma/Trafigura supply and post-coup direct Myanmar trade including shipments to the then-designated MCM. No IOCL response is documented. (Frontier Jan 2024)

5
Recommended Actions
•Raise IOCL's Myanmar fuel trade directly with India's government. IOCL is a government-owned company under the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas. Engagement with IOCL is simultaneously engagement with the Indian state. Governments with Myanmar sanctions programmes, particularly Quad partners (the US, Australia, and Japan) and significant trade partners (the UK and EU), should raise the Frontier Myanmar and JFM findings through diplomatic channels, requesting an account of IOCL's post-coup deliveries to MCM while MCM was designated and its current Myanmar supply policies. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Refer IOCL's post-designation MCM shipments to OFAC and UK OTSI. IOCL shipped Servo-branded products to MCM after MCM was designated by the US and UK on 25 March 2022. Whether these shipments constitute sanctions violations depends on product classification and any applicable exemptions. A formal referral to OFAC and the UK Office of Trade Sanctions Implementation should present the Frontier Myanmar trade data and request an assessment. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Engage IOCL on human rights due diligence through its BSE and NSE listing and GDR holders. IOCL's global depository receipts on the Luxembourg Stock Exchange provide access for European institutional investors. Civil society should engage IOCL directly on its post-coup Myanmar due diligence, what end-use screening it applied to MCM as a customer, and whether it has assessed its obligations under the UN Guiding Principles. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
