Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL)
Profile 9.4
Summary Assessment
Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL, also trading as IndianOil) is a Maharatna Public Sector Undertaking under the ownership and administrative control of India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE: 530965) and National Stock Exchange of India (NSE: IOC). It is the largest government-owned integrated energy company in India. IOCL has two documented connections to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. The first is its pre-coup role as an upstream jet fuel supplier: between 2017 and 2020, IOCL supplied nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. in Singapore and its parent Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd., both of which were central to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply chain during that period (Profile 5.1). The second is its post-coup direct fuel trade: since the February 2021 coup, IOCL has shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel directly to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel, with Servo brand engine oil, motor spirits, lubricants and brake fluids shipped primarily to Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM), a known arms broker for the Myanmar military that was designated by the US (from 25 March 2022, removed 24 July 2025), the UK (from 25 March 2022, still active), and Canada (still active). IOCL does not appear in any of the thirty documented aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar's Thilawa terminal. IOCL has not been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. This profile is a reference entry, cross-referenced from Profile 5.7. The principal supply chain documentation is addressed in Profile 5.1 (Puma Energy / Trafigura) and Profile 5.7 (Indian Oil Corporation Limited).

1
Connection to the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

IOCL's first documented connection to Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain is its pre-coup role as an upstream jet fuel supplier. Between 2017 and 2020, IOCL supplied nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd., Puma Energy's Singapore-based petroleum trading entity, and to Puma's parent Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. This period falls entirely before Myanmar's February 2021 military coup. The downstream pathway from Puma Energy to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply chain was established by Amnesty International's November 2022 Deadly Cargo investigation (Profile 5.1), which documented that Puma Energy was the main foreign business involved in handling, storage and distribution of aviation fuel in Myanmar through its Puma Energy Asia Sun terminal at Thilawa and its joint venture in the National Energy Prime Aviation Services Company Limited (NEPAS) with the state-owned Myanmar Petrochemical Enterprise. IOCL's commercial fuel transactions with Puma Energy in Singapore thus formed part of the upstream supply chain that supported Puma's Myanmar operations, though IOCL had no direct contractual relationship with any Myanmar-based entity in the documented record. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024; AI Nov 2022)

IOCL's second documented connection is its post-coup direct fuel trade with Myanmar. Since the February 2021 coup, Frontier Myanmar's investigation documented that IOCL shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel directly to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel. In the first eighteen months following the coup, many shipments consisted of engine oil, motor spirits, lubricants and brake fluids manufactured under the Servo brand. The majority of these Servo exports were shipped to Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM), a company described by Justice For Myanmar as a known arms broker for the Myanmar military. At the time of these deliveries, MCM was designated by the US (OFAC, from 25 March 2022) and the UK (from 25 March 2022). IOCL's trade with Myanmar continued after Frontier Myanmar's reporting in January 2024, with Justice For Myanmar documenting at least three additional shipments in late January 2024 and five more in February 2024. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

IOCL does not appear in any of the thirty documented aviation fuel deliveries to the Thilawa terminal from February 2021 onwards. Its post-coup trade consisted of engine oil, lubricants, diesel and other petroleum products. The connection to Group 9 arises because IOCL's pre-coup jet fuel supply to Puma Energy forms part of the historical supply chain documentation for the same Thilawa terminal infrastructure that, after Puma Energy's exit in December 2022, continued to receive aviation fuel under the management of the Shoon Energy network (Profile 2.1). (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

Indian Oil Corporation Limited has not been sanctioned by any government in connection with Myanmar. Its pre-coup transactions with Puma Energy Supply and Trading and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020 predate both Myanmar's February 2021 coup and the post-coup international sanctions framework targeting Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain. Its post-coup direct fuel shipments to Myanmar, including Servo brand products to MCM, occurred while MCM was an active OFAC-designated and UK-designated entity, a materially different evidentiary position from the pre-coup supply chain involvement. India has not joined the Myanmar sanctions coalition and has no documented policy restricting fuel exports to Myanmar. As a Maharatna PSU under the administrative control of India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, any advocacy targeting IOCL simultaneously engages the Government of India. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)

Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM) is currently designated by the UK (OFSI/FCDO, MYA0043, from 25 March 2022, ongoing as of April 2026; additionally subject to a Director Disqualification Sanction imposed 9 April 2025) and by Canada (Global Affairs Canada, SEMA, ongoing as of April 2026). MCM's US OFAC designation under Executive Order 14014 was removed on 24 July 2025. MCM is not designated by the EU or Australia. MCM's chairman, Aung Hlaing Oo, retains UK designation (MYA0042) and designations by the EU (from 20 February 2023), Canada and Switzerland (from 2 March 2023) as of April 2026. His US OFAC individual designation was also removed.

Key Relationships
•Puma Energy Supply and Trading Pte. Ltd. / Trafigura Group Pte. Ltd. (Profile 5.1): The direct purchasers of jet fuel from IOCL in the 2017 to 2020 period. Puma Energy Supply and Trading is Puma Energy's Singapore-based petroleum trading entity. Trafigura held approximately 92% of Puma Energy during the relevant period. Both entities are identified in Frontier Myanmar's January 2024 investigation and the JFM March 2024 report as the recipients of IOCL's pre-coup jet fuel supply. The downstream connection from these entities to Myanmar's military aviation fuel supply chain is documented in Profile 5.1. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Myanmar Chemical and Machinery Company Limited (MCM): The primary confirmed recipient of IOCL's post-coup Servo brand fuel shipments. MCM is described by Justice For Myanmar as a known arms broker for the Myanmar military. At the time of IOCL's post-coup deliveries, MCM was designated by OFAC (from 25 March 2022) and by the UK (from 25 March 2022). MCM's OFAC designation was removed 24 July 2025. MCM retains UK designation (MYA0043) and Canada designation as of April 2026, and was additionally subject to a UK Director Disqualification Sanction from 9 April 2025. MCM's chairman, Aung Hlaing Oo, retains UK (MYA0042), EU, Canada and Switzerland designations as of April 2026; his US OFAC individual designation was also removed. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•IOCL Singapore Pte. Ltd.: IOCL's Singapore-registered subsidiary. The source record does not specify whether the jet fuel transactions with Puma Energy Supply and Trading were conducted through the Singapore subsidiary or directly by the Indian parent entity. This distinction is relevant to any assessment of the applicable legal framework and jurisdiction for potential advocacy or disclosure requests. (Frontier Jan 2024)
•Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, Government of India: The oversight ministry with direct administrative responsibility for IOCL as a Maharatna PSU. The Government of India holds approximately 51.50% of IOCL's shares. Because IOCL is majority-owned by the Indian government, India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas is the primary addressee for any government-to-government engagement targeting IOCL's Myanmar fuel relationships. (JFM Mar 2024)
4
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•15 January 2024. Frontier Myanmar published its investigation documenting that since the February 2021 coup IOCL had shipped over USD 3.7 million in fuel to Myanmar, including USD 1.5 million in navy grade diesel, and that in the first eighteen months following the coup many shipments of Servo brand engine oil, motor spirits, lubricants and brake fluids were directed primarily to the then-OFAC-designated and UK-designated MCM. The investigation also documented IOCL's pre-coup supply of nearly USD 140 million in jet fuel to Puma Energy Supply and Trading and Trafigura between 2017 and 2020. (Frontier Jan 2024)
•27 March 2024. Justice For Myanmar published its investigation into India's role in facilitating aviation fuel supply to Myanmar's military, confirming Frontier Myanmar's findings on the Puma Energy and Trafigura jet fuel supply relationship and documenting that following Frontier Myanmar's reporting on 15 January 2024, IOCL's trade with Myanmar continued, with at least three additional shipments in late January 2024 and five more in February 2024. No advocacy contact with IOCL is recorded in either report and no response from IOCL has been documented as of April 2026. (JFM Mar 2024)
•3 April 2025. Justice For Myanmar published its BIMSTEC statement documenting continued Indian state PSU military supply to Myanmar. The report confirmed that in January 2025, Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL) shipped two electro-optical surveillance systems used for maritime surveillance to the Myanmar army's Directorate of Procurement, following multiple BEL shipments in 2024. IOCL is not named in this report, but the documented pattern of Indian state PSU military supply to Myanmar is directly relevant to engagement with India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas on IOCL's Myanmar fuel trade. (JFM BIMSTEC Apr 2025)
•24 July 2025. OFAC removed MCM and its owner Aung Hlaing Oo from the SDN list, two weeks after Myanmar junta head Min Aung Hlaing sent a letter praising President Trump as a "strong leader" and requesting easing of US sanctions. UK and Canada designations of MCM and Aung Hlaing Oo remain in force. Human Rights Watch described the removal as "extremely worrying." The removal does not affect the UK sanctions compliance question arising from IOCL's post-coup Servo brand shipments to MCM, which occurred while MCM was an active UK-designated entity under the Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021. (Reuters Jul 2025)
5
Recommended Actions
•Raise IOCL's Myanmar fuel trade directly with India's government through diplomatic channels: IOCL is a majority government-owned company under the administrative control of India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas. This means engagement with IOCL is simultaneously engagement with the Indian state. Governments with Myanmar sanctions programmes, particularly those that are also Quad partners of India (the United States, Australia and Japan) or significant trading and investment partners (the UK and EU), should raise the Frontier Myanmar and JFM March 2024 findings directly through diplomatic channels, asking the Indian government to account for IOCL's confirmed post-coup fuel deliveries to the then-designated MCM and to explain what end-use screening IOCL applied. This is a targeted and proportionate ask: IOCL is not a weapons manufacturer, and the documented fuel trade is factually distinct from the BEL and HAL arms sales for which Norway and Sweden have excluded comparable Indian PSUs. Civil society should separately contextualise IOCL's role to relevant diplomatic contacts by noting that SIPRI data for 2018 to 2022 (the most recent five-year data available) confirms India as the third largest supplier of arms and equipment to Myanmar after Russia and China, accounting for 14 percent of Myanmar's arms imports, and that SIPRI data for 2017 to 2021 shows over 50 percent of India's total arms exports went to Myanmar, establishing a pattern of state enterprise involvement in military supply to Myanmar that extends across defence and energy sectors. (SIPRI; Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Engage institutional shareholders using the BEL and HAL exclusion decisions as directly applicable precedent: IOCL's non-government institutional shareholders include ONGC (approximately 14.2%), LIC (approximately 6.43%), and Oil India Limited (approximately 5.16%), all Indian state enterprises, alongside international shareholders including, as of 2024, Nippon Life Asset Management and Franklin Templeton Asset Management. Civil society should engage international institutional shareholders with submissions citing IOCL's confirmed upstream role in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain, the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights obligations applicable to institutional investors, and the exclusion decisions that have been made against comparable Indian PSUs over Myanmar supply. Sweden's Council on Ethics of the AP pension funds (AP1-AP4) recommended exclusion of BEL from its 2024 Annual Report (published March 2025), on the basis that BEL's sale of weapons to the Myanmar military contributed to violations of international humanitarian law. AP7 excluded both BEL and HAL on 8 December 2025 on the same basis. Norway's Government Pension Fund Global excluded BEL on 24 January 2023. These exclusion decisions establish that Swedish and Norwegian pension funds are prepared to act against Indian defence PSUs specifically over Myanmar military supply connections, and provide directly applicable precedent for engagement with any institutional investor holding IOCL equity. Engagement should note that the post-coup direct fuel trade with MCM, while involving petroleum products rather than weapons, raises analogous due diligence failures under the UN Guiding Principles: IOCL continued shipping to MCM while MCM was designated by OFAC and the UK as a Myanmar arms broker. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Submit a UK sanctions compliance inquiry to the FCDO and relevant parliamentary committees: IOCL's post-coup Servo brand fuel shipments to MCM occurred while MCM was designated under the UK's Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations 2021, which prohibit making economic resources available, directly or indirectly, to or for the benefit of a designated person. Civil society should submit a formal inquiry to the FCDO and to relevant parliamentary committees documenting the confirmed shipments to MCM during its period of UK designation, and requesting that the FCDO investigate whether UK sanctions compliance obligations were breached. Any such inquiry should address the jurisdictional question: the UK Myanmar (Sanctions) Regulations apply to acts by UK persons and to acts within the UK, and whether IOCL or its Singapore subsidiary IOCL Singapore Pte. Ltd. falls within scope depends on the specific transaction structure, which has not been publicly confirmed. Establishing that IOCL Singapore Pte. Ltd. handled the transactions would create a stronger UK nexus, given Singapore's status as a UK-recognised financial centre and the potential for UK-linked correspondent banking exposure. Even if direct UK jurisdiction over IOCL is not established, an FCDO inquiry would create a public record of the compliance question and could prompt broader due diligence engagement by UK-connected financial institutions. This UK compliance question is entirely unaffected by OFAC's removal of MCM from the US SDN list in July 2025. MCM's additional UK Director Disqualification Sanction of 9 April 2025 confirms that the UK continues to treat MCM as a priority sanctions target, strengthening the case for a compliance review of entities that supplied MCM during its designation period. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
•Engage India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas on end-use screening policy: As IOCL's administrative authority, India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas is the appropriate state-level engagement point. Civil society and governments with Myanmar sanctions programmes should engage the Ministry to raise the Frontier Myanmar and JFM March 2024 findings and to encourage the adoption of end-use screening policies for fuel sales by Indian state-owned enterprises to purchasers connected to Myanmar's military or to entities subject to international sanctions in any jurisdiction. This engagement is particularly important given that India has not joined the Myanmar sanctions coalition. IOCL is majority-owned by the Indian government, meaning any meaningful change in IOCL's Myanmar fuel trade policy requires engagement at the ministerial level. (JFM Mar 2024)
•Monitor IOCL's ongoing direct Myanmar fuel trade: Justice For Myanmar confirmed that IOCL's direct Myanmar trade continued after Frontier Myanmar's January 2024 reporting, with at least three additional shipments in late January 2024 and five more in February 2024. No source reviewed after March 2024 documents further IOCL Myanmar activity. Given the documented pattern of continued trade following public exposure, civil society and institutional investors should monitor shipping and trade data for any further IOCL fuel exports to Myanmar. If renewed or continuing direct Myanmar shipments are identified, or if any IOCL entity is identified in connection with a Myanmar aviation fuel shipment, that finding should trigger immediate re-engagement, formal disclosure requests and escalation to the Indian government. (Frontier Jan 2024; JFM Mar 2024)
