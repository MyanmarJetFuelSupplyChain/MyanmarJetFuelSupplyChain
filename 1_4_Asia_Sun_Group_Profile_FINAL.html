Asia Sun Group Company Limited
Profile 1.4
Summary Assessment
Asia Sun Group Company Limited is the Myanmar crony conglomerate that co-controls the aviation fuel import chain alongside MPE. Through its Singapore holding companies it owns the Thilawa terminal and holds the NEPAS joint venture share. Through Myanmar-registered proxy companies it purchased approximately USD 15 million of aviation fuel in 2023. Khin Phyu Win controls the group; she transferred formal ownership of eleven network companies to Zaw Min Tun in April 2023. Both are sanctioned by the US, UK, and Canada. Asia Sun Group Company Limited itself is sanctioned by all five jurisdictions. The operating entities through which aviation fuel is currently imported and distributed (NEPAS, Swan Energy, and Myan Oil Terminal) remain unsanctioned or only partially sanctioned.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Asia Sun Group has been embedded in Myanmar's aviation fuel supply chain since before the coup, first as a minority partner in the Puma Energy-operated Thilawa terminal, and from December 2022 as a full co-controller alongside MPE of the entire import chain. Through its Singapore holding companies, Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd.) and P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. (formerly Puma Energy Irrawaddy Pte. Ltd.), it holds the NEPAS joint venture share and owns the Myan Oil Terminal. MPE ran a post-coup tender for Air Force fuel supply in which Puma did not participate; Asia Sun Group and MPE operated as joint controllers from early 2021. (AI Nov 2022; UNSR Jun 2024)

In 2023, Asia Sun Group purchased aviation fuel through three Myanmar-registered proxy companies: Swan Energy and Myan-Oil (both buying from CB Energy through United Amara Bank to Krung Thai Bank) and Rich Ray Trading (buying from Tawan-Oil Trading through United Amara Bank to Kasikorn Bank). Together the three proxies paid approximately USD 15 million. Swan Energy's own website claimed it supplied 10,000 metric tonnes of aviation fuel to Myanmar per month, representing 100 percent of the market. (UNSR Jun 2024)

In late 2022 Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd. renamed itself Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd., and the network adopted the informal Shoon Group identity. The UN Special Rapporteur assessed the rebranding was intended to obscure the network's role following the Deadly Cargo investigation. The function and ownership structure did not change. Khin Phyu Win's April 2023 transfer of formal ownership of eleven network companies to Zaw Min Tun followed the same logic. Despite successive designations of the parent company and key individuals, the operating entities beneath them remain functional and largely unsanctioned. (UNSR Jun 2024)

2
Evidence
Aviation fuel deliveries to Myanmar Air Force bases, January to August 2022

Amnesty International's November 2022 investigation documented that between January and August 2022 (air base datasets; NEPAS invoices date from October 2021), aviation fuel from the MPE and Asia Sun Group co-controlled Thilawa terminal was delivered directly to ten Myanmar Air Force bases, totalling more than 17 million litres. A further 37.8 million litres were delivered to NEPAS-managed civilian airports over the same period. This establishes direct military end-use for fuel passing through Asia Sun Group-controlled infrastructure. (AI Nov 2022)

Asia Sun Group as purchaser in the 2023 supply chain

The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 Banking on the Death Trade report documented, from confidential trade records, that Asia Sun Group used three Myanmar-registered proxy companies to buy aviation fuel in calendar year 2023. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil each bought from CB Energy, a Thailand-registered trading company, paying through United Amara Bank to CB Energy's account at Krung Thai Bank. Rich Ray Trading bought from Tawan-Oil Trading, a second Thailand-registered company, paying through United Amara Bank to Tawan-Oil's account at Kasikorn Bank. Together, the three proxies paid approximately USD 15 million for these purchases. The Special Rapporteur's documentation does not break this figure down by individual company. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Asia Sun Trading's military-purpose import declarations

Amnesty International's November 2022 investigation obtained copies of two separate letters, one addressed to the Myanma Port Authority and one to the Customs Department, in which Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. declared to Myanmar state institutions that it was importing Jet A-1 aviation fuel "for use in Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s Aircrafts and Helicopter." One key document was a leaked Ministry of Defence letter dated 16 December 2021 sent to the Myanma Port Authority, stating that the SYNERGY vessel carrying 21,000 tonnes of Jet A-1 was "required for use in Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s Aircrafts and Helicopters purchased by Asia Sun Trading Co., Ltd. from Singapore-based Singapore Petroleum Co." AI also obtained a proforma invoice dated 18 November 2021 between Asia Sun Trading and Singapore Petroleum Company relating to this shipment. These documents constitute direct official evidence that Asia Sun Group entities formally declared military end-use of the fuel they were importing. (AI Nov 2022)

Avgas imports via Myeik port, 2022

In addition to importing Jet A-1 aviation fuel, Asia Sun Group imported aviation gasoline (avgas 100 LL) in drums from Thailand on at least two separate occasions in 2022, declaring it for military use. Two shipments of avgas 100 LL arrived at Myeik port from Ranong port, Thailand: the first in June 2022, the second on 27 July 2022. For the June 2022 shipment, AI obtained a proforma invoice between Asia Sun Trading (consignee) and the seller. For the 27 July 2022 shipment, AI obtained a Customs Department letter from the Myeik Coastal Township Office stating the fuel was "required for use by the Tatmadaw (Air Force)." Myeik air base is located close to Myeik port. These avgas imports establish that Asia Sun Group's military fuel supply role extended beyond Jet A-1 to include aviation gasoline. (AI Nov 2022)

Swan Energy's market share claim

Swan Energy Company Limited, an Asia Sun Group proxy, states on its own company website that it sells 10,000 metric tonnes of aviation fuel to Myanmar every month, which it claims represents 100 percent of the Myanmar market share. This claim was cited by the UN Special Rapporteur as evidence of Swan Energy's centrality to the supply chain, and noted as information that effective due diligence by the Thai banks handling the transactions should have surfaced. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The Asia Sun Group sanctions gap: The parent company Asia Sun Group Company Limited is sanctioned in all five jurisdictions. All three Singapore holding companies and two key individuals are sanctioned by the US. Yet the operating entities that actually conduct the day-to-day aviation fuel business, NEPAS, Myan Oil Terminal, and (until October 2024) Swan Energy, Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading, were unsanctioned for nearly two years after the parent was designated. Even following the October 2024 Canada and UK round, NEPAS and Myan Oil Terminal remain unsanctioned in all jurisdictions, leaving the import licence and the physical terminal outside any sanctions regime. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Oct 2024)
Note on Canada / Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.: Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. does not appear as a named entry in Canada's Special Economic Measures (Burma) Regulations (SEMA) sanctions list. Canada is marked as sanctioned here because Canada's SEMA regulations include Asia Sun Group Company Limited, of which Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. is a subsidiary and affiliated company. Under Canadian sanctions law, entities owned or controlled by a designated person or entity are subject to the same prohibitions as the designated entity. Canada's October 2023 aviation fuel ban (SOR/2023-228, effective 31 October 2023) also prohibits the export, sale, supply and shipment of aviation fuel, and the provision of shipping insurance, to Myanmar, a prohibition that applies to all actors in the supply chain regardless of individual designation status. Advocates should verify the precise legal basis for any enforcement action under Canadian law.

6
Recommended Actions
•Designate NEPAS and Myan Oil Terminal in all five jurisdictions. Successive designations of Asia Sun Group and its Singapore holding companies have not disrupted operations because the operating entities beneath them remain unsanctioned. NEPAS holds the import licence; Myan Oil Terminal is the physical entry point. Until both are designated, the supply chain retains its infrastructure regardless of what happens to the ownership layer. (UNSR Jun 2024; AI Nov 2022)
•Designate Swan Energy in the US, EU, and Australia. Swan Energy is sanctioned by Canada and the UK but not by the US, EU, or Australia. Its own website's claim of 100 percent of Myanmar's aviation fuel market constitutes direct evidence of operating in Myanmar's jet fuel sector under the August 2023 OFAC sector determination. The same evidence supports EU and Australian submissions. (OFAC Aug 2023; UNSR Jun 2024)
•Extend Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading designations to the US, EU, Canada, and Australia. Both are documented proxy purchasers in the UNSR's 2023 transaction table and currently sanctioned only by the UK. Coordinated action by the remaining jurisdictions would close the gap. (UNSR Jun 2024; UK Oct 2024)
•Press Singapore to act on sanctioned entities that remain on its corporate registry. Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd., PEIA Pte. Ltd., and P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd. all remain active on Singapore's corporate registry despite US designations, confirmed by JFM in May 2025. Singapore's ACRA and Commercial Affairs Department should be formally requested to investigate. Singapore's Ministry of Foreign Affairs should explain publicly why Singaporean corporate infrastructure continues to support a supply chain that allied governments have designated as enabling war crimes. (JFM May 2025)
