Asia Sun Group Company Limited
Profile 1.4
Summary Assessment
Asia Sun Group Company Limited is the Myanmar crony conglomerate that has served as the private-sector partner to Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE) in the military junta's aviation fuel supply chain since before the February 2021 coup. Through its network of Myanmar and Singapore subsidiaries and affiliated companies, Asia Sun Group co-controlled the Thilawa aviation fuel terminal during the Puma Energy era as a minority partner, and following Puma Energy's exit in December 2022 became a full co-controlling entity of the entire imported aviation fuel supply chain alongside MPE. The conglomerate operates under the informal name the Shoon Group following a corporate rebranding in late 2022 that was intended, in the assessment of the UN Special Rapporteur, to obscure its role in the aviation fuel supply chain in the face of public exposure. Asia Sun Group is controlled by Khin Phyu Win, who transferred formal ownership of 11 Asia Sun network companies to Zaw Min Tun in April 2023. Both individuals are sanctioned by the United States, United Kingdom and Canada. Asia Sun Group Company Limited itself is sanctioned by all five key jurisdictions: the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, Canada and Australia. Despite the designation of the parent company and key individuals, the operating entities through which aviation fuel is currently imported, stored and distributed, including NEPAS, Swan Energy and the Myan Oil Terminal, remain unsanctioned or only partially sanctioned as of April 2026.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Asia Sun Group Company Limited is the private-sector conglomerate that has partnered with the Myanmar military's state enterprise, MPE, to control the aviation fuel import, storage and distribution chain inside Myanmar. Its role spans the full supply chain: through its Singapore holding companies it holds ownership interests in the legal importer (NEPAS) and the physical terminal (Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited), and through its Myanmar-registered operating companies it conducted and facilitated fuel purchases and distribution before and after Puma Energy's exit from Myanmar.

Pre-coup role

At the time of the February 2021 coup, Asia Sun Group was already embedded in the aviation fuel supply chain as a minority partner in the Puma Energy-operated Thilawa terminal, operating as Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS). An Asia Sun Group affiliate in Singapore, Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd., was also a joint venture partner in NEPAS alongside Puma Energy. Asia Sun Group was thus the junta's domestic private-sector intermediary from the outset of the post-coup aviation fuel supply chain. (AI Nov 2022)

Post-coup and post-Puma structure

Following the coup, MPE ran a tender for the supply and distribution of aviation fuel to the Myanmar Air Force, in which Puma Energy declined to participate. MPE and Asia Sun Group began operating as joint controllers of the aviation fuel import and distribution chain from early 2021. In December 2022, Puma Energy completed the sale of its Myanmar assets to a joint venture between an Asia Sun Group network company and MPE, transferring the Thilawa terminal and the NEPAS joint venture share entirely to Asia Sun Group and MPE. From this point, Asia Sun Group and MPE became the sole controlling entities of the entire imported aviation fuel supply chain. (AI Nov 2022; UNSR Jun 2024)

In calendar year 2023, Asia Sun Group operated through three Myanmar-registered proxy companies, Swan Energy, Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading, purchasing aviation fuel from two Thailand-registered trading companies through United Amara Bank. The transaction structure and financial flows are documented in full in Section 2. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The Shoon Group rebranding

In late 2022, Asia Sun Aviation Pte. Ltd. changed its name to Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. The UN Special Rapporteur noted that this renaming was likely intended to obscure the aviation fuel supply chain in the face of public exposure following Amnesty International's November 2022 Deadly Cargo investigation. The broader network adopted the informal Shoon Group identity at the same time, though Asia Sun Group Company Limited itself retained its registered name. The function and ownership structure of the network did not change; only the names were altered. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Ownership obfuscation: Khin Phyu Win to Zaw Min Tun

Khin Phyu Win, the beneficial owner and controller of Asia Sun Group, transferred formal ownership of 11 Asia Sun network companies to a man named Zaw Min Tun in April 2023. The UN Special Rapporteur noted this transfer occurred possibly in anticipation of action against aviation fuel suppliers. Both Khin Phyu Win and Zaw Min Tun have been sanctioned by the United States, United Kingdom and Canada. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The significance of Asia Sun Group's role: Asia Sun Group is the only private-sector entity embedded in every layer of the Myanmar aviation fuel import chain: as terminal co-operator (before the coup through Asia Sun Energy's PEAS stake, and again from December 2022 following Puma Energy's exit), as NEPAS joint venture partner through Asia Sun Aviation / Shoon Energy, and as direct fuel purchaser through its proxy companies Swan Energy, Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading. For analysis of the resulting sanctions gap, see Section 4.
2
Evidence
Aviation fuel deliveries to Myanmar Air Force bases, January to August 2022

Amnesty International's November 2022 investigation documented that between January and August 2022 (air base datasets; NEPAS invoices date from October 2021), aviation fuel from the MPE and Asia Sun Group co-controlled Thilawa terminal was delivered directly to ten Myanmar Air Force bases, totalling more than 17 million litres. A further 37.8 million litres were delivered to NEPAS-managed civilian airports over the same period. This establishes direct military end-use for fuel passing through Asia Sun Group-controlled infrastructure. (AI Nov 2022)

Asia Sun Group as purchaser in the 2023 supply chain

The UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 Banking on the Death Trade report documented, from confidential trade records, that Asia Sun Group used three Myanmar-registered proxy companies to buy aviation fuel in calendar year 2023. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil each bought from CB Energy, a Thailand-registered trading company, paying through United Amara Bank to CB Energy's account at Krung Thai Bank. Rich Ray Trading bought from Tawan-Oil Trading, a second Thailand-registered company, paying through United Amara Bank to Tawan-Oil's account at Kasikorn Bank. Together, the three proxies paid approximately USD 15 million for these purchases. The Special Rapporteur's documentation does not break this figure down by individual company. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Asia Sun Trading's military-purpose import declarations

Amnesty International's November 2022 investigation obtained copies of two separate letters, one addressed to the Myanma Port Authority and one to the Customs Department, in which Asia Sun Trading Co. Ltd. declared to Myanmar state institutions that it was importing Jet A-1 aviation fuel "for use in Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s Aircrafts and Helicopter." One key document was a leaked Ministry of Defence letter dated 16 December 2021 sent to the Myanma Port Authority, stating that the SYNERGY vessel carrying 21,000 tonnes of Jet A-1 was "required for use in Tatmadaw (Air Force)'s Aircrafts and Helicopters purchased by Asia Sun Trading Co., Ltd. from Singapore-based Singapore Petroleum Co." AI also obtained a proforma invoice dated 18 November 2021 between Asia Sun Trading and Singapore Petroleum Company relating to this shipment. These documents constitute direct official evidence that Asia Sun Group entities formally declared military end-use of the fuel they were importing. (AI Nov 2022)

Avgas imports via Myeik port, 2022

In addition to importing Jet A-1 aviation fuel, Asia Sun Group imported aviation gasoline (avgas 100 LL) in drums from Thailand on at least two separate occasions in 2022, declaring it for military use. Two shipments of avgas 100 LL arrived at Myeik port from Ranong port, Thailand: the first in June 2022, the second on 27 July 2022. For the June 2022 shipment, AI obtained a proforma invoice between Asia Sun Trading (consignee) and the seller. For the 27 July 2022 shipment, AI obtained a Customs Department letter from the Myeik Coastal Township Office stating the fuel was "required for use by the Tatmadaw (Air Force)." Myeik air base is located close to Myeik port. These avgas imports establish that Asia Sun Group's military fuel supply role extended beyond Jet A-1 to include aviation gasoline. (AI Nov 2022)

Swan Energy's market share claim

Swan Energy Company Limited, an Asia Sun Group proxy, states on its own company website that it sells 10,000 metric tonnes of aviation fuel to Myanmar every month, which it claims represents 100 percent of the Myanmar market share. This claim was cited by the UN Special Rapporteur as evidence of Swan Energy's centrality to the supply chain, and noted as information that effective due diligence by the Thai banks handling the transactions should have surfaced. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The Asia Sun Group sanctions gap: The parent company Asia Sun Group Company Limited is sanctioned in all five jurisdictions. All three Singapore holding companies and two key individuals are sanctioned by the US. Yet the operating entities that actually conduct the day-to-day aviation fuel business, NEPAS, Myan Oil Terminal, and (until October 2024) Swan Energy, Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading, were unsanctioned for nearly two years after the parent was designated. Even following the October 2024 Canada and UK round, NEPAS and Myan Oil Terminal remain unsanctioned in all jurisdictions, leaving the import licence and the physical terminal outside any sanctions regime. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM Oct 2024)
Note on Canada / Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd.: Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. does not appear as a named entry in Canada's Special Economic Measures (Burma) Regulations (SEMA) sanctions list. Canada is marked as sanctioned here because Canada's SEMA regulations include Asia Sun Group Company Limited, of which Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. is a subsidiary and affiliated company. Under Canadian sanctions law, entities owned or controlled by a designated person or entity are subject to the same prohibitions as the designated entity. Canada's October 2023 aviation fuel ban (SOR/2023-228, effective 31 October 2023) also prohibits the export, sale, supply and shipment of aviation fuel, and the provision of shipping insurance, to Myanmar, a prohibition that applies to all actors in the supply chain regardless of individual designation status. Advocates should verify the precise legal basis for any enforcement action under Canadian law.

6
Recommended Actions
•Designate NEPAS and Myan Oil Terminal in all five jurisdictions: The designation of Asia Sun Group Company Limited and its Singapore holding companies has not disrupted operations because the operating entities beneath them remain unsanctioned. NEPAS holds the import licence; Myan Oil Terminal is the physical point of entry for every documented aviation fuel shipment. Until these entities are designated, the supply chain retains its legal and physical infrastructure regardless of what happens to the ownership layer above them. The UNSR's documented transaction data for 2023 provides the evidentiary basis for OFAC, OFSI, EU EEAS, Canadian Global Affairs and Australian DFAT submissions.
•Close the Swan Energy gap in the US, EU and Australia: Swan Energy Company Limited is now sanctioned by Canada and the UK but remains unsanctioned by the US, EU and Australia as of April 2026. Its own website's claim of 100 percent market share of Myanmar aviation fuel sales constitutes public evidence of operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy within the meaning of the August 2023 OFAC sector determination, and should support a straightforward US designation. The same evidence supports EU and Australian submissions.
•Extend Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading sanctions to US, EU, Canada and Australia: Both entities are currently sanctioned only by the UK. They are documented in the UNSR's 2023 transaction table as Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers (Myan-Oil purchasing from CB Energy alongside Swan Energy, and Rich Ray Trading purchasing from Tawan-Oil Trading) and both fall squarely within the August 2023 OFAC sector determination. Coordinated designation by the remaining four jurisdictions would close the gap left by the UK acting alone in October 2024.
•Engage Singapore authorities on persistent corporate registry non-compliance: All three Shoon Group Singapore companies, Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd., PEIA Pte. Ltd. and P.E.I. Energy Pte. Ltd., remain active on the Singapore corporate registry as of April 2026 despite being sanctioned by the US and, in the case of Shoon Energy, by the UK and Canada. Justice for Myanmar documented this publicly in May 2025. Civil society should formally request that Singapore's Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA) initiate an investigation into whether these companies have complied with Singapore's obligations as a financial centre and explain publicly why sanctioned entities remain on its live registry. Singapore's Commercial Affairs Department, which handles financial crime enforcement, should be called upon to investigate whether the continued operation of these entities constitutes a breach of Singapore's obligations under UN Security Council resolutions and bilateral commitments to sanctions partners. Singapore's Ministry of Foreign Affairs should also be engaged to explain publicly why Singaporean corporate infrastructure continues to be used in support of a supply chain that allied governments have designated as enabling war crimes. (JFM May 2025; JFM Oct 2024)
