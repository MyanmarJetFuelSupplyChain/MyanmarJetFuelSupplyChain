Myanmar Jet Fuel Supply Chain

Since the military coup of 1 February 2021, Myanmar's Air Force has conducted thousands of airstrikes on civilians, targeting hospitals, schools, markets, and villages. Every one of those attacks has required Jet A-1 aviation fuel. This database is an open-source accountability resource documenting the international supply chain that has enabled those attacks by providing Myanmar's military with aviation fuel since the coup.

The supply chain has never been interrupted. Between 2021 and 2022, major international refiners in Singapore, Thailand, and India sold fuel directly to Myanmar. When that model was exposed by investigators in 2022, the chain adapted, routing fuel through Vietnam to obscure its destination. From mid-2024, Iranian-linked vessels began delivering fuel using ghost fleet evasion tactics, and documented imports reached a post-coup high in 2025.

Each of the 49 profiles in this database documents an entity's role in the supply chain, the evidence base for that role, its current sanctions status, the advocacy interventions that have been attempted, and recommended actions for governments and financial institutions. Sanctions status is current to April 2026. The profiles are organised across nine supply chain groups.

  • Group 1: Myanmar Receiving and Distribution Network: The Myanmar-based entities that receive, store, and distribute aviation fuel inside the country.
  • Group 2: Singapore Holding Companies: The Singapore-registered holding companies through which the Thilawa terminal is owned.
  • Group 3: Thai Trading Companies: The Thailand-registered trading companies through which payments for aviation fuel were processed.
  • Group 4: Vietnam Transit Hub: The Vietnamese terminal operator through which fuel was staged and transited to obscure its destination.
  • Group 5: Fuel Traders and Upstream Suppliers: The refiners, national oil companies, and commodity traders that have supplied Jet A-1 at the origin end of the supply chain.
  • Group 6: Vessels, Vessel Owners and Maritime Service Providers: The tanker vessels and their owners, managers, and port agents that transported fuel to Myanmar.
  • Group 7: Transit and Loading Terminals: The terminals outside Myanmar at which fuel was loaded, staged, or transferred before delivery to Thilawa.
  • Group 8: Financial Facilitators: The banks in Myanmar and Thailand through which aviation fuel payments were processed.
  • Group 9: Domestic Crude-to-Aviation-Fuel Chain: The entities involved in supplying crude oil and producing domestically refined aviation fuel for the junta.

In addition to the 49 profiles, the database includes a supply chain overview, which documents the Myanmar jet fuel supply chain since the coup, and three reference documents. The master shipment table documents all 30 confirmed Jet A-1 deliveries to Myanmar since the coup, with vessel, load port, volume, and source data for each. The sanctions status table provides the sanctions status of all entities profiled in the database. The airstrike tracker directory lists the active tools and databases available for tracking Myanmar military airstrikes. All are accessible from the navigation bar above.
