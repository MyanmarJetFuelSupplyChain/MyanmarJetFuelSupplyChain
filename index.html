Myanmar Jet Fuel Supply Chain

Since the military coup of 1 February 2021, Myanmar's Air Force has conducted thousands of airstrikes against civilians across the country, targeting hospitals, schools, markets, and villages. Every one of those airstrikes has required Jet A-1 aviation fuel. This database documents the international companies, vessels, banks, and individuals who have supplied, transported, financed, or otherwise enabled the flow of that fuel to the Myanmar military.

The supply chain has passed through three distinct phases. In the first, major international refiners in Singapore, Thailand, and India sold fuel directly to Myanmar through the Thilawa terminal in Yangon, operated at the time by Puma Energy. When that model was exposed in 2022, the supply chain shifted to routing fuel through Vietnam, using a storage terminal near Ho Chi Minh City to obscure the fuel's final destination. From mid-2024 onwards, Iranian-linked vessels began delivering fuel directly to Myanmar using the tactics of the global ghost fleet: disabling tracking systems, falsifying positions, and loading from Iranian terminals. Imports reached a post-coup high in 2025.

The 49 profiles in this database span the full length of the supply chain, from upstream refiners and traders to the terminal operators, shipping companies, and banks that make each delivery possible, and the Myanmar-based entities that receive and distribute the fuel. Each profile sets out what is publicly known about the entity's role, what evidence exists, what advocacy has been attempted, and what governments and financial institutions should do next. The database is intended as a working tool for sanctions authorities, investigative journalists, civil society organisations, and legal teams.

The sanctions status of every entity has been verified against the official lists of the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, Canada, and Australia. The database will be updated as new information becomes available.

About the database

How to use this database

Each of the 49 profiles follows a consistent structure: an identification section giving registered name, jurisdiction, and role; a role section explaining how the entity fits into the supply chain; an evidence section setting out the primary sources; a key relationships section cross-referencing connected profiles; a designation status table showing sanctions across five jurisdictions; an advocacy section recording what interventions have been attempted and when; recommended actions directed at governments, financial institutions, and civil society; a chronological timeline; and a full source list.

Use the search bar to find any entity by name, country, or profile number. Use the filter buttons in the sidebar to show only sanctioned or unsanctioned entities. Click any profile name to open its page.

Evidence standards

  • Sanctions data is drawn from official government lists only. An entity shown as unsanctioned has been checked against all five jurisdictions as of April 2026.
  • Shipment data comes from Amnesty International, the UN Special Rapporteur, Reuters, and Global Witness, corroborated by AIS vessel tracking, satellite imagery, and customs records where available.
  • Financial transaction figures come from the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report, based on confidential trade records and rounded to protect sources.
