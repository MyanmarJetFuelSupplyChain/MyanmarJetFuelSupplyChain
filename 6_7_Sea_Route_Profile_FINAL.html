Sea Route Ship Management FZE
Profile 6.7
Summary Assessment
Sea Route Ship Management FZE is a UAE-registered ship management company domiciled in the Hamriyah Free Zone, Sharjah, that serves as the ISM manager and commercial manager of both REEF (IMO 9263382, Profile 6.5) and NOBLE (IMO 9162928, Profile 6.6), the two vessels responsible for all eight documented Iranian aviation fuel deliveries to Myanmar's military junta between October 2024 and December 2025. As ISM manager, Sea Route holds responsibility for the safety management systems of both vessels; as commercial manager, it bears responsibility for the commercial deployment decisions that directed both vessels toward Myanmar. Reporters were unable to reach the owners of REEF and NOBLE; an email listed as a contact was not valid. For Sea Route's designation status, see Section 2.

Under the International Safety Management Code, the ISM manager of a vessel is the company that holds the Document of Compliance, responsible for implementing and maintaining the Safety Management System that governs the vessel's operations. The commercial manager is responsible for voyage planning, cargo decisions, and the commercial deployment of the vessel. Sea Route's concurrent designation as ISM manager and commercial manager of both REEF and NOBLE places it at the operational centre of the ghost fleet supply chain: it is the single company whose decisions governed when, where and how both sanctioned vessels were deployed for each of the eight documented Myanmar deliveries. (JFM Feb 2026)

The June 2024 OFAC action that designated Sea Route also designated two further vessels linked to the company: NILE (also known as LIA), IMO 9411288, and YAMUNA (also known as ARGO), IMO 9572111. These two vessels are not part of the documented Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain, but their designation in the same action confirms that Sea Route's involvement in sanctioned Iranian petroleum trades extended beyond REEF and NOBLE. (JFM Feb 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)

Sea Route Ship Management FZE was designated by the United States (OFAC) on 27 June 2024 under the Iran Sanctions program (Executive Order 13846). The designation was made on Iran sanctions grounds and was simultaneous with the designations of REEF (then BALTIC HORIZON) and NOBLE (then ASTRA). Sea Route is not designated by the UK, EU, Canada or Australia. Justice For Myanmar's February 2026 press release called for designation across all four remaining jurisdictions, noting that the US designation is already in place. No further designations have been confirmed as of April 2026. (JFM Feb 2026)

5
Recommended Actions
•Designate Sea Route Ship Management FZE across the UK, EU, Canada and Australia: Sea Route is the ISM and commercial manager of both REEF and NOBLE, the only two designated vessels in the Myanmar aviation fuel ghost fleet. JFM's February 2026 call for coordinated multilateral designation remains unimplemented across all four jurisdictions. Designation of Sea Route across the UK, EU, Canada and Australia would directly target the operational management layer of both ghost fleet vessels, complementing vessel-level designations and potentially disrupting the company's ability to source insurance, financing, port services and crew from non-US providers. The existing US designation of Sea Route has demonstrably failed to prevent continued Myanmar deliveries by both managed vessels; multilateral designation is necessary to close the jurisdictional gaps that have allowed evasion to continue. (JFM Feb 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
•Engage UAE authorities regarding Sea Route Ship Management FZE and its co-located Hamriyah Free Zone entities: Sea Route shares its Hamriyah Free Zone address with Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA (registered owner of REEF) and Sea Hill Maritime SA (registered owner of NOBLE). UAE engagement through the Hamriyah Free Zone Authority and the UAE Executive Office of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Terrorism Financing could target the entire corporate infrastructure of the ghost fleet in a single jurisdictional action. The UAE's 2025 AML Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025), in force since 14 October 2025, lowered the evidentiary threshold for establishing AML liability and expanded offences relating to sanctions evasion, providing domestic legal grounds for action. The UAE's removal from the FATF grey list in February 2024 and from the EU's high-risk countries list in July 2025 gives the UAE a strong institutional incentive to act against sanctions-evading entities within its jurisdiction. (JFM Feb 2026)
•Investigate the beneficial ownership structure linking Sea Route, Amethyst Maritime Ventures SA and Sea Hill Maritime SA: All three entities share a Hamriyah Free Zone address. Sea Route manages both vessels; Amethyst and Sea Hill own them. The concentration of management and ownership of two simultaneously designated ghost fleet vessels in co-located UAE free zone entities strongly suggests a common controlling party behind the structure. Designation submissions to UK, EU, Canadian and Australian authorities should include an explicit assessment of this structural connection, and relevant financial intelligence authorities should be engaged to pursue beneficial ownership disclosure under the UAE's Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023 on beneficial ownership registers. (JFM Feb 2026; Reuters Jan 2026)
