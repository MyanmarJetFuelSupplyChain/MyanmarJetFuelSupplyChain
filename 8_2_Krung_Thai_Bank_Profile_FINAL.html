Krung Thai Bank Public Company Limited
Profile 8.2
Summary Assessment
Krung Thai Bank (KTB) is a Thai state-owned commercial bank majority-owned by the Thai Ministry of Finance. CB Energy, the primary Thai seller in Myanmar's 2023 aviation fuel supply chain, held accounts at KTB into which it received over USD 55 million routed from Myanmar through United Amara Bank. The UN Special Rapporteur found that effective due diligence by KTB, including a basic review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. Separately, KTB held nostro accounts for two sanctioned Myanmar state banks: MFTB and MEB. As of May 2025, neither nostro relationship had been publicly confirmed as terminated. KTB has not been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

KTB was the Thai receiving bank for CB Energy's accounts. All payments from NEPAS (over USD 40 million), Swan Energy, and Myan-Oil ran from Myanmar through United Amara Bank to CB Energy at KTB. The UNSR noted that Swan Energy's own website claimed 100 percent of Myanmar's aviation fuel market, publicly available information that KTB could have reviewed as part of standard counterparty due diligence on CB Energy's customers. The same basic Myanmar corporate records review the UNSR identified as sufficient for the Thai banks would have revealed that NEPAS, Swan Energy, and Myan-Oil were connected to Myanmar's military-linked aviation fuel trade. (UNSR Jun 2024)

2
Nostro Account Relationships with Sanctioned Myanmar State Banks

KTB held nostro accounts for both MFTB and MEB. MFTB was designated by Canada (2012), OFAC (June 2023), and Australia (January 2024). MEB was designated by Canada (October 2024). As of JFM's May 2025 report, KTB had not publicly confirmed termination of either relationship. This contrasts with Kasikorn Bank, which confirmed to the UNSR that it terminated its nostro accounts with junta-controlled banks following June 2023 US sanctions. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

4
Key Relationships
•United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1): The Myanmar-side bank through which aviation fuel payments were routed to CB Energy's accounts at KTB. UAB processed the outgoing international transfers on behalf of NEPAS and Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers; KTB received the corresponding incoming transfers on behalf of CB Energy. The UAB-to-KTB channel represents the Myanmar-to-Thailand financial leg of the aviation fuel payment structure documented in calendar year 2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS) (Profile 1.2): The primary Myanmar aviation fuel importer and the source of more than USD 40 million in payments to CB Energy through UAB and KTB in 2023. NEPAS's payments represent the largest single payment flow in the documented aviation fuel procurement structure for that year. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Swan Energy Company Limited (Profile 1.5) and Myan-Oil Company Limited (Profile 1.6): Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers whose payments to CB Energy were received at KTB through UAB in 2023. Their payments form part of the combined total of more than USD 15 million made by all three Asia Sun Group proxies; no per-buyer or per-seller breakdown is given in the source. Rich Ray Trading, the third Asia Sun Group proxy, paid Tawan-Oil Trading rather than CB Energy, with those payments received at Kasikorn Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Kasikorn Bank (Profile 8.5): The Thailand-based bank that received Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in 2023, serving the parallel role to KTB in the aviation fuel payment structure. The UN Special Rapporteur named both KTB and Kasikorn Bank when assessing that effective due diligence could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. (UNSR Jun 2024)
5
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•June 2024. The UNSR's Banking on the Death Trade report names KTB as the receiving bank for CB Energy's aviation fuel payments, documents its MFTB and MEB nostro accounts, and finds due diligence failures. Nine financial institutions responded to the UNSR's contact. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•July 2024. Thai banks including KTB appear before Thailand's parliamentary national security committee. KTB Senior Executive Vice President Pongsit Chaichutpornsuk, speaking for the Thai Bankers' Association, states banks lack the capacity to investigate indirect transactions and that all banks would comply if regulators made clear which entities they cannot do business with. KTB does not respond to Reuters. AMLO and the Bank of Thailand announce a task force to investigate Myanmar military procurement transactions. (Reuters Jul 2024)
•27 December 2024. The BOT and AMLO issue a joint directive requiring stricter UBO verification, enhanced due diligence for high-risk country transactions including Myanmar, and new dual-use item monitoring. The joint review acknowledged "varying levels of rigour" across institutions. No KTB-specific compliance disclosure has been published. (BOT/AMLO Dec 2024)
•May 2025. Justice for Myanmar confirms KTB still holds nostro accounts for MFTB and MEB as of publication. No public confirmation of termination has been documented. (JFM May 2025)
6
Recommended Actions
•Engage the Thai Ministry of Finance as KTB's controlling shareholder. The Ministry holds approximately 55 percent of KTB through the Financial Institutions Development Fund, making it the primary accountability lever. Governments with bilateral relations with Thailand should engage the Ministry directly to request confirmation that KTB has terminated its MFTB and MEB nostro accounts, implemented the December 2024 AML directive in full, and suspended processing of payments linked to Myanmar aviation fuel procurement. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Request that KTB publicly confirm termination of its MFTB and MEB nostro accounts. As of May 2025, KTB had not done so, in contrast to Kasikorn Bank which confirmed termination to the UNSR. Civil society should formally write to KTB's board requesting a date-specific public confirmation. Non-response should be documented and referenced in engagements with the Ministry of Finance and the BOT. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Press the BOT to publish KTB-specific compliance assessments under the December 2024 directive. The directive acknowledged varying levels of rigour across Thai institutions. The BOT as banking supervisor has jurisdiction to require and publish KTB-specific compliance data, including whether CB Energy accounts have been subject to enhanced screening and whether MFTB and MEB nostro relationships have been closed. (BOT/AMLO Dec 2024)
