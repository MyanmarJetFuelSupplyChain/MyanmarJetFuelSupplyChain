Krung Thai Bank Public Company Limited
Profile 8.2
Summary Assessment
Krung Thai Bank Public Company Limited (KTB) is a Thai state-owned commercial bank whose majority shareholder is the Thai Ministry of Finance. It is identified by Justice For Myanmar's May 2025 report as a bank that the UN Special Rapporteur found had been "involved in transactions for the shipment of aviation fuel to the Myanmar junta" in calendar year 2023. Specifically, CB Energy Co. Ltd., a Thai fuel seller, held accounts at KTB into which it received aviation fuel payments routed from Myanmar through United Amara Bank (UAB). These payments included more than USD 40 million from NEPAS directly and a portion of the combined total of more than USD 15 million made by the three Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers (Swan Energy, Myan-Oil and Rich Ray Trading), with Swan Energy and Myan-Oil paying CB Energy via KTB and Rich Ray Trading paying a separate Thai seller, Tawan-Oil Trading, via Kasikorn Bank. The UN Special Rapporteur assessed that effective due diligence by KTB, including a review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. Separately from the aviation fuel payment channel, KTB also held nostro accounts for two sanctioned Myanmar state banks: Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Economic Bank (MEB). KTB has not been sanctioned by any government as of April 2026.

1
Role in the Myanmar Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

KTB appears in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain as the Thai-side receiving bank for CB Energy Co. Ltd., a Thailand-registered aviation fuel seller. In calendar year 2023, CB Energy received Myanmar aviation fuel payments into its KTB accounts. These payments originated from Myanmar-based purchasers and were routed through United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1) in Myanmar before reaching CB Energy's accounts at KTB. As the receiving bank, KTB processed the incoming international transfers but did not directly transact with the Myanmar-side purchasers. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The UN Special Rapporteur assessed that "effective due diligence by Kasikorn and Krung Thai, including a review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchaser's connections to the aviation fuel trade." The report specifically noted that Swan Energy's website openly states that it sells 10,000 metric tonnes of aviation fuel to Myanmar every month, which it claims constitutes 100 per cent of Myanmar's market share, information that was publicly available and that KTB could have reviewed as part of standard customer due diligence on CB Energy's counterparties. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The following Myanmar-side purchasers made payments to CB Energy that were received at KTB, routed through UAB, in calendar year 2023. For cross-reference with all documented jet fuel shipments, see the master shipment table. (UNSR Jun 2024)

•NEPAS (direct) (Profile 1.2): National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS) made direct payments of more than USD 40 million to CB Energy through UAB, received at KTB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Swan Energy and Myan-Oil (Profiles 1.5 and 1.6): Two Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers whose payments to CB Energy, routed through UAB, were received at KTB. Their payments form part of the combined total of more than USD 15 million made by all three Asia Sun Group proxies in 2023; no per-buyer or per-seller breakdown of that combined figure is given in the source. Rich Ray Trading, the third Asia Sun Group proxy, paid Tawan-Oil Trading rather than CB Energy, with those payments received at Kasikorn Bank (Profile 8.5). (UNSR Jun 2024)
2
Nostro Account Relationships with Sanctioned Myanmar State Banks

Separately from its role in the aviation fuel payment chain, KTB held nostro accounts for two Myanmar state-owned banks as documented by the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 report and confirmed by Justice For Myanmar in May 2025. In the context of the UNSR's reporting, a nostro account refers to an account that a Myanmar bank holds at a foreign correspondent bank such as KTB, used to facilitate international currency settlements. KTB's provision of correspondent banking services to these institutions represents a distinct connection to the Myanmar junta's financial infrastructure from the aviation fuel payment channel.

•Myanma Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB): KTB held a nostro account for MFTB. MFTB is a Myanmar state-owned bank designated by Canada (SEMA, 24 April 2012), the United States (OFAC, 21 June 2023) and Australia (DFAT, 31 January 2024). It has not been designated by the UK or EU. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Myanma Economic Bank (MEB): KTB held a nostro account for MEB. MEB is a Myanmar state-owned bank. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

Key Relationships
•United Amara Bank (UAB) (Profile 8.1): The Myanmar-side bank through which aviation fuel payments were routed to CB Energy's accounts at KTB. UAB processed the outgoing international transfers on behalf of NEPAS and Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers; KTB received the corresponding incoming transfers on behalf of CB Energy. The UAB-to-KTB channel represents the Myanmar-to-Thailand financial leg of the aviation fuel payment structure documented in calendar year 2023. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS) (Profile 1.2): The primary Myanmar aviation fuel importer and the source of more than USD 40 million in payments to CB Energy through UAB and KTB in 2023. NEPAS's payments represent the largest single payment flow in the documented aviation fuel procurement structure for that year. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Swan Energy Company Limited (Profile 1.5) and Myan-Oil Company Limited (Profile 1.6): Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers whose payments to CB Energy were received at KTB through UAB in 2023. Their payments form part of the combined total of more than USD 15 million made by all three Asia Sun Group proxies; no per-buyer or per-seller breakdown is given in the source. Rich Ray Trading, the third Asia Sun Group proxy, paid Tawan-Oil Trading rather than CB Energy, with those payments received at Kasikorn Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Kasikorn Bank (Profile 8.5): The Thailand-based bank that received Tawan-Oil Trading's aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in 2023, serving the parallel role to KTB in the aviation fuel payment structure. The UN Special Rapporteur named both KTB and Kasikorn Bank when assessing that effective due diligence could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. (UNSR Jun 2024)
5
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report identifies KTB as the Thai bank that received CB Energy's aviation fuel payments from Myanmar in calendar year 2023 and documents KTB's nostro account relationships with MFTB and MEB. The report assesses that effective due diligence by KTB, including a review of Myanmar corporate records, could have revealed the Myanmar purchasers' connections to the aviation fuel trade. Of the financial institutions the UN Special Rapporteur contacted regarding their nostro account relationships with MFTB, MEB and MICB, nine responded. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•July 2024. Representatives of Thai banks named in the UN Special Rapporteur's report, including KTB, told Thailand's parliamentary national security committee that they lacked the capacity to investigate indirect transactions potentially linked to Myanmar military procurement. Pongsit Chaichutpornsuk, Senior Executive Vice President at KTB, speaking on behalf of the Thai Bankers' Association, stated that banks "do not have this kind of investigators" and that all commercial banks would comply if the government and regulators made clear which entities they cannot do business with. In the same month, Thailand's Anti-Money Laundering Office (AMLO) and Bank of Thailand (BOT) announced the establishment of a task force to investigate transactions linked to Myanmar military procurement. The UN Special Rapporteur welcomed the announcement as "a real step in the right direction." KTB did not respond to requests for comment from Reuters. (Reuters, July 2024)
•December 2024. The BOT and AMLO published the results of their joint investigation and issued a new regulatory directive effective 27 December 2024. The joint review found that Thai financial institutions had adhered to AML measures and that transactions with individuals named in the OHCHR report could not be linked to arms procurement, while acknowledging "varying levels of rigour in operations" across institutions. The directive mandated: stricter ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) verification with the threshold lowered to below 25% shareholding; enhanced due diligence for transactions involving high-risk countries including Myanmar; and new industry standards for dual-use item monitoring to be implemented from January 2025. KTB's response to the directive or any specific actions taken by KTB in connection with its Myanmar-related relationships have not been publicly disclosed as of April 2026. (BOT/AMLO Joint Statement, December 2024)
•May 2025. Justice For Myanmar's Fuelling Atrocities report confirms that KTB continued to hold nostro accounts for MFTB and MEB as of the time of publication. The report states that the UN Special Rapporteur found that KTB "was involved in transactions for the shipment of aviation fuel to the Myanmar junta, which is essential to sustain the junta's widespread campaign of airstrikes, which indiscriminately target civilians." No public statement from KTB confirming termination of its MFTB or MEB nostro account relationships has been documented in any reviewed source. No government action directed specifically at KTB has been confirmed as of April 2026. (JFM May 2025)
6
Recommended Actions
•Engage the Thai Ministry of Finance as KTB's controlling shareholder: The Thai Ministry of Finance holds approximately 55 per cent of KTB's shares through the Financial Institutions Development Fund, making it the primary lever for influencing KTB's conduct beyond regulatory directives. Despite the BOT/AMLO December 2024 directive, no public confirmation that KTB has terminated its nostro account relationships with MFTB or MEB has been documented. Governments with bilateral relations with Thailand should engage the Ministry of Finance directly, requesting confirmation that KTB has: terminated all correspondent banking relationships with MFTB and MEB; implemented the December 2024 AML directive in full; and suspended any further processing of payments linked to Myanmar aviation fuel procurement. The Ministry's dual role as regulator-via-proxy and controlling shareholder makes it the most direct accountability pathway outside formal sanctions. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Monitor and press for public disclosure of the BOT/AMLO directive's implementation by KTB: The BOT and AMLO issued a joint directive effective 27 December 2024 mandating enhanced due diligence, stricter UBO verification and new dual-use item monitoring standards. However, the directive's joint review explicitly found that Thai banks conducted transactions with individuals named in the OHCHR report, and acknowledged varying levels of rigour across institutions. Civil society should formally request that the BOT publish KTB-specific compliance assessments, including whether KTB's MFTB and MEB nostro accounts have been closed, and what enhanced screening has been applied to CB Energy or other customers linked to Myanmar aviation fuel procurement. The BOT's institutional role as banking supervisor gives it direct jurisdiction to require and publish this information. (BOT/AMLO Joint Statement, December 2024)
•Request that KTB publicly confirm the termination of its nostro account relationships with MFTB and MEB: As of Justice For Myanmar's May 2025 report, KTB had not publicly confirmed termination of its MFTB or MEB nostro account relationships, in contrast to Kasikorn Bank, which confirmed to the UN Special Rapporteur that it had terminated its relationships with junta-controlled banks following sanctions. Civil society should formally write to KTB's board requesting public confirmation of whether these relationships have been terminated and, if so, on what date. Any non-response should be documented and referenced in future advocacy materials directed at the Thai Ministry of Finance and the BOT. The contrast with Kasikorn Bank's confirmed exit provides a clear public benchmark against which KTB's conduct can be assessed. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
