CB Energy
Profile 3.1
Summary Assessment
CB Energy is a Thailand-registered trading company with no publicly available ownership, registration, or address. In calendar year 2023 it was the largest single seller of aviation fuel to Myanmar's military procurement network, receiving over USD 55 million in payments from NEPAS and Asia Sun Group proxy companies. All payments cleared through United Amara Bank in Myanmar to CB Energy's accounts at Krung Thai Bank. It has not been sanctioned by any government.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

CB Energy sold aviation fuel to two categories of Myanmar purchasers in 2023. NEPAS, the formal licensed importer jointly controlled by the junta's Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise, purchased over USD 40 million directly from CB Energy, with payments processed through United Amara Bank to Krung Thai Bank. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, proxy purchasers within the Asia Sun Group network, together accounted for a further share of a combined approximately USD 15 million paid to Thai sellers, also through United Amara Bank to Krung Thai Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Swan Energy's own website stated it supplied 10,000 metric tonnes of aviation fuel to Myanmar per month, representing 100 percent of Myanmar's aviation fuel market. The UN Special Rapporteur found that a basic review of Myanmar corporate records by Krung Thai Bank would have been sufficient to identify the purchasers' connection to the aviation fuel trade. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Thailand replaced Singapore as the dominant procurement jurisdiction between fiscal year 2022 and fiscal year 2023. Thai-registered companies went from facilitating over USD 60 million in SAC military procurement to over USD 120 million in that single year. CB Energy and Tawan-Oil Trading are the only two companies through which all documented 2023 aviation fuel payments flowed. (UNSR Jun 2024)

No corporate records, shipping documents, or other documentation relating to CB Energy have been identified in any other source. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Key Relationships
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS): CB Energy's largest documented customer in 2023. NEPAS purchased more than USD 40 million of aviation fuel directly from CB Energy, with payment processed through United Amara Bank to CB Energy's accounts at Krung Thai Bank. NEPAS is the joint venture between the junta-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (Singapore) that holds Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence. NEPAS is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 1.2. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Swan Energy Company Limited and Myan-Oil Company Limited: Two Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers confirmed as buyers from CB Energy in 2023. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, together with Rich Ray Trading Company Limited, are recorded in Figure 7 of the UNSR report as having collectively purchased approximately USD 15 million from Thai sellers, with Swan Energy and Myan-Oil purchasing from CB Energy (payments via UAB to Krung Thai Bank) and Rich Ray Trading purchasing from Tawan-Oil Trading (payments via UAB to Kasikorn Bank). No per-entity breakdown of the combined USD 15 million is given in the report. Swan Energy is sanctioned by the UK and Canada (October 2024). Myan-Oil is sanctioned by the UK (October 2024). See Profile 1.5 and Joint Profile 1.6. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•United Amara Bank (UAB): The Myanmar private bank through which all Myanmar-side payments to CB Energy were processed in 2023. UAB handled payments from both NEPAS and the Asia Sun Group proxies. The UN Special Rapporteur explicitly calls for UAB's international banking relationships to be frozen. UAB is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 8.1. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank: The Thai bank at which CB Energy held the accounts into which all Myanmar payments were received. Krung Thai Bank is a state-owned Thai bank. The UNSR identified it as one of 16 banks facilitating SAC military procurement in FY2023 and confirmed it held nostro accounts for MFTB and MEB. Justice for Myanmar described Krung Thai Bank as one of the banks specifically involved in aviation fuel transaction financing. Krung Thai Bank is not sanctioned in any jurisdiction as of April 2026. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Tawan-Oil Trading: The other Thailand-registered aviation fuel trader documented in the UNSR's 2023 transaction table, operating in parallel with CB Energy. Tawan-Oil Trading supplied NEPAS's proxy Intraco and Asia Sun Group proxy Rich Ray Trading, receiving payments at Kasikorn Bank. Tawan-Oil Trading is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions.. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Asia Sun Group Company Limited / Shoon Group: The parent conglomerate behind Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, which were CB Energy's documented customers on the Asia Sun Group side of the 2023 transaction structure. Asia Sun Group is sanctioned by the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia. See Profile 1.4. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The CB Energy sanctions gap: CB Energy is the largest documented aviation fuel seller to the Myanmar military's supply chain in calendar year 2023, accounting for more than USD 40 million in direct sales to NEPAS alone. It is a Thailand-registered trading company with no apparent physical infrastructure in Myanmar; its only documented Myanmar connection is through United Amara Bank, which processed payments from Myanmar to CB Energy's accounts at Krung Thai Bank. Despite this, it has not been sanctioned by any government. The UNSR explicitly calls for governments to holistically sanction trading companies providing aviation fuel to the Myanmar military. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the legal authority in the United States to designate any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy. CB Energy's documented role in Figure 7 of the UNSR report, as a seller of more than USD 40 million directly to NEPAS, provides the evidentiary basis for designations across all five jurisdictions. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023; JFM Feb 2026)
5
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•23 August 2023. OFAC issues a determination under Executive Order 14014 that Myanmar's jet fuel sector falls within the scope of Burma-related sanctions, allowing designation of any person operating in that sector without proof of direct military end-use. No designation of CB Energy follows. (OFAC Aug 2023)
•27 October 2023. Canada enacts SOR/2023-228, a blanket ban on the supply and shipment of aviation fuel to Myanmar and on maritime shipping insurance for vessels transporting it. No designation of CB Energy follows. (Canada Gazette Oct 2023)
•26 June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report names CB Energy as the primary Thai seller in Myanmar's 2023 aviation fuel supply chain and calls on governments to sanction trading companies involved. Findings were communicated to Thailand's government before publication. No designation of CB Energy follows. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•17 July 2024. Fair Finance Thailand publishes an open letter to five Thai banks named in the UNSR report, including Krung Thai Bank, calling for enhanced due diligence on Myanmar-related transactions and closure of accounts held by Myanmar military-controlled banks. (Fair Finance Thailand Jul 2024)
•July 2024. Thai banks appear before a parliamentary committee. The Bank of Thailand and the Thai Bankers' Association agree to establish a task force on Myanmar military procurement transactions. Whether Krung Thai Bank's account relationship with CB Energy was specifically reviewed has not been confirmed. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•29 October 2024. The UK designates Swan Energy (MYA0085) and Myan-Oil (MYA0081) for making aviation fuel available to Myanmar's security forces. Canada simultaneously designates Swan Energy. Both are confirmed CB Energy buyers in 2023. CB Energy is not designated. (UK Oct 2024)
•27 December 2024. The Bank of Thailand and Thailand's Anti-Money Laundering Office issue a joint directive requiring enhanced due diligence on high-risk country transactions with mandatory verification of ultimate beneficial ownership. No confirmed action against CB Energy follows. (JFM May 2025)
6
Recommended Actions
•Designate CB Energy in all five jurisdictions. The UNSR's Figure 7 transaction table provides the evidentiary basis. For the US, the August 2023 OFAC sector determination removes the need to prove direct military end-use. Coordinated designation across jurisdictions reduces the risk of CB Energy simply shifting its banking pathway. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage Krung Thai Bank through the Bank of Thailand. Krung Thai Bank is state-owned, making it reachable through government channels as well as commercial ones. The UNSR found that a review of Myanmar corporate records by the bank would have revealed the purchasers' connection to the aviation fuel trade. The Bank of Thailand's December 2024 directive on high-risk country transactions provides a direct regulatory hook. Civil society engagement should formally reference the October 2024 UK and Canada designations of Swan Energy and Myan-Oil as confirmed CB Energy buyers and request confirmation that the accounts in question have been closed or reviewed. (UNSR Jun 2024; Fair Finance Thailand Jul 2024)
•Follow up on the July 2024 Thai parliamentary task force. The task force was established specifically to investigate Myanmar military procurement transactions through Thai banks. A formal request for its findings, directed to the Bank of Thailand, should ask specifically whether Krung Thai Bank's accounts held for CB Energy were reviewed and what action, if any, followed. (UNSR Jun 2024)

26 June 2024UN Special Rapporteur Tom Andrews publishes Banking on the Death Trade (A/HRC/56/CRP.7), naming CB Energy in Figure 7 as the Thai seller to NEPAS (>USD 40M) and to Swan Energy and Myan-Oil as part of the combined approximately USD 15 million Asia Sun Group proxy total. The UNSR calls for holistic sanctioning of aviation fuel supply chain networks including trading companies. CB Energy is confirmed unsanctioned in all jurisdictions at time of publication. Findings were communicated to Thailand's government prior to publication. No designation follows. (UNSR Jun 2024)
17 July 2024Fair Finance Thailand publishes an open letter to five Thai banks named in the Banking on the Death Trade report, including Krung Thai Bank, calling for enhanced due diligence for Myanmar-related transactions, transparent procedures for rejecting high-risk transactions, and closure of accounts held by Myanmar military-controlled banks. (Fair Finance Thailand Jul 2024)
July 2024Thai banks are called before a parliamentary committee following publication of the Banking on the Death Trade report. The Thai government announces the formation of a task force to investigate Myanmar military procurement transactions through Thai banks. The Bank of Thailand and the Thai Bankers' Association agree to establish the task force. Whether any regulatory action followed or whether Krung Thai Bank's accounts related to CB Energy were specifically reviewed has not been confirmed in open sources as of April 2026. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Canada simultaneously designates Swan Energy Company Limited. Both are confirmed buyers of CB Energy in calendar year 2023. CB Energy itself is not designated. (UK Oct 2024; JFM Oct 2024)
May 2025Justice for Myanmar's ASEAN companies report confirms Krung Thai Bank's role in aviation fuel transaction financing, providing an additional public record consistent with the UNSR's findings on CB Energy's banking pathway. No designation of CB Energy follows. (JFM May 2025)
February 2026Justice for Myanmar calls for new coordinated sanctions on the aviation fuel supply chain. CB Energy remains unsanctioned in all five jurisdictions as of April 2026. (JFM Feb 2026)
