CB Energy
Profile 3.1
Summary Assessment
CB Energy is a Thailand-registered company identified by the UN Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in Myanmar as the single largest seller of aviation fuel to the Myanmar military's supply chain in calendar year 2023. Based on confidential trade records reviewed by the Special Rapporteur, CB Energy sold more than USD 40 million of aviation fuel directly to National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS), the formal aviation fuel importer in Myanmar, and a further portion of a combined approximately USD 15 million to Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, both Asia Sun Group proxies. All payments from the Myanmar side were processed through United Amara Bank and received at CB Energy's accounts at Krung Thai Bank in Thailand. No corporate registration number, ownership details, individuals, or address for CB Energy appear in any of the sources reviewed for this database. CB Energy has not been sanctioned by any government as of the date of the most recent source covering this entity. The UNSR explicitly calls for governments to holistically sanction trading companies providing aviation fuel to the Myanmar military, a category into which CB Energy squarely falls.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

CB Energy is a Thailand-registered trading company that sold aviation fuel directly to Myanmar military-connected purchasers in calendar year 2023. It operated on the supply side of the transaction structure documented by the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 Banking on the Death Trade report, selling fuel to entities on both sides of the Myanmar military's aviation fuel procurement network: to NEPAS, the formal licensed importer, and to Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, proxy purchasers operating under the Asia Sun Group. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Sales to NEPAS

CB Energy's largest documented customer in 2023 was NEPAS (National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd.), the joint venture between the junta-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (Singapore) that holds Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence. The UN Special Rapporteur's Figure 7 transaction table, based on confidential trade records, documents that NEPAS purchased more than USD 40 million of aviation fuel directly from CB Energy in calendar year 2023. Payments were processed from Myanmar through United Amara Bank to CB Energy's accounts at Krung Thai Bank. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Sales to Asia Sun Group proxies

CB Energy also sold aviation fuel to Swan Energy Company Limited and Myan-Oil Company Limited, two Myanmar-registered companies in the Asia Sun Group / Shoon Group network that operated as proxy purchasers alongside NEPAS's own direct and proxy purchasing structure. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, together with Rich Ray Trading Company Limited, are recorded in the UNSR's Figure 7 table as having purchased a combined total of approximately USD 15 million from Thai sellers in 2023. Within that combined figure, Swan Energy and Myan-Oil purchased from CB Energy, with payments processed through United Amara Bank to CB Energy's accounts at Krung Thai Bank; Rich Ray Trading purchased from Tawan-Oil Trading, with payments processed through United Amara Bank to Tawan-Oil's accounts at Kasikorn Bank. No per-entity breakdown is given for the three Asia Sun Group proxies within the combined USD 15 million total. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Thailand's position in the supply chain

The UNSR's June 2024 report documented a structural shift in Myanmar's military procurement from Singapore-registered companies to Thailand-registered companies between fiscal year 2022 and fiscal year 2023. Thai-registered companies went from supplying more than USD 60 million in military goods in FY2022 to more than USD 120 million in FY2023, a doubling driven in part by the collapse of Singapore-based supply following a Singaporean government investigation. CB Energy is one of the two Thailand-registered aviation fuel trading companies identified by the UNSR as the primary sellers in 2023, alongside Tawan-Oil Trading. The UNSR noted that Thailand has no explicit public policy opposing weapons or fuel transfers to Myanmar, in contrast to Singapore. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The due diligence failure: The UN Special Rapporteur noted that the Thai banks processing these transactions, including Krung Thai Bank, were among the 16 banks that facilitated SAC military procurement in FY2023 and held nostro accounts for Myanmar state banks. The UNSR observed that Swan Energy's own website publicly stated that it controlled 100 percent of Myanmar's aviation fuel market, meaning that any bank reviewing transactions to and from Swan Energy should have identified the likely end-use without difficulty. The same due diligence failure applies to CB Energy as the seller on the Thai side of the same transaction chain. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The sole source of documented evidence on CB Energy is the UN Special Rapporteur's June 2024 Banking on the Death Trade report (A/HRC/56/CRP.7), based on confidential trade records reviewed by the Special Rapporteur. Figure 7 of the report sets out the 2023 aviation fuel transaction structure as follows:

Figure 7: Aviation Fuel Sales to Myanmar, calendar year 2023 (based on UNSR Jun 2024, A/HRC/56/CRP.7, Figure 7).

The total value of aviation fuel purchased from all Thai sellers in 2023 was at least USD 80 million, a nearly 30 percent increase over 2022. CB Energy accounts for the majority of this total, with NEPAS's direct purchases alone exceeding USD 40 million. The exact amount paid to CB Energy by Swan Energy and Myan-Oil is not separately specified; it forms part of the combined Asia Sun Group proxy figure alongside Rich Ray Trading's purchases from Tawan-Oil Trading. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Methodological note

The UNSR's report states that figures are rounded, not exact, to protect confidential sources. The evidence base for CB Energy's role therefore rests entirely on this single primary source. No corporate registry records, corporate filings, company website, shipping manifests, or other documentation relating to CB Energy appear in any source reviewed for this database. The UNSR confirmed in writing to Thailand's government the findings of its report prior to publication. (UNSR Jun 2024)

Krung Thai Bank's role

CB Energy received payments at accounts held at Krung Thai Bank, a state-owned Thai bank. The UNSR identified Krung Thai Bank as one of 16 banks that facilitated SAC military procurement in FY2023 and as a bank that held nostro accounts for both Myanmar Foreign Trade Bank (MFTB) and Myanma Economic Bank (MEB). Justice for Myanmar's May 2025 report on ASEAN companies fuelling Myanmar's international crimes confirmed the UNSR's finding that Krung Thai Bank was involved in transactions for the shipment of aviation fuel to the junta, describing this as essential to sustaining the junta's campaign of airstrikes against civilians. The specific CB Energy accounts at Krung Thai Bank are identified in the UNSR's transaction table as the destination for payments originating from United Amara Bank in Myanmar. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)

3
Key Relationships
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Co. Ltd. (NEPAS): CB Energy's largest documented customer in 2023. NEPAS purchased more than USD 40 million of aviation fuel directly from CB Energy, with payment processed through United Amara Bank to CB Energy's accounts at Krung Thai Bank. NEPAS is the joint venture between the junta-controlled Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise and Shoon Energy Pte. Ltd. (Singapore) that holds Myanmar's aviation fuel import licence. NEPAS is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 1.2. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Swan Energy Company Limited and Myan-Oil Company Limited: Two Asia Sun Group proxy purchasers confirmed as buyers from CB Energy in 2023. Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, together with Rich Ray Trading Company Limited, are recorded in Figure 7 of the UNSR report as having collectively purchased approximately USD 15 million from Thai sellers, with Swan Energy and Myan-Oil purchasing from CB Energy (payments via UAB to Krung Thai Bank) and Rich Ray Trading purchasing from Tawan-Oil Trading (payments via UAB to Kasikorn Bank). No per-entity breakdown of the combined USD 15 million is given in the report. Swan Energy is sanctioned by the UK and Canada (October 2024). Myan-Oil is sanctioned by the UK (October 2024). See Profile 1.5 and Joint Profile 1.6. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•United Amara Bank (UAB): The Myanmar private bank through which all Myanmar-side payments to CB Energy were processed in 2023. UAB handled payments from both NEPAS and the Asia Sun Group proxies. The UN Special Rapporteur explicitly calls for UAB's international banking relationships to be frozen. UAB is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 8.1. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Krung Thai Bank: The Thai bank at which CB Energy held the accounts into which all Myanmar payments were received. Krung Thai Bank is a state-owned Thai bank. The UNSR identified it as one of 16 banks facilitating SAC military procurement in FY2023 and confirmed it held nostro accounts for MFTB and MEB. Justice for Myanmar described Krung Thai Bank as one of the banks specifically involved in aviation fuel transaction financing. Krung Thai Bank is not sanctioned in any jurisdiction as of April 2026. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025)
•Tawan-Oil Trading: The other Thailand-registered aviation fuel trader documented in the UNSR's 2023 transaction table, operating in parallel with CB Energy. Tawan-Oil Trading supplied NEPAS's proxy Intraco and Asia Sun Group proxy Rich Ray Trading, receiving payments at Kasikorn Bank. Tawan-Oil Trading is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 3.2. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Asia Sun Group Company Limited / Shoon Group: The parent conglomerate behind Swan Energy and Myan-Oil, which were CB Energy's documented customers on the Asia Sun Group side of the 2023 transaction structure. Asia Sun Group is sanctioned by the US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia. See Profile 1.4. (UNSR Jun 2024)

The CB Energy sanctions gap: CB Energy is the largest documented aviation fuel seller to the Myanmar military's supply chain in calendar year 2023, accounting for more than USD 40 million in direct sales to NEPAS alone. It is a Thailand-registered trading company with no apparent physical infrastructure in Myanmar; its only documented Myanmar connection is through United Amara Bank, which processed payments from Myanmar to CB Energy's accounts at Krung Thai Bank. Despite this, it has not been sanctioned by any government. The UNSR explicitly calls for governments to holistically sanction trading companies providing aviation fuel to the Myanmar military. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the legal authority in the United States to designate any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy. CB Energy's documented role in Figure 7 of the UNSR report, as a seller of more than USD 40 million directly to NEPAS, provides the evidentiary basis for designations across all five jurisdictions. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023; JFM Feb 2026)
5
Advocacy Interventions to Date
•23 August 2023. OFAC issues a determination under Executive Order 14014 that the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy is subject to sanctions, allowing the designation of any foreign person operating in that sector without requiring proof of direct military end-use. This determination is the primary legal basis for a potential US designation of CB Energy. No designation of CB Energy follows. (OFAC Aug 2023)
•31 October 2023. Canada enacts a ban on the provision of maritime shipping insurance for vessels transporting aviation fuel to Myanmar under Canada Gazette SOR/2023-228. Canada is the first and, as of April 2026, the only jurisdiction to impose such a ban. The ban addresses the insurance dimension of the supply chain through which CB Energy's sales are shipped to Myanmar. (Canada Gazette SOR/2023-228)
•26 June 2024. The UN Special Rapporteur's Banking on the Death Trade report names CB Energy as the Thai seller in the dominant strand of Myanmar's 2023 aviation fuel supply chain, documenting more than USD 40 million in direct sales to NEPAS and further sales to Asia Sun Group proxies Swan Energy and Myan-Oil. The UNSR calls for governments to holistically sanction aviation fuel supply chain networks, including trading companies. Findings were communicated to Thailand's government in advance of publication. No designation of CB Energy follows. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•17 July 2024. Fair Finance Thailand publishes an open letter to five Thai banks named in the Banking on the Death Trade report, including Krung Thai Bank, calling for enhanced due diligence for all Myanmar-related transactions, transparent procedures for rejecting high-risk transactions, and closure of accounts held by Myanmar military-controlled banks. This is the first documented civil society action in Thailand directly targeting the banking pathway through which CB Energy received payments. No confirmed regulatory action follows as of April 2026. (Fair Finance Thailand Jul 2024)
•July 2024. Thai banks are called before a parliamentary committee following publication of the Banking on the Death Trade report. The Thai government announces the formation of a task force to investigate Myanmar military procurement transactions through Thai banks, with the Bank of Thailand and the Thai Bankers' Association agreeing to establish the task force. Whether any regulatory action followed, or whether Krung Thai Bank's account relationship with CB Energy was specifically reviewed, has not been confirmed in open sources as of March 2026. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•29 October 2024. The UK designates Swan Energy Company Limited (MYA0085) and Myan-Oil Company Limited (MYA0081) for making available aviation fuel to Myanmar's security forces. Canada simultaneously designates Swan Energy Company Limited. Both Swan Energy and Myan-Oil are confirmed buyers of CB Energy in calendar year 2023. CB Energy itself is not designated in this round. (UK Oct 2024; JFM Oct 2024)
•May 2025. Justice for Myanmar's ASEAN companies report confirms Krung Thai Bank's named role in aviation fuel transaction financing as documented by the UNSR, providing a further public record of the banking pathway through which CB Energy received payments. No designation of CB Energy follows. (JFM May 2025)
•February 2026. Justice for Myanmar calls for new and coordinated sanctions on the Myanmar junta's aviation fuel supply chain, describing the absence of sanctions on key trading companies as an ongoing gap. CB Energy is not named explicitly in this press release. No designation follows as of April 2026. (JFM Feb 2026)
6
Recommended Actions
•Designate CB Energy in all five jurisdictions: CB Energy is the single largest documented supplier of aviation fuel to Myanmar's military-controlled supply chain in calendar year 2023, with more than USD 40 million in direct sales to NEPAS alone. For submissions to OFAC, the 23 August 2023 jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the legal authority: CB Energy is a company operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy, and no proof of direct military end-use is required. The UNSR's Figure 7 transaction table, based on confidential trade records with findings communicated to Thailand's government prior to publication, constitutes a substantial evidentiary basis for designation submissions to OFAC, UK OFSI, EU EEAS, Canadian Global Affairs and Australian DFAT. Coordinated designation across all five jurisdictions would maximise the disruptive effect and reduce the risk of CB Energy shifting its banking pathway. (UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage Krung Thai Bank directly, through Thai civil society, and through international financial institution networks: Krung Thai Bank is a Thai state-owned bank under the Ministry of Finance, making it subject to engagement not only as a commercial institution but also through government channels. The UNSR specifically found that effective due diligence by Krung Thai Bank, including a review of Myanmar corporate records, could have identified Swan Energy's publicly declared 100 percent market share in Myanmar aviation fuel, establishing the likely end-use of the transactions it processed. Civil society should engage Krung Thai Bank directly, formally referencing the UNSR's findings and the October 2024 UK and Canada designations of Swan Energy and Myan-Oil as confirmed CB Energy buyers, requesting confirmation that the accounts through which CB Energy received aviation fuel payments have been closed or subject to enhanced scrutiny. This engagement should be coordinated with Fair Finance Thailand's existing campaign and referenced to the Bank of Thailand's regulatory obligations under the FATF framework. (UNSR Jun 2024; JFM May 2025; Fair Finance Thailand Jul 2024)
•Follow up with the Thai government on the July 2024 task force: In July 2024, the Thai government announced the formation of a task force involving the Bank of Thailand and the Thai Bankers' Association to investigate Myanmar military procurement transactions through Thai banks. Civil society should formally request an update on the task force's findings, specifically whether Krung Thai Bank's account relationship with CB Energy was reviewed, whether any adverse findings were made, and whether any regulatory action has been taken or is planned. This follow-up should be coordinated with Thai civil society organisations including Fair Finance Thailand. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•Engage the Bank of Thailand and Thai financial regulators under the FATF framework: Myanmar was added to the FATF blacklist (High-Risk Jurisdictions subject to a Call for Action) on 21 October 2022, triggering a requirement for enhanced due diligence on all transactions with Myanmar-based entities under FATF enhanced due diligence requirements for high-risk jurisdictions. The Bank of Thailand, as the regulatory authority over Krung Thai Bank, has the power to require the bank to apply this enhanced due diligence framework to its account relationship with CB Energy and to its processing of payments originating from United Amara Bank, itself a private Myanmar bank called out by name in the UNSR's recommendations for freezing. Civil society should formally request that the Bank of Thailand confirm whether its supervised institutions have complied with the enhanced due diligence requirements triggered by Myanmar's FATF blacklisting, specifically in respect of accounts held by Thai aviation fuel traders and payments originating from UAB. (UNSR Jun 2024)
