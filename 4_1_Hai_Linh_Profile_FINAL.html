Hai Linh Co. Ltd.
Profile 4.1
Summary Assessment
Hai Linh Co. Ltd. is a Vietnam-registered private enterprise founded in 1999, principally controlled by Le Van Tam, that owns and operates the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal near Ho Chi Minh City, and functioned as both terminal operator and trader in a transit supply chain that routed nine documented shipments of aviation fuel to the Myanmar military between April 2023 and February 2024. All seven documented aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar in calendar year 2023 and both confirmed shipments in the first quarter of 2024 transited through the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal before onward delivery to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon. The supply chain mechanism identified by Amnesty International and documented by Justice for Myanmar involved fuel being purchased by traders, resold through multiple intermediaries, received at the Cai Mep terminal for storage of between a few hours and several days, sold on to Myanmar purchasers, and transported by vessel to Myanmar. Vietnamese customs data indicates Hai Linh functioned not only as terminal operator but also as a seller of jet fuel to Myanmar-connected purchasers. Separately from the petroleum terminal, Hai Linh developed the USD 500 million Cai Mep LNG Terminal, in which Singapore-based AG&P LNG (a subsidiary of US-headquartered Nebula Energy) acquired a 49% stake in March 2024; the LNG terminal received its first commissioning cargo in May 2025. Justice for Myanmar became the first organisation to call explicitly for Hai Linh's designation by name in February 2026. No government has sanctioned Hai Linh in any jurisdiction.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Hai Linh Co. Ltd. is a Vietnam-registered company identified by Amnesty International in January 2024 as the owner and operator of the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal, a small petroleum storage terminal near Ho Chi Minh City in southern Vietnam. From April 2023 through February 2024, the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal functioned as the final transit point for nine documented international shipments of aviation fuel before they reached Myanmar. Amnesty International's investigation established that Vietnamese customs data also indicates Hai Linh functioned as a seller of jet fuel to Myanmar-connected purchasers, meaning it operated in a dual capacity as both terminal operator and trader within the supply chain. (AI Jan 2024)

The transit mechanism

Amnesty International documented the supply chain mechanism as follows: the original supplier sold fuel to a trader, which off-sold it once or multiple times; the second-to-last sale before transfer to Myanmar was from a trader to a Vietnamese company; that Vietnamese company received the fuel at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal managed by Hai Linh; after storing the fuel for between a few hours and several days, it was sold on to the Myanmar purchaser and transported by vessel directly to the Thilawa terminal in Yangon. This structure inserted Vietnam as a transit layer that obscured the fuel's onward destination. Amnesty International noted that the role played by Vietnam was "particularly problematic" because the Cai Mep port was essential for this supply chain to function. (AI Jan 2024)

Scale of documented involvement

All seven aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar documented in calendar year 2023 loaded at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh before departing for Yangon. The vessels carrying these shipments were YIDA 8 (one shipment in April 2023) and HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (six shipments between May and December 2023). Both confirmed 2024 shipments, arriving at Thilawa on 14 January and 29 February 2024, also loaded at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal. The combined total of nine shipments via Hai Linh's terminal is confirmed by Justice for Myanmar's May 2025 report, which describes Hai Linh as a confirmed transit point for seven shipments in 2023 and two shipments in 2024. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; JFM May 2025)

The Vietnam transit gap: The insertion of the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal as a transit point served to obscure the fuel's final destination. Shipments with diverse upstream origins, including Pengerang (Malaysia), CNOOC terminal (Huizhou, China), Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal (Singapore), and BP Hua Dong terminal (Ningbo, China), were consolidated through the Cai Mep terminal before proceeding to Myanmar. The consistent use of this single terminal across all nine documented shipments suggests a deliberate structural choice to use Vietnam as a laundering layer. Justice for Myanmar called on the Vietnamese government to ensure its ports are not used for activities linked to human rights violations. (AI Jan 2024; JFM Feb 2026)
2
Evidence
Amnesty International documentary evidence, 2023 shipments

Amnesty International's January 2024 report documented that all seven aviation fuel shipments to Myanmar in 2023 loaded at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operated by Hai Linh Co. Ltd. Shipments occurred in April (YIDA 8, 12.81 kilotonnes), May (two HUITONG 78 shipments, 10.00 kt and 9.99 kt), June (HUITONG 78, 5.01 kt), July (HUITONG 78, 10.09 kt), August (HUITONG 78, 9.91 kt), and December 2023 (HUITONG 78, 9.81 kt). Satellite imagery confirmed a vessel of the same general size, shape and colour as HUITONG 78 at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal and at the former Puma Energy terminal in Thilawa. Amnesty International contacted Hai Linh for comment in connection with this investigation. (AI Jan 2024)

Amnesty International documentary evidence, 2024 shipments

Amnesty International's July 2024 report confirmed that both documented 2024 shipments loaded at the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal before departure for Myanmar. The January 2024 shipment (HUITONG 78, approximately USD 8 million in value, arriving Thilawa 14 January 2024) was preceded by a stop at the Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal and involved Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. (Singapore) as the second-to-last seller before the fuel transited Vietnam. The February 2024 shipment (HUITONG 78, approximately USD 8 million, arriving Thilawa 29 February 2024) was preceded by a stop at the BP Hua Dong terminal in Ningbo, China, and involved CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. as the second-to-last seller. Amnesty International noted the same supply chain mechanism continued in 2024 as in 2023. (AI Jul 2024)

OFAC sector determination applicability

Justice for Myanmar's May 2025 report explicitly noted that Hai Linh's activities continued "despite the US Government's issuance of a determination on the jet fuel sector that 'allows for sanctions to be imposed on any foreign individual or entity determined to operate in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy.'" This directly establishes that Hai Linh's documented role as a transit point and trader in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain falls within the scope of the August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014. (JFM May 2025)

Key Relationships
•HUITONG 78 / BARAAWE 1 (IMO 9646479): The vessel that transported six of the seven 2023 shipments and both 2024 shipments via the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal to Thilawa. HUITONG 78 was subsequently renamed BARAAWE 1. Per the West of England P&I Club, the vessel is owned by an affiliate of the Chinese state-owned defence company China Aerospace Science and Industry Corp (CASIC). Justice for Myanmar calls for the vessel's designation across all five jurisdictions. See Profile 6.1. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; JFM Feb 2026)
•YIDA 8 (IMO 9936941): The vessel that transported the first documented 2023 shipment (April 2023, 12.81 kt) via the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal. Liberian-flagged oil tanker; ownership not confirmed by Amnesty International at time of publication. See Profile 6.3. (AI Jan 2024)
•Sahara Energy International Pte. Ltd. (Singapore): The Singapore branch of global fuel trader Sahara Energy, identified by Vietnamese customs data as the second-to-last seller before fuel transited Vietnam for the January 2024 shipment. Sahara Energy sold fuel to a Vietnamese company, which then received it at Hai Linh's Cai Mep terminal. Sahara Energy did not respond to Amnesty International's contact. (AI Jul 2024)
•CNOOC Trading (Singapore) Pte. Ltd.: The Singapore trading branch of Chinese state-owned enterprise China National Offshore Oil Corporation (CNOOC), identified by Vietnamese customs data as the second-to-last seller before fuel transited Vietnam for the February 2024 shipment. CNOOC Trading did not respond to Amnesty International's contact. (AI Jul 2024)
•BB Energy (Asia) Pte. Ltd. (Singapore): The Singapore branch of privately owned BB Energy (Dubai), identified by Vietnamese customs data as the second-to-last seller in at least three of the seven 2023 shipments that transited Vietnam. BB Energy sold fuel to Vietnamese companies before it arrived at Hai Linh's terminal. Amnesty International noted uncertainty about whether BB Energy knew the fuel would ultimately reach Myanmar. BB Energy did not respond to Amnesty International's contact. (AI Jan 2024)
•Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited (Thilawa, Yangon): The physical destination terminal in Myanmar to which all nine Hai Linh-transited shipments were delivered. During the 2023 and early 2024 period the terminal was operated under the name Shoon Energy Thilawa Terminal Company Limited before arriving at its current registered name. See Profile 1.3. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•Pengerang Independent Terminals (Malaysia) / Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal / BP Hua Dong Terminal (Ningbo, China) / CNOOC Terminal (Huizhou, China): Upstream storage points documented in the supply chain preceding the Cai Mep terminal. Two 2023 shipments (April and May) passed through Pengerang before reaching Cai Mep. The January 2024 shipment was preceded by the Vopak Singapore Banyan Terminal. The February 2024 shipment was preceded by the BP Hua Dong terminal. The August 2023 shipment was preceded by the CNOOC terminal in Huizhou. For cross-reference with all documented shipments, see the master shipment table. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024)
•AG&P LNG / Nebula Energy (Cai Mep LNG Terminal partners): AG&P LNG, a Singapore-based LNG terminal and infrastructure company and subsidiary of US-headquartered Nebula Energy (Florida), acquired a 49% stake in Hai Linh's Cai Mep LNG Terminal on 7 March 2024. The USD 500 million Cai Mep LNG Terminal is a distinct facility from the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal used in the Myanmar aviation fuel supply chain; both are at the Cai Mep port complex and both are owned by Hai Linh, but they are separate assets serving different purposes. AG&P LNG and Hai Linh also established Vietfirst Gas, a downstream joint venture for LNG trading and distribution. The terminal commenced commissioning in May 2024. On 25 May 2025, the terminal received its first commissioning cargo, loaded from Russia's Sakhalin 2 LNG project on 10 April 2025 aboard the Blue Dragon 1 tanker. Nebula Energy acquired its majority stake in AG&P LNG in early 2024 for USD 300 million; AG&P LNG's US-headquartered parent status creates a direct OFAC compliance nexus: if Hai Linh were designated under the August 2023 jet fuel sector determination, Nebula Energy as a US company would face OFAC obligations regarding its existing contractual and investment relationships with a designated entity, absent specific OFAC authorisation. (AG&P LNG press release, 7 March 2024; Reuters/multiple sources, June 2025)

Designation Status
Entity USUKEUCanadaAustralia
Hai Linh Co. Ltd.Profiled entity; Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal operator and trader; not sanctioned in any jurisdiction

No government has designated Hai Linh Co. Ltd. in any jurisdiction as of April 2026. Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 press release was the first publicly documented call for Hai Linh's designation, explicitly naming it and calling on the EU, UK, Canadian, US and Australian governments to act. The August 2023 OFAC sector determination under Executive Order 14014, which allows sanctions on any entity operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy, provides the primary legal basis for a potential US designation. Equivalent legal bases exist under UK, EU, Canadian and Australian Myanmar sanctions frameworks, which cover entities making available economic resources that benefit Myanmar's security forces. (JFM Feb 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)

The Hai Linh sanctions gap: Hai Linh Co. Ltd. operated as the exclusive transit hub for every documented international aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar between April 2023 and February 2024, a period covering nine confirmed or likely deliveries. No other Vietnamese entity or facility appears in the documented supply chain for this period. Amnesty International explicitly named Hai Linh as a company whose full value chain due diligence obligations under the UN Guiding Principles are engaged. Despite the clear and documented role, no government has sanctioned Hai Linh. The OFAC August 2023 sector determination provides direct legal authority for a US designation without requiring proof of direct military end-use. Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 call for coordinated designation provides civil society's evidentiary basis for action. Vietnam's absence from any coordinated Myanmar sanctions coalition means that absent direct pressure on Hanoi, Hai Linh may continue to serve as a transit conduit even if designated by Western governments. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; JFM May 2025; JFM Feb 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)

6
Recommended Actions
•Designate Hai Linh Co. Ltd. in all five jurisdictions: Hai Linh is the only terminal documented as serving as the transit hub for every aviation fuel shipment to Myanmar in 2023 and the first two months of 2024. Nine shipments transited its terminal in a fourteen-month period. For submissions to OFAC, the August 2023 jet fuel sector determination under Executive Order 14014 provides the legal authority: Hai Linh is a company operating in the jet fuel sector of the Burmese economy, and no proof of direct military end-use is required. Justice for Myanmar's February 2026 press release provides a publicly documented evidentiary basis for designation submissions to OFAC, UK OFSI, EU EEAS, Canadian Global Affairs and Australian DFAT. These submissions should reference Amnesty International's January 2024 and July 2024 investigative findings, which were communicated to the Vietnamese government prior to publication. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; JFM May 2025; JFM Feb 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Engage the Vietnamese government directly on Hai Linh and the Cai Mep terminal: Vietnam has no explicit public policy opposing fuel transfers to Myanmar. The 4 April 2024 UN Human Rights Council resolution, which Vietnam did not oppose while holding a Council seat, provides the most direct international legal hook: it called on all member states to refrain from the export, sale or transfer of jet fuel when there are reasonable grounds to suspect it might be used to violate human rights, and the two Amnesty International investigations provide exactly that documented evidence in relation to Vietnamese territory. Formal diplomatic engagement should be directed at the Ministry of Industry and Trade, Ministry of Transport, and customs authorities, requesting that Vietnam investigate Hai Linh's documented role, consider domestic regulatory or enforcement action against use of the Cai Mep port for Myanmar-bound aviation fuel transshipment, and confirm whether Vietnamese customs data is available to sanctioning jurisdictions. The Vietnamese government received advance notice of Amnesty International's January 2024 findings; further follow-up is warranted. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; JFM Feb 2026)
•Engage AG&P LNG and its US-headquartered parent Nebula Energy on their OFAC compliance exposure: AG&P LNG's 49% stake in the Cai Mep LNG Terminal and its Vietfirst Gas joint venture with Hai Linh create direct reputational and potential regulatory exposure for a US-headquartered firm. If OFAC were to designate Hai Linh under the August 2023 jet fuel sector determination, Nebula Energy as a US company would face direct OFAC compliance obligations regarding its existing contractual and investment relationships with a designated entity, absent specific OFAC authorisation. Civil society should engage AG&P LNG and Nebula Energy requesting: conduct and public disclosure of human rights due diligence on Hai Linh as required under the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights; and use of their leverage as a 49% co-investor to press Hai Linh to ensure the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal is not reactivated for Myanmar-bound aviation fuel shipments. This engagement may also influence US government designation decision-making, given Nebula Energy's US nexus. (AI Jan 2024; JFM Feb 2026; OFAC Aug 2023)
•Investigate current operational status and whether Hai Linh continues to serve as a transit point: The documented use of the Cai Mep Petroleum Terminal covers April 2023 through February 2024. No Hai Linh-transited shipments are documented in 2025, when the supply chain shifted to direct deliveries by REEF and NOBLE to Thilawa. Investigators and sanctions authorities should verify whether Hai Linh continues to operate the Cai Mep petroleum terminal and whether it could resume serving as a transit hub if the 2025 supply chain route comes under greater scrutiny or disruption. (AI Jan 2024; AI Jul 2024; AI Jan 2026)
•Use ASEAN mechanisms to press Vietnam to restrict the Cai Mep supply chain: Justice for Myanmar's May 2025 ASEAN companies report documented that ASEAN member state companies have helped sustain junta atrocities while ASEAN "continues to impede an effective international response." ASEAN member states with stated Myanmar commitments, including Malaysia as a recent ASEAN Chair, should be engaged to use ASEAN mechanisms to press Vietnam to restrict the use of its ports for Myanmar-bound aviation fuel transshipment. This engagement should reference the 4 April 2024 UN Human Rights Council resolution as reflecting the international community's existing consensus. (JFM May 2025; JFM Feb 2026)
