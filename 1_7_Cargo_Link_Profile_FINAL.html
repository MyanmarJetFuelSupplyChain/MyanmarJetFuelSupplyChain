Cargo Link Company Limited & Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited
Profile 1.7
Summary Assessment
Cargo Link Company Limited (CLC) is a Myanmar-registered company owned by May Thwe Aung (100%), an Asia Sun Group-affiliated individual, and the corporate parent of Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited (CLPL). Asia Sun Group is the ultimate parent network. CLC is beneficially owned 100% by May Thwe Aung, identified in Deadly Cargo as one of the key players in the Asia Sun Group. May Thwe Aung has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction. CLPL was originally a joint venture with Thai company Pongrawe Co. Ltd. before Cargo Link Co. Ltd. bought out Pongrawe's stake on 24 June 2022, simultaneously renaming the entity. Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics is the operational entity and functioned as the junta's primary domestic distributor of aviation fuel between at least October 2021 and August 2022, using a fleet of tanker trucks to deliver Jet A-1 and avgas to NEPAS storage facilities at commercial airports and directly to military air bases across Myanmar. Amnesty International documented 54 invoices issued by the company (then named Cargo Link Pongrawe Logistics Company Limited) to NEPAS, covering October 2021 to January 2022, and over 1,500 truck trips across the December 2021 to August 2022 period, with a combined volume of 54,861,888 litres delivered to NEPAS facilities and air bases. The company also made deliveries to and from the military-controlled Mann Refinery. The two entities sit at the last mile of the aviation fuel supply chain, connecting the Thilawa port terminal to the air bases from which war crimes have been launched. CLC is sanctioned by the UK and Canada. CLPL is sanctioned by the US, Canada and Australia. Neither has been designated by the EU. The asymmetric sanctions coverage across the parent and subsidiary in different jurisdictions represents a structural gap in the international sanctions regime.

1
Role in the Aviation Fuel Supply Chain

Cargo Link Company Limited (CLC) is the holding entity and Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited (CLPL) is its operational subsidiary responsible for the physical last-mile distribution of aviation fuel across Myanmar. CLC is owned by May Thwe Aung (100%), an Asia Sun Group-affiliated individual, with Asia Sun Group as the ultimate parent network; CLC holds CLPL as its listed subsidiary on the UK sanctions list. The operational evidence documented by civil society and the UN Special Rapporteur pertains almost entirely to CLPL, which carried out deliveries under contract to NEPAS using tanker trucks. The two entities are profiled together because they constitute a single functional unit within the Asia Sun Group aviation fuel distribution structure: CLC as the corporate shell, CLPL as the vehicle fleet operator.

Corporate history and origin of the NEPAS contract

CLPL, then operating under its former name Cargo Link Pongrawe Logistics Company Limited, was originally a joint venture between Thai company Pongrawe Co. Ltd. and Cargo Link Co. Ltd., the latter owned by May Thwe Aung (100%) and affiliated with Asia Sun Group. In 2017, NEPAS contracted its truck transportation of aviation fuel to this joint venture, establishing a distribution relationship that predated the February 2021 coup by four years. On 24 June 2022, Cargo Link Co. Ltd. bought out Pongrawe Co. Ltd.'s stake and simultaneously renamed the company Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited. Since that date the entity has been solely owned by Cargo Link Co. Ltd. and, through it, by May Thwe Aung and the Asia Sun Group network. (AI Nov 2022)

Distribution role: documented operations October 2021 to August 2022

Amnesty International identified CLPL, then operating under its former name Cargo Link Pongrawe Logistics Company Limited, as "the main distributor of Jet A-1 and avgas" in Myanmar for NEPAS, using tanker trucks to deliver aviation fuel after it arrived at the Thilawa port terminal. The report drew on two sets of confidential documents: 54 invoices issued by the company (then named Cargo Link Pongrawe Logistics Company Limited) to NEPAS covering October 2021 to January 2022, and three datasets covering over 1,500 truck trips from December 2021 to August 2022. (AI Nov 2022)

The documented deliveries fell into two categories. The first comprised deliveries to NEPAS storage facilities at commercial airports, which NEPAS paid for. The second comprised deliveries directly to military air bases. Between December 2021 and early August 2022, CLPL delivered 37,837,844 litres of Jet A-1 to NEPAS airport storage facilities and 17,024,044 litres directly to air bases, for a combined total of 54,861,888 litres. CLPL also made deliveries to and from the military-controlled Mann Refinery, from which the military distributed aviation fuel onward to Meikhtila, Hmawbi, Nay Pyi Taw, Taungoo and Monywa air bases, two of which Amnesty International linked directly to war crimes. (AI Nov 2022)

Amnesty International's analysis of the truck fleet used in these operations found that approximately 50% of trucks were used for NEPAS airport storage facility deliveries, approximately 50% were used for military air base deliveries, and approximately 15% were used for both. Trucks used for NEPAS facility deliveries were almost all owned or managed by Cargo Link Pongrawe or Asia Sun Logistics Co. Ltd., another Asia Sun Group subsidiary. Trucks used for military air base deliveries were affiliated with a number of Myanmar companies, including Asia Sun Group. (AI Nov 2022)

Almost all airports in Myanmar have at some point been considered dual use because the military has used most civilian airports for military purposes, even if only occasionally. Amnesty International identified five airports where commercial and military facilities formally share the same airstrip: Heho Airport and Heho air base; Mandalay International Airport and Tada-U air base; Myeik Airport and Myeik air base; Nay Pyi Taw International Airport and Nay Pyi Taw air base; and Yangon International Airport and Mingaladon air base. This means fuel delivered to these airports' NEPAS storage facilities cannot be meaningfully segregated between civilian and military use. (AI Nov 2022)

Of the five air bases from which military aircraft involved in attacks departed between March 2021 and August 2022, Amnesty International directly linked four, Hmawbi, Magway, Tada-U and Taungoo, to attacks amounting to war crimes. Amnesty's research established that CLPL's deliveries to NEPAS storage facilities at Nay Pyi Taw and Mandalay airports supplied fuel that reached air bases used for those attacks, because satellite imagery showed no separate military storage facility and because air force defectors confirmed that fuel held in NEPAS facilities was accessed by the air force. (AI Nov 2022)

Justice for Myanmar described CLPL in November 2023 as "the junta's main transporter of jet fuel to military bases and airports across Myanmar." OFAC's March 2023 designation basis stated that CLPL was involved in "the importation, storage, and distribution of jet fuel to Burma/Myanmar's military." (JFM Nov 2023; OFAC Mar 2023)

Document authentication

Amnesty International assessed the confidential invoice and trip datasets as authentic on the following grounds: the source was credible to Amnesty International; licence plate numbers of tanker trucks in the datasets could be independently verified through the Myanmar Car Checker application; photographs of eleven of the trucks appeared on the public Facebook account of the company operating them; a former air force member provided two additional photographs of tanker trucks at air bases whose licence plate numbers matched entries in the military air base dataset; and a YouTube video (FUSO Myanmar, "Customer Testimonial: Pongrawe Cargo Link Logistics with FUSO TV 3340") showcasing the use of tanker trucks by Cargo Link Pongrawe provided a further independent source of photographic corroboration. (AI Nov 2022)

Key Relationships
•Asia Sun Group Company Limited / Shoon Group: Ultimate parent network of both entities. CLC is owned by May Thwe Aung, an Asia Sun Group-affiliated individual, with Asia Sun Group confirmed as the ultimate controlling network per the UNSR's June 2024 Figure 9 sanctions network diagram. Asia Sun Group is sanctioned in all five jurisdictions. See Profile 1.4. (UNSR Jun 2024)
•May Thwe Aung: Beneficial owner of Cargo Link Company Limited (100%), per the Asia Sun Group entity table in Deadly Cargo. May Thwe Aung is identified in Deadly Cargo footnote 224 as one of three key individuals in their 30s who were among the central players in the Asia Sun Group alongside Win Kyaw Aung and Myo Myint Aung. She has not been sanctioned in any jurisdiction and does not appear on any known sanctions list. Her unsanctioned status means the individual ultimately controlling CLC and, through it, CLPL faces no targeted financial measures. (AI Nov 2022)
•Asia Sun Logistics Co. Ltd.: A further Asia Sun Group-affiliated entity whose trucks were used to service NEPAS storage facility deliveries. Amnesty International found that trucks used for NEPAS airport deliveries were almost all owned or managed by Cargo Link Pongrawe or Asia Sun Logistics Co. Ltd. This establishes Asia Sun Logistics as an operational participant in the distribution network alongside CLPL. (AI Nov 2022)
•National Energy Prime Aviation Services Company Limited (NEPAS): Primary client of CLPL for tanker truck distribution services. NEPAS paid CLPL for deliveries to NEPAS storage facilities at commercial airports. The 54 invoices document NEPAS as the paying party for airport deliveries. Amnesty International noted that the Myanmar military also appears to have contracted CLPL separately for air base deliveries. NEPAS is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 1.2. (AI Nov 2022)
•Myanma Petrochemical Enterprise (MPE): State-owned enterprise that controls the downstream aviation fuel sector in Myanmar. CLPL's deliveries to and from the military-controlled Mann Refinery formed part of the documented distribution network. MPE is unsanctioned in all jurisdictions. See Profile 1.1. (AI Nov 2022)
•Mann Refinery (Mann Thanbayarkan Petrochemical Complex): Military-controlled refinery that served as a storage facility for Jet A-1. NEPAS paid CLPL for truck deliveries to and from Mann Refinery. Documents reviewed by Amnesty International show that the military then distributed fuel from Mann Refinery to Meikhtila, Hmawbi, Nay Pyi Taw, Taungoo and Monywa air bases. See Profile 1.8. (AI Nov 2022)
•Puma Energy Asia Sun Company Limited (PEAS) / Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited: The Thilawa port terminal from which CLPL collected aviation fuel for onward distribution by tanker truck. At the time of the documented operations, the terminal was operated by PEAS. It was subsequently renamed Myan Oil Terminal Company Limited following Puma Energy's exit. See Profile 1.3. (AI Nov 2022)

The UK sanctioned CLC on 31 January 2023 on the basis that it had been and was involved in making available economic resources, specifically aviation fuel, directly or indirectly to or for the benefit of the Myanmar security forces. The US sanctioned CLPL on 24 March 2023 for involvement in the importation, storage and distribution of jet fuel to the Myanmar military. Canada sanctioned CLC in its January 2023 round and CLPL in its October 2023 round. Australia sanctioned CLPL in its February 2024 round. (UK Jan 2023; JFM Mar 2023; JFM Nov 2023; UNSR Jun 2024)

Sanctions gap: Canada has designated both CLC (January 2023) and CLPL (October 2023), though in separate rounds. No other jurisdiction has designated both entities. The UK designated CLC but not CLPL. The US and Australia designated CLPL but not CLC. The EU has designated neither entity, despite having sanctioned Asia Sun Group's parent structure and other network members. Coordinated designation of CLPL by the UK, of CLC by the US and Australia, and of both entities by the EU would close these structural gaps and ensure that no jurisdiction holds only half the corporate structure within its sanctions perimeter. (UNSR Jun 2024; AI Nov 2022)

Advocacy Interventions to Date
•November 2022. Amnesty International's Deadly Cargo report, produced in research collaboration with Justice for Myanmar, publicly identifies CLPL (then named Cargo Link Pongrawe Logistics Company Limited) as the primary domestic distributor of Jet A-1, documenting its tanker truck operations in detail and calling for it to be sanctioned. The report names the Asia Sun Group, of which CLPL forms part, among the companies that should immediately suspend aviation fuel supply to the Myanmar military. (AI Nov 2022)
•November 2022. Justice for Myanmar publishes its Myanmar military jet fuel companies for targeted sanctions report, identifying both CLC and CLPL within the Asia Sun Group network and calling for their designation across multiple jurisdictions. (JFM Nov 2022)
•31 January 2023. The UK designates Cargo Link Company Limited as part of its coordinated round targeting the Asia Sun Group aviation fuel network, marking the second anniversary of the coup. CLC is listed with its subsidiary CLPL on the UK sanctions list. Canada designates CLC in the same January 2023 round. Justice for Myanmar welcomes the designations. (UK Jan 2023; JFM Mar 2023)
•24 March 2023. The US designates Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited for involvement in the importation, storage and distribution of jet fuel to the Myanmar military, as part of a broader round targeting the Asia Sun Group network ahead of Myanmar Armed Forces Day. Justice for Myanmar welcomes the US action. (JFM Mar 2023; OFAC Mar 2023)
•October 2023. Canada designates Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited in a coordinated round with the US and UK. Justice for Myanmar welcomes the round and identifies CLPL's former name as Cargo Link Pongrawe Logistics Company Limited, confirming the name change. JFM characterises CLPL as "the junta's main transporter of jet fuel to military bases and airports across Myanmar." (JFM Nov 2023)
•February 2024 round. Australia designates Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited, bringing the number of jurisdictions that have sanctioned CLPL to three. CLC remains unsanctioned by the US, EU and Australia. CLPL remains unsanctioned by the UK and EU. (UNSR Jun 2024 Figure 9)

Recommended Actions
•Designate May Thwe Aung individually in all jurisdictions: May Thwe Aung is the 100% beneficial owner of Cargo Link Company Limited and, through it, the indirect beneficial owner of Cargo Link Petroleum Logistics Company Limited. She is identified in Deadly Cargo as one of the three key players in the Asia Sun Group. Her ownership of the entity that controlled the junta's primary aviation fuel distributor, combined with her complete absence from any sanctions list, represents a significant individual accountability gap. As an individual beneficial owner of sanctioned or sanctions-eligible entities, she falls squarely within standard individual designation criteria in all five jurisdictions. (AI Nov 2022)
•Designate both CLC and CLPL in the EU: The EU has sanctioned Asia Sun Group and its broader network but has not designated either Cargo Link entity. Both CLC and CLPL are documented as integral to the aviation fuel distribution chain that supplies the Myanmar military. The UK's designation of CLC (MYA0062) and the US and Australian designations of CLPL provide direct multilateral precedent for EU action. The OFAC designation basis for CLPL, covering importation, storage and distribution of jet fuel to the Myanmar military, maps directly to EU Myanmar sanctions criteria. (AI Nov 2022; UNSR Jun 2024; OFAC Mar 2023)
•Designate CLPL in the UK and CLC in the US and Australia: The asymmetric pattern of designations, with the UK and Canada covering only the parent and the US and Australia covering only the subsidiary, leaves each jurisdiction with half the corporate structure outside its sanctions perimeter. The UK designated CLC under the basis that it makes aviation fuel available to Myanmar security forces; CLPL as its operational subsidiary carries out that function directly and falls within the same basis. OFAC designated CLPL; CLC as its corporate parent and the entity through which Asia Sun Group controls it is a logical extension of that designation under OFAC's 50 percent rule and E.O. 14014. Civil society should submit the AI Nov 2022 evidence package and UNSR Jun 2024 Figure 9 to OFAC, OFSI, and the Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade as the evidentiary basis for filling the respective gaps. (AI Nov 2022; UNSR Jun 2024; UK Jan 2023; OFAC Mar 2023)
•Investigate current operational status: The documented distribution operations cover the period from October 2021 to August 2022. No subsequent source has confirmed whether CLPL continues to operate tanker truck deliveries to air bases under the same name or has restructured. Given the pattern of renaming and restructuring observed across the Asia Sun / Shoon Group network after public exposure, investigators and sanctions authorities should verify whether CLPL remains the active distribution vehicle or whether a successor entity has assumed its role. The UNSR's June 2024 report does not name CLPL in the updated 2023 transaction structure, which documents procurement at the purchasing level rather than at the domestic distribution level. (AI Nov 2022; UNSR Jun 2024)
